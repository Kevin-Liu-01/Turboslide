# Audit: people on the same slide

Read on the worktree `Turboslide-realtime` (branch `realtime/round` at `f50b209d`, origin/main) on 2026-10-01. Line numbers are of the files at that commit. The target is Google Slides as its own pages and the press describe it; every Google claim below carries its source URL. Everything about Turboslide was read in the code first and then measured on production, https://www.turboslide.com, with the drive `people-drive/drive.mjs` (Playwright 1.62.1 from the worktree's `node_modules`, headless Chromium, two browser contexts at 1440 by 900, device scale factor 1, keys 70 ms apart). Three full runs were made (`people-drive/facts-run1.json`, `facts-run2.json`, `facts-run3.json`, the pictures of run 3 at the top of the folder and of runs 1 and 2 under `run1/` and `run2/`) and two probes of the filmstrip chip (`people-drive/probe-card-marks.mjs`, the pictures `probe-*.png`). Five scratch decks were made from `/new` and each was removed by its id through `deck.info`, `deck.trash` and `deck.remove` with the bearer; `/edit/<id>` answered 404 for every one (`untitled-20261001-ogba`, `-f1u1`, `-7dyw`, `-wk6u`, `-4i57`). The bearer reached the drive as an environment variable from a wrapper outside the repository and is printed nowhere. A is the deck's owner, B is an anonymous editor admitted by the link `share.setGeneralAccess` opened; both are anonymous principals with generated labels, which is every seller on production.

## 1. What exists today for two people on one slide

### 1.1 The wire

- A tab's presence state is `PresencePost` (`packages/realtime/src/protocol.ts` 238 to 256): `clientId`, `clock`, `slideId`, `selection { blockIds (64 at most), caret { blockId, path, offset?, range? } }` (226 to 231), `pointer { x, y }` in sheet units, `follow`, `pointerOn`, `presenting`; at most 2048 bytes (36) and 15 states a second (55). The server adds the identity fields and never reads them from the body (`apps/studio/src/server/room.ts` `rosterEntryFor` 2449 to 2489): `principalId`, `label`, `trust`, `mark`, `hueSlot` 0 to 5, `kind`, `role`; the pointer is kept only for an owner or editor among the first 20 clients by join order and only with `pointerOn` (2473 to 2480; `LIVE_POINTERS_MAX` protocol.ts 41).
- A state change posts after an 80 ms batch (`PRESENCE_BATCH_MS` protocol.ts 54; `packages/realtime/client/room-client.ts` `setPresence` 1986 to 1998). The heartbeat is 5 s while the pointer, the selection or the slide moved in the last 30 s and 10 s otherwise (`heartbeatWaitMs` 1763 to 1766, `heartbeat` 1775 to 1785; `PRESENCE_QUIET_AFTER_MS` 238; protocol.ts 60 to 66). A row is stale after 30 s and expires after 120 s (protocol.ts 67 to 68). Measured on production: 45 to 62 presence POSTs per tab over the two and a half minutes of a run (`facts-run3.json` `network`), every one answered 200.
- The stream carries `hello` with the roster, then one `presence` event per state (`{ clientId, clock, state }`) and `leave` (protocol.ts 320 to 340). The route writes the entry with `room.channel.presence.set(deckId, clientId, entry, PRESENCE_EXPIRY_MS)` (`apps/studio/src/routes/api/decks.$deckId.presence.ts` 117 to 118).

### 1.2 What a tab reports

`apps/studio/src/editor/EditorRoot.tsx` 787 to 805 reports the slide, `selection.blockIds` (the selected block and the multi selection, 64 at most), the caret as `{ blockId, path: '/<pointer>', range }` while an inline session is open, and `presenting`. It reports no `pointer`: no file under `apps/studio/src` or `packages/viewer/src` writes `pointer: { x, y }` into a presence state (the people round's `audit-presence.md` defect 3, line 89, still holds; `grep -rn "pointer:" apps/studio/src/editor/EditorRoot.tsx` finds none). The client maps a roster entry to a participant in `apps/studio/src/editor/identity-index.ts` `participantOf` 39 to 80: the caret's `offset` is `range[0]` when the wire carried no offset (67), `idle` is always `false` and `lastSeenAt` is the mapping time (77 to 78), so the 30 s caret dim of `CARET_DIM_MS` cannot fire (`audit-presence.md` defect 8, line 99, still holds).

### 1.3 What the overlay draws

`packages/chrome/src/CollabLayer.tsx` 68 to 76 mounts `RemoteCursors` inside the editor overlay when the shell has presence and the tab is not presenting. `packages/chrome/src/presence/RemoteCursors.tsx`:

- Filters the roster to the participants on this slide (95).
- Draws one "being edited by" outline per selected block in the first holder's hue, with at most two name flags at the block's top right and then a "+N" flag (113 to 126, 168 to 218). A block the own selection holds gets no outline, the own ink ring wins; the remote flags still draw (96 to 98, 179). A block the remote caret sits in is excluded from the outline list (116).
- Draws the remote caret as a 2 px bar at the run's line box with a 120 by 18 flag above it (129 to 148, 219 to 243), the flag faded 3 s after the caret last moved and the caret dimmed 30 s after the last update (144 to 145; `presence-model.ts` 32 to 34). The caret's box is measured from the sheet DOM in `packages/chrome/src/presence/remote-caret.ts` `remoteCaretBox` 61 to 97: the run element `data-run="<blockId>/<path>"`, a collapsed `Range` at the plain offset, the rectangle read against the run's own rectangle; when the range cannot be measured the caret is drawn at the run's start (87 to 90). The sheet body the carets are measured in is `EditorOverlayView.body` (`packages/viewer/src/Editor.tsx` 409 to 414).
- Draws the pointer as a 12 by 16 polygon moved by `translate3d` with the flag beside it, under `pointersDrawn` (163, 245 to 284; `presence-model.ts` 250 to 258: pointers visible, not present mode, at most 20 participants). Since no tab publishes a pointer (1.2), the pointer layer draws only from simulated data.
- Flags that would overlap stack 20 px higher (`presence-model.ts` `stackFlags` 264 to 287).

### 1.4 The chips

- The title row holds four 24 px slots in roster order and a "+N" opener (`packages/chrome/src/presence/PresenceSlot.tsx` 86 to 130; `presence-model.ts` `PRESENCE_SLOTS` 26, `slotChips` 45 to 52). A presence event replaces the row where it stands, so join order holds and the slots never swap (`apps/studio/src/editor/controller.tsx` 2081 to 2084).
- The filmstrip card carries `FilmstripMarks` (`packages/chrome/src/presence/FilmstripMarks.tsx` 22 to 58): 16 px chips for the people on that slide, at most three and then a "+N" chip, absolute at the card's top right (`packages/chrome/src/Filmstrip.css` 219 to 232), mounted after the `Thumb` in the card frame (`packages/chrome/src/Filmstrip.tsx` 404 to 407). The outline density draws one 12 px chip (`OutlineMarks` 61 to 79).

### 1.5 Follow

- `canFollow` (`presence-model.ts` 176 to 184): the caller needs the `follow` capability, the target is not an agent, is an editor or owner, and has a slide open. An anonymous editor can be followed since the polish round's item 104.
- The `follow` capability is a role cell of editor and above (`packages/identity/src/access.ts` 184 to 195) and is in `NOT_BY_LINK` (160 to 170), so a person admitted by a link never holds it, whatever the link's role. The window API refuses `presence.follow` without it (`controller.tsx` 3986 to 3991; `apps/studio/src/server/authorize.ts` 524).
- The roster row `title.presence.follow` is parked behind Tools > Advanced tools (`packages/chrome/src/menus/model.ts` 794 to 799, `advanced: true`); `RosterMenu.tsx` 69 and 116 to 119 draw the Follow word only while that row is present and otherwise offer the one time "Go to slide N" (`PresenceSlot.tsx` 101 to 116). `collab.follow.anonymous-editor` is the parked row of record on production (VERIFICATION.md 9241, 9260).
- Following moves the stage on every `presence` event of the followed client whose `slideId` differs from the active slide (`controller.tsx` 2085 to 2093); it ends on the followed client's `leave` (2096 to 2098) and on the tab's own commit (`stopFollowing` 1632 to 1634, its one caller 2597). A click on another slide, a comment, Slideshow and Version history do not end it (`audit-presence.md` defect 7, line 97). `FollowingPlate.tsx` 22 to 48 draws the 240 by 24 plate with Stop.

### 1.6 How a state reaches the other browser on production

Production runs the blob tier (`TURBOSLIDE_REALTIME=blob`). Every function instance holds its own memory channel; a stream is held by one instance for its life, and every presence POST is routed by the platform on its own (`packages/store/src/presence-store.ts` 1 to 6 records the cause). The roster the instances agree on is the record `decks/<id>/.turboslide/presence.json` (7 to 11, 96 to 107):

- An instance pushes its rows to the record at most every 5 s (`PRESENCE_PUSH_SPACING_MS` 76) and only on a change that travels: a join, a leave, a slide change, the identity fields, the presenting flag, or a row whose life is under half (26 to 32, 78, 88).
- `selection`, `follow` and `pointerOn` are `PRESENCE_VOLATILE_FIELDS` (99, 37 to 38): they ride the next push and start none. The `pointer` never travels at all (`PRESENCE_OMITTED_FIELDS` 93, 20 to 22).
- The other instances read the record on their pulse tick: 2 s while the deck is in use or has company, 10 s when one tab is alone and quiet (`packages/store/src/pulse.ts` 30 to 32; `packages/realtime/src/blob.ts` 494 to 510). `docs/hosting.md` 170 still says 1 s; `docs/SYNC.md` 3.10 (161 to 176) is the version the code follows.

So a caret or a selection posted by B reaches A at once only when B's POST lands on the instance that holds A's stream (the memory channel announces it to that instance's listeners). Otherwise it waits for the next push of B's row, which is the row's half life refresh, and then A's instance's next tick. The drive measured both paths (section 3).

### 1.7 Agent writes

`announceAgentWrite` (`controller.tsx` 1660 to 1670) announces entries whose author kind is `agent`, whose client id is not this tab's or one of its earlier ids, and which carry mutations; nothing else reaches the banner. This pass did not drive an agent write; the standing finding is `docs/POLISH.md` section 6.

## 2. What Google Slides shows

- The caret with the name flag as a person types: "much like in Google Docs, you'll now see contributors' cursors with names in real-time as they type" (9to5Google, 5 November 2013, https://9to5google.com/2013/11/05/google-adds-real-time-text-cursors-improved-themes-to-slides/).
- The pointer in the person's colour with a floating name: live pointers, "see each other's mouse pointers in real-time", turned on under "View > Live pointers > show my pointer" or "the pointer icon in the toolbar", off by default, rolled out from 18 September 2023 (Google Workspace Updates, https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2023/09/collaborate-more-seamlessly-with-live-pointers-google-slides.html). "You can only show your pointer if you have edit access to the presentation, but all access levels can view pointers"; pointers stop when "More than 20 collaborators are in the presentation" (Google Docs Editors Help, https://support.google.com/docs/answer/13853477). The colour and the name label beside the pointer are the press's description (9to5Google, 18 September 2023, https://9to5google.com/2023/09/18/google-slides-live-pointers/; `docs/gslides-parity/research-3/01-google-collaboration.md` 27 to 29, source T3).
- The chip on the filmstrip card: "Active users appear as a brightly colored square or profile image on the thumbnail of the slide they're looking at" (Envato Tuts+, https://business.tutsplus.com/tutorials/google-slides-online-presentation-collaboration-tools--cms-30066).
- Follow: "At the top right, click on the collaborator's avatar to follow them"; "If you're following a collaborator, there will be a 'Following' badge beneath their avatar at the top right"; following stops when "The collaborator refreshes or leaves the presentation", "You make any edits to the presentation", "You add or edit a comment", "You click a different slide", "You enter Slideshow mode", "You open Version history"; it is refused when the collaborator is "on an unsupported device", "anonymous", lacks "Edit permission to the presentation", or has no "slide selected" (Google Docs Editors Help, https://support.google.com/docs/answer/12815819).
- The coloured selection outline per collaborator and an "editing" lock on a shape another person drags: no Google page or press page read for this round or the people round describes either (`research-3/01-google-collaboration.md` 48 records both as unverified). Google shows no conflict dialog and no lock on concurrent edits (the same page, 23, citing its source T22). The outline is therefore a Turboslide reading of the caret rule applied to a selection, not a measured Google behaviour; the rows of section 5 keep it because the chrome already draws it and a seller reads it the same way as the caret.

## 4. The defects

1. A caret or a selection arrives within a second only when the POST lands on the reader's instance. Mechanism: 1.6, `presence-store.ts` 37 to 38 and 99 (`selection` volatile, rides the next push), 76 (5 s floor), `pulse.ts` 30 (2 s tick), with the platform routing every POST on its own. Measured: carets in 305 to 790 ms in every step 1 run and in 3,447 ms or never within 3.6 s in step 2; a bare selection in 8.8 to 16.8 s in eight of eight rounds. Google draws the caret as the person types (section 2). This is the standing item 14 of `audit-collab.md`, now with the mechanism split in two paths.
2. The pointer is never published and could not travel if it were. `EditorRoot.tsx` 787 to 805 reports no pointer; `presence-store.ts` 93 strips it from the shared rows. The row `view.live-pointers.second-browser` is parked (VERIFICATION.md 9185). Google: live pointers for every access level (section 2).
3. The filmstrip card's chip is painted under the thumbnail. The chip is in the DOM at the card's top right, 16 by 16, `display: flex`, `opacity: 1`, `visibility: visible`, paper background and a 1 px edge border (`people-drive/facts-probe.json`, `cardMarks.A`), and nothing is painted at its place at 1x or 3x (`probe-a-card-corner.png`, `probe-c-card-corner-3x.png`). The thumbnail's live clone `div.ts-sheet.sheet.is-clone` (`packages/viewer/src/LiveClone.tsx` 63) is `position: absolute; z-index: 1` as computed in the page (`thumb.zIndexed` in the probe's facts), while `.ts-card-marks` has no z-index (`Filmstrip.css` 219 to 232) and `.ts-thumb` makes no stacking context (`packages/chrome/src/Thumb.css` 7 to 12), so the clone paints above the marks although the marks come later in the tree. Either `.ts-card-marks { z-index: 1 }` or `.ts-thumb { isolation: isolate }` makes the chip appear (`probe-a-card-corner-marks-z1.png`, `probe-a-card-corner-thumb-isolated.png`). The people round's rows read the chip from the DOM and never from the pixels, which is why it passed.
4. The remote caret is drawn at a stale or wrong place. In run 2 A drew B's caret at offset 0 of "alphabravo" while B's caret stood after "bravo" (`run2/04-r1-a-both-in-title.png`): the posted offset is B's offset in B's document at post time (`EditorRoot.tsx` 801 to 803) and nothing transforms it past the remote ops that landed since, and a range that cannot be measured falls to the run's start (`remote-caret.ts` 87 to 90). In run 3 A kept B's outline on the subtitle for the whole of a round while B typed in the title (`04-r1-a-both-in-title.png`), defect 1's late arrival shown as a wrong picture rather than a missing one.
5. A block being dragged by another person shows nothing. B's drag publishes no position until its commit; A sees the block jump 0.7 to 6.8 s after the release (step 3). When both hold the block the own ring wins and the remote outline is dropped (`RemoteCursors.tsx` 96 to 98, 179), so neither person sees that the other holds it; only the flag stays. Google's behaviour here is unverified (section 2); the requirement is Turboslide's own: a person must not be surprised by a block that moves under their selection.
6. Follow is not in the default view and is refused for every link visitor. `model.ts` 796 parks the row; `access.ts` 160 to 170 keeps `follow` out of every link's cells; `canFollow` 176 to 184 then refuses. Measured: the chip offers "Go to slide N" by default; with the switch on, Follow works for the owner and moved A 7.2 s after B's click, the slide change travelling on the push floor (1.6). Google follows any signed in editor from the avatar and refuses anonymous people (section 2); on a deployment where everyone is anonymous that rule, applied as written, would refuse everyone, which is the people round's and polish round's reason for following labels.
7. Following ends on two of Google's six triggers (1.5; `audit-presence.md` 97). Not re-driven this pass.
8. The caret never dims and the flag's fade is the only time signal (1.2; `identity-index.ts` 77 to 78). Not re-driven.
9. The joiner's chip is bound by the tick and the instance: 1.8 to 2.0 s from navigation in this pass, 2.6 to 9.9 s in the polish audit (`audit-collab.md` 13). The row `collab.presence.join-within-2s` was red twice on the preview and green on production's run of record (VERIFICATION.md 8305, 9154).
10. Two people's words in one title are kept in six of six rounds here, in 0.6 to 4.4 s; the standing red row `sync.title.concurrent-both-kept` (VERIFICATION.md 9186, "one word of two in a round of three") was not reproduced in this pass. The order is the server's: the second typist's word landed after the first in every round of this pass and before it in the polish audit's three rounds (`audit-collab.md` readings). A seller cannot predict it and neither tab shows the other's word while it is typed.

## 5. What a redesign must deliver

Rows for `docs/gslides-parity/focus/core-matrix.json`, in the file's shape; A and B are two browsers of two people on one deck, both on slide 1, on production or on a preview with the production tiers. Every bound is measured from the keystroke, the click or the release in the other browser, read in the DOM and in the pixels.

These bounds cannot be met by a presence record on the store polled at 2 s with a 5 s push floor (1.6), and they cannot depend on which instance answers a POST. Presence needs a channel of its own that every tab of a deck shares and that carries carets, selections, drag positions and pointers at the 80 ms batch: the redis tier that exists in code and never ran on production (`packages/realtime/src/redis.ts`, `select.ts`), a connection holding service, or a peer channel for the presence frames alone while the document stays on the store. The choice is the sync note's; this note only fixes what the people surfaces require of it. Two chrome fixes stand on their own and need no transport: the card chip's stacking (defect 3) and the Follow row's park and the link's `follow` cell (defect 6).

# Judge 2: the bill

Judge 2 of three in the Cloudflare design round, written 2026-10-01 in the worktree `/Users/kevinliu/repos/Turboslide-realtime` (branch `realtime/round`, `HEAD` `c978bb43`, six lanes editing). The lens: Kevin paying the bill. That is the Free plan fit at today and at ten times today, the day it breaks, and the honesty of the cost rows. This note read `design-a.md`, `design-b.md`, `design-c.md`, the nine notes of this folder (`read-channel.md`, `read-auth.md`, `read-store.md`, `read-runtime.md`, `research-durable-objects.md` W1, `research-d1-auth.md` W2, `research-r2.md` W3, `research-workers.md` W4, `research-access.md` W5), `docs/REALTIME.md` sections 1 to 3 and 5.4, and four tree facts the cost rows rest on. It fetched no page; every product figure below is a research note's and is cited by note and section with the URL that note gives. Nothing was edited outside this file. The load model throughout is `docs/SYNC.md` 4.3 at `HEAD` ("One editor hour is 12 minutes of editing at one edit per 5 s and 48 idle minutes") as W1 1.2 counts it: 144 ops, about 1,440 presence frames, 576 heartbeats, a room of two, 30 day months, 50 editor hours a day today's scenario and 500 the ten times one.

## 1. What the lens scores

Two of the five columns are the lens's own: `free_tier` (the Free plan holds at today and at ten times, with the math shown) and `honesty` (what cannot run is named with a source). The other three (`latency`, `migration`, `size`) are scored as the task asks and weigh the total. The winner under the lens is read from the two lens columns and from the total bill the design leaves Kevin, Cloudflare and Vercel together; the total out of 50 is a different reading and section 7 says where the two disagree.

The caps every design counts against, as the pages printed them on 2026-10-01 (W1 1.1, W2 1.1, W3 2.2, W4 section 1, W5 3.4): Durable Objects Free 100,000 requests a day, 13,000 GB-s a day, 5 million rows read a day, 100,000 rows written a day, 5 GB stored, reset at 00:00 UTC, and "If you exceed any one of the free tier limits, further operations of that type will fail with an error" (https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/platform/pricing/, dateModified 2026-09-30); Workers Free 100,000 requests a day and 10 ms of CPU per invocation, Paid $5.00 a month (https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/pricing/, 2026-08-28); D1 Free 5 million rows read and 100,000 written a day, 500 MB a database (https://developers.cloudflare.com/d1/platform/pricing/); R2 free 10 GB-month, 1 million Class A and 10 million Class B a month, egress free, then $4.50 and $0.36 a million (https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/pricing/, 2026-10-01), behind a subscription whose checkout is a purchase (https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/get-started/) and "A primary payment method is required to purchase Cloudflare products and services" (https://developers.cloudflare.com/billing/create-billing-profile/). The tree constants every design cites were read at `HEAD`: `CHECKPOINT_IDLE_MS` 2,000, `CHECKPOINT_MAX_MS` 10,000, `CHECKPOINT_MAX_ENTRIES` 2,000, `CHECKPOINT_MAX_BYTES` 1 MB, `STREAM_RETAIN_ENTRIES` 10,000 (`apps/studio/src/server/checkpoint.ts` 25 to 37). The Vercel unit prices Design A's residual line uses are the bills note's (`research-costs-actual.md` 3, read 2026-09-20: invocations $0.60 a million, provisioned memory $0.0106 a GB hour, Blob simple $0.40 a million, advanced $5.00 a million).

Two readings of the tables. First, no design's Free plan holds at ten times today: every one passes the rows written cap, because an op durable before its acknowledgement is one row and `setAlarm` and deletes are rows too (W1 1.1, pricing lines 104 and 106; W1 finding 3); the Paid plan at $5.00 is every design's answer at 500 hours and the designs differ only in the overage and in what they leave on Vercel. ## 3. Design A under the lens

The free tier math (A section 6) is the most checkable of the three: every count carries its mechanism, the duration line is built from handler counts, and the table carries the D1 and R2 lines as "none in this design" rather than leaving them out. What it leaves undone for the lens: the break day is not printed as an editor hours figure, only as the UTC hour the caps pass at 500 ("about 11:00 UTC", which is the 432 shape; the 576 shape passes at about 08:20 UTC), and the reader must divide. The honest half of A that no other design matches: it prices the Vercel line it leaves behind, about $7 a month at 50 hours and about $70 at 500 with $54 of Blob advanced operations, and names the one lever (the 2 s idle cadence, `CHECKPOINT_IDLE_MS`; a 10 s trigger divides the checkpoint, alarm and record lines by about five) without taking it, because `docs/SYNC.md`'s one record per typing run is the product rule. Under the bill lens that residual is the largest line any design leaves: at 500 hours it is ten times B's whole Cloudflare bill. A's Paid fact is stated before the numbers: the Worker's own 100,000 a day is spendable by anyone who reaches `turboslide-realtime.<subdomain>.workers.dev` (A section 6 last paragraph; W1 1.4 fact 2), so a design that must never fail closed is a Paid design from day one. A ships no account database and says why with the sources (A 1.3: D1 is a binding, the HTTP API is the control plane under 1,200 requests per five minutes, a `workers.dev` cookie never reaches `www`; W2 answers 1 and 2), and lists the two rows it leaves flaky and the five `accounts.*` rows it leaves undriven (A section 8). Risks 1 to 18 are the fullest list of the three. The cost rows for the `do` tier (A section 8, `cost.do.requests` at 300 units, 600 rows written, 20 GB-s, 10 Worker requests per driven hour) are consistent with section 6's counts.

Scores: latency 6 (the estimate is everyone's; the wake path pays a Vercel invocation and the store's sync, 150 to 400 ms on the first keystroke after a pause, A risk 2; no `locationHint`, risk 15; two cross instance rows not met, section 8). Free tier 6 (holds at today; two lines over at ten times; the Vercel residual is the largest bill of the three; the break day is left to the reader). Migration 8 (no store moves; the forced row and Instant Rollback; the object's checkpoint lands on a tier agnostic route and the object answers `resync`, 5.3; the idle tab's Reconnecting across the flip is named, 5.1; no runtime kill switch, risk 14). Size 8 (three new lanes beside R1 to R6, 9.1). Honesty 9. Total 37.

## 4. Design B under the lens

B is the design written for this lens. Its one paragraph answer states the largest line as a percentage of a daily cap (23 %), the day it breaks (about 220 editor hours a day), and the three facts that force Paid, each with its URL (6.4: the rows written cap, the fail closed rule against `docs/FOCUS.md` 6.2, the stranger spendable Worker budget with the rate limit binding's "permissive, eventually consistent" sentence and the WAF needing a zone). The margin is bought by four design choices and B says so: the checkpoint cadence moved from 2 s idle and 10 s max to 10 s idle and 30 s max (3.1), which cuts the alarm and commit lines to 72 and 24 an hour and is named as a product change in risk 10 (a Version history row covers up to 30 s); the document kept as SQLite chunks so a wake reads local rows (3.2), costed at 3 chunks plus a `meta` row per commit; the pulse put skipped on this tier (3.4); and R2's strongly consistent reads in place of the 5 s cached miss (4.4). The R2 and D1 and Workers lines each have a table with a break day (6.2, 6.3), and 6.3 names the swing term no other design names in its table: the public set through a `workers.dev` Worker, where a 200 twin deck viewed by 1,000 browsers is 200,000 requests, over the Worker cap alone, with three fallbacks in order. The checkout for R2 is marked PAYMENT (7 step 5) and risk 5 says what stands if Kevin does not clear it (everything but the store move; the object reads the snapshot through Vercel Blob instead).

What B leaves for the reader. The rows written line assumes 3 document chunks per commit; the median deck is 2.3 MB (`read-store.md` 147, the listing of 2026-09-20), so 3 chunks of 900 KB is the median, but `CAPS.deckMaxBytes` is 25 MB (`admission.ts` 37 as A risk 18 cites it), which is 28 chunks and 24 × 29 = 696 rows an hour from the chunks alone for such a deck, 1,056 in all, a break day of about 95 editor hours of such decks; B does not flag the sensitivity. The Vercel residual is given at 50 hours for the commits alone ($0.14) with "the page loads" unpriced, and not given at 500. The "$0" headline is right for Cloudflare and is written as the whole answer. The D1 line rests on the 300 s per instance cache of the identity facts (2.5), a design choice that makes layout C cheap (W2 section 3 names it), and the per request latency of the identity hop is unmeasured (W2 open 1); B says so in 6.2.

Scores: latency 7 (the wake rebuilds from local chunks; `locationHint: 'enam'`; every row met in estimate; the commit's four S3 rounds from `iad1` to R2 and the loader's R2 reads from `iad1` are new unmeasured legs, risk 2). Free tier 8 (holds at today with the largest line at 23 %; the break day stated; five product tables; the swing term named; the chunk sensitivity and the Vercel residual at 500 unflagged). Migration 7 (three cutovers; the drain from `rt_open` and the D1 flag as the runtime kill switch, 5.5; the re-admission of uncommitted entries past external records, 3.3 item 3; the store cutover's way back is not written, only the week of the legacy host fallback, 5.2 and 3.4). Size 4 (seven lanes, 9.2, including a `packages/accounts` extraction, better-auth over D1 behind a same origin proxy, an R2 client with a 12,000 object copy and a second Worker). Honesty 8. Total 34.

## 5. Design C under the lens

C's cost section (6.1 to 6.4) is the broadest, with sixteen lines including Browser Run and the app Worker's CPU, and it is honest where it knows it does not know: the 10 ms CPU per invocation on the SSR of the editor page is named as the one unmeasured condition that decides Free against Paid (6.2, risk 1), W4 row 1 says an SSR "probably is" over it, so C's Free column for the app Worker is conditional and C says so in the 6.4 table ("$0 (Free), or $5"). The R2 checkout is a PAYMENT stop (7 step 7) and the store is ship one (9.2), so the whole design waits on it, which C states. The DNS cut is Kevin's and the end state waits on it (5.4, risk 5).

Three places where the cost rows are less than they look. First, the rows written line (6.1: 144 ops plus 72 alarms, 216, plus 144 if compaction is row deletes) omits two writes C's own state table requires: `meta` (`head`, `covered`, `revision`, `etag`, `snapshotMd5`, 2.5) is set at every commit (1.7 sets `covered` and the etag), and `doc` (one row per slide plus the manifest, 2.5) must be written for the wake path 2.5 describes to read it. At one `meta` row and one `doc` row per commit the floor is 360 an hour and the break day 278; with row deletes 504 and 198; if `meta` is a key value table and four keys move per commit, 576 to 720 an hour and 139 to 174. So C's printed "278 to 463 editor hours a day" reads as about 140 to 280 once the design's own tables are counted, under B's 219 in the worst shape. Second, R2 Class A at 288 an hour (72 commits at 4 puts) is 432,000 a month at 50 hours, 43 % of the free million and the largest "today" fraction of any line in the three designs, and $14.94 a month over at 500 hours (6.2); it is the price of the 2 s idle cadence against the binding, which B's 24 commits an hour avoids. Third, the app Worker request line (6.2: 23,000 a day at 50 hours with 1,000 show views) carries the public set, and the 6.2 row names only the gate day as what breaks it; the seller's own 200 twin deck viewed by 1,000 browsers (200,000 a day) is in 6.1's text as the worst case for Class B and not set against the Worker cap, which it passes alone. B 6.3 names that case. One more under honesty: C section 8 says every `realtime.*` row is met, and `realtime.share-link.every-instance` is met because "the 5 s TTL caches stay and the row's 1 s bound holds because a fresh index read is an R2 get"; B 4.4 meets the same row by never caching a miss in `grantCache`, which is a code change, and A section 8 says the row stays flaky on 5 s caches. C's sentence asserts what B changes.

Scores: latency 7 (the commit through the binding with no Vercel hop; the wake pays one R2 `head` per wake, 2.5; no `locationHint` this round, risk 9; the phase 1 loader reads the whole document from the object per page load). Free tier 5 (holds at today only if the SSR's CPU stays under 10 ms, which W4 doubts; the rows written line undercounts its own tables; the tightest "today" line of the three on R2 Class A; the Worker cap exposed to a seller's twins without saying so). Migration 5 (three ships each with a rollback written, 5.1 to 5.3, the drain flag; but the end state waits on Kevin's DNS act, the mirror less `r2-store.ts` is a new `DeckStore` W4 4.1 sized at two rounds, and the store move is on the critical path behind the payment stop). Size 2 (nine lanes, 9.1; the app on Workers with a third Vite config, wasm for the native addon, a render service on Vercel, and everything of B). Honesty 6. Total 25.

## 7. The winner under the lens, and where the total disagrees

Under the bill lens the winner is Design B. Its cost rows are the most complete on the Cloudflare side (five products, each with a break day), the choices that make Free hold with the largest margin are B's (the 10 s and 30 s cadence, the chunks, no pulse, strong reads, the identity cache), the facts that force Paid are three and sourced, and the total bill Kevin would pay, Cloudflare and Vercel together, is the lowest at today (about $1 a month) and at ten times (about $10). A wins the total because it is a quarter of B's build and the most honest note of the three, and under the lens its weakness is the one it names itself: the Vercel Blob residual of about $7 at 50 hours and about $70 at 500, which is the largest line any design leaves. C's bill sits between the two and rests on an undercount and a condition W4 doubts.

The synthesizer should read the disagreement this way: the cheapest honest path is A's shape with B's cost levers. A with B's cadence (24 commits an hour in place of 144) and B's chunks takes A's Vercel residual from about $7 to about $1.50 a month at 50 hours and from about $70 to about $17 at 500 (the Blob advanced line divides by six, the function time with it), with the Version history granularity question B risk 10 asks put to Kevin. Against that, the store move's own saving is about $1 to $12 a month at the two loads, bought with a payment method on file, a 12,000 object copy and a two week build; the accounts move is Kevin's ask and not a bill item, and it is what B 4.7 and C 4.6 add that A lacks.

The grafts, one line each:

- From B 3.1 and 6.1: the 10 s idle and 30 s max checkpoint cadence on the `do` tier, with B risk 10's product consequence put to Kevin; it divides A's commit, alarm and Blob record lines by about six.
- From B 3.2: the document as SQLite chunks rewritten at each commit, so a wake rebuilds from local rows in place of A's seed route invocation (A risks 2 and 18), at 3 to 4 rows written per commit.
- From B 6.3 and 6.4: the public set swing term (a 200 twin deck times 1,000 browsers against the Worker's 100,000 a day) and the three Paid forcing facts, written into A's section 6 and C's 6.2.
- From B 2.5 and 4.7: the principal `touch` written at most once an hour per principal and the principal records on D1 behind the Worker, which is what meets `realtime.departed-guest.name-stable` without Redis; A's alternative A1 should carry the touch rule.
- From B 5.5: the `rt_open` registry with the drain before a rollback, and the D1 flag row as the runtime kill switch A lacks (A 5.3, risk 14).
- From B 2.2: `locationHint: 'enam'` at `idFromName`, absent from A (risk 15) and C (risk 9).
- From B 10.5: the fallback when the R2 checkout stops (the object reads the snapshot through Vercel Blob's fetch client), so a payment stop never blocks the channel.
- From C 3.2: entries partitioned by month and dropped as tables in place of row deletes, pending the one line probe of W1 open 1; it removes up to 144 rows written an hour at the floor.
- From C 8: the `cost.do.rows-written` row with a production day over 50,000 holding the next ship, which is the early warning for the one line that breaks Free in every design.
- From C 5.1: the store cutover's way back in one sentence (unset the R2 rows, redeploy, run the delta pass backwards), which B 5.2 lacks.
- From A section 6: the Vercel residual (function time and store operations) priced beside the Cloudflare lines in every cost table, with the cadence lever named; B leaves it unquantified at 500 hours.
- From A 1.3 and section 7: the sourced statement of what a Vercel function reaches on Cloudflare Free (nothing) with the two alternatives, and the STOP marks per step (account, payment, terms, DNS) with the plan read before any click.

## 8. What none of the three says

1. The break day is a UTC day figure and the load is not flat: a seller's hours cluster in a working day, so a cap passed at "278 editor hours a day" is passed earlier in the afternoon of a day with that many; every design's break day should be read as an upper bound on a working day.
2. The Free caps are per account (B 9.3 says so for the preview Worker; W1 1.1 counts the Worker's and the object's 100,000 apart). If the account is General Translation's and `gt-edge` already runs there (W5 7.1, W1 open 2), Turboslide's rows share that account's caps and plan; if that account is on Workers Paid already, every Free column above becomes an included allowance and the overage lines are the whole Cloudflare bill. Kevin's answer to A step 1, B step 0 and C step 1 changes section 2's tables before anything is built.
3. Whether Workers Paid asks for a payment method at upgrade was read on no page (W1 open 7); if it does, the Paid plan is the same class of stop as the R2 checkout, and A's "Paid from day one if production may never fail closed" is a stop too.
4. The one row that would tell Kevin where he stands is a measurement none of the designs can give before a preview: the object's rows written per driven editor hour (A `cost.do.requests`, B `cost.do.rows`, C `cost.do.rows-written`) read from the cursors' `rowsWritten` (W1 finding 15). Section 2's break days are counts from the designs' text; the preview's number replaces them.

## 3. Design A, the reasons

Latency 6. A meets 15 of the 17 `realtime.*` rows by a mechanism it names (A section 8, the first table) and says plainly that two stay flaky, `realtime.share-link.every-instance` and `realtime.departed-guest.name-stable`, because their mechanism is among the Vercel functions and not the channel's (A section 8, the second table; `read-channel.md` finding 10). Every number is `research-options.md` 5b's estimate; A says so (A 10 item 1) and names the measurement rule, an application message and not a protocol ping (W1 section 5). The one path A adds that B and C do not have: a hibernated object rebuilds its live document from a Vercel seed route, a function invocation plus the document's JSON, 150 to 400 ms on the first keystroke after a pause over 10 s (A 10 item 2), with a 4.5 MB response cap (A 10 item 18). The row types one character a second and stays awake inside the burst, so the row is met, but the seller's first keystroke after reading is the case A's own risk list points at.

Free tier 7. A's math (A section 6) uses the most conservative alarm count of the three, 144 an hour at the 2 s idle cadence, and still holds every line at 50 editor hours a day (rows written 22 to 29 %, requests 11.5 %, duration 5.1 %). At 500 it breaks rows written at about 11:00 UTC and requests at about 20:50 UTC each day and names the Paid plan at $5.45 a month with the pricing page's sentences (W1 1.1). Two things keep it from a higher score: the Vercel line does not go to zero (about $7 a month at 50 editor hours, about $70 at 500, of which Blob advanced operations are $54; A section 6's last paragraphs), and A's answer to the 500 hour day is the Paid plan, not a code change (A 10 item 12). What earns the 7 against B and C: no checkout, no card, no plan purchase anywhere in A's steps (A section 7, steps 5, 17 and 18), and the public set stays on Vercel Blob's CDN, so no Worker request cap is exposed to a thousand browsers reading two hundred twins (the swing B 6.3 and C 6.2 name for themselves).

Migration 8. The store never moves, so there is no copy, no two store window and no etag fact to measure. The flip is one variable (`realtime-env.mjs flip --tier do`, A 4.1 and 7 step 15); the rollback is the forced `blob` row or Vercel's Instant Rollback (A 5.3 items 1 and 2), and A's object keeps committing through a route that is bearer authenticated and tier agnostic, so the acknowledged tail of at most 10 s or 2,000 entries lands before the object publishes `resync` and closes (A 5.3 item 1). The two deployments of the switch are handled by the revision jump the object reads in its checkpoint answer (A 3, 5.2), the class `docs/REALTIME.md` 3.7 already accepts. A deployment without the Worker falls to `blob` (A 5.4). What costs the two points: no runtime kill switch without a deploy (A 5.3, last paragraph; the redis tier has one, `realtime-env.mjs` 491), a tab idle across the flip reads Reconnecting until a person reloads it (A 5.1, "the honest statement"), and the object's callbacks to a protected preview need Kevin's project setting, so the hosted gate for the `do` tier may run on production alone (A 9.3 item 2, 10 item 3), which weakens the gate `docs/REALTIME.md` 5.4 item 2 wanted.

Size 8. Three new lanes with disjoint files (D1 the Worker, D2 the Vercel side, D3 the transport; A 9.1), one seam commit by the integrator (the move of the pure admission into `room-core.ts`), one Worker, one new deploy step in the guard (A 9.2). `research-workers.md` 4.1 prices the channel at one to two rounds, three to five days each, so three to ten days with the first ship possible in the window the task asks for. Nothing of the store, the accounts or the app moves.

Honesty 9. Eighteen risks, each with a source (A section 10). A says in its own brief that it ships no account database and gives the three facts (D1 is a Worker binding, Hyperdrive brings no database, a `workers.dev` cookie never reaches `www`; A 1.3, citing W2 answers 1 and 5 and the Public Suffix List), then costs the two alternatives it does not build. It says the Vercel bill stays, which rows stay flaky, where the wake pays, and that `wrangler secret put` on stdin and the vitest pool's version were not read (A 10 item 16). It fetched nothing itself except DNS and says so. The one point off: "the route is tier agnostic" (A 5.3 item 1) is asserted for a route that does not exist yet, and A does not say that its checkpoint route's retry re-bases without transforming past a foreign record (section 2 item 1 above), which is the whole content of its two deployment window.

## 4. Design B, the reasons

Latency 7. B claims every `realtime.*` row and the five `accounts.*` rows (B section 8). It meets the two rows A gives up, through R2's strong read and `ts_principal` on D1 (B 4.4, 4.7). The agent row holds because the banner fires on the fan out before the commit (B 1.5). B pins every object to `enam` with a `locationHint`, which is a decision for the US seller and against the European one (B 4.2).

Free tier 7. The cleanest dollar line of the three: $0 on Cloudflare at 50 editor hours a day with the largest line at 23 % (B 6.1 to 6.4), and the forcing facts for Paid named with their sources (about 220 editor hours a day on rows written, the fail closed rule, the spendable `workers.dev` budget; B 6.4). The margin is bought with a product change, the checkpoint cadence from 2 s idle and 10 s maximum to 10 s and 30 s (B 3.1, 6.1), so a Version history row covers up to 30 s and a commit lands 10 s after the last keystroke; B names it (B 10 item 10) and it is Kevin's call. Migration 6. B is three cutovers, not one: the store (B 5.2), the accounts (B 5.3), the channel (B 5.4), each with a rollback, and the best rollback tooling of the three: `drain` flushes every open deck listed in `rt_open` before the switch and `rt_flags.realtime` is a runtime kill switch read within 30 s with no deploy (B 5.5). The object re-admits its uncommitted entries transformed past a foreign record before the tabs reload (B 3.3 item 3), which is more careful than A's inherited class and more code to get right. What costs the points: the store cutover's two store window rests on a delta pass and on a copy script that has never run against a real store (section 2 item 6; B 3.4 "the delta covers the old deployment's last writes"); the R2 client's three day 0 facts are unmeasured (B 10 item 5); the channel's wake path reads the store through the R2 binding, and B's fallback if the checkout stops is "Vercel Blob's fetch client instead of the binding, which T1 open 1 left unread" (B 10 item 5), so the channel flip is gated on Kevin's card or on an unread path; and better-auth 1.7.4 over D1 "has not run on this tree" (B 10 item 7) while B's W5 moves the whole identity runtime behind it.

A is the only design with no checkout and no card anywhere; B and C both need the R2 subscription, whose checkout needs "A primary payment method" by Cloudflare's billing page (W5 3.4), and C may need Workers Paid on day one if the SSR's CPU passes 10 ms (C 10 item 1). - The pipeline rate used for the size scores is `research-workers.md` 4.1's three to five days a round, read from the bills' day table; it is an estimate from that note and not a measurement of this round.

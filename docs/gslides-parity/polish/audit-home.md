## 4. What a seller reads and what the best pages do

Reading the copy against Kevin's rules. Sentence case and no trailing period on headings hold everywhere (the lints of `copy.test.ts` do their job). What the lints do not catch: the comma clause in a heading (item 14), the semicolon list (item 19), the apology (item 4), the citation (items 5, 16) and the specimen (item 7). Sentences a seller meets in the first three screens, with the thought count:

- "Build the pitch, present it and send the link, in one place" (two thoughts; the second is an afterthought after a comma).
- "Google Slides' menus, toolbar and shortcuts, a canvas on every slide, and a PowerPoint export that matches the screen pixel for pixel." (three thoughts, no verb).
- "No account needed to start; free under the MIT licence." (two thoughts joined by a semicolon).
- "Google Slides' frame, on every slide a canvas" (two thoughts, the second inverted).
- "The default view holds what a seller uses weekly; a feature not yet tested end to end sits behind Tools > Advanced tools." (a rule of the repository written to the buyer).
- "Twelve groups of features. What is in the default view is driven end to end against production before a release; the rest sits behind Tools > Advanced tools until its own interactions pass." (a release process written to the buyer).

The reference pages, read by the auditor as they stand in 2026 and as the brief names them: Linear's home is a 56 px sentence, one 20 px lead, two buttons, then the product at the rail's width, then six sections of one heading, one or two sentences and one picture or diagram each, with about 120 px between sections and no hairlines; Vercel's home is the same shape with a diagram per section drawn in its own grammar; Stripe's documentation home is a grid of cards with one sentence each and one icon at the text size. None of them prints a file path, a date, a version number or the name of a test.

# The site: `/home` and `/docs`

The Workbench direction Kevin picked on 2026-10-14 for the landing and the docs, built on `site/bench` from `8c9465b6`. It replaces `docs/LANDING.md` for `/home`. The build plan, with its decisions D1 to D12 in full and the file by file work, is the orchestrator's (`/Users/kevinliu/turboslide-scratch/site/plan.md`); this file keeps what a builder of these pages needs.

## 1. The page

- `/home` is one route file (`apps/studio/src/routes/home.tsx`) over `apps/studio/src/components/site/`: the bar with the T in a box (`SiteBar`, `BoxedMark`), screen 01 (`Hero`: the h1 with its two stacks, the lead with New Presentation and Open the Example Deck, the editor's figure), screen 02 (`CanvasScreen`: the lighthouse slide with its heading moved and turned), screen 03 (`AgentsScreen`: the recorded CLI run beside `slide.new` on each transport, and the one path every caller joins), screen 04 (`PresentScreen`: the viewer, the slideshow keys, and the slideshow beside presenter view), screen 05 (`PatternsScreen`: an animated pattern and its still), screen 06 (`ExportScreen`: the three files a download writes, and the two PowerPoint files at a seam) and the footer (`SiteFooter`). Section 9 has the figures.
- Every word is in `copy.ts`, every count in `facts.ts` (pinned to the action table by `facts.test.ts`), the recorded run in `run.ts` (pinned to `home-deck/recorded/run.json` by `run.test.ts`).
- The stylesheet `site.css` is linked from the route's head with `?url`, so no rule reaches another page; every selector names the root `.ts-site` (`routes/-home-css.test.ts`). `/docs` takes the same root and stylesheet in its own build step.
- The page imports no editor module (`graph.test.ts`). The live editor is a same origin frame of its own route, `/try` (section 5); the hero box holds a poster until the frame says it is ready, which it says once every dithered picture on its slides has drawn its first frame (until then such a picture shows its continuous source).

## 2. Decisions

- D6. The four agent marks (Claude Code, Codex, OpenClaw, Hermes) are inlined once from thesvg.org in `packages/chrome/src/presence/agent-marks.tsx`, MIT, each in its own colours; Hermes draws in ink on a dark ground.
- D7. `/home` and `/docs` share `components/site/`.
- D8. The display size is 72 px at 1440 (`clamp(40px, 5vw, 72px)`; 36/44 on a phone), so the one line h1 and its two stacks fit the 1280 px column.
- D9. The capital labels name figures, tables, navigation groups and screen numbers; the brand lint accepts `.ts-site .t-label` by name.
- D11. The solid icons are Heroicons 20 solid from the chrome's one icon module, picked by name from its exported constants (`components/site/SiteIcon.tsx`), so a page carries the paths it draws and none of the editor's table.
- D12. No local production build: the byte lines and the tooltip audit are read on a preview.

## 3. Type, grid and colour

- One face, the product's InterVariable 4.1, weights 400 and 500, `font-optical-sizing: auto`, `font-synthesis: none`.
- Display 72/72 (500, -0.032em); Title 40/48 (400); Head 24/32 (500, opsz 32); Lead 20/28; Read 17/28; Small 14/20; Label 12/16 capitals (500, +0.06em, `case`, tabular figures); code in the platform monospace on the `#101010` panel.
- The column is 1280 px with 32 px gutters (16 px at 720 px and under); one layout breakpoint at 1023 px; the bar is 56 px.
- Colours are the generated `--pt-` tokens, so both appearances follow `html[data-theme]`. Corners: 0 for frames and slides, 4 px key chips, 6 px buttons, round avatars.

## 4. The title and the hero

- The h1 reads "Presentations for people and agents" and is named by that sentence (`aria-label`). From the top right of "people" rises an arc of five people and from the top right of "agents" an arc of the four agents (Kevin, 2026-10-15: "make them look better coooolrr in an arc and make them interactable"). Each arc is positioned from its word and out of the line's flow, so the sentence keeps its word spacing; site.css places avatar `i` on a circle at the angle `--a0` less `i` steps of `--da`, the first with its lower left at the word's top right corner (40 px avatars at 1440, 26 px on a phone, where the line breaks after "people" and the arc runs flat under the line above). Avatars overlap with a 2 px ring in the page colour between them, a solid ring in their live hue and a soft shadow (the one accepted `css/no-shadow`). People are generated portraits (`Portrait.tsx`: a head and shoulders in one of five hair cuts drawn from the name, in the hue mixed with paper and ink, the same in both appearances); agents are the four marks on a white plate of their own, in their own colours in both appearances.
- Each avatar is a button named by its person or tool and described by what pressing does. A hovering pointer or the keyboard's focus fans the arc open and lifts the avatar under it with its name in a label (on a touch screen the press alone lifts it for a moment); each arc is one tab stop whose arrows, Home and End move along it; Enter and Space press. Pressing sends `ts-try:press` to the live editor: Claude Code or Codex takes its next step at once and the visitor's stage goes to its slide, OpenClaw or Hermes joins the room for twelve seconds and leaves a comment on the slide the visitor is looking at, and a person joins the roster under their generated name with a pointer that visits that slide and leaves. The chip and the pointer of whoever was pressed flash once in their hue. A press before the editor is live loads it at once and waits for its ready; a press with less than half of the box on screen brings the box into view first, at once, since the product does not smooth-scroll. Under reduced motion the arc keeps its place, the label and the ring change at once, and the pointers jump.
- The hero box is 2:1 above 720 px (1280 by 640 at 1440, so at 1440 by 900 the editor's stage shows its whole slide above the fold) and `min(560px, 72svh)` tall on a phone, where New Presentation carries "No account is needed." beside it so the phone editor's sheet ends above the fold at 390 by 844; its poster is a capture of the real editor in each appearance and width (`scripts/site/capture.mjs`, lossless WebP so dithered cells stay exact), on the title slide with both agents at work, and only the poster of the page's appearance is fetched. The title slide carries NASA's Blue Marble (Kevin, 2026-10-16: "i kind of liked the diagrams and the dithered visuals that we had before"): a picture object masked to an ellipse and dithered by the editor's own picture dither (the deck's 8 by 8 screen at its 2 px cell, polarity `same`, so the oceans print as ink in both appearances), with its credit; the file is `apps/studio/public/hero/earth.jpg`.

## 5. The live editor in the hero

- D1. The editor runs in a same origin frame of `/try` (`apps/studio/src/routes/try.tsx`, `ssr: false`, not indexed). Inside a frame of the hero's size the editor's fixed root, its document key maps, its address hash, the phone layout under 720 px and the dialogs' scrim are already right, so the real editor is unchanged. `/try` answers `X-Frame-Options: SAMEORIGIN` and `frame-ancestors 'self'` (`server/headers.ts`); every other page but the embed answers DENY. It is rendered per request, not prerendered: its shell takes the request's CSP nonce.
- D2. One seam for the editor's server calls: `apps/studio/src/editor/backend.ts`. The server backend (`backend-server.ts`) is every server function, the room's wire, the IndexedDB mirror, sessionStorage, the page's requests and records as the editor called them before; `/try` binds the tab backend once, before the editor mounts. `backend.test.ts` pins that the editor's files import no runtime module of `../server/` and no route address.
- D3. The tab backend (`apps/studio/src/try/tab-backend.ts`) runs the room in the tab: the realtime package's room server over its memory channel, a checkpoint two seconds after the last op, comments in memory (`tab-comments.ts`), version rows in memory, the unsent queue's mirror in memory. Nothing is written to the server, no deck id reaches the store, a reload starts from the shipped deck (`hero-deck.ts`, "Northwind quarterly review"), and its asset paths resolve under `/hero/` (`HERO_ASSET_BASE`), the site's static copies of the deck's two pictures (the Earth on the title slide and the warehouse drawing).
- D4. Claude Code and Codex are headless editor controllers on the same tab room, each with its own author and undo history; every agent write is one action of the table through `editorAdapter().invoke` with the agent's `baseRevision` (`session.ts`).
- D5. An agent whose roster row names a brand mark (only the tab room writes one) draws like a person: its chip shows the brand mark, its outline is dashed in its hue, and it has a pointer and a drag ghost; its writes raise no outside write banner. Every other agent draws as before. Such an agent names the step it is taking in a few words (Kevin, 2026-10-16: the agents "far more proactive"): the runner says them (`AgentHandle.say`), the tab backend writes them into the row's mark as `doing` (`tab-backend.ts` `say`, read at every presence post), and the chrome draws them as `PresenceParticipant.doing`: every flag of the agent reads "Codex: adding a chart" (the step alone with its mark on a stage under 560 px), as wide as its words; its outline draws no flag of its own while its pointer carries one; its chip's name and tooltip read the step; and under the presence slot a row of the same flags hangs over the menu bar's empty right side, the title row then clipping sideways only (`presence.css` `.ts-presence-steps`). The roster's row adds the step after the slide.
- D10. A feature that needs the server answers one sentence with New Presentation (`TAB_ONLY` of `packages/chrome/src/menus/strings.ts`, one line of the snackbar): the dialogs of `TAB_ONLY_DIALOGS` (`session.ts`), Presenter view, a page of the presentation (Print, the viewer), and every server function the tab backend refuses. The save cell reads "In this tab only", and the editor asks for no name. A navigation away from `/try` loads in the window around the frame. No leave of `/home` or `/try` asks for the browser's leave prompt: the router's blocker turns `enableBeforeUnload` off (`try/frame.ts` `tryBlocker`), and the editor's leave warning skips the tab backend (`editor/leave-warning.ts`).
- The script (`script.ts`) is data; the runner (`agents.ts`) plays it at a pace a person can follow, and both agents work the whole time the hero is on screen, mostly at the same moment on different slides (a loop is about fifty seconds). Claude Code adds a slide of rows and a picture slide (a warehouse drawing made for the deck, which no slide shows until then, served from `public/hero/`), writes and rewrites a point and a title a letter at a time, crops the picture, animates a heading (`motion.add`), writes speaker notes a word at a time, adds a row, moves a slide, changes a layout, tries the Coral theme and goes back to Swiss, evens out the shapes Codex drew and puts back the number Codex raised. Codex raises a quarter, adds a chart slide, inserts a chart and fills it from smaller values, switches it to columns and later to a pie, adds a table and types into its cells, animates the chart, draws three boxes, drags one and aligns them, connects them with arrows and tidies a sentence Claude Code wrote. They comment on each other's work, answer by changing it, reply and resolve the threads, and at the end each removes the slides it added, so the loop ends on the deck it started from. Every task names itself in a few words (D5); no step shows less than 600 ms of work (the pointer travels 600 to 900 ms, the agent rests 600 to 800 ms on what it changes before each write); a write shows at once and the agent waits for its answer only when a later step needs it, since the tab room answers at its next checkpoint. A task that waits on the other agent (`after`) lets the agent do its next tasks first; while every task left waits, and at the end of a loop, the agent reads a slide (`REVIEW`) and writes nothing.
- Once the visitor clicks or types in the frame, no agent writes the slide the visitor's stage shows or the block they selected, a deck level write waits while they type or drag, and no agent writes a block, slide or deck field the visitor wrote. A write that stays blocked for 1.5 s ends its task, the agent moves to its next task on another slide, and the task comes back once at the end of its list, where a `write` step carries on from the letter it stopped at. Until then the director keeps the visitor's stage with one agent at a time, on the slide it sets out for and the slides it writes (at most one move every 1.5 s), and turns to the other agent after nine seconds or once the followed one only reads. A press on an agent in the title counts the visitor as watching until their next click or key in the frame. Every slide reads at every moment: what an agent adds arrives whole (a new slide with its heading, sentence and rows or picture, a chart with its data, a table with every cell, a box with its words) and a `write` step only erases back to the words the old and the new text share, so no text empties (`script.test.ts`, `agents.test.ts`). The agents run only while the frame is on screen and its document visible; under reduced motion the pointer jumps and no drag ghost is drawn, and every write still happens. OpenClaw and Hermes, pressed in the title, name their steps too ("reading this slide", "leaving a comment").
- The frame protocol (`components/site/try-frame.ts`): the frame says `ts-try:ready` once its filmstrip is drawn with both agents in the roster; the page then fades the frame in over the poster (150 ms, none under reduced motion) and removes the poster, so nothing moves; the page says `ts-try:visible` as the box enters and leaves the screen, and `ts-try:press` when a visitor presses an avatar of the title (`guests.ts` runs the visits of people, OpenClaw and Hermes). The appearance travels as the viewer's `gt-theme` message.

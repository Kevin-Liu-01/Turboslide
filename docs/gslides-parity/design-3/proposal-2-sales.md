# Proposal 2: sales first and safe by default

Design proposal for round three of the Turboslide Google Slides parity work (multiplayer, optional accounts, security, layout shift, background images with dithers), written 2026-09-13 against `main` at `61b16e4`. This is the second of three proposals. Its angle is the sales organisation that will use the product: a manager who reviews with comments, two reps who tailor one deck at the same time, decks that leak when they are shared the wrong way, and people who will not create an account to edit a slide. Every design choice below is the shortest path that is also the safe one, with the rest of the surface behind a labelled second step.

## How to read this proposal

- Facts about Google Slides and about the platforms come from the eleven round three research reports in `docs/gslides-parity/research-3/`, cited as R01 to R11 with the section (R01 3 is report 01 section 3), and from round one's report `docs/gslides-parity/research/10-identity-sharing-and-presence.md`, cited as research/10. Those reports read every public page on 2026-09-13; the URLs this proposal relies on are repeated in section 14 so the document stands alone. No web page was opened for this proposal and no account was signed in to. Where a report marks a behaviour unverified, the proposal says so at the point of use and designs a behaviour that does not depend on it.
- Facts about Turboslide are lines of source at `61b16e4` named by path, or facts from `docs/gslides-parity/SPEC.md` (SPEC), `docs/gslides-parity/SPEC-2.md` (SPEC-2), `docs/gslides-parity/VERIFICATION-2.md`, `AGENTS.md`, `docs/hosting.md`, `docs/deck-transfer.md`, `docs/pptx.md` and `docs/freeform.md`.
- "Principal" is an identity: an anonymous id in a sealed cookie or a signed in account. "Grant" is a role given to a principal or to an email address. "Room" is the set of connections open on one deck. "Checkpoint" is a committed revision on the store. "Label" is the generated name an anonymous principal carries.
- Labels follow Google's words in sentence case where Google has the feature. Nothing of Google's artwork is copied; icons are Heroicons 20 solid from the theme sprite.
- Rules of the text: plain technical English, sentence case, no em dashes, no metaphors, no trailing periods on headings, full sentences in the body.

## The decisions this proposal does not reopen

Kevin and the round briefs settled these and the proposal builds on them: no Google services; the block document, the action table of `packages/schema/src/actions.ts`, the string renderer and the Perfect export stay the foundation; every capability is an action with CLI, MCP and window handlers and works on a local checkout; the Prototemplate look (Inter, paper and ink, hairlines, square boxes, the `--pt-` tokens); Google's labels and positions where Google has the feature; anonymous use stays possible; the agent bearer stays; the shared Blob store stays the hosted backend unless the design shows it cannot carry the change feed. Round two's canvas model stands: every block on every slide is a positioned object on the first manipulation, and the background picture is a picture object at the bottom of the stack (SPEC-2 0.69, 0.74).

## The decisions in one page

Each row is binding on the build if this proposal is chosen. Where a row departs from a research report's default or from Google, the reason follows.

1. The mutation language stays and gains an operation stream in front of the revision log. Every keystroke, drag and menu action is a `Mutation` list admitted through one ordered stream per deck (Redis Streams on Upstash hosted, in process on a checkout), fanned out within about 60 to 250 ms, and coalesced by a checkpointer into the existing `versions/<n>.json` records on Blob at most every few seconds (R02 7.1). No CRDT replaces the document; the text operations of one Text are transformed at admission (R02 3.3), every other JSON pointer is last writer wins by server order, and structural operations re-anchor. No accepted keystroke is ever dropped: an operation that cannot be placed is returned to its author with its content (R02 7.5).
2. Server-Sent Events down and POST up, on the dev server and on Vercel alike; WebSockets are a later swap behind a flag (R02 2.3, 7.7). The Blob store stays the hosted backend; the smallest addition it needs is one Redis database from the Vercel Marketplace on the fixed 250 MB plan, because Blob's 75 advanced operations per second for the whole deployment cannot carry per keystroke commits (R02 1.3) and the fixed plan keeps the Redis bill outside the attack surface (R10 F33).
3. No locks for people. The "Maya is editing this" outline comes from presence, as in Google, Figma and tldraw (R02 5). Leases stay for agents only, bound to the agent's heartbeat (R02 7.5). The conflict card of round one is retired for text and property edits and stays only for a `version.restore` while others edit and for a returned operation.
4. Presence is ephemeral: a roster and a cursor channel in Redis with 120 s expiry, never on Blob and never in a version record (R02 4). The title row gets a fixed 184 px presence slot, chips of 24 px, a `+N` chip and the own chip; Follow is a click on a chip; carets, pointers, and selection outlines draw in six hues that pass 3:1 on both papers and both inks; everything stored stays monochrome (R11 decisions 1 to 4).
5. Comments are a sidecar per deck (`decks/<id>/comments/`), never document data, with six anchor kinds resolved at read time, threads, mentions, one assignee, reactions, resolve and reopen, a comments panel with For you, search and a state filter, and an in app inbox (R08 decisions 1 to 6). Departure from Google, for the sales case: viewers do not see comments unless the owner turns on "Viewers can see comments"; commenters and editors always do; the published player and present mode never show them.
6. Roles are owner, editor, commenter and viewer on one access record per deck (`access.json` in a private Blob store; `.turboslide/access.json` on a checkout); general access is Restricted or Anyone with the link, plus the legacy `open` mode every existing deck starts in so no link breaks (R09 decisions 1 to 3, 15). Notes are read by editors and owners only; skipped slides are hidden from viewers (R09 decision 6).
7. A share link is a 128 bit token exchanged once at `/s/<token>` for a session grant and a redirect to the deck without the token (R10 F40), so a token never sits in the address bar during a screen share, in a Referer or in browser history. The published player and the embed keep a `?p=` token in the query, because an iframe cannot receive a first party cookie (R09 3.5). This closes SPEC 0.25's "revocable per deck view token with Stop sharing".
8. Anonymous by default: a server minted `__Host-` sealed cookie is the principal; the first write, comment or lease opens a one field prompt "How should others see you?" with a generated label prefilled; nothing else is asked (R03 B2, B3). Sign in is optional and needed only to own a shared team deck, to be invited by email, to receive email, and to keep an identity across browsers.
9. Sign in without Google: an email code and magic link in one mail through Resend, passkeys once the production domain is decided, GitHub when its two variables are set; better-auth 1.7.4 with the TanStack Start integration; Neon Postgres and Upstash Redis hosted, `node:sqlite` and memory on a checkout (R03 J). No passwords.
10. Names say what they are: a generated label reads as a label (`Titanium 471`), a typed name carries "guest", a verified account carries the check badge, an agent is dashed (R11 5.2). Reserved names, confusables, bidi controls and per deck uniqueness are server rules (R10 F44, R11 5.3).
11. The security plan of R04 and R10 ships in its priority order: authorization on every server function and route first, then rate limits and quotas, dependency fixes, the `html` block in a sandboxed frame with DOMPurify at write and render, uploads re-encoded through sharp with SVG refused hosted, `safeFetch` with pinned DNS, CSRF widened to the new routes, a nonce based CSP report only then enforced, two Blob stores, structured logging. The hosted asset write bug (R10 F49, a picture added on one instance is gone on the next) lands first because it loses people's work today.
12. Zero measured layout shift on every route and state, gated by `scripts/layout-shift-audit.mjs` in `pnpm check` (R05 5). Every round three surface follows the nine rules of R05 4: reserved slots, fixed width flags and count chips, absolute overlays, transform and opacity only.
13. A dither is a property of the picture object, not of the file: `PictureDither` on `picture` and `shot`, the deck's own pipeline extended with three patterns and a strength, a live canvas while editing and a materialized 1-bit variant for every export (R06 decisions 1 to 4). Change background gains a Dither toggle on its Image row and a Material row for the shader materials; the default parameter set reproduces the GT deck's photographs, so the toggle is the one click brand look (R06 4.8, 4.10).
14. Every new capability is an action: about seventy join the table (comment, notification, activity, version, share, account, admin, presence, sync, picture and slide background actions), each with a CLI usage, an MCP tool and a window handler, working over the file store with no server and over the Blob store behind the bearer (R08 8, R09 7, R06 5, R02 7.9, R03 J).
15. Schema version stays 1. Every new document field is optional, no stored slide byte changes, and the canvas fidelity gate keeps passing; the access record, the comments sidecar and the per identity index are new files beside the deck, and every existing deck reads as `open: editor` until its owner claims it (R09 1.4, 11).

## 0. The sales scenario and the rules it imposes

Round one's report 07 established who the user is: a seller who edits an existing deck far more often than building one, who tailors a small set of slides per prospect, presents over a call and sends a link or a PDF afterwards. Round three adds the people around that seller. Four situations decide the design:

| #   | Situation                                                                                              | What must be true                                                                                                                                                                                                        | Where the design answers it |
| --- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------- |
| S1  | A manager reviews a rep's deck before a call and leaves feedback on three slides                       | The manager needs no account to comment by link, the comment lands on the object it is about, the rep sees it inside the app within a second and can resolve it, and the thread never travels to the prospect            | Sections 3.1 to 3.6, 9.3    |
| S2  | Two reps tailor one deck for one prospect at the same time, one on the pricing slide, one on the cover | Both edits land, nobody is locked out, each sees where the other is, and a typing collision in one text box loses no character                                                                                           | Sections 1, 2, 9.2          |
| S3  | A rep shares the deck with a prospect after the call                                                   | The default share is view only, notes and skipped slides never leave, the link can be revoked, and the token is never on screen during the next screen share                                                             | Sections 3.8, 9.4           |
| S4  | Nobody on the team wants another account                                                               | Every edit, comment and share by link works anonymously; the one prompt is a display name; sign in is offered where it buys something (owning a team deck, being invited by email, email notifications) and nowhere else | Sections 4, 9.1             |

The rules that follow, applied in every section:

1. The shortest path is the safe path. Share opens on Restricted with the people field first; Anyone with the link defaults to Viewer; Copy link copies a link that carries no notes and no skipped slides. Nothing about safety is a setting the rep must find.
2. Progressive disclosure. The title row shows the presence slot, the comments glyph and the inbox plate and nothing more; roles, links, expiry, tokens, sessions and dither parameters live one step behind a labelled control in Google's position.
3. Honest defaults and honest words. Every surface that shows a name says whether it is a label, a typed name or a verified account (R11 5.2). The Share dialog says what a link carries. A refusal says what to do next and never who else exists (R09 8.2).
4. Zero shift. A collaborator joining, a comment arriving, a notification, a save state change, a font or a picture never moves anything the rep is looking at (R05 4).
5. One click brand look. Upload a photograph as a background and switch Dither on; the defaults are the GT deck's, and the Format options section is there for the person who wants to tune it.

## 1. Multiplayer architecture

### 1.1 Two paths: the operation stream and the revision log

Today every editor write is one Blob commit of five advanced operations, four of them sequential HTTPS calls, and every other tab learns of it through a 20 s long poll fed by a 3 s manifest poll (`packages/store/src/blob-store.ts`, `apps/studio/src/server/write.ts`; R02 1.3, 1.4). The Pro store allows 75 advanced operations per second for the whole deployment (R02 V5), so per keystroke commits through Blob fail at two typists. The design adds one path in front of the log and keeps the log as the truth.

The operation stream: one ordered sequence of admitted operations per deck, each entry `{ seq, rev, author, clientId, opId, kind: 'edit' | 'comment' | 'presence', mutations }`, in Redis as `XADD deck:<id>:ops` with a head counter, fanned out by `PUBLISH deck:<id>` to every instance's subscribers, trimmed after each checkpoint to the larger of 10,000 entries or 24 hours and at most 16 MB per deck (R02 7.1, R10 F35).

The revision log: the existing `deck.json`, `slides/<id>.json`, `versions/<n>.json` and `snapshots/<md5>.json`, written by a checkpointer that takes the stream since the last checkpoint, coalesces it into one `Write` per author run (consecutive `text.replace` on one run into one, consecutive `block.set` of one pointer into the last value, a drag's `pos` sets into the final box), applies it through `BlobStore.write`, and announces `checkpoint { revision, fromSeq, toSeq, snapshot }` on the stream. It runs on the instance that admitted the latest operation after 2 s without operations, every 10 s during continuous activity, or when the retained bytes pass 1 MB, under a Redis lock with a heartbeat that a waiter breaks after 3 s (R02 7.1, R10 F36). An agent write forces a checkpoint so its answer carries a revision.

Two acknowledgement levels: `seqAck` means the operation is in the stream, `revAck` means a checkpoint covers it. The client keeps every operation until `revAck`. If Redis loses the stream, clients resend their un-checkpointed operations against the Blob document, transformed against what landed, so an accepted keystroke is never lost; only its position may move (R02 7.1). Eviction stays off on the database so the failure mode is a refused write the client sees, never a silent loss (R10 F35).

### 1.3 Admission and the merge rules

The function that receives an `ops` POST (R02 7.3, hardened by R10 F26 to F29):

1. Checks that `clientId` is bound in Redis to the posting session and this deck (`deck:<id>:client:<clientId> = <sessionId>`, TTL 320 s refreshed by presence); otherwise 403 and a log line. The `clientId` was issued by the server in the stream's `hello` event, so a tab cannot impersonate another (R10 F26).
2. Refuses `base.seq` more than 500 entries behind the head with 409 and `resync` (R10 F28); refuses the 65th entry or the 257th kB with 400 before any transform.
3. Loads the document at the last checkpoint from the instance's mirror and applies the stream entries since it, cached per instance by `seq`.
4. Transforms each incoming operation from `base.seq` to the head: text operations by the transform functions of `packages/schema/src/transform.ts`, `after` anchors re-resolved, everything else unchanged.
5. Applies it through `applyMutation`, validates the touched slide and, for deck level operations, the manifest; checks the deck caps of section 5 (200 KB per slide, 25 MB per deck) before the transform work; an operation that fails is answered `reject { opId, reason, mutations }` with one of the fixed reasons `stale`, `invalid`, `forbidden`, `too-large`, `locked` and never enters the stream (R10 F29).
6. Appends atomically through the Lua script; on a miss reads the new entries with `XRANGE` outside the script and transforms once more; after two misses takes a 2,000 ms token lock.

The rules per operation, binding on the transform module and on the client (R02 7.5):

| Operation                                                                  | Two concurrent writers                                                                     | Rule at admission                                                  | Undo rule                                                             |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `text.replace` (insert or delete in one Text)                              | both kept, offsets transformed, equal positions by server order                            | transform against the operations since base, in plain text offsets | the inverse is transformed against later operations before it applies |
| `text.style`, `text.case`, `text.insert`                                   | ranges shift and split around concurrent inserts and deletes                               | transform as range operations                                      | same                                                                  |
| whole Text `block.set` or `slide.set` (an agent, a paste, `slide.replace`) | the last arrival wins the string; the other side's pending inserts re-anchor by `textDiff` | last writer wins                                                   | applied only if the pointer still holds this author's value           |
| `block.set` of any other pointer                                           | last arrival wins the pointer; different pointers never conflict                           | last writer wins per pointer                                       | same                                                                  |
| `block.insert`, `slide.insert`                                             | both inserted; a removed `after` resolves to the end                                       | re-anchor                                                          | the inverse removes the id if it still exists                         |
| `block.move`, `slide.move`                                                 | the last arrival decides; `z` last writer wins                                             | re-anchor `after`                                                  | same                                                                  |
| `block.remove`, `slide.remove`                                             | the removal wins; concurrent edits to the target return to their author with content       | the target must exist                                              | the inverse re-inserts at the recorded anchor                         |
| `slide.replace`                                                            | last arrival wins the slide; pending operations re-anchor by id where the block survives   | last writer wins                                                   | as whole Text                                                         |
| `section.set`                                                              | last arrival wins the order; slides added since are appended to their section              | last writer wins with id reconciliation                            | same                                                                  |
| `asset.set`, `asset.remove`, `deck.set`                                    | last writer wins per asset id or pointer                                                   | last writer wins                                                   | same                                                                  |
| `version.restore`                                                          | admitted as one operation; everyone rebases on the restored document                       | forces a checkpoint                                                | it is itself a version                                                |

One change to the mutation language makes the text rows possible: `text.replace` ranges move from markup offsets to the plain text offsets `text.style` already uses (`packages/schema/src/text.ts` `styleRange`), with the marks carried by the run the caret is in; records already written keep their meaning because a record's mutations replay against the document as it was (R02 3.3). The transform module is property tested for convergence under random concurrent insert, delete, style and case operations on the fixture deck's Texts; the interleaving anomaly of two inserts at one offset is accepted and documented (R02 7.11 risk 3).

Undo is per author and never reverts another author's later change: an inverse `block.set` applies only if the pointer still holds the value this author wrote, otherwise the step is skipped with the snackbar "That change was already changed by Maya" (R02 5).

### 1.4 The client

The controller in `apps/studio/src/routes/edit.$deckId.tsx` keeps the local document, a queue of pending operations (not yet `seqAck`), and the retained set (`seqAck` but not `revAck`). A gesture applies its mutation locally at once, records the undo entry, and flushes: immediately for structural operations, coalesced at 50 ms for `pos` during a drag and at 100 ms for typing; undo grouping keeps its own 400 ms rule (R02 7.4). An incoming `op` from another client transforms the pending queue against it (text against text; a pending `block.set` of the same pointer stays as the local prediction), applies it, and re-renders the touched slides through `setDocument`. The author's own admitted `op` replaces its pending copy and, when the server transformed it, adjusts the document by the difference. A `reject` removes the operation and shows the inline recovery card with the content and a Copy button. A `checkpoint` moves the covered operations out of the retained set, appends the version to History and updates the save words.

The save words in the title row keep their four phrases in one fixed slot (R05 E5) and gain their meaning from `sync.status`: "Saving…" while `pending + retained > 0`, "All changes saved" at `revAck`, "Couldn't save, retrying" during a reconnect, and "Offline. Changes will save when you reconnect" (a fifth phrase in the same slot) when the stream is down and the last POST failed. A tab with pending operations warns on `pagehide` (R10 F36).

### 1.5 The presence channel

Per client: `clientId`, `clock`, `slideId`, `selection { blockIds (at most 64), caret? { path, offset | range } }`, `pointer { x, y }` in sheet units when Show my pointer is on, `follow: clientId | null`. The identity fields (principal id, label, trust, mark, kind) are written by the server from the session into the roster and ignored in the body; a body that carries a name is refused by the schema (R10 F31). Rates: one batch per 80 ms per client, coalesced server side to 15 per second, a 5 s heartbeat, 30 s stale, 120 s expiry, live pointers for the first 20 clients by join order and for editors only, hidden in present mode (R02 4, R01 1). Presence is off on `/deck`, `/embed`, `/present` and `/print`, so a prospect with a view link is never in the roster and never sees the team (R02 7.6, R10 F32).

Agents appear in the roster as agent chips with the token record's registered name and the `runId` as a monospaced suffix limited to 32 characters of `[A-Za-z0-9._-]`; their leases expire 60 s after the last heartbeat (R02 6 item 5, R10 F45).

### 1.6 Sequences

A keystroke from rep A to rep B, both hosted, both in `iad1`:

1. A types "Q" in the pricing heading. The editor applies `text.replace` locally, pushes an undo entry, and flushes after 100 ms with the characters since the last flush.
2. `POST /api/decks/q4-review/ops` (one round trip, 20 to 40 ms) reaches instance X. X checks the session and the bound `clientId`, transforms against the entries since A's `base.seq`, validates the slide, and appends through the Lua script (a few ms).
3. Redis publishes to X and Y. Y writes the `op` event into B's open stream (a few ms). A's own tab receives the admitted form and drops its pending copy.
4. B's controller transforms its pending queue, applies the operation, and re-renders the pricing slide; B's remote caret for A moves. Total about 60 to 100 ms; from the US West coast about 150 ms; from Europe 200 to 250 ms (R02 7.10, typical values, not measured).
5. After 2 s without operations X's checkpointer wins the lock, coalesces A's burst into one `text.replace`, commits through `BlobStore.write` (five advanced operations), and announces the checkpoint. Both tabs show "All changes saved" and the History panel gains one row.

An agent write over `POST /api/actions/block.set`:

1. The route resolves the API key to a record and a principal, calls `authorize(ctx, deckId, 'write')`, and dispatches the action as author `{ kind: 'agent', name: record.name, runId, principalId }`.
2. The mutations are admitted through the room as in step 2 above, with the per touched slide `baseRevision` rule: a 409 with `since` and `current` when a slide the write touches, or the deck level, changed since the base; `strict: true` keeps the exact revision check (R02 6 item 1).
3. The checkpointer is forced, and the answer carries the new revision and the record, as today.
4. Every open editor receives the `op` within the stream's latency and the version row within the checkpoint; the external revision banner names the agent's chip and name.

A reconnect after the 300 s stream boundary or a network loss:

1. The browser's `EventSource` reconnects with `Last-Event-ID`; the route answers `hello` and the entries since that `seq`, or `resync { revision }` when the position is older than 2,000 entries, in which case the client reloads the document at `revision` and re-applies its pending queue by transform (R10 F25).
2. Pending POSTs that failed are resent with their `opId`; the server deduplicates per `(deckId, clientId)` within the retention window.
3. A `base.seq` more than 500 behind answers 409 and `resync` before any transform (R10 F28).

`version.restore` while others edit: the restore is admitted as one operation and forces a checkpoint; every tab rebases its pending operations on the restored document, an operation whose target is gone comes back as `reject`, and the conflict card appears only for that author with the returned content. A deck level `section.set` that reorders slides someone is editing applies; only the filmstrip moves.

### 1.7 Offline and reconnection

The editor keeps working against its local document while offline; every gesture queues. The save slot reads "Offline. Changes will save when you reconnect" after the first failed POST and the stream's `error`. On reconnect the pending queue is sent in order and transformed as in 1.6; when the base has fallen out of the retention window the client resyncs and re-applies its queue against the current document, and anything the diff cannot place returns in the recovery card with its text. Pending operations live in memory only; `pagehide` with a non empty queue asks "Changes are still saving. Leave anyway?" (R10 F36). Google's offline mode with a browser extension has no counterpart here; R01 1 records that Google documents no offline merge rule, so this design's rule is its own.

### 1.8 What the CLI and MCP see

- `POST /api/actions/<id>` and `/mcp` admit operations through the room, force a checkpoint and answer a revision, so an agent's next call can name it (1.6). The conflict rule is per touched slide with `strict: true` for the exact check.
- `GET /api/decks/<id>/events?since=<revision>` is the SSE stream for agents, behind the bearer, carrying checkpoint events (revision, author, note, the record), comment entries, and on `&ops=1` the raw operations. `deck.watch { since, timeoutMs }` is the polling form.
- The MCP server declares `resources: { subscribe: true, listChanged: true }` and sends `notifications/resources/updated` for `deck://<deckId>` per checkpoint, for `deck://<deckId>/comments` per comment checkpoint and for `deck://inbox` per notification (R02 6 item 2, R08 3.1); over stdio on a checkout the same notifications come from `fs.watch`.
- `window.turboslide.studio.describe().state` gains `presence` (the roster) and `sync` (`seq`, `revision`, `pending`, `retained`); the actions `presence.list`, `presence.follow`, `presence.unfollow`, `sync.status` join the table (R02 7.9).
- The attached studio session registry moves from `globalThis` to Redis so `deck_goto_slide` reaches the page whichever instance holds its poll (R02 6 item 3).

### 1.9 A local checkout following a hosted deck

`turboslide deck follow <id> --from <studio>` subscribes to the hosted events since the local revision and applies each version record forward through the `FileStore` with the record's stamps and the same reducer, checks the result against the record's `snapshot` md5, and brings `comments/` along unless `--no-comments`; the local editor shows the same banner it shows for any external revision. `--push` sends local records the host lacks as writes under the per slide conflict rule and prints both sides on a conflict with `--force` or `--theirs`. `deck pull` and `deck push` stay for whole deck transfer (R02 6 item 4). On the checkout the `memory` channel means the CLI, the MCP server over stdio and the dev server's pages are one room.

## 2. Presence and collaborator UI

Google's presence has one avatar surface (the title row), one filmstrip marker, one text caret, one optional pointer, and Follow as the only click on an avatar (R01 2, 9). Turboslide takes those positions and draws them in the Prototemplate look with R11's drawing rules.

### 2.1 The title row

The right group of `packages/chrome/src/TitleRow.tsx` today holds the comments glyph, the Slideshow split button and Share. It becomes, left to right: the comments glyph (now live, section 3.4), the inbox plate (section 3.5), the presence slot, Slideshow, Share. The presence slot is `--pt-presence-w: 184px` by 32 px, present from the first paint whether nobody or twenty people are present (R05 rule 4, R11 6.2): four other chips of 24 px with 4 px gaps filling from the left in join order, a `+N` chip of 32 by 24 that is drawn empty with its border when four or fewer others are present, an 8 px gap, a hair rule, and the own chip of 24 px with a 1 px ink border and the tooltip "<name> (you)". Chips appear and leave by opacity over 120 ms; a departing chip's slot is reused only after the fade, so nothing moves. In compact mode the four slots collapse by a width breakpoint to the `+N` chip and the own chip (64 px).

A chip is the identity mark of R11 6.1: an initials plate on a Bayer field of 1/8 to 4/8 density with 2 px cells, or a glyph field, or the uploaded picture, inside a 1 px `--pt-edge` border, with a 2 px live stripe at the plate's bottom in the participant's hue while they are present. Hover shows "Maya · guest · slide 12" or "Kevin Liu · slide 4" for a verified account (the check badge in the roster row, not on the chip). Click follows (2.2). The `+N` chip opens the roster menu, a `Menu` anchored below with one row per participant: chip, name and trust word, role word ("editor", "commenter", "viewer", "by link"), "slide 12", and Follow or Go to slide; the own row reads "(you)" and opens the profile menu of section 4.7. The own chip's menu is where sign in lives: "Signed in as kevin@…" or "Not signed in", then "Change name", "Change avatar", "Sign in" or "Sign out", "Forget this browser", "Sessions". Nothing else in the chrome mentions accounts (rule 2 of section 0).

### 2.2 Follow

### 2.5 Colours in the monochrome look

Hue is the one sanctioned colour exception of the round and it appears only on live surfaces: carets, pointers, remote outlines, the Following ring and the 2 px live stripe on a chip. ### 2.6 Who sees whom

## 3. Comments and share roles

### 3.1 Comments are a sidecar, not document data

Comments live under `decks/<id>/comments/` beside `deck.json`: `index.json` with its own `revision`, one `<threadId>.json` per thread, and `authors.json` mapping principal ids to the last label seen (R08 2.2). They never enter the mutation log, the version records, the snapshots, the undo stack, the standalone build, the view payload or the canvas fidelity gate. The deciding fact is that a per slide `ext.comments` list would be one JSON pointer, and the merge rule for one pointer is last writer wins, so two people commenting on one slide at once would lose a comment (R08 2.1). On the Blob backend the folder lives in the private store under the deck's prefix with `ifMatch` on `index.json` as the commit point; on a checkout it is gitignored (`decks/*/comments/`) because the repository is public and a comment names people; `deck push` and `deck pull` carry it, `deck pack` only with `--comments` (R08 2.2, 7.1).

Comment operations ride the operation stream as `kind: 'comment'` entries and are checkpointed to the sidecar (two advanced Blob operations per comment, a burst of n costs n plus 2), so a comment reaches every tab within the stream's latency and the History panel never shows one (R08 2.3, 2.4). Every comment operation commutes or has one winner (add is append only, edit is author only and last write wins, resolve and assign are last writer wins by server order, delete is a tombstone that keeps the replies, reactions are a set per emoji keyed by principal), so no comment ever produces a conflict card (R08 3.2).

### 3.2 Anchors

Six kinds, mapped to Google's six through the Drive API's anchor model (R01 3, R08 1.1): `deck`, `slide`, `block`, `text { path, range, quoted }`, `cell { row, column }`, `notes`. An anchor names ids and plain text offsets and is resolved against the document at read time by one pure function `resolveAnchor(document, anchor)`; nothing stores an orphaned flag, so a removed block orphans its threads without deleting them (the panel lists them under the slide with the quoted text), undo and `version.restore` revive them, and `slide.replace` keeps the threads whose ids survive (R08 1.2). Text ranges shift through the same transform functions as `text.style` ranges: on the hosted stream the admission step emits one `comment.shift` entry per changed anchor after the text operation that caused it; on a checkout `FileStore.write` runs `shiftAnchors` inside its lock after `applyWrite` (R08 1.2).

### 3.3 Threads, mentions, assignment, reactions

A thread is `{ id (a ULID), anchor, comment, replies[], resolved?, assignee?, createdAt, updatedAt, revision }`; a comment is `{ id, author: { principalId, label, kind }, createdAt, editedAt?, body: { text, mentions[] }, reactions?, deleted? }`; the body is plain text with `{@n}` tokens naming `mentions[n]`, never markup, rendered through React text nodes (R08 1.1, 4.2). Caps: 4,000 code points per body, 20 mentions, 100 replies per thread, 200 threads per slide, 5,000 per deck, a 24 emoji palette, 200 code points of quoted text; 10 comments per minute anonymous, 30 signed in, 60 agent, and 200 and 1,000 per day (R08 1.3, R10 F37).

Mentions store a principal id or an invitation id, never a display string, and who may mention whom is a disclosure rule: present people and past commenters are mentionable by anyone who can comment; grantees and raw email addresses are mentionable by editors and the owner, because naming a grantee reveals the share list and a raw address creates an invitation, which is a share action (R08 4.1). A mention sends an email only when the mentioned principal already holds a grant on the deck and the author is signed in; a mention of an address without a grant sends nothing and shows "Invite them from Share" to a person who may share; anonymous authors never trigger an email (R10 F38). Both `@` and `+` open the autocomplete (R01 3).

Assign: after a mention, a checkbox "Assign to <name>", "Reassign" in the More menu, "Done" in place of Resolve for the assignee, an `assigned` notification to the assignee; an agent's token principal is assignable and learns of it through its inbox or the event stream (R08 8). Resolve is the tick at the card's top right and `e` when the card has focus; Re-open appears on a resolved thread in the panel; a resolved thread stays in the panel under its filter (R01 3). Reactions are a hover button with the fixed palette. Edit and Delete sit under More for the author, Delete also for the deck owner; deleting the first comment tombstones it and keeps the replies, a departure from Google (which deletes the whole thread) because the replies are other people's words (R08 3.2); Undo in the snackbar and `comment.delete { restore: true }` bring a comment back within 30 days. "Get link to this comment" copies `comment.link`'s URL.

### 3.4 The panel, the card and the markers

The comments glyph in the title row (Google's "Show all comments") flips from a disabled stub to live and opens the right panel "Comments" (`PanelId` `'comments'`), one frame with the refit (R05 E6); View > Comments gains Google's four rows Show all comments, Expand comments, Minimize comments, Hide comments as radios with Ctrl+Alt+Shift+j hiding (R01 3). The panel has a For you tab (threads that mention the reader, that the reader wrote or replied to and that moved since, and the reader's assignments), a search field "Search all comments" by text or author label, a state filter (All, Open, Resolved) and a "Slide order" toggle; the default order is open threads by last activity newest first then resolved threads newest first, because Google documents no sort and Figma sorts by date (R08 9). Rows have fixed heights and tabular relative times (R05 rule 5).

Add a comment: the toolbar's Insert comment, Insert > Comment, the last row of every context menu, a right click on a thumbnail, and Ctrl+Alt+m; on a selection the card anchors to the block, the text range or the cell, on the empty sheet to the slide, on a thumbnail to that slide. The card is a floating layer in the stage overlay with the placeholder "Comment or add others with @" and a Comment button; the reply box reads "Reply or add others with @" (R01 3 records these as Docs wording seen in a search listing; adopted as data in `strings.ts`). The marker is a 20 by 20 ink plate with the chat glyph at the anchored object's top right outside the box, or the slide's top left for a slide thread, with a count chip of minimum width 20 px in tabular figures drawn from count 1; over a picture the marker carries the two ring halo (R11 6.8). The filmstrip card shows the open thread count to the left of the thumbnail inside the frame, present at zero as an empty box (R08 9). Keyboard: Ctrl+Enter enters the comment under focus, j and k move, r replies, e resolves, u or Escape leaves (R01 10).

### 3.5 Notifications inside the app

Google routes comment notifications to Gmail and has no centre inside the editor (R01 3); Kevin asked for notifications inside the app, so this is Turboslide's own design after Liveblocks, Linear and Notion (R08 5). A per principal inbox (Redis with a 30 day TTL for anonymous principals, the identity database for signed in ones, one shape behind one `Inbox` interface) receives `mention`, `reply`, `assigned`, `resolved`, `reopened`, `reaction`, `accessRequest`, `granted`, `versionNamed` and, under the "All comments" level, `comment`, coalesced per thread inside a window; at most 500 records per principal. The title row's inbox plate sits between the comments glyph and the presence slot in a fixed slot sized for the glyph plus two digits, present at zero, with `99+` beyond (R08 5.3); it opens the right panel "Notifications", one sentence per row ("Maya replied on slide 4", "Kai mentioned you"), a click that runs `comment.link`'s target, and "Notification settings" at the foot. The presence stream carries an `inbox { unread }` event to the principal's connections so the plate updates without a poll.

Per deck settings are Google's three levels, All comments, Comments for you, None, under Tools > Notification settings and at the panel's foot; the default is Comments for you, and All comments for the deck's owner (Google's default for owners is unverified, R08 12.1). Email goes only to signed in principals, through Resend, for `mention`, `reply`, `assigned`, `resolved`, `accessRequest` and `granted`, coalesced per person per deck into a 15 minute digest (2 minutes for a direct mention), capped at 10 mails per person per day with an 18:00 summary beyond, with `List-Unsubscribe` and one click unsubscribe, and the deck link in a mail is a plain `/deck/<id>`, never a share token (R08 5.5, R10 F38).

### 3.6 Who sees comments, a departure recorded

Google's pages state who may comment (commenters and editors) and not who may read comments (R08 12.1). Notion, Figma and Google all show comments to anyone who can open the document. This proposal departs: a viewer sees comments only when the owner turns on "Viewers can see comments" in the Share dialog's gear (off by default); commenters, editors and the owner always see them; the published player, present mode and the embed never do. The reason is S1 and S3 together: a manager's review thread on the pricing slide is written for the rep, and the prospect who receives the view link an hour later must not read "drop the price 10 percent if they push back". A rep who forgets to strip comments before sharing loses nothing with this default; a team that wants Google's behaviour flips one switch per deck. `authorize(ctx, deckId, 'readComments')` is the capability; `comment.list` answers 403 for a viewer without it.

### 3.7 Actions, CLI and MCP

The eighteen actions of R08 8 join the table with `group: 'comment'`, `'version'` or `'studio'`, transports all four, and window handlers that run server side through `runDeckAction` because the sidecar is the store's: `comment.add`, `comment.reply`, `comment.edit`, `comment.delete`, `comment.resolve`, `comment.reopen`, `comment.assign`, `comment.done`, `comment.react`, `comment.list`, `comment.get`, `comment.link`, `notification.list`, `notification.markRead`, `notification.settings`, `activity.list`, `version.diff`, `version.delete`. The `<anchor>` positional is `deck`, `slide:<slideId>`, `<slideId>#<blockId>`, `<slideId>#<blockId>/<path>:<start>-<end>`, `<slideId>#<blockId>/cell:<row>,<column>` or `notes:<slideId>`; `<who>` is a principal id, `agent:<tokenId>` or an email for an editor's invitation. Authors come from the session or the token, never from the input. On a checkout the CLI writes `comments/` under the FileStore's lock and `notification.*` read `.turboslide/inbox/<principalId>.json`; over `turboslide mcp` on stdio the same functions are the tools and `deck://<deckId>/comments` and `deck://inbox` are subscribable resources; hosted, `/api/actions` and `/mcp` run them over the private store behind the key (R08 8).

Transfer and export: comments travel in the bundle as a `documents` group with digests when asked, are copied by `deck.copy { copyComments: true }` (the Make a copy dialog's "Copy comments" checkbox, unticked as Google's), never cross decks with `slide.import`, and export to PPTX as classic `p:cmLst` and `p:cmAuthorLst` parts written in the OOXML post process (pptxgenjs 4.0.1 has no comment API), only for a caller with the editor role and `includeComments` on; PDF, print, the standalone build and the view payload never carry them; TXT appends them with `--include-comments` (R08 7).

### 3.8 Share roles

**The record.** One `access.json` per deck (R09 1.1): `owner`, `pendingOwner`, `generalAccess { mode: 'restricted' | 'link' | 'open', role }`, `links[]` (id, `sha256` of the token, role, label, created, expires, revoked), `publish` (one token), `grants[]` (principal or email, role, invited by and at, accepted at, expiry, message), `requests[]`, `settings { editorsCanShare, viewersCanDownload, viewersCanSeeComments, showNamesToLinkVisitors, allowHtmlBlocks }`, `assetKey` (section 5), `revision`. Hosted it lives in the private Blob store under `decks/<id>/access.json`, written with `ifMatch`, cached in Redis for 60 s with invalidation on write, excluded from the mirror and from bundles like `leases.json`; on a checkout at `decks/<id>/.turboslide/access.json`, gitignored, because a committed record in a public repository publishes email addresses (R09 1.2). A deck without a record reads as `owner: null, open: editor`, which is today's behaviour, until someone claims it (R09 1.4).

**The matrix.** Owner everything; editor read, write, comment, history, export with notes, share when `editorsCanShare`, rename, trash, restore, publish; commenter read, comment, export without notes when download is on, skipped slides visible; viewer read and export without notes when download is on, no skipped slides, no notes, comments only with the owner's switch; link visitor the link's role, attributed to the anonymous label; agent token the owner's role intersected with the key's scopes `read`, `comment`, `write`, `export`, `share`, `admin`; Delete forever owner only; transfer owner only; Follow on editors and owners only (R09 2.1, with `readComments` added by 3.6). Notes go to editors and owners only, a narrowing of Google's implied behaviour recorded for Kevin, because a customer given comment access must not read the presenter's notes (R09 decision 6).

**Per route** (R09 2.2). `/edit/:id` opens for a commenter in Commenting mode with Insert > Comment live and no handles, for a viewer in Viewing mode with no Mode menu and a View only button at the toolbar's right end whose click requests edit access (R01 1); `/deck/:id`, `/embed/:id`, `/present/:id` and `/print/:id` shape their payloads by role and answer one 404 page for a deck the caller may not read ("This presentation is not available to you, or does not exist." with the Request access form); `/decks` reads the caller's own index (Owned by me, Shared with me, Recent, Trash) instead of syncing every deck; every server function and route calls `authorize(ctx, deckId, capability)` first and answers 401 without identity, 403 with identity and no right, 404 when existence must not be shown (R09 8.1, 8.2). Menus hide what a role cannot do rather than disabling it (R09 2.3).

**Links.** The token is 16 bytes from `crypto.getRandomValues`, base64url, stored as its SHA-256 with its role, revocable one by one, rotated by Rotate and wholesale by Stop sharing, with optional expiry (none, 7, 30, 90 days, a date) (R09 3.2). The URL a rep copies is `https://<host>/s/<token>`; the exchange route validates the token, writes a `linkGrant { linkId, role }` on the session, sets `Referrer-Policy: no-referrer`, renders no third party content, and redirects to `/deck/<id>` or `/edit/<id>` with no query (R10 F40). What the rep sees during a screen share afterwards is `/deck/q4-review`, which is worthless to a viewer of the share. Copy link in the toolbar copies the address without a token; the Share dialog's Copy link copies the `/s/` form of the current general access link. A dead token answers the 404 page (R09 3.2). Every response reached through a token carries `X-Robots-Tag: noindex`. Departure from R09 3.3 recorded: R09 chose `?k=` in the query; R10 F40 asked for the one time exchange; this proposal takes the exchange because the sales rep's screen is the leak the query form cannot close, and it pays the cost R09 named (a server rendered page must authorize before it renders) with the session grant the redirect lands on.

**Publish to web.** `deck.publish` mints a `?p=` token stored hashed under `publish`; the Publish dialog's Link tab shows `/deck/<id>?p=<token>&present=1` and the Embed tab the iframe with `/embed/<id>?p=<token>`; the player always shows the current revision (Google republishes automatically for Slides, research/10 A16); `deck.unpublish` revokes and the URL answers 410 "This presentation is no longer published"; the query form is kept here because an iframe cannot receive a first party cookie and a published URL is public by definition (R09 3.5). The gt-brand deck stays `open: viewer` for the Prototemplate embed for as long as Kevin wants (SPEC 0.25).

**Invitations and request access.** `share.invite` writes one grant per address, binds it to an existing account with that verified email at once, sends one mail with the deck link and the message when Notify people is on, and never says whether an account exists; the recipient signs in with the email code (section 4) and the grant binds on the first session with that verified email (R09 4.1). Pending invitations expire after 30 days. Request access is the 404 page's form (a role picker defaulting to Viewer, a message, an email field for anonymous requesters) and the View only button's "Request edit access"; the owner sees a dot on Share and a Review banner in the dialog with Approve as <role> and Decline, and receives at most one mail per deck per hour that batches requests; the requester's answer is always "If this presentation exists, its owner has been asked." (R09 4.4, R10 F41). Transfer ownership follows Google's accept flow to a signed in account only (R09 4.3).

**The Share dialog** (R09 3.6), Google's two halves in Turboslide's words, fixed width 520, rows of fixed height inside a scroll region with `scrollbar-gutter: stable`: heading "Share <title>"; a people field "Add people by email" with a role dropdown (Viewer, Commenter, Editor), "Notify people" on by default, a message field, Send; the people list (Owner first, then grants with chip, name or email, "Pending", a role dropdown per row with Transfer ownership, Add expiration, Remove access); General access with Restricted and Anyone with the link, a role dropdown under the latter defaulting to Viewer, the legacy `open` state shown as "Anyone with the deck's address can view (legacy)" with Switch to a link; a Review banner slot present at every moment ("No pending requests" when empty); the gear for the owner with Editors can change permissions and share, Viewers and commenters can download, print and copy, Viewers can see comments, Show names to link visitors, Allow embedded HTML blocks in this shared presentation; the footer Copy link and Done with the sentence "Speaker notes and skipped slides never travel with a view or comment link". The Present link and the embed snippet move to Publish to web, where Google keeps them.

**The view token of round one's later list.** SPEC 0.25 named a revocable per deck view token with Stop sharing as the item that could not ship without breaking `/deck/:id` links and the embed. The `open` legacy mode keeps every existing link working, `link` mode gives new decks the token, Stop sharing revokes every link, and the embed keeps its own `?p=` token; the item is discharged (R09 3.4).

**Enforcement and actions.** `authorize()` is one function in `packages/store/src/access.ts`, table tested for every role and capability, called first in every server function of `write.ts`, `decks.ts`, `download.ts`, `bundle.ts`, `render.ts`, `sessions.ts` and `agent-actions.ts`, in `handleActionRequest` and in the MCP tool wrapper, and re-checked by the stream on connect, every 60 s and on every `access:<deckId>` message so a removed editor loses the stream within a second (R09 8.1, 9). ## 4. Optional accounts and the avatar

### 4.1 The anonymous identity

The first request without an identity cookie gets a `__Host-ts_id` cookie: a v4 UUID sealed with h3's `seal` under `TURBOSLIDE_SESSION_SECRET`, `Secure`, `HttpOnly`, `SameSite=Lax`, `Path=/`, a long `Max-Age`; the browser mirrors the display name and avatar choice in `localStorage` for instant paint (R03 B2). The id is the principal for every write, comment, lease, presence record and rate limit counter; `Author` gains an optional `principalId` beside the label (R03 E2), and `?author=` leaves the editor and `/new` (the CLI keeps `--author` for the file store). Two visitors are two people from the first request, which today's `studio` label cannot do (R07 3.1). Roles never bind to an anonymous id (a role granted to a cookie is a role granted to a browser); an anonymous visitor's role is whatever the link carries (R03 B4).

### 4.2 The one prompt

The first edit, comment or lease from an anonymous principal opens a 360 by 168 dialog over the scrim: "How should others see you?", one field prefilled with the generated label ("Titanium 471"), Continue, and a "Sign in" link (R03 B3, R11 8 P6). Nothing else is asked, ever, of a person who edits by link. A kept label reads as a label; a typed name carries "guest" beside it on every surface (R11 5.2). The label is `<Material> <NNN>` from a closed list of 64 material and element words and a number from 100 to 999 minus a deny list, derived from `sha256(principalId)`, a space of 57,280 labels; a collision inside one deck renders a "(2)" suffix (R11 4). Google's animals are not copied (their list is unpublished, R01 2).

The server validates a typed name on every write that sets it: NFC, control and format characters and bidi controls stripped, an unterminated override refused, 1 to 40 code points with a letter or digit, UTS 39 Moderately Restrictive with `Identifier_Status` Allowed characters, reserved words by skeleton (turboslide, admin, owner, agent, system, presenter, studio, anonymous, you, me, everyone, every label word alone and the label grammar), per deck uniqueness by skeleton against the owner's name and every name in the roster and the version log under another principal ("That name is already in use in this presentation"), and the LDNOOBW list for the request's languages (R10 F44, R11 5.3). The prompt's refusal sentence is fixed and names the rule in plain words ("That name is reserved"; "That name is already in use in this presentation").

### 4.3 Sign in

Methods, in the order the dialog offers them (R03 C, J):

1. Email: one field, Continue; one mail carries a six digit code and a magic link; the code is typed into the waiting tab (the cross device case), the link opens where the mail is read; five minute expiry, three attempts per code, hashed storage (`storeToken: 'hashed'`), the same answer whether or not the address exists. Resend on a verified General Translation sending domain (Kevin decides the domain and the address, R03 J1 item 2); the free tier's 100 mails a day is enough for a pilot and Pro is $20 a month.
2. Passkeys: `@simplewebauthn` through better-auth's passkey plugin, autofill on Safari 16.1+, Chrome 108+, Firefox 122+, Edge 122+; enabled only after Kevin decides the production domain, because the rpID cannot move later without breaking every passkey, and `turboslide.vercel.app` sits under a public suffix so previews cannot share passkeys with production (R03 C1).
3. GitHub: present only when `GITHUB_CLIENT_ID` and `GITHUB_CLIENT_SECRET` are set; for engineers and agent operators, never the only method (R03 C3).

No Google sign in (the directive) and no passwords (R03 C5). The library is better-auth 1.7.4 with the official TanStack Start integration (`auth.handler` on `src/routes/api/auth/$.ts`, `tanstackStartCookies` last), because it is the one maintained library with that integration and with the anonymous to account link built in (R03 C4). Storage: Neon Postgres through the Kysely adapter for `user`, `account`, `passkey`, `verification`, the alias table and `inbox_notification`; Upstash Redis as `secondaryStorage` for sessions, verification rows, challenges and rate limit counters; on a checkout `node:sqlite` under `.turboslide/auth.sqlite` and memory (R03 D2). Sign in from the terminal is the device authorization flow (`turboslide login --to <url>` prints a user code, the person opens `/device`, the CLI receives a session and mints an API key it stores in `hosts.json`; the session token is discarded) (R09 6.2).

Sessions: seven days idle refreshed daily, `__Host-` prefixed, `HttpOnly`, `Secure`, `Lax`; a fresh session within 10 minutes is required to add a passkey, link GitHub, change the email, change share settings or delete forever (R03 F, R10 F47). The dialog is one box of 400 by 320 for every state (method list, code entry, passkey wait, error) with a reserved 20 px error row, so no state moves the page (R11 8 P7).

### 4.4 Linking the anonymous identity to an account

The alias table: signing in records the anonymous id as an alias of the account, the account's user id becomes the principal for new writes, and every stored `principalId` resolves to the current profile at render time, so history is never rewritten and edits made before sign in render as the person with the check badge (R03 E2). Two browsers linked to one account are two aliases and one person. The per identity home index of the anonymous id merges into the account's (R09 5.1). Deletion (`deleteUser` with verification) refuses while the person owns decks other people hold grants on, then removes the avatar files, aliases, sessions and inbox and renders the principal as "Deleted account" without touching version bytes (R03 G, R09 5.4).

### 4.5 Sign out and Forget this browser

Sign out ends the sign in session server side and keeps the anonymous cookie, so the person becomes the visitor they were, not a new one. "Forget this browser" deletes the anonymous cookie and the `localStorage` mirror and mints a new id; the old edits keep the old label forever and nothing links the two (R03 F, R11 4.4). Both are actions (`account.signOut`, `account.forget`) and rows in the own chip's menu.

### 4.6 The profile

The own chip's menu opens the profile dialog (560 by 640, fixed for every tab): the display name, the avatar builder (4.7), the trust state in one sentence ("Not signed in" or "Signed in as kevin@…"), the sessions list (browser and platform from the user agent, coarse location, created and last active, "This browser", Sign out per row, 40 px rows with a three row minimum), the API keys list for a signed in account (name, scopes, created, last used, Revoke; a key is shown once), and the privacy text in the plain sentences of the Share dialog's style (R03 F, G, R09 6.1, R11 8 P9). `account.me`, `account.setName`, `account.setAvatar`, `account.sessions`, `account.signOut`, `account.tokens.*` are the actions.

### 4.7 The avatar builder

Four tabs in the order a person wants them (R03 I3): Initials (one or two letters from the display name on a Bayer dithered plate whose density comes from the hash, retypeable), Glyph (a mirrored 5 by 5 field of the theme's five glyphs from the hash, with "Another" rerolling a stored salt), Dither (a radial or angled dithered ramp from the hash), Picture (an upload cropped square in the browser to at most 1024 px, refused above 5 MB, JPEG, PNG, WebP or GIF's first frame, never SVG). Every tab shows the result at 24, 32, 64 and 256 px in light and dark chrome in a fixed 2 by 4 strip from first paint; the panel body is 320 px for every tab and the crop box 256 by 256 with a plate until the image decodes (R11 8 P8). The picture path runs sharp on the server with `failOn: 'error'`, `limitInputPixels`, `rotate()`, `extract` for the crop, `resize` with `fit: 'cover'` at 32, 64, 128 and 256 to WebP at quality 80 plus one 256 PNG, metadata stripped, named by content hash under `u/<avatarKey>/` on the public store, the old prefix deleted and `avatarKey` rotated on every change; no original is kept (R03 I4, R10 F43). Anonymous principals may use Initials, Glyph and Dither and not Picture (uploads need an identity to rate limit and to delete); the generated variants are computed from the principal id by `packages/identity` with no dependency, the same function drawing SVG for the chrome and a 1-bit PNG for exports and the CLI (R11 7.1).

### 5.3 The `html` escape block and the standalone export (closes F4)

The block's five regular expressions miss attribute separators other than whitespace, entity encoded schemes, `formaction`, `@import` and `position: fixed` (R04 F4). Three layers, all three:

1. Isolation at render time in the studio, the viewer, print, present and the standalone file: each `html` block renders into `<iframe sandbox srcdoc>` with no `allow-scripts` and no `allow-same-origin`, sized to the block's `pos` box, carrying the theme CSS and a `<meta http-equiv="Content-Security-Policy" content="default-src 'none'; img-src 'self' data: https://<public store>; style-src 'unsafe-inline'; font-src 'self'">`. The editor shows the frame and an "Edit HTML" side panel with the source, since editing inside a sandboxed frame is impossible (R04 8.4). The PPTX and PDF exporters keep rasterizing the block in Chromium.
2. A real sanitizer at write and render: DOMPurify 3.4.15 in `packages/render/src/blocks/html-sanitize.ts` with an injected window (the browser's own in the editor, jsdom on the server at admission and in the CLI), profiles html and svg, `FORBID_TAGS` script, iframe, object, embed, base, meta, link, style, form, input, button, textarea, select, math, `FORBID_ATTR` style, `ALLOWED_URI_REGEXP` limited to `https:`, `data:image/`, `#` and `assets/`; the `css` field through a small tokenizer that drops `@import`, `@font-face`, `@namespace`, `url()` outside `assets/`, `expression(`, `behavior:`, `-moz-binding`, `position: fixed` and `position: sticky`, then `scopeCss` as today. ## 7. Background images with dithers, and shader backgrounds

### 7.1 The field

`PictureDither` joins `pictureToolFields` in `packages/schema/src/blocks.ts`, so `picture` and `shot` take it (R06 4.1):

```ts
type PictureDither = {
  pattern: 'bayer8' | 'bayer4' | 'blue64' | 'random';
  tone?: 'two' | 'three' | 'original';
  steps?: number; // 2 to 7 for tone 'original'
  cell?: 1 | 2 | 3 | 4; // sheet pixels; 2 is the deck's cell
  strength?: number; // 0 to 1, the dithered plane's opacity over the picture
  black?: number;
  white?: number;
  gamma?: number; // the deck's tone LUT
  invert?: boolean;
  polarity?: 'auto' | 'dark-ground' | 'light-ground' | 'same';
  blur?: number;
  minFilter?: number;
  channel?: 'gray' | 'r' | 'g' | 'b';
  seed?: number;
};
```

Defaults when absent: `tone` two, `steps` 4, `cell` 2, `strength` 1, `black` 0, `white` 255, `gamma` 1, `invert` false, `polarity` auto, `blur` 0, `minFilter` 0, `channel` gray, `seed` 0. The Dither toggle is the presence of the field: on writes `{ pattern: 'bayer8' }`, off removes it; `block.resetImage` removes it with `trim`, `mask` and `adjust`, as Google's Reset image returns to the original photo (R06 2). The zod schema carries `annotate` groups so the generated controls, the window API and the CLI usage agree; ranges are bounded (blur at most 8, minFilter at most 9, `seed` a 32 bit integer) because both cost time with the radius (R06 6.4). A block level dither over an asset that already carries a two tone `treatment` and no `sourceFile` is refused with "the asset has no continuous source (sourceFile); the committed twins are already dithered"; `asset.add --replace-source <id> <file>` attaches a source to the 16 GT assets so they can be re-toned in the app (R06 6.3).

### 7.2 The pipeline and the renderer path

One browser safe function `ditherPicture(source, box, view, dither, theme, adjust?)` in `packages/effects/src/dither.ts`, shared by the studio's worker, the sheet runtime and Node: cover fit of the trimmed region to the screen size (`w / cell` by `h / cell`) through the pinned Lanczos3 after gray or one channel, invert, min filter and blur (cached as `toneBase`); autocontrast at 0.5 percent and the tone LUT; the block's brightness and contrast folded in; the threshold against `bayer8` (the deck's table), `bayer4`, a 64 by 64 CC0 blue noise texture pinned by sha256, or a deterministic hash for `random`; polarity by the lit fraction; paint in the theme's ink and paper, three tones with titanium in the middle, or posterised colours, at `cell` sheet pixels by nearest upscale; strength under 1 composites the plane over the continuous picture (R06 4.2). With `bayer8`, tone two, cell 2 and strength 1 the stages are the deck's pipeline unchanged, and a parity test pins that a picture over the Rosetta source with the recorded parameters regenerates the committed cells at agreement 1.0 (R06 7).

The live preview meets 100 ms by caching `toneBase` and re-running only the LUT, the threshold and the paint per slider event (a few milliseconds over 360,000 cells), dropping stale requests, and committing `block.set /dither` on pointer up so the store sees one write per drag (R06 4.3). The renderer stays one string function: `renderPicture` writes `data-dither`, `data-dither-key` and `data-dither-state` on the picture root, points `src` and the twins at a materialized variant when the asset record lists one for the key (state `variant`), and otherwise shows the continuous twin with a `canvas.picture-dither` overlay at `position: absolute; inset: 0; image-rendering: pixelated` that the runtime paints from the worker (state `live`, editing only) (R06 4.4). Variants are recorded on the asset (`Asset.variants[key] = { key, twins, size, scale, metrics, producedAt }`) as `assets/<asset>.dither-<key12>-light.png`, `-dark.png` or one neutral file, 1-bit or palette PNGs of about 4 KB each at cell 2 (R06 4.5), written by `picture.materialize` through `putAsset` of 5.4 so they reach the store on the hosted instance (R10 F49 is the precondition).

### 7.3 The export paths

The Material section keeps preset, uniforms, anchor, plate and Capture frame, and its Two-tone toggle becomes the same Dither toggle and section, so a shader frame takes `dither` as a block field like any picture. In editing a material picture with `dither` shows the dithered frozen frame and the shader does not play under it (dithering a moving frame per animation frame through the integer pipeline is outside the budget and would not match the export); a Play button plays the undithered shader and stops on any edit (R06 4.9). The frozen frame is the export, as today. Paper's own image dithering shader is recorded for round four as a preview only: its float luminance and screen space grid do not reproduce the integer pipeline (R06 4.4).

### 7.9 Storage and hosting

Variants and sources are committed under `assets/` on a checkout and land on the two stores of 5.8 hosted (variants public under the deck's key, sources private); the GT deck's 16 pictures materialized at both scales add under 1 MB against its 30 MB of twins; materialization is CPU (under a second per picture in Node) inside the export budgets, rate limited as a capture class action, and routed through the render worker's queue for a deck with more than 64 dithered pictures (R06 6.1, 6.2). The acceptance includes a hosted round trip: add a picture, dither it, materialize, open the deck on a second instance, see the variant (R06 7).

## 12. Risks

1. Instance spread. Nothing pins a deck to a function instance; admission reads the mirror plus the stream on whichever instance takes the POST. The measurement of R02 7.12 item 1 must show admission under 10 ms with the per instance incremental apply; if it does not, the Durable Objects plug in is the escape.
2. Stream loss. Redis eviction stays off and the fixed plan removes the command budget as an attack, but an outage still loses admitted operations at the server for up to a few seconds; the two level acknowledgement and client resend bound the loss to position, never content. The `blob` tier is the automatic fallback and the editor says so.
3. Transform correctness. The text transform is the one piece of new algorithmic code; the property tests are the gate, and the design confines transformation to insert, delete, mark and case on one Text so everything else stays last writer wins or re-anchoring.
4. Agents typing over people. An agent's whole Text rewrite wins over a person's in flight typing; the person's characters re-anchor by diff and, when the diff cannot place them, return in the recovery card. The wording must make the card read as "here is your text" and never as an error.
5. The exchange route. `/s/<token>` depends on a first party cookie; a browser with cookies blocked cannot open a shared link (the page says so and offers the sign in path). The embed keeps `?p=` for that reason.
6. The comments departure. Hiding comments from viewers by default departs from Google and Notion; a team that expects Google's behaviour must find the gear switch. The Share dialog's sentence names it.
7. The hosted asset write fix (P11) must land first. Every picture, dither variant and material frame of this round depends on `putAsset`; a build order that lets B5 land before B1's day one delivery ships pictures that vanish on the next instance.
8. Resend and DNS. Invitations, codes and digests need a verified sending domain under General Translation's DNS and a person who holds it; until then sign in is passkeys or GitHub only, which excludes the sales audience. Kevin's decision (R03 J1 item 2) gates R5.
9. The production domain and passkeys. A passkey made under `turboslide.vercel.app` breaks on a move to a custom domain; the design gates passkeys on the decision and never makes them the only method.
10. Anonymous owners. A rep who never signs in and clears cookies loses the way back to a deck they own; the editor's persistent line on such decks ("Not signed in. Sign in, or copy an edit link, to get back to this presentation from another browser") and the admin's `assignOwner` are the mitigations.
11. Legacy `open: editor` decks. Until claimed, every existing deck stays editable by anyone with its address, as today; the shadow week and the Claim banner shorten the window, and the admin claims the rest after R7.
12. The `html` block sandbox and the GT deck. The GT import's escape blocks must render identically inside the sandboxed frame at their `pos` boxes; the canvas fidelity gate is the check, and a mismatch means the frame's theme CSS is incomplete, not that the sandbox is wrong.
13. DOMPurify on the server needs jsdom in the admission path and the CLI; it is loaded only when a deck holds an `html` block, and the bundle size of the studio function is watched.
14. The CLS gate's flakiness. A zero tolerance gate on a shared machine can fail on a slow frame; the frame sampler and the fixed marks make failures attributable, and the gate runs against the production preview, never the dev server.
15. Costs outside Vercel's spend cap. Upstash and Resend bill separately; the fixed Redis plan, the per identity command budget, the mention email gate and the daily alert at 60 percent of the Resend plan bound them.
16. Name rules refusing legitimate names. UTS 39's Moderately Restrictive level refuses a Cyrillic plus Latin name; the refusal sentence is fixed and the person may sign in, where the verified email disambiguates.
17. Single region. Europe pays one Atlantic round trip per operation; the numbers are typical and unmeasured.
18. Scope. About seventy actions, six builders and three merges is the largest round yet; the integrator's day one merge of the seams is what keeps the builders parallel, and the rollout order of 8.4 keeps every link working if the round ships in parts.

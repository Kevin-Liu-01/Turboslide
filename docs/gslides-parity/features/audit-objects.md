## 1. What a seller feels today

The structured objects work. A seller inserts a 4 by 3 table in two clicks, double clicks a cell and types a row of numbers with Tab between the cells, drags a column seam with a readout and one undo, selects a range by a drag from an open cell or a Shift click, merges and unmerges it from the right click menu or the toolbar, clears it with Delete, fills a header row from the toolbar, aligns the numbers column from the toolbar, adds and removes rows and columns from the right click menu or the Format menu with the table selected by one click, and removes the table with Backspace. A chart lands in two clicks with sample data, Edit data opens a grid at the top of the panel where a value, a category name and a series name each take a double click and Enter, a paste of tab separated rows from a spreadsheet replaces the data, the type, legend and number format change from the toolbar in two clicks, Show values and a title write and draw, the pie warns which series it dropped, and Cmd+Z takes every one of those back in one step, from the panel or the stage. A diagram lands from a panel with a live preview, its labels edit, and the group takes a fill from the toolbar. Word art and a labelled shape insert and edit as text.

What the seller feels is the distance between clicking and typing. One click on a table selects the table and every cell reads "Click to add text", so the seller clicks, nothing happens, and then double clicks (Google puts the caret in the cell on one click). A stray key with the table selected overwrites the first cell. The arrow keys never leave a cell. A row is four clicks away through a right click menu because nothing on the table itself adds one, and nothing drags a row edge. Bold on a range opens an engineering dialog ("Unknown field typography") and the title row reads "Couldn't save, retrying". In the chart grid, typing on a cell does nothing because the stage swallows the letters, Escape closes the panel, the remove buttons are invisible until hovered, and under the tidy Chart data section the panel draws a second chart type, raw JSON for the categories and series and a second Legend that reads "none" while the first reads Right. A diagram step is two objects with the same box, so a double click always lands on the label, a drag moves the label away from its box, and removing one step is three enter and delete cycles. A shape's label sits in the top left corner until the seller centres it twice. Word art keeps its 88 px letters when its box grows.

## 4. The intuitive editing model

The model below is the proposal the items above add up to. It keeps the click model of AMENDMENTS.md A1 for the object as a whole and gives each structured object a second, inner layer that Google, Notion, Pitch and Keynote all have: once the object is selected, its parts are direct.

### 4.1 Direct manipulation first

- The table. One click selects the table (A1 rule 1); a drag from anywhere inside an unselected table moves it (rule 2). On the selected table a second click on a cell places the caret in that cell at the point clicked, a drag from a cell selects a range, a Shift click extends one, and the table moves by its ring band, its chip and its handles (item 2). Seams between columns and rows drag with the readout and one undo (item 8). A "+" on the right and bottom edges adds a column or a row at the seam under the pointer, and the hover bands above the columns and left of the rows select a whole column or row (item 9). A bar under the selection carries the row and column buttons, Merge or Unmerge, Fill, Border, Align and the header toggle (item 3), each one existing `tablePlan`. Every command keeps the caret or the range (item 18). Bold, Italic, size, colour and Align on a range write every cell (item 7). A spreadsheet paste makes a table or fills cells from the caret (item 12).
- The chart. A double click opens the data; a click on a bar, a point or a slice selects its cell in the grid; an Edit data button sits under the selected chart (item 17). The grid owns its keys: typing edits the active cell, Enter opens it, Escape restores it and stays, Tab and the arrows move, a paste replaces the data (item 1). The remove controls and the swatches are visible (item 21); the kit's colours lead the swatches (item 19); the toolbar's Legend and Number format picks write what the panel writes (item 15); the panel lists each property once (item 16).
- The diagram. A step is one shape with its label (item 10): a double click opens the label, a drag moves the step and its connector follows, Delete removes it and closes the gap. Tab moves from label to label (item 11). The group's tail has the text controls (item 13). A "+" after the last step adds one (item 14); the panel takes the labels as text (item 23).
- Word art and shapes. A shape's label is centred by default (item 4). Word art scales its letters with its box (item 20) and shows fill and outline on its tail (item 25).

### 4.2 Keyboard parity with Google Slides

Inside a cell: Tab and Shift+Tab move between cells and Tab on the last cell adds a row (works today); the arrows at the text edges cross cells (item 6); Cmd+B, Cmd+I, Cmd+U and the alignment chords apply to the range or the cell (item 7); Delete on a range clears the texts (works today); Escape leaves the cell with the table selected and a second Escape clears the selection (works today). With the table selected: a printable key or Enter opens the remembered cell with the caret at the end and nothing selected (item 5). In the chart grid: the keys the section documents, owned by the panel (item 1). In a diagram: Tab from label to label, Enter on the group opens the first label (item 11).

### 4.3 One undo per gesture

Measured today and to keep: typing a cell's text is one Cmd+Z (row 25: "alpha beta gamma" to "" in one step), a seam drag is one, a column or row insert is one, a fill on a range is one, a merge is one, a kind change to pie with a dropped series is one, a category removed from the grid is one from the panel or the stage. Every proposal above is one commit with one history entry and a snackbar with Undo where the result is not under the pointer (a pasted table, a step removed).

### 4.4 The panel as the second path

Format options stays the complete map: the Table section leads for a table (item 22) with the border, the header toggle, one Height field for the selected rows, the cell fill and border, Merge and Unmerge and the insert and delete buttons; the Chart data section leads for a chart and lists each property once (item 16); the generated JSON stays behind Advanced tools for the agent's reading. The right click menus keep every row they have. Nothing in this model removes a route a seller already knows.

## 5. Summary

What a seller feels today: the objects are there and they are one gesture too far. A table takes a double click to type into, four clicks to add a row, and cannot bold its header; a chart grid ignores typing and closes on Escape; a diagram is a pile of separate boxes and labels; a shape label lands in the corner; word art does not scale.

The ten items this round should do first, in the order of value then effort:

1. Item 1, the chart grid's keys: the panel's root joins the chrome controls so typing edits the active cell and Escape stays (value 5, effort 1).
2. Item 2, the second click into a cell of the selected table places the caret, a drag from a cell selects a range, the frame moves the table (value 5, effort 3).
3. Item 4, a shape's label centred and middle by default (value 4, effort 1).
4. Item 5, a printable key on a selected table appends in the remembered cell instead of replacing cell 1,1 (value 4, effort 2).
5. Item 6, the arrows cross cells at the text edges, Shift with an arrow extends a range (value 4, effort 2).
6. Item 11, a double click on a diagram label opens it at once and Tab moves to the next label (value 4, effort 2).
7. Item 7, Bold, Italic, size, colour and Align on a range write every cell and the refusal card never opens for a chrome control (value 4, effort 3).
8. Item 8, row seams with the readout and one undo (value 4, effort 3).
9. Item 9, the "+" at the table's right and bottom edges and the row and column heads (value 4, effort 3).
10. Item 10, a diagram step as one shape with its label, so a drag, a double click and Delete act on the step (value 4, effort 3).

Behind those: item 3 (the floating table bar, value 4 and effort 4, once items 2, 9 and 18 have given it its gestures), items 15 and 16 (the toolbar's Legend None and the duplicate chart controls, one day together), item 18 (commands keep the caret), items 19 and 21 (the kit colours and the visible grid controls), item 12 (the spreadsheet paste), item 20 (word art scales), item 22 (the Table section first).

What to leave: the rotation handle on a table (item 26) and the cell prompts (item 24) are polish for a quiet day; the add step handle (item 14) waits until item 10 has made a step one object; the labels field and Chart this table (item 23) ride with the assist round; the inserted object's selection is the product round's; the connector end that did not bind was one try and belongs to the return matrix's connector rows. Nothing here proposes a change to the export paths: every proposal writes fields the renderer and the exporters already read.

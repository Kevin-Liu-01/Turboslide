# Google Slides parity specification, round five

The binding specification for round five of Turboslide, written 2026-09-14 against `main` at `d5d7f07` (round three and its hotfix) while round four (the identity, `/home` and performance, `SPEC-4.md`, `MILESTONES-4.md`) is being built in the working tree. Every code fact below was read with `git show d5d7f07:<path>`; round four's files are read from its documents, never from the tree. This document is an addendum in the shape of `SPEC-3.md` and `SPEC-4.md`: where it is silent, `SPEC.md`, `SPEC-2.md`, `SPEC-3.md` and `SPEC-4.md` stand; where they disagree, this document wins for round five.

It is the synthesis of the three proposals in `docs/gslides-parity/design-5/` (`proposal-1-google.md`, `proposal-2-sales.md`, `proposal-3-architecture.md`) under the three judgments beside them (`judge-1.md`, `judge-2.md`, `judge-3.md`). Judges 2 and 3 chose proposal 3 (38 and 40 points against 33 and 38 for proposal 1 and 36 and 40 for proposal 2); judge 1 chose proposal 1 (40 against 38 and 39). The totals across the three judges are 117 for proposal 3, 114 for proposal 2 and 111 for proposal 1. Proposal 3, the architecture, is the spine of this document: the resting rule, one schedule, one scene, one id with four spellings, the schema table with the meaning of every absent field, the day 0 seam. Proposal 1's surface tables (the panel controls, the dialogs, the eight templates, the building blocks, the Dictionary panel, the drawing box, the pen and the in show downloads) are grafted where the judges named them and are the reference for labels and positions. Proposal 2's seven sales scenarios open this document, and its security default for media, its import defaults, its chat sentence and its presenter preview are grafted. Where two judges disagreed, section 0 names the judge whose reading holds and records the other.

Kevin's directives, verbatim: "...it has all the features and exact behaviors of google slides...literally search up and research everything google slides offers...mimic it perfectly"; "keep going on all of these and dont stop until literally all google slides features are supported with full agent queryability and editability esp on locals"; "have shaders and all the fun effects we can make". Round five is the last parity round: everything Google Slides offers that a standalone product can offer and that earlier rounds deferred, plus the deferred items of SPEC-2 section 12, SPEC-3 section 17 and SPEC-4 section 7 that need no infrastructure decision from Kevin. The items that need one (a worker host, Redis, Postgres, Resend, the domain, a stock picture provider, the mail sender) stay on his list in section 17 and are not designed away.

The eleven research reports of `docs/gslides-parity/research-5/` are the facts behind every claim, cited as R01 to R11 with their sections (R01 6.2 is report 01 section 6.2); their URLs and read dates (2026-09-14) are in their Sources sections and are not repeated here. No Google page was read for this document, no account was signed in to, and no Google, YouTube or Microsoft icon or artwork is proposed: the glyphs Turboslide draws (the play glyph, the speaker glyph, the person glyph, the motion glyph, the equation toolbar's symbols) are its own or Heroicons 20 solid from the theme sprite.

Rules of the text, Kevin's: plain technical English, sentence case, no em dashes, no metaphors, no trailing periods on headings, full sentences in body text; buttons Title Case; the product is written "Turboslide"; a number on a page carries its source line.

## The sales scenarios and the rules they impose

Round one's report 07 established the user: a seller who edits an existing deck far more often than building one, tailors a small set of slides per prospect, presents over a call and sends a link or a PDF afterwards. Round five is the last parity round, so the scenarios are the ones that still send a seller back to PowerPoint today (proposal 2 section 0, grafted by judge 3). Every later section names the scenario it answers.

| S2 | The demo video must play on the call, at the right moment, with sound | Insert > Video takes the file or the YouTube link, the default is Play (on click), the poster is the frame at Start at, the show plays it on the next click and the presenter console shows what is playing | Section 3 |

| S4 | The team wants a sales template in their colours with their mark in the corner | Start from Sales pitch, open Edit theme, set Text and background 1 and Accent 1, put a picture in the corner slot, Rename; every slide follows; the deck is saved as a template of the organisation | Sections 4, 9 |

The rules that follow, applied in every section:

1. The document is the truth and the renderer emits the resting state. A transition, an animation, a media reference, a page size, a language, a theme edit and an equation are fields of the deck or the slide; what a surface does with motion or media is a layer over the rendered nodes, never a second render path.
2. Google's words in Google's places. The Motion panel, the Insert video dialog's tabs, the Page setup dropdown, the print layout dropdown, the Preferences tabs, the spell check card and the Accessibility menu carry Google's labels; where a label is unverified the fixture says so and the verifier's audit names it. A row Google does not have carries `turboslide: true`.
3. One implementation per capability, read by every surface. The schedule of a slide is computed once; the page size is one helper; the media controller is one module the show and the standalone runtime share; the autocorrect engine is one pure function the editor, the notes and the CLI call; the print layout is one pure function the route and the PDF share.
4. Nothing leaves that should not. Media on restricted decks take the keyed prefix from the first day; the YouTube thumbnail is never stored without an explicit action; chat is not written to the deck; the standalone HTML never carries a token; every byte that enters passes the zip guard, a signature sniff, the per tier caps, `safeFetch` and the link scheme filter.
5. Zero shift and the budgets hold. The Motion panel, the sidebar strip, the spell check card, the Chat panel and the theme mode's toolbar are reserved slots or absolute overlays under the nine rules of SPEC-3 9; the equation renderer, the spelling Worker, the media parsers and the motion CSS are lazy chunks under the round four ceilings.
6. Import shows what dropped, in rows an agent and a dialog both read; a still shows every object because a PDF that hides a pricing row a procurement reader needs is a defect; chat says it is not saved.

## 1. The one truth, extended

### 1.1 The rules

1. The document is the truth. A transition, an animation, a media reference, a page size, a language, a theme edit, an imported theme record and an equation are fields of the deck or the slide, written through `slide.set`, `deck.set`, `block.insert` and `block.set` as today, so undo, version history, the operation stream, `deck.pack`, `deck.push` and `diff.run` carry them with no new mutation op. `MUTATION_OPS` is unchanged.
2. The renderer emits the resting state (0.3). What a surface does with motion or media is a layer over the rendered nodes, addressed by `data-block` and `data-media`, never a second render path.
3. The schedule is computed once (0.4). `compileMotion` is pure, tested in Node, and its output is the input of the show, the standalone script, the PPTX timing writer, the ODP animation writer and the `motion.compile` action.
4. The scene is the one reader for the writers (0.6). `extractScenes` reads the document and the rendered DOM; `pptx/build.ts`, `odp/build.ts` and `svg/write.ts` read the scene. A field the exporters need (the schedule, the media records, the equation OMML, the page, the theme CSS, the language) is a scene field.
5. Every capability is an action (SPEC 7.1). The CLI flags are the input's fields, the MCP tool takes the same object, the window handler runs the same store action over the editor's store, and the hosted `POST /api/actions/<id>` runs it server side. A control in a dialog or panel writes exactly one action.
6. The security plan applies to every byte that enters: the store's zip guard, a signature sniff before a decoder, per tier caps, `safeFetch` for URLs, the link scheme filter, no external entities in XML, no `<math>` from the escape block (the equation renderer is the one writer of `<math>`; `sanitize/html.ts` keeps `math` in `FORBIDDEN_TAGS`).

export type MotionSchedule = {
  steps: MotionStep[];                 // steps[0] plays on entry, the rest on clicks
  hiddenAtStart: BlockId[];            // entrance effects hide their block first
  transition: { kind: TransitionKind; durationMs: number } | null;

Semantics, the contract every consumer's test pins: a `click` opens a new step; `withPrevious` joins the current step at delay 0; `afterPrevious` joins the current step at the previous effect's end; a first `afterPrevious` or `withPrevious` joins step 0; `byParagraph` expands to one effect per paragraph, each its own step under `click`, chained under `afterPrevious`, together under `withPrevious`; entrance effects add their block to `hiddenAtStart`; exit effects hide their block at their end; `spin` never changes visibility; a `playMedia` effect's duration is the media's remaining length after the trim, or the step's length when it loops; an animation whose block has no rendered node is skipped and named in `skipped` and in the export residual. `paragraphs(blockId)` is supplied by the caller: the renderer's `.para` span count plus list item count in the browser, the count of distinct `SceneLine.paragraph` values in the exporter, so the count the show gates on is the count the writer emits as `a:p` and indexes in `p:pRg`.

# The site: `/home` and `/docs`

The Workbench direction Kevin picked on 2026-10-14 for the landing and the docs, built on `site/bench` from `8c9465b6`. It replaces `docs/LANDING.md` for `/home`. The build plan, with its decisions D1 to D12 in full and the file by file work, is the orchestrator's (`/Users/kevinliu/turboslide-scratch/site/plan.md`); this file keeps what a builder of these pages needs.

## 1. The page

- `/home` is one route file (`apps/studio/src/routes/home.tsx`) over `apps/studio/src/components/site/`: the bar with the T in a box (`SiteBar`, `BoxedMark`), screen 01 (`Hero`: the h1 with its two stacks, the lead with New Presentation and Open the Example Deck, the editor's figure), screen 02 (`AgentsScreen`: the recorded CLI run beside `slide.new` on each transport), screen 03 (`PresentScreen`: the viewer and the slideshow keys) and the footer (`SiteFooter`).
- Every word is in `copy.ts`, every count in `facts.ts` (pinned to the action table by `facts.test.ts`), the recorded run in `run.ts` (pinned to `home-deck/recorded/run.json` by `run.test.ts`).
- The stylesheet `site.css` is linked from the route's head with `?url`, so no rule reaches another page; every selector names the root `.ts-site` (`routes/-home-css.test.ts`). `/docs` takes the same root and stylesheet in its own build step.
- The page imports no editor module (`graph.test.ts`). The live editor is a same origin frame of its own route, `/try` (section 5); the hero box holds a poster until the frame says it is ready.

## 2. Decisions

- D6. The four agent marks (Claude Code, Codex, OpenClaw, Hermes) are inlined once from thesvg.org in `packages/chrome/src/presence/agent-marks.tsx`, MIT, each in its own colours; Hermes draws in ink on a dark ground.
- D7. `/home` and `/docs` share `components/site/`.
- D8. The display size is 72 px at 1440 (`clamp(40px, 5vw, 72px)`; 36/44 on a phone), so the one line h1 and its two stacks fit the 1280 px column.
- D9. The capital labels name figures, tables, navigation groups and screen numbers; the brand lint accepts `.ts-site .t-label` by name.
- D11. The solid icons are Heroicons 20 solid from the chrome's one icon module, picked by name from its exported constants (`components/site/SiteIcon.tsx`), so a page carries the paths it draws and none of the editor's table.
- D12. No local production build: the byte lines and the tooltip audit are read on a preview.

## 3. Type, grid and colour

- One face, the product's InterVariable 4.1, weights 400 and 500, `font-optical-sizing: auto`, `font-synthesis: none`.
- Display 72/72 (500, -0.032em); Title 40/48 (400); Head 24/32 (500, opsz 32); Lead 20/28; Read 17/28; Small 14/20; Label 12/16 capitals (500, +0.06em, `case`, tabular figures); code in the platform monospace on the `#101010` panel.
- The column is 1280 px with 32 px gutters (16 px at 720 px and under); one layout breakpoint at 1023 px; the bar is 56 px.
- Colours are the generated `--pt-` tokens, so both appearances follow `html[data-theme]`. Corners: 0 for frames and slides, 4 px key chips, 6 px buttons, round avatars.

## 4. The title and the hero

- The h1 reads "Presentations for people and agents" and is named by that sentence (`aria-label`). From the top right of "people" rises an arc of five people and from the top right of "agents" an arc of the four agents (Kevin, 2026-10-15: "make them look better coooolrr in an arc and make them interactable"). Each arc is positioned from its word and out of the line's flow, so the sentence keeps its word spacing; site.css places avatar `i` on a circle at the angle `--a0` less `i` steps of `--da`, the first with its lower left at the word's top right corner (40 px avatars at 1440, 26 px on a phone, where the line breaks after "people" and the arc runs flat under the line above). Avatars overlap with a 2 px ring in the page colour between them, a solid ring in their live hue and a soft shadow (the one accepted `css/no-shadow`). People are generated portraits (`Portrait.tsx`: a head and shoulders in one of five hair cuts drawn from the name, in the hue mixed with paper and ink, the same in both appearances); agents are the four marks on a white plate of their own, in their own colours in both appearances.
- Each avatar is a button named by its person or tool and described by what pressing does. A hovering pointer or the keyboard's focus fans the arc open and lifts the avatar under it with its name in a label (on a touch screen the press alone lifts it for a moment); each arc is one tab stop whose arrows, Home and End move along it; Enter and Space press. Pressing sends `ts-try:press` to the live editor: Claude Code or Codex takes its next step at once and the visitor's stage goes to its slide, OpenClaw or Hermes joins the room for twelve seconds and leaves a comment on the slide the visitor is looking at, and a person joins the roster under their generated name with a pointer that visits that slide and leaves. The chip and the pointer of whoever was pressed flash once in their hue. A press before the editor is live loads it at once and waits for its ready; a press with less than half of the box on screen brings the box into view first, at once, since the product does not smooth-scroll. Under reduced motion the arc keeps its place, the label and the ring change at once, and the pointers jump.
- The hero box is 2:1 above 720 px (1280 by 640 at 1440, so at 1440 by 900 the editor's stage shows its whole slide above the fold) and `min(560px, 72svh)` tall on a phone, where New Presentation carries "No account is needed." beside it so the phone editor's sheet ends above the fold at 390 by 844; its poster is a capture of the real editor in each appearance and width (`scripts/site/capture.mjs`), on the title slide with both agents at work, and only the poster of the page's appearance is fetched.

## 5. The live editor in the hero

- D1. The editor runs in a same origin frame of `/try` (`apps/studio/src/routes/try.tsx`, `ssr: false`, not indexed). Inside a frame of the hero's size the editor's fixed root, its document key maps, its address hash, the phone layout under 720 px and the dialogs' scrim are already right, so the real editor is unchanged. `/try` answers `X-Frame-Options: SAMEORIGIN` and `frame-ancestors 'self'` (`server/headers.ts`); every other page but the embed answers DENY. It is rendered per request, not prerendered: its shell takes the request's CSP nonce.
- D2. One seam for the editor's server calls: `apps/studio/src/editor/backend.ts`. The server backend (`backend-server.ts`) is every server function, the room's wire, the IndexedDB mirror, sessionStorage, the page's requests and records as the editor called them before; `/try` binds the tab backend once, before the editor mounts. `backend.test.ts` pins that the editor's files import no runtime module of `../server/` and no route address.
- D3. The tab backend (`apps/studio/src/try/tab-backend.ts`) runs the room in the tab: the realtime package's room server over its memory channel, a checkpoint two seconds after the last op, comments in memory (`tab-comments.ts`), version rows in memory, the unsent queue's mirror in memory. Nothing is written to the server, no deck id reaches the store, a reload starts from the shipped deck (`hero-deck.ts`, "Northwind quarterly review").
- D4. Claude Code and Codex are headless editor controllers on the same tab room, each with its own author and undo history; every agent write is one action of the table through `editorAdapter().invoke` with the agent's `baseRevision` (`session.ts`).
- D5. An agent whose roster row names a brand mark (only the tab room writes one) draws like a person: its chip shows the brand mark, its outline is dashed in its hue, and it has a pointer and a drag ghost; its writes raise no outside write banner. Every other agent draws as before.
- D10. A feature that needs the server answers one sentence with New Presentation (`TAB_ONLY` of `packages/chrome/src/menus/strings.ts`, one line of the snackbar): the dialogs of `TAB_ONLY_DIALOGS` (`session.ts`), Presenter view, a page of the presentation (Print, the viewer), and every server function the tab backend refuses. The save cell reads "In this tab only", and the editor asks for no name. A navigation away from `/try` loads in the window around the frame.
- The script (`script.ts`) is data; the runner (`agents.ts`) plays it at a pace a person can follow. Once the visitor clicks or types in the frame, no agent writes the slide the visitor's stage shows or the block they selected, a deck level write waits while they type or drag, and no agent writes a block, slide or deck field the visitor wrote. Until then the director moves the visitor's stage to the slide an agent sets out for or last wrote, once every four seconds at most. A press on an agent in the title counts the visitor as watching until their next click or key in the frame. Every slide reads at every moment: what an agent adds arrives whole (the new slide with its heading, sentence and rows; the label box with its words) and typing only adds to a text that already reads (`script.test.ts`, `agents.test.ts`). The agents run only while the frame is on screen and its document visible; under reduced motion the pointer jumps and no drag ghost is drawn.
- The frame protocol (`components/site/try-frame.ts`): the frame says `ts-try:ready` once its filmstrip is drawn with both agents in the roster; the page then fades the frame in over the poster (150 ms, none under reduced motion) and removes the poster, so nothing moves; the page says `ts-try:visible` as the box enters and leaves the screen, and `ts-try:press` when a visitor presses an avatar of the title (`guests.ts` runs the visits of people, OpenClaw and Hermes). The appearance travels as the viewer's `gt-theme` message.

## 6. The docs

- `/docs` draws on the site's root and stylesheet: the route's head links `components/site/site.css` and then `components/docs/docs.css` (`?url`), so the docs take the type scale, the bar and the footer from the site, and every selector of `docs.css` names the docs' root `.ts-docs` (`routes/-home-css.test.ts`). `DocsShell` draws the site's bar with the docs' slot (a hairline, Documentation and the 288 px search field that opens the search window; Cmd+K, Ctrl+K and /), the navigation, the page and the site's footer (`SiteFooter`, section 7). The bar names Documentation once and draws no Sign In.
- Three columns across the 1280 px column: the navigation (224 px), the article (720 px) and the on this page column (208 px), 64 px apart. At 1024 to 1279 px the on this page column leaves and its tools move under the article; at 1023 px and under one column, where the navigation is the Contents disclosure at the top of the page (a `<details>`: no sheet, no scrim) and the search field is a 32 px button. New Presentation leaves the bar at 720 px and under.
- The navigation (`DocsSidebar`): three groups under capital labels with their solid icons (Use Turboslide `book-open`, Agents `command-line`, Reference `queue-list`) on one hairline rail; a folder's pages indented 32 px in titanium; the current page in ink at weight 500 with a 2 px ink rule (`font-synthesis: none` on the root, so no weight is faked); Action reference with its count from `components/site/facts.ts`. The front page is titled "Turboslide documentation" and named Overview in the navigation and the pager (`navName`).
- A page (`DocsArticle`): the title at the Title step and the description as the lead, nothing between the lead and the body; reading text held at 32em, tables, figures, code and index rows across the 720 px column; the on this page column numbers the h2s, draws the section the reader is in in ink and holds the page tools (Copy Page with its menu, View as Markdown, Edit this page, Report an issue, each with its solid icon); previous and next on a hair rule; the front page adds three facts counted from the page tree.
- The MDX components (`mdx.tsx`): an h2 is a numbered section whose anchor, a link named "Link to <title>" after the heading, draws its number (a CSS counter) in the gutter 16 px left of the article, inline before the title under 1024 px, so the heading's text stays its title; code sits on the `#101010` panel with a head row naming the language and Copy (4 px), a shell line drawn with a prompt that Copy never writes; inline code has no ground; a callout is a 2 px ink rule with its icon and capital label; steps and index rows (`Cards`) are ruled rows; tables take capital heads over an edge rule; tabs are words over a hairline.
- Figures (`Figure`, plan E.5): `<Figure name alt caption />` draws `/docs/figures/<name>-light.webp` or `-dark.webp` after the page's appearance (the other is never fetched), the `-390-` cut at 720 px and under where one exists, at its native size at most, in the square frame with corner crosses, with "Figure N" before the caption and a leader under it where the capture names one. `node scripts/site/capture.mjs --only figures` makes a deck for them in its own browser (/new, then the page API), cuts the six figures from the editor and the viewer in both appearances and writes their sizes and the leader's place to `apps/studio/src/docs/figures.ts`. A figure's markdown form is its light picture and its caption. `src/docs/content.test.ts` checks that every figure's files exist with alt text and a caption.
- The front page lists every page with its own title and description and the action reference's counts per group; `content.test.ts` pins both to the pages' frontmatter and to the generated `reference/index.mdx`, so a page or a count that moves fails there.
- The figures under `/docs/figures/**` take the docs' page cache rule (`max-age=0, must-revalidate`): their names carry no content hash, so a longer rule would hold a stale picture after a capture.

## 7. The footer

- One footer for every site page and the docs (`SiteFooter.tsx`; Kevin, 2026-10-15: "make a ton better footer using the animated turbolsiide logo"). On the 12 column grid: the lockup (the animated mark at 80 px, 48 px on a phone, and the word at 40/48, 28/36 on a phone) with one sentence under it; four columns under capital labels with a solid icon each: Product (New presentation, Your presentations, which the row `decks.home.your-presentations` opens, Features, the example deck), Agents (the command line, MCP, HTTP, skills, `llms.txt`), Docs (getting started, sharing, export, self-hosting) and Project (About, FAQ, GitHub, the MIT License, the third party notices); then on a soft rule "Made by General Translation" linked, the build's commit linked to GitHub, and the appearance switch (Light and Dark, `applyTheme`; the pressed one is drawn from `html[data-theme]`, so it is right before any script runs). On `/home` the lockup goes back to the top; elsewhere it goes to `/home`.
- The animated mark (`AnimatedMark.tsx`) is the boxed T drawn from `ROWS16` of `packages/theme/src/brand.ts`, one rectangle per run (`rowRuns`, the runs `rowsPath` draws), so the one drawing stays in one module. When it is first half in view the rows build from the top, each run drawn from its left end 45 ms after the row above, and then the T slides out to the right and back in from the left, once (1.5 s; site.css `site-mark-build`, `site-mark-slide`); an inner square clips the T to the frame. It plays again on hover and focus of the lockup, pauses off screen, and under `prefers-reduced-motion` (the system's Reduce motion) it never plays. Without script, and until it is first in view, it is the finished mark. Every edge is a whole pixel of the 16 px grid, so at 3 and 5 times `crispEdges` keeps it sharp.
- The build line reads `__TURBOSLIDE_BUILD__`, which both Vite configs define at build time from `server/build-commit.ts` (`buildDefine`: `VERCEL_GIT_COMMIT_SHA`, else the `TURBOSLIDE_BUILD_COMMIT` build variable the production guard passes, at seven characters); a build without either draws no build line.

## 8. Features, questions and about

- `/features`, `/faq` and `/about` (Kevin, 2026-10-15: "add an about, faq, and features page") are route files over `components/site/`: the bar, `main` and the footer on the site root, the site stylesheet linked from the head, and each page's title, description, canonical address and Open Graph lines from `meta.ts` (`siteHead`). They are prerendered with `/home` (`vite.deploy.config.ts`), listed in `/sitemap.xml` and named in the bar: Features, Documentation, About, FAQ and GitHub, with About, FAQ and GitHub out of the bar under 1024 px (the footer carries every link).
- A page is its name at the display step with its lead (`PageHead`), then numbered ruled blocks (`SiteBlock`: the landing's screen head, the block's body on the same nine columns). `/features` has six groups, each with a crop of the product from the docs' figures (`ProductCrop`, section 6) or a code panel, and one sentence per feature linked to its docs chapter; the counts under its lead come from `facts.ts`. `/faq` is one list of native `<details>` disclosures. `/about` closes with Open an Issue.
- Every word is in `pages-copy.ts`; a claim is one the docs or the code states, and a fact the code does not settle, such as a price, is left out. Inline code is written between backticks (`InlineCode`). Each page carries its schema.org data in the page (`JsonLd`, `structured-data.ts`): a WebPage about the SoftwareApplication with its feature list, a FAQPage, an AboutPage. `pages.test.ts` checks that every docs link names a page and a section the docs have.

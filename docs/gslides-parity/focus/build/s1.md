## Cycle 3 stream fix round

### The mechanism, as read on the tree before the fix

1. The stream route (`apps/studio/src/routes/api/decks.$deckId.stream.ts`) took a slot from
   `createStreamCounters` (`apps/studio/src/server/room.ts`) before it minted the client id, and
   released it only from the `cleanup` that the lifetime timer, the request's abort, the body's
   `cancel()` or a refused recheck ran through `close()`. The `write()` catch set `open = false`
   without running `cleanup`, and `close()` returned early once `open` was false, so a stream
   whose bytes the runtime refused kept its slot until the runtime cancelled the body itself. On
   the preview that came 47 to 73 s after the client had closed the stream (the probe's log), so a
   tab that reloaded or reconnected a few times inside the 240 to 290 s lifetime filled its own
   cap of four (`CAPS.streams.anonymous`).
2. The room client (`packages/realtime/client/room-client.ts`) returned from `flush()` while
   `!connected`, so no `POST /ops` left a tab whose stream was refused, and its `onError` set
   `connected = false` and scheduled nothing: the reopen was the browser's EventSource's, which
   reconnected after the server's `retry` on a 5xx and told nobody the status or the
   `retry-after`.
3. The studio's transport (`apps/studio/src/editor/controller.tsx` `sseTransport`) was an
   EventSource, which exposes neither the status nor the headers of a refused open.

### The change, per file

- `apps/studio/src/server/room.ts`
  - `createStreamCounters`: a slot is counted under its client id when the route names one
    (`take(identity, kind, address, { clientId, retire })`); the `retire` list's slots and a slot
    already held under the same id are released before the caps are judged, only when the same
    identity took them; the refusal answers the cap and the counts (`StreamCounts`); `release()`
    is idempotent and a slot released by a `retire` list is gone when the route's own `close()`
    runs later; `counters.release(identity, ids)` and `counters.counts(identity, address)` are
    exported for the route and the tests. `CAPS.streams` is untouched (Kevin's product decision).
  - `createStreamCloser(release, signal)`: the one close of a stream. `close()` runs the cleanup
    set so far, releases (always, a throwing cleanup notwithstanding), and closes the controller;
    every later call finds it closed. `write()` closes on a controller that refuses the bytes;
    `attach()` closes at once when the request is already aborted; `onClose()` runs a cleanup set
    after the close at once. The abort listener is registered on the request's signal.
  - `streamRefusalLine(deckId, kind, refusal)`: the one log line of a refusal with the cap name,
    the identity kind and the counts (never the identity, a token or a cookie).
  - `retireCandidates(deckId, raw, identity, exceptClientId)`: the filter `retireClients` applied
    (trimmed, deduplicated, at most RETIRE_MAX, never the new id, only ids whose MAC names this
    deck and this identity), shared with the route's slot release so both read the same list.
  - `bindClient(room, identity, clientId?)`: takes the id the route minted; the two existing
    callers without an id are unchanged.
- `apps/studio/src/routes/api/decks.$deckId.stream.ts`: mints the client id before the cap is
  judged, passes it and the retire candidates to `take`, logs the refusal line, keeps the 503
  `too_many_streams` body with `retry-after: 5`, and runs every ending (the lifetime timer, the
  refused recheck, the abort, `cancel()`, a refused write, an error during set up) through the
  closer. An error while the stream is set up closes the slot and errors the controller instead
  of leaving a stream that never says hello.
- `packages/realtime/client/room-client.ts`
  - `SyncStatus.streamDown`: true from the transport's report that the stream ended or its open
    was refused until the next hello; false before the first open answers.
  - `flush()` posts whenever the client holds an id and its binding stands (`bound`), stream or
    not; the POST's answer settles the ops (`take(entry)` at the position; an entry ahead of a gap
    waits for the replay, as before), `settleOwnEcho` and `settleBehind` stay. A hello leaves the
    ops of the POST in flight in flight (`inflightBatch`), so they are never re-minted under the
    new id while their answer is still coming.
  - The client owns the reopen: `openStream()` opens with `since` at the position and `retire`
    carrying the tab's earlier pages' ids and this page's own earlier ids (the newest last, at
    most RETIRE_MAX); `reopenStream(failure)` closes the handle, marks `streamDown`, and opens
    again after the server's word (`retry-after` of a refused open, the `retry:` field of a stream
    that closed, capped at REOPEN_WAIT_MAX_MS) or the ladder 500 ms doubling to BACKOFF_MAX_MS
    (a 403 or a 404 waits the cap at once). One report per open; a report from a handle the
    client has replaced is ignored. `stop()` clears the reopen timer.
  - `client_unbound` from the ops route: `bound = false`, the stream is reopened by the client
    and the ops wait for the new hello (before this they waited for the EventSource's reconnect at
    the lifetime's end).
  - `StreamFailure`, `streamFailureOf(error)`: the shape a transport reports and its reader.
  - `splitSseBlocks(buffer)`: the block splitter of the streamed fetch, pure and tested here so
    a frame split across two chunks is covered.
- `packages/realtime/client/fake-transport.ts`: `transportFor` answers `FakeTabTransport` with
  `refuseOpens(failure | null)` (every open reported as refused through `onError` on the next
  tick, no hello, no slot), `opens()` and `retires()`; `reconnectingTransport` is replaced by
  `tabTransport` (a plain transport; the client reopens, the fake reconnects nothing).
- `apps/studio/src/editor/controller.tsx` `sseTransport`: a streamed `fetch` with
  `accept: text/event-stream` in place of the EventSource. A refused open is reported with its
  status, the body's `error` and `retry-after`; a stream that ends is reported with the server's
  `retry:` value; the frames are parsed by `protocol.ts` `parseSseBlock` and `roomEventOf` over
  `splitSseBlocks`; the handle's `close()` aborts the fetch, which is the close the server sees.
  Why this and not a probe fetch beside an EventSource: the client now owns the reopen, so the
  EventSource's own reconnect had to be suppressed anyway, and a probe fetch would itself take a
  slot on a runtime that sees the abort late (the preview); one connection per open, the status
  read from the connection that matters, nothing to suppress. The route's frames and the
  `x-turboslide-client` header are unchanged, so a raw `fetch` of the stream (realtime.spec.ts
  `sync.status` row, the probes) reads the same bytes as before.
- `packages/chrome/src/editor-shell.ts` `EditorSync`: `storeDegraded?: boolean` (b1 R46) and
  `streamDown?: boolean`.
- `packages/chrome/src/TitleRow.tsx`: `saveWords(state, draft, offline, reconnecting)` answers
  `TITLE_ROW.reconnecting` in place of "All changes saved" alone; the offline sentence wins (b1
  R46, b7 FR3-R5); a write in flight keeps Saving and a refused one keeps the retry word.
  `SaveState` reads `reconnecting = !offline && (sync.streamDown || sync.storeDegraded)`, sets
  `data-state="reconnecting"` and the class `is-reconnecting` while the word shows, and the
  longest phrase list carries the sixth word (the offline sentence stays the longest).

### The deviation from R46's letter, recorded

R46 spells `if (reconnecting) return TITLE_ROW.reconnecting;` right after the offline line, so
the word would also replace Saving and the retry word. This lane puts it in the `default` arm:
the round's prompt reads "the title row reads the Reconnecting word while the stream is down and
every write is acknowledged, and All changes saved once the POST answers; a seller never sees
Saving for a stream problem". With the client posting while its stream is down, Saving is the
true word of a POST in flight (under a second on the blob tier) and the retry word the true word
of a refusal; the word for the stream alone is Reconnecting. R46's two named tests hold as
written (`saveWords('saved', false, false, true)` is the word, `saveWords('saved', false, true,
true)` is the offline sentence). If b1 or the integrator wants R46's letter, it is the one line
in `saveWords`.

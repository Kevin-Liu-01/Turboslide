### 1.3 The picture against its box after a one edge resize (rank 18; `images.resize.edge-fill`)

- Mechanism. A picture object is a `shot` figure: `figure.shot-fig`, a grid with `gap: 12px`, holding `img.shot` at `width: 100%; height: auto` (sheet.css) and an optional figcaption. The canvas rules sized `.free > .picture` and `.shot-crop` but not the figure, so after an east drag the image's height followed its aspect and stood taller than the box (audit-images row 34: img 251.7 in a box 211.5 css px), after n and s drags it stopped short, and the walk's 23 W7 rows failed on "content did not scale". hotfix 4 deferred the fix because making the image fill its box moved the converted `gt-brand/dossier` slide 2.7 percent over the fidelity budget.
- Decision. On the canvas the figure takes its box, the image row takes what the caption row leaves, and the image fills that row by stretching (`object-fit: fill`), the way Google Slides and PowerPoint stretch a picture dragged by a side handle; a corner drag keeps the aspect through the resize model (`snapResize` locks a picture's corners without Shift). Stretch, not cover, because the crop model (`trim` as fractions scaled by `1 / kept`) is a stretch model: the kept region maps to the frame exactly whatever the box, so the crop handles stay exact after an edge drag, while cover would crop the kept region a second time. FOCUS.md rank 18 names cover in parentheses as one of the two options; the row's criterion, "the picture fills its box", holds either way; the choice is recorded here for Kevin (section 8).
- The fidelity gate holds by construction, measured rather than argued. The first version of the rule took the image's border off the row (`height: calc(100% - 2px)` with a `--shot-border` variable the renderer wrote) on the reading that `img.shot` was content-box with its border overflowing the figure; the gate then failed on the 22 shot pairs of `gt-brand` (worst `glyphfield-tools` light 1.947 percent, `dossier` 1.350) and a measurement in the fidelity script's own pages (`<scratch>/measure-dossier.mjs`, the flow and the canvas render of `gt-brand/dossier`) gave the facts: the sheet sizes the image border-box, the flow image is exactly the figure (731.5 by 457.94, the measured box), and on the canvas the first rule had made the image 728.3 by 455.94, because the `fit` form's `width: auto` followed the aspect of the shortened height. The rule that holds is the plain one: on the canvas the image takes the whole row in both axes, `width: 100%; height: 100%; max-width: none; max-height: none; object-fit: fill`, so at the measured box the image is the figure's row, which is the flow's border box, and the pixels are the same; after an edge drag it stretches. A trimmed, masked or dithered shot's `.shot-crop` frame takes the row with its inline aspect ratio ignored (`aspect-ratio: auto !important`) and its image stretches inside it. The flow rules are untouched. The gate (`scripts/canvas-fidelity.mjs` over `decks/gt-brand`, `decks/templates/gt-brand`, `decks/templates/blank` at 0.5 percent) is section 7.2.
- Change: `packages/render/src/block-css.ts`, four canvas rules under the `.free > .shot-fig` prefix; `figures.ts` is unchanged in the end (the border variable of the first version was removed with it).
- Test: `packages/render/src/__tests__/focus-objects.test.ts` (a positioned shot renders exactly as the flow's, no sizing of its own on the block; a trimmed shot keeps its frame; the four rules and the empty layer rule present in `BLOCK_CSS`; the flow figure rule unchanged).
- The box a dropped picture lands in: `insertPicture` uses `DROP_PICTURE_SIZE` 480 by 272 whatever the asset's aspect (a 640 by 400 picture stood 300 tall in a 272 box, audit-images row 6), so with the image now filling its box a dropped picture would be squashed at the insert. `droppedPictureBox(at, width, size)` in `Gestures.tsx` gives the box at the asset's aspect (16 by 9 when the size is unknown, on the 8 px grid, an 8 px floor); wiring it into `insertPicture` is request R2. Test in `viewer/__tests__/focus-objects.test.ts`.

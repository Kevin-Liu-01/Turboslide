## 1. Kevin's three asks and the decision

### 1.1 The three asks, verbatim

Kevin, 2026-10-01, in this order:

### 1.2 The decision in five sentences

The realtime channel and presence move to one Cloudflare Worker (`turboslide-realtime`) holding one SQLite backed Durable Object per deck over hibernating WebSockets, which is design A's channel (`design-a.md` sections 1 to 5) with the grafts the three judges named: the live document kept in the object's SQLite as a row per slide so a wake after a pause reads local rows and no Vercel route (B 3.2 and C 2.5, `judge-3-operator.md` 6.2), the re-admission of acknowledged entries transformed past a foreign record before the tabs reload (B 3.3 item 3, `judge-1-latency.md` 5 graft 2), a runtime kill switch and a drain before a rollback (B 5.5, every judge), the hello, the replay and the first live frame from one handler (B 2.3 item 3), compaction by partition drop instead of row deletes (C 3.2), the loader reading the object's document at the head (C 1.5), a `reauth` frame with a 10 s grace on an access change (C 2.2), and a `cost.do.rows-written` row whose production threshold holds the next ship (C 8). The account database is D1 on the same Worker, reached from the Vercel function through a bearer SQL route (design A's alternative A1, `design-a.md` 1.3) with the principal records on it and the principal `touch` written at most once an hour (B 2.5 and 4.7, `judge-2-cost.md` 7), so Google sign in ships in this round with the identity runtime, the cookies and the auth routes staying on `www.turboslide.com` and no Marketplace product anywhere. The store stays on Vercel Blob and the app stays on Vercel in stage 1, because the R2 subscription is a checkout and a checkout is a payment stop under the account boundary (`research-access.md` 3.4; `docs/hosting.md` `HEAD` 802 to 804), because A's channel does not need the store to move (`judge-3-operator.md` 7), and because the app's own move waits on a CPU measurement nobody has made (`research-workers.md` 7 item 1); both are stage 2 with their triggers, lanes and cost lines in section 7. The setup is the orchestrator's through Kevin's Google session, in the order of section 6, stopping at every account creation, payment, terms acceptance or DNS change, which Kevin's message does not lift (`research-access.md` section 5; `judge-3-operator.md` 7, first bullet). Peer to peer stays refused for the reasons of `docs/REALTIME.md` section 1, and the redis tier the realtime round built stays in the tree as code and never reaches production.

### 1.3 The money on the Free plan and the day it breaks

The load model is `docs/SYNC.md` 4.3 (`HEAD` 240 to 243: "One editor hour is 12 minutes of editing at one edit per 5 s and 48 idle minutes") as `research-durable-objects.md` 1.2 counts it: 144 ops, about 1,440 presence frames, 576 heartbeats, 1 to 3 connects, a room of two, 300 bytes a message, 30 day months. The caps are the pricing page's as W1 1.1 printed them (https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/platform/pricing/, dateModified 2026-09-30, read 2026-10-01): Free "100,000 / day" requests, "13,000 GB-s / day", "5 million / day" rows read, "100,000 / day" rows written, "5 GB (total)"; "If you exceed any one of the free tier limits, further operations of that type will fail with an error"; "Daily free limits reset at 00:00 UTC"; "Each setAlarm() is billed as a single row written"; "Deletes are counted as rows written". Workers Free is "100,000 per day" requests and "10 milliseconds of CPU time per invocation", Paid "$5 USD per month for an account" (https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/pricing/, 2026-08-28, W1 1.1). D1 Free is "5 million / day" rows read, "100,000 / day" rows written, "500 MB" a database (https://developers.cloudflare.com/d1/platform/pricing/ and /d1/platform/limits/, W2 1.1).

- Today: production logged 18 requests in three quiet hours (`research-costs-actual.md` 32 as `docs/REALTIME.md` 1.1 reads it), two orders of magnitude under the 50 editor hour scenario. The Cloudflare bill is $0.00 and the Vercel residual of the channel is cents.
- At 50 editor hours a day, the default checkpoint cadence of today's tree (2 s idle, 10 s under typing, `checkpoint.ts` 25 to 27): the Free plan holds on every line. The largest line is the object's rows written at 28,800 to 36,000 a day (29 to 36 percent of the cap), then object requests at 11,550 (12 percent), duration at 655 GB-s (5 percent), Worker requests about 6,000 to 9,000 (6 to 9 percent with every tab signed in), D1 under 1 percent (section 2.2 for the arithmetic). The Cloudflare bill is $0.00 a month. The Vercel residual (the checkpoint route's invocations and the Blob records) is about $7 a month on the Pro seat the General Translation team pays anyway (`design-a.md` section 6: $5.40 of Blob advanced operations, $0.89 of memory, $0.26 of simple operations, $0.14 of invocations, prices of `research-costs-actual.md` 3).
- The day it breaks: the object's rows written cap at 139 to 174 editor hours a day (100,000 divided by 720 to 576 rows an hour), 116 to 139 if `DROP TABLE` turns out to count rows written (W1 open 1). With the 10 s idle and 30 s maximum cadence of design B (3.1), a Worker variable this design carries as a switch (section 3.6.2), the break day is 347 to 379 editor hours a day (231 to 245 with row deletes) and the Vercel residual at 50 hours is about $1.50 (`judge-2-cost.md` 7).
- At 500 editor hours a day: the rows written cap is passed about 06:40 to 08:20 UTC on a flat load and every realtime row reads red until 00:00 UTC, which under `docs/FOCUS.md` 6.2 (working copy 262 to 268) is a stop. The answer is the Workers Paid plan at $5.45 a month ($5.00 plus 3 million requests over the included million rounded up at $0.15 a million, `design-a.md` section 6), and the Vercel residual is about $70 a month at the default cadence or about $17 at the 10 s cadence (`judge-2-cost.md` 7). Two facts force Paid before the numbers do if production may never fail closed: the cutoff rule above, and the Worker's own daily cap being spendable by anyone who reaches the public `workers.dev` hostname with an upgrade request, since a request counts before the ticket is verified ("Validate requests in your Worker to avoid billing for invalid requests against a Durable Object", https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/best-practices/websockets/ line 471 as W1 1.4 quotes it). Whether Workers Paid asks for a payment method at upgrade was read on no page (W1 open 7); the purchase is Kevin's (section 8 question 3).
- The object's CPU budget on the Free plan is read on no page in words. The Durable Objects limits page (https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/platform/limits/, last updated 2026-06-01, fetched 2026-10-01) gives the SQLite backed object "CPU per request | 30 seconds (default) / configurable to 5 minutes of active CPU time" with no plan column, its footnote says "Each incoming HTTP request or WebSocket message resets the remaining available CPU time to 30 seconds", and the same page says "Workers Free plan: Only Durable Objects with SQLite storage backend are available." The Workers limits page (https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/limits/, last updated 2026-09-05, fetched 2026-10-01) gives the Worker "CPU time per HTTP request | 10 ms | 5 min (default: 30 seconds)" for Free and Paid and advises "Move expensive computation to Durable Objects or process data in smaller chunks across multiple requests", which reads as the object's budget being its own row and not the Worker's 10 ms; neither page says so in one sentence. Day 0 of R1 answers it on the preview Worker, which is on Free: one `GET /rooms/:id/counters?spin=50` that burns 50 ms of CPU inside the object; refused or evicted, the 10 ms reaches the object, the rebuild of a large deck (3.4 item 7) cannot run on Free, Workers Paid is forced on day one, and this list and section 8 question 3 change. The Worker's own router stays under 10 ms by construction: one HMAC verify and one JSON parse per upgrade, one header compare per bearer call.
- The caps are the account's: if the Worker lands in the General Translation account that `gt-edge` deploys to and that account is already on Workers Paid, every Free column above is an included allowance and the overage lines are the whole Cloudflare bill (`judge-2-cost.md` 8 item 2; W5 7.1). Kevin's answer to section 6 step 1 changes the tables before anything is built.

## 3. The design

### 3.1 The shape in one paragraph

A Worker `turboslide-realtime` on `turboslide-realtime.<subdomain>.workers.dev` exports the Durable Object class `DeckRoom` with the SQLite backend, one object per deck by `idFromName(deckId)`, and binds a D1 database `turboslide-accounts`. Every tab holds one WebSocket to its deck's object. The object is the order: it assigns the seq, transforms each entry past what landed since its base, runs the reducer and the validator, writes the entry to SQLite before it acknowledges, fans the entry out to every socket the reader's role may see, keeps the live document in memory and in `doc` rows, and holds the roster in memory and in each socket's attachment. The object writes no version record itself: on its alarm it posts the uncommitted entries to a Vercel route `POST /api/decks/:id/checkpoint` under a shared bearer, and that route runs the existing commit path (`coalesceEntries`, `store.write` with `ops` and `origin`, the comments applier) against Vercel Blob and answers the records. A tab proves its right to join with a ticket the Vercel editor loader mints, signed with HMAC-SHA256 under a secret the Worker also holds; the Worker verifies the ticket before the socket reaches the object. The Vercel app keeps every page, loader, server function, export, the agent HTTP routes, the auth routes and the store; its stream, ops and presence routes answer "go to the Worker" on this tier; the identity runtime stays on Vercel and reads its database through the Worker's bearer SQL routes. The three existing tiers stay: `memory` for a checkout and the tests, `blob` hosted without a Worker and as the rollback, `redis` as the realtime round built it and unused. The fourth tier's word is `do` (`design-a.md` 1.1, revised).

### 3.3 The authority and the signed room token

The object admits and transforms. The admission is `admitOps` (`room.ts` 1693 to 1927) in shape: the budgets with the keys of `keys.ts` 106 to 128 as in memory counters per object, the base window (`checkBaseWindow`, `admission.ts` 73 to 77), the landed entries since the base, the dedupe by `(clientId, opId)` through a unique index instead of the 2,000 entry tail scan, `transformEntry` with the tie rule (`landedOf` 1462 to 1470, 1485 to 1509), the reducer and the validator in `landCandidate` (1596 to 1642), the undo of a refused sibling (`undoOfSplices` 1584 to 1593), the working tree's `yieldConcurrentConversion` first in `transformEntry`, then one `INSERT` with no compare and no retry loop, since one object has one writer. The pure half of these functions moves out of `room.ts` into `packages/realtime/src/room-core.ts` with no `node:` import, because `room.ts` imports `node:crypto` and `ioredis` (lines 1 and 3) while the schema's reducer, transform and validator import none (T1 1.3 item 8); `room.ts` re-exports every name so its callers do not move (the integrator's seam commit, 5.3).

The object holds the roster: in memory while awake; while hibernated, each socket's `serializeAttachment` holds the entry's identity fields, hue slot and slide ("Serialized attachments persist through hibernation as long as the WebSocket remains healthy", "Maximum serialized size is 16,384 bytes", https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/best-practices/websockets/, last updated 2026-09-30, fetched 2026-10-01), written at the accept, at a ticket refresh and at a slide change and not per presence frame, because no page read today says whether an attachment write is billed (that page and the pricing page of 2026-09-30 carry no sentence on it); the selection and the caret live in memory, and a woken object sends `{ type: 'resend' }` to every socket, which the client answers with its presence as it does after a hello (`room-client.ts` `HEAD` 1337 to 1338), so the roster is whole within one frame of the wake. Day 0 of R1 writes 1,000 attachments on the preview and reads the dashboard's rows written and stored bytes before and after; `cost.do.rows-written` (2.2) carries the reading. A roster entry's identity fields come from the ticket, because the identity cache, the principal record and the deck index row are the function's (`rosterEntryFor` 2449 to 2484; T1 1.3 item 7). The hue slot (`assignHueSlot`, `preferredHueSlot`, imported at `room.ts` 7 with no `node:`), the pointer and drag rule for the first 20 editors and owners (2466 to 2475; `LIVE_POINTERS_MAX` 20, `protocol.ts` 41) and the editing ceiling (`EDITING_TABS_MAX` 100, `protocol.ts` 43) run in the object as pure functions over the roster. The caps of `CAPS.streams` (`admission.ts` 39: anonymous 4, signed in 8, agent 4, address 16) become the object's count of open sockets per identity key and per address from the ticket, closed with 4429 and a `retry-after` in the close reason; a newer socket of the same client id supersedes the older with 4409; the per instance cap of 256 and the 240 to 290 s lifetime have no meaning in one object and go (T1 finding 13).

The function decides who may join and mints the ticket; the function also writes the version log (3.5) and holds the identity runtime (section 4). The Worker verifies tickets and bearers before anything reaches the object, which is the rule the WebSocket page gives (W1 1.4 fact 2).

The room ticket. Minted by the editor loader (`write.ts` `readEditorDeckFn` 401 to 495, which already runs `requestIdentity` at 429, `decideFor` at 431 and hands the page `room: { seq, tier, notice }` at 493) and by a new same origin route `GET /api/decks/:id/ticket?client=<cid>` for the refresh. A new module `apps/studio/src/server/room-ticket.ts` holds the shape:

- Claims, canonical JSON (`canonicalJson` is imported at `room.ts` 53): `v` 1 (the protocol version), `deck`, `cid` (minted by `mintClientId` 2663 to 2668 so the MAC rule of 2647 to 2652 stays the function's; a refresh checks `clientIdMatches` 2671 to 2677 before re-signing), `id` (the identity string the budgets key on), `kind` (`anonymous`, `signedIn`, `agent`; `admission.ts` 20), `pid` (the principal id, optional), `role`, `via`, `names`, `rc` (the `ViewerFacts` of 2500 to 2508), `label`, `trust`, `mark`, `email` (the roster entry's identity fields; `email` only when `trust` is `verified`), `org` (the request's origin, which the function served and trusts), `tab` (the tab token, `tabTokenOf` 2859, optional), `dpl` (the build commit, `TURBOSLIDE_BUILD_COMMIT`, so the ship note reads the skew between the Vercel deployment and the Worker version from the tickets open tabs hold; B 2.2), `iat`, `exp`.
- The token: `base64url(claims) + '.' + base64url(hmacSha256(TURBOSLIDE_ROOM_SECRET, claims))`, `node:crypto` on the function as `clientIdMac` does, `crypto.subtle` HMAC verify in the Worker with a constant time compare.
- Life: `ROOM_TICKET_TTL_MS` 600,000 (10 minutes); the client refreshes at `exp` minus 120 s and at once on a `reauth` frame, sending `{ t: 'ticket', ticket }` up the socket; the object re-verifies and replaces the socket's facts. A ticket 30 s past `exp` with no refresh closes the socket with 4401; the client fetches a ticket and reopens at once.
- Access changes: every writer of an access record or a link grant on Vercel (`access.ts` `writeAccess`, `noteLinkGrant` 186 to 195 and the two other `publishDrop('link', ...)` sites in the working tree) calls `POST /rooms/:id/access-changed { principalIds? }` under the bearer after its store write. The object sends `{ t: 'reauth' }` to the sockets whose `pid` is named (every socket when none is) and closes with 4403 any whose fresh ticket does not arrive within 10 s or arrives with a role below `read` or below the attachment's role for the frames it has been receiving (C 2.2). An editor whose role did not drop keeps the socket; a viewer whose link was revoked loses it within the Vercel write's round trip plus the grace. This replaces the stream route's 60 s recheck (`AUTHORIZE_RECHECK_MS`, `decks.$deckId.stream.ts` 70) and its `access` drop.
- Carriage: the upgrade carries the token as the second subprotocol, `['turboslide.v1', 'ticket.<token>']` (the constructor takes `url` and `protocols` and nothing else, MDN as W4 5.3 reads it; a token of base64url characters and `.` is a valid header token); the Worker answers `Sec-WebSocket-Protocol: turboslide.v1`. An HTTP `POST /rooms/:id/ops` or `/presence` carries `Authorization: Ticket <token>`. Nothing rides a query string, so no ticket lands in a request log on either host (the Worker's 256 KB per request log, W4 section 1; the app's one JSON line per request, T4 2.13). MDN states no size limit on the header and Cloudflare's header limits were not read (B 10 item 8); the fallback shape if a 500 byte subprotocol is refused anywhere is the first frame `{ t: 'join', ticket }` within 5 s of the upgrade, verified by the object (A risk 17).
- The Worker checks: the signature, `exp`, `deck` equals the path, `org` equals the request's `Origin` header, `v` is 1 or the one before it during a two version window. This is the cross site rule of `refuseCrossSite` (`room.ts` 3165 to 3184) and the CSRF filter (`start.ts` 19 to 22, 42 to 45) moved to the Worker with no host list to maintain: the pipeline's preview hosts pass because the loader that served the page put them in `org` (W4 5.2 asked for a pattern or a per deployment claim; this is the claim).
- CORS for the HTTP POSTs: the Worker answers `OPTIONS` with the request's `Origin` echoed, `Access-Control-Allow-Methods: POST`, `Access-Control-Allow-Headers: authorization, content-type`, no credentials (the ticket is a header, not a cookie). The page's CSP `connect-src` gains `https://` and `wss://` of `TURBOSLIDE_ROOM_HOST` (`headers.ts` 220 to 236; the policy is report only today, `cspMode` 195 to 200).

The bearer between the two hosts: `TURBOSLIDE_ROOM_BEARER`, a second secret both hold, on `Authorization: Bearer` for the function's calls to the Worker (`/rooms/:id/{write,flush,external,publish,roster,document,counters,access-changed}`, `/db/{query,batch,counters}`, `/control/{open,flags}`) and the object's calls to the function (`/api/decks/:id/checkpoint`, `/api/decks/:id/seed`). The agent surface's own bearer (`TURBOSLIDE_TOKEN`) never reaches the Worker, against design C's reuse of it (`judge-3-operator.md` 5): an agent's write enters through the Vercel actions route and is forwarded under the room bearer. The bearer's reach is the whole account database and every deck's order (`/db/query` runs any statement the function posts), and the design accepts one secret for both rather than a second for `/db/*`, because both values live in the same two places (the Vercel environment and the Worker's secrets) and a second name would not shrink what a leak of either place reaches. `docs/security.md` section 12's text (R4, 5.2) therefore states the route's reach, the rotation runbook (a new value from `openssl rand -hex 32`; `wrangler secret put` on both Workers; `vercel env rm` then `vercel env add --sensitive` on both environments; a Worker deploy and a Vercel deploy; the old value dead once both deployments serve), the logging rule (the Worker logs a statement's kind and first table name and never its parameters, and never a ticket) and the fact that a WAF rule in front of the route needs a zone the account does not hold (W4 open 11).

### 3.4 The ordering guarantees and the wake

1. One object is one thread. Ops are admitted in arrival order per deck; the seq is `head + 1`; `rev` is the object's revision. The soft limit is 1,000 requests a second per object (W1 section 3); the worst room is 20 pointer publishers at 15 a second, 300 a second.
2. An entry is durable before its acknowledgement: the `INSERT` runs in the same synchronous block as the ack, and the runtime's output gate holds the response until the write is confirmed: the SQLite announcement (https://blog.cloudflare.com/sqlite-in-durable-objects/, 2024-09-26, as W1 section 5 cites it) says "The application is able to work on constructing the response in parallel with the storage layer confirming the write", which W1 reads as the write path running behind output gates; the sentence is the page's and the phrase is W1's.
3. Every admitted entry is transformed past every entry between its base and the head before placement, and the answer carries the placed mutations (`admitOps` 1819 to 1846). Nothing moves the head between the read and the insert in one thread.
4. An op id is admitted at most once while its first attempt is in the retained window: the entries tables carry a unique index on `(client_id, op_id)`, looked up per POST (0 to 64 rows read). Entries are inserted into `entries_<yyyymm>`; a month's table is dropped once `covered` is above its last seq and the table is older than the replay window (2,000 entries, `REPLAY_MAX_ENTRIES` `protocol.ts` 30) and the retention (10,000 entries behind `covered`, `STREAM_RETAIN_ENTRIES` `checkpoint.ts` 37, read as a floor), so no hot path deletes rows (C 3.2). Whether `DROP TABLE` counts rows written is W1 open 1; day 0 of R1 probes it with one table and `rowsWritten` on the cursor, and if it counts, the design falls back to `DELETE` every 20th commit and the cost rows carry the higher count.
5. The replay covers exactly the gap: a socket opens at `since`; the object answers `hello` with `seq: head` and `covered`, then the entries `(since, head]` by `replayPlan` (`admission.ts` 85 to 89) paged by 256 up to 2,000 entries or 1 MB, or `resync` when `since` is above the head, more than 2,000 behind, or below the first retained seq (the working tree's `replayFor` rule, `build/r1.md` item 5). The hello, the replay and the first live frame come from one event handler with no `await` between the SQLite cursor and the frames (the sqlite page's rule to consume cursors before the next `await`, W1 section 2), so no entry falls between the replay and the live stream (B 2.3 item 3). The ack carries `between` (`betweenEntries` 1351 to 1366, pure).
6. A reader receives every frame its role may see: `filterEventForReader` (2566 to 2599), `rosterEntryForReader` (2515 to 2539) and `stripNotes` (2542 to 2556) run per socket over the ticket facts.
7. A hibernated object wakes on the first frame or alarm with its constructor, which reads the one `meta` row (`head`, `covered`, `revision`, `last_seq_by_month`), rebuilds the roster from `getWebSockets()` and the attachments, and sends `{ type: 'resend' }` to every socket (3.3). The hello, the replay, a presence frame and a leave need no document and run at once from the `meta` row and the entries (design B 2.4's rule, kept here). The live document is rebuilt on the first thing that needs it, an `ops` frame, a `/write`, a `/document` GET or the alarm's checkpoint: the `doc` rows (one per slide plus one for the manifest, each under 2 MB: `slideMaxBytes` is 200 KB, `admission.ts` 35, and a manifest over 1.5 MB is chunked at 900 KB) and the entries above `covered` are read and applied in memory, 20 to 80 rows read and no network call. An object with a `meta` row and no `doc` rows reads the seed route then, on its first `ops` frame, `/write` or alarm, and never for a hello or for a `/document` GET, which answers 204 `{ none: true }` so the loader reads the store itself (3.2); an object with no `meta` row (the first socket after the flip) reads the seed route before its first hello, since the hello carries `head`, `revision` and `covered` (3.7 item 5). The `doc` rows live while the deck has a socket or an uncommitted entry and are dropped as a table at the last socket's close after the final commit, so the account's storage holds the open decks alone (B 3.2); the live document leaves memory with them.
8. Three more, which `docs/hosting.md` section 9's Redis rule does not cover as written and T1 section 3 asked the design to state: the object's SQLite holds the only copy of the entries between the last checkpoint and the head for at most the checkpoint interval (2 s idle, 10 s under typing at the default cadence) and at most 2,000 entries or 1 MB, after which the alarm commits them, and the storage API's point in time recovery covers "any point in time in the past 30 days" for a SQLite backed object (https://developers.cloudflare.com/durable-objects/best-practices/access-durable-objects-storage/, last updated 2026-07-03, fetched 2026-10-01; the page carries no plan qualifier, while D1's Time Travel on the same account reads "7 days (Free)" against "30 days (Workers Paid)", W2 1.1, so the 30 days on Free is unconfirmed and the design does not lean on it: the only copy window is the checkpoint interval above, and the recovery is an aid whose bookmark the ship note records once with `getCurrentBookmark()`); a wake answers the hello, the replay and presence from the `meta` row and the entries, and rebuilds the live document from the `doc` rows and the entries above `covered`, or from the seed route when no rows exist, before it admits an `ops` frame, a write or a checkpoint (3.4 item 7); and no socket is admitted without a ticket whose MAC, deck, origin and expiry verify, a change of access reaches the sockets it touches within the Vercel write's round trip plus the 10 s grace, and a ticket is never logged.

## 4. Google sign in and the account database

### 4.1 The placement

The facts that decide it: the D1 binding exists inside a Worker alone, and no supported path reaches D1 from a Vercel function ("To interact with your D1 database from your Worker, you need to access it through the environment bindings provided to the Worker (env)", https://developers.cloudflare.com/d1/worker-api/d1-database/ as W2 answer 1 quotes it); the D1 HTTP API is the control plane ("REST API primarily interacts with the control plane", https://developers.cloudflare.com/d1/best-practices/query-d1/) under "1,200 requests per five minute period per user" (https://developers.cloudflare.com/fundamentals/api/reference/limits/, W2 1.3), two concurrent signed in editors at today's per request counts (W2 section 4); a session cookie set by a `workers.dev` host never reaches `www.turboslide.com`, since `workers.dev` is line 12707 of the Public Suffix List (https://publicsuffix.org/list/public_suffix_list.dat on 2026-10-01, T2 3.5); Hyperdrive brings no database and is a Worker binding (W2 answer 5); no free Postgres exists on Cloudflare (W2 section 6). So the database is D1 and the runtime that speaks to it is a Worker.

The decision for stage 1 is design A's alternative A1 (`design-a.md` 1.3) with design B's two rules (B 2.5, `judge-2-cost.md` 7): the database is D1 `turboslide-accounts` bound to the realtime Worker as `ACCOUNTS`; the identity runtime, the auth routes, the cookies and the Google callback stay on Vercel and `www.turboslide.com`; the function reaches the database through the Worker's bearer routes `POST /db/query` and `POST /db/batch` (3.6.2) by a Kysely dialect that posts one compiled statement per `executeQuery`; the account facts of a session are cached per instance under the SHA-256 of the session token for the cookie cache's 300 s (`cookieCache: { enabled: true, maxAge: 5 * 60 }`, `better-auth.ts` `HEAD` 187, which T2 2.4 cites at the working copy's 267) and dropped by `forgetIdentity`; the principal records move to a `ts_principal` table on D1 with `touch` written at most once an hour per principal. Why not design B's identity runtime on the Worker (B 4.7): it moves `packages/accounts` out, runs better-auth 1.7.4 on a runtime it has not run on (B 10 item 7), proxies `/api/auth/*` through h3 and is a round of its own (`research-workers.md` 4.1; `judge-3-operator.md` 7); it stays the next step if the proxy dialect's latency reads badly on the preview (section 7.3). Why not Neon's own free plan (A2): it is neither Vercel's nor Cloudflare's and Kevin's words were "cloudflare instead of vercel stuff". What the identity reads cost on the `do` tier: the ops and presence POSTs no longer pass `requestIdentity`, so a signed in editor hour makes 15 to 30 identity calls instead of 1,600 to 2,200 (B 2.5; T2 4.1), and with the 300 s cache at most about 12 misses an hour per instance reach D1 at 4 to 5 indexed rows each (`cost.d1.reads`, 2.2). The latency of one Worker hop plus one D1 statement is unmeasured (W2 open 1; the floor from this machine to the API edge was 50 to 116 ms, W2 1.3); a cache miss costs 4 to 5 sequential statements on a loader, paid at most once per 300 s per instance per session, and the preview's `accounts.google-roundtrip` on localhost against the preview Worker's D1 is the first reading. The Google callback is the heavier path: T2 4.2 counts about 15 sequential statements inside it (the state read and delete, the account and user lookups, the session insert, `onSessionCreated`'s facts, touch, alias and link rows) and prices them at about 15 × L for a remote database at L ms a statement, 1.5 s at 100 ms; with W2 1.3's measured floor of 50 to 116 ms from this machine to the API edge, the proxy dialect adds about 0.75 to 1.7 s to the callback once per sign in, which 2.1's `accounts.google-roundtrip` records against a 5 s bound; a reading over 3 s is the trigger for 7.3's identity runtime on the Worker.

### 1.2 The decision

1. Build every picked element once, on top of the first pass's code, in the order of section 2: B's first screen, B's numbers row, then eleven bands from the menus to the close, with a dither interlude between every two bands.
2. Where two picks show one feature, one band holds both. The agents band holds the terminal with its CLI, MCP and HTTP tabs (A+B), the command chips and the agent's ring (C), and Version history as its rows (A) with C's scrubber over them, because the rows and the scrubber are one feature. The export band holds C's seam, B's loupe and the PDF download. A's "Turboslide today" and B's "Features and where to find them" are one table, with every figure said once on the page (section 2.14).
3. The page stays one deck. The editor frame in the first screen, the miniature editor, every band's slides, the show and the print draw the same page deck, and every change made on the page reaches all of them and Version history. The hero's agent loop is the exception and says so: it is a staged sequence on the frame that writes nothing.
4. Motion follows B's rule as Kevin picked it: loops start when their band is in view and pause when it leaves, a Pause Motion control in the navigation pauses every automatic motion and is remembered across visits, and reduced motion shows every still. One shot motions from the first pass stay where their band stays.
5. Every band below the first screen is loaded after `load` and one idle callback. The document carries the first screen and every band's words, so a crawler reads the whole page, and each band's instrument is inserted before the band comes into view.
6. Every claim stays true of production at the ship. The two people band shows presence that production's realtime rows do not yet read green, so its push is the last and ships only when they do (question 13). The loupe compares two real rasters. No sentence says "pixel for pixel".
7. Ship it as thirteen pushes after the first pass's seven, keyed `V1#8` to `V4#20`, one mechanism a push (section 6.8).

### 2.0 What every band shares

**The column.** The branch's grammar (`apps/studio/src/components/home/grammar.css`): the 1,104 px column with one rail on each side drawn once by `.ts-rails`, a 40 px gutter, so the content is 1,024 px wide at 1440 (x 208 to 1232, the rails at x 168 and 1272). Under 1,136 px the column is `min(1104px, 100% - 32px)`; under 720 px the gutter is 0, so at 390 the content is 358 px wide (x 16 to 374). Each band is a `section[data-band]` labelled by its h2 (the numbers row by `aria-label`, it has no h2), with 96 px of padding above and below at 1,024 px and over and 64 px under 720 px, closed by `.ts-seam` with its 9 px crosses. An interlude (2.4) sits between every two bands, inside the seams.

**Type.** Inter only (`'Inter', 'Inter Fallback', ...`, `packages/fonts/src/inter.css`), `font-optical-sizing: auto`, weights 400 and 500 only for page text (slide content keeps the weights its slide sets). The h1 is in 2.2. The h2 is `clamp(30px, 3.9vw, 54px)`, weight 500, -0.034 em, line height 1.04. The lead is 19 px on 1.55 in `--pt-ink-2` (17 px under 720 px). Captions are 14 px on 1.45 in `--pt-titanium`. Labels and the chips' and step tabs' second lines are 13 px. Figures are 32 px, weight 500, tabular (24 px under 720 px).

**The two panels.** Monospace appears only on the page's two `#101010` terminal panels (`--pt-panel-ink`, the same near black in both appearances): the hero's terminal (2.2) and the agents band's console (2.9). Both carry `.ts-home-panel`, the one selector of the brand lint's `/home` entry in `CODE_SURFACES` (`packages/lint/src/brand/config.ts`), whose reason becomes "The /home terminals: the hero's agent terminal and the agents band's console, the page's only code surfaces (LANDING.md 2.0)". `--pt-mono` (`ui-monospace, 'SF Mono', Menlo, Consolas, monospace`) at 14 px on 20 px lines with 20 px of padding a side for the agents console at 1,024 px and over, and 12 px for the hero's terminal at every width and for the console under 720 px (on 16 px lines in the hero at 1,024 px and over, 18 px elsewhere), with 12 px of padding a side. The widest advance in the stack is 0.602 em, so 64 columns take 539 px of the 548 px inside the 588 px console, and 44 columns take 318 px at 12 px, inside the hero's terminal (340 px inside at 1440) and the 334 px inside a 358 px panel. `panel-format.ts` formats every screen to those two widths and never splits a token (the first pass's rule, `panel-format.test.ts`). Every other line on paper, the canvas band's Command row and the step tabs' command words included, is set in Inter.

**Colour.** The paper and ink tokens of `packages/chrome/src/tokens.css`. `#2f5ce0` (`--pt-select`) appears only while the visitor works on something: the selection ring, handles and chip, the snap guide, the Tailor highlight, the lifted slide's outline, an open menu row's ground and the focus ring. Nothing blue is drawn at rest or by any automatic motion: the agent's ring and flag are ink in both terminals' runs, the scrubber's thumb is ink, the step tabs' countdown rule is ink. The text caret is the browser's in the text's colour. No other hue at rest but the Globex swatch's ground (2.8). Errors on a panel are set in the panel's own text colour.

**Shape.** Square corners everywhere (grammar.css "no radius"). No shadows, gradients or blur. Ruled rows, never boxed cards. Heroicons 20 solid only in key cells (the features table, Version history's author cell), never before a heading, never `sparkles` or `cursor-arrow-rays`.

**Slides.** Every slide on the page is the `renderSlide` output (`@turboslide/render/slide`) of the page deck, rendered at build in both appearances and checked to differ only in the sheet's `data-theme` and picture sources; the renderer never ships to the browser. Page code only places, clones and restyles the renderer's markup: a text box or shape the miniature editor inserts is a clone of the renderer's own markup for that block, rendered at build (2.5). The document inlines the first screen's instances (the frame's slide 1 and its nine filmstrip thumbnails); every other instance travels in its band group's chunk (4.2), each one HTML for both appearances, about 1.4 KB decoded (the five full size instances of today's `slides.generated.ts` read 7,200 B decoded and 1,312 B brotli together, read with Node at 10:12 PDT). A slide sits in a `HomeSheet` stage whose container units make 1 unit equal 1/1,600 of the sheet. No sheet wider than a thumbnail draws a frame: its rails, rules and crosses are its edge. Thumbnails keep the renderer's 1 px `--pt-edge` frame. Under 720 px slide type keeps the first pass's reading floor (titles at least 17 px, body at least 12 px, credits at least 10 px). Every heading element inside a slide instance is rewritten to `div` by the build, so the page has one h1 and one h2 a band.

**The page deck.** Nine slides in the GT theme. The fixture (`apps/studio/home-deck/`) holds eight of them, every slide but `next-steps`; the build runs the recorded run's three commands on a temporary copy, and the nine slide deck that results is the page deck. Every counter reads "n / 9" at rest and renumbers on every change of the order. The deck's title (`deck.json` `title`) is "Onboarding plan".

## 3. The motion system

### 3.1 The rule

B's rule, as Kevin picked it. Every motion on the page is one of three kinds:

- **A loop** starts on its own when its band is in view and pauses when the band leaves the view, the tab hides, or the visitor presses Pause Motion; a paused loop holds its frame and resumes from it. The loops: the hero's staged run (L-H), the eleven interludes (I1), the two people (P-L) and the pattern's shader (P-T). L-H, P-L and P-T are demonstrations: at most one runs at a time, the one most in view of those at least half in view. The interludes are fields: at most two run at a time, the two most in view with any part in view.
- **A one shot motion** plays once when its band reaches 35 percent in view and ends at its end state: H5 (the Blue Marble develops in the frame), C1 (the lighthouse develops), E1 (the seam's hint) and K1 (the mark assembles). It arms its first pose only when its band is below the viewport at first observation; a band above the viewport renders final.
- **A visitor motion** follows a press, drag or key and ends within 5 s: the selection, Undo's return, Tailor's names, the kit's clear, the filmstrip, a chip's or a step tab's step, the show, the seam, the loupe, the mirrored typing, button presses.

Under reduced motion no loop starts and no one shot motion plays: every still shows from the first paint, every visitor motion is a cut, and Pause Motion is hidden.

### 3.2 Pause Motion

- **Where and its words.** In the navigation, after Light and Dark and before Sign In and New Presentation (on the second row at 390): a 32 px square text button with a 1 px hairline reading "Pause Motion", "Play Motion" while pressed, `aria-pressed="true"` while paused, `[data-motion-toggle]`. Tab, Enter and Space as any button.
- **What it pauses.** Every automatic motion: within one frame of the press every running loop holds its current frame (its frame callback and timers cancelled, the shader's speed set to 0), a running one shot motion finishes at its end state, and a one shot motion not yet played is drawn at its end state when its band arrives. Every loop and every staged sequence obeys it, the hero's run and the two people included. A CSS guard sets `animation-play-state: paused` under `html[data-motion="paused"]`. Play resumes each loop from its held frame under the in view rule.
- **What it leaves.** Visitor motions (3.1) still run, each within 5 s, because the visitor asked for them.
- **Remembered across visits.** The choice is stored in `localStorage` under `ts-home-motion` (`paused`, or the key removed on Play), every read and write wrapped in try and catch. Storage that throws or is empty means playing, and the button still works for the visit. The boot script reads the key before the first paint and sets `html[data-motion="paused"]`, so a paused visitor's page paints still: the loops at their rest frames, H5's still from the first paint, every one shot motion at its end state. The key is per browser, as `gt-theme` is.
- **Under reduced motion** the button is hidden by a `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` rule, decided at the first paint, so its absence shifts nothing; a change of the preference while the page is open hides or shows it.
- **Owner.** V1 renders the button in `HomeNav.tsx`; V4's `boot.ts` and `live/motion.ts` give it its behaviour (section 6.3 fixes the hook).

### 3.8 The in view and pause mechanics

- `live/motion.ts` (V4) holds one frame clock (`requestAnimationFrame` runs only while a loop plays or a sequence runs), one `IntersectionObserver` for the loops (thresholds 0, 0.1, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75 and 1, from which B's `schedule()` picks the demonstration and the two fields) and the first pass's one for the one shot motions (`threshold: 0.35`, armed only below the viewport, unobserved after).
- A loop registers with `loop(band, element, { kind, play, pause, still })`; the scheduler alone calls `play` and `pause`. A paused loop holds no frame callback and no timer.
- A hidden tab (`visibilitychange`) pauses every loop and finishes every running one shot and visitor sequence at its end state; a visible tab resumes the loops in view.
- A change of `prefers-reduced-motion` finishes every running motion, stops every loop at its still and applies section 3.9 from then on.
- Any new input on a band finishes that band's running one shot and visitor sequences at their end state before it acts (the first pass's guard). The hero's and the people's loops stop for good on a press or key in their stage.
- `window.tsHomeMotion` exposes `{ paused: boolean, running(): string[], registered(): string[] }` (the loop ids playing now, and every loop id registered with `loop` on the tree) for the drivers; `?slow=10` multiplies the page's timers by ten for frame strips and does nothing else.

### 3.9 Reduced motion

Under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` the boot script adds no intro class, the duration tokens are 0 ms, no Web Animation is created, no loop starts and no shader chunk is requested, every field and picture is drawn at its final tone at once (the interludes gathered, the people band's still, the pattern's still frame on both sides), Undo, the ring, the kit and the show cut, the seam sits at 50 percent, the mark is still, and Pause Motion is hidden. Colour changes (the Tailor highlight, hover grounds) still happen without transitions. Every interaction works.

### 3.10 The 5 s rule and WCAG 2.2.2

The loops run longer than 5 s, start on their own and sit beside other content, so WCAG 2.2.2 (https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pause-stop-hide.html, read 2026-10-02 by the motion researcher) asks for a way to pause them: Pause Motion is that control, early in the Tab order, acting within one frame, and the hero's and the people's loops also stop for good on a press. Every one shot motion ends within 5 s of its start (H5 1.5 s, C1 2.4 s, E1 0.6 s, K1 0.93 s). Every visitor motion ends within 5 s (a chip's or step tab's step at most about 4.8 s with a 24 character name, by the first pass's arithmetic; Tailor 1.4 s; a kit 0.5 s; the show 0.5 s in and 0.4 s out).

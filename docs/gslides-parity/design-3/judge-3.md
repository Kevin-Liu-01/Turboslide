# Judge 3: the security engineer's and the founder's reading

Judgment on the three round three design proposals for Turboslide (multiplayer, optional accounts, security, layout shift, background pictures with dithers), written 2026-09-13 against `main` at `61b16e4`. This is judge 3 of 3. The lens is a security engineer's and Kevin's at once: does the threat model of reports 04 and 10 land in full, do the rate limits and quotas hold on Vercel as deployed, are the stored XSS and the upload paths closed rather than narrowed, does an optional account cost the person no privacy they did not choose to spend, and does the document say plainly what ships this round and what waits for round four.

## Where the three agree

The three proposals share one realtime design (an operation stream in Redis in front of the Blob revision log, Server-Sent Events down and POST up, server serialized admission, a checkpointer at 2 s idle or 10 s continuous, two level acknowledgement), one identity design (a sealed `__Host-` cookie with a v4 UUID, better-auth 1.7.4 with magic link plus code, passkeys gated on the domain, GitHub optional, no passwords, no Google), one sharing design (roles on one access record, `authorize()` first in every server function and route, `/s/<token>` exchanged once, `?p=` for the published player), one XSS fix (DOMPurify at write and render, a sandboxed `srcdoc` frame in every renderer including the standalone file, a link scheme check, the owner's switch for shared decks), one upload pipeline (sniff, sharp under limits, HEIF and JXL blocked, re-encode, SVG refused hosted, the presigned path above 3 MB), one `safeFetch` with pinned DNS, the same WAF and quota numbers from reports 04 and 10, the same kill switch list, the same structured log with the 48 hour IP salt, the same zero shift gate, and the same `PictureDither` field with materialized variants. On those shared parts the security reading is the same for all three, and it is good: the plan of reports 04 and 10 is applied rather than summarized, each item carries a test, and the numbers hold on Vercel as the reports describe it (WAF counters per region set a third under the intended figure, at most 40 rules on Pro, windows between 10 s and 10 min, the 4.5 MB body cap driving the presigned path, streams on the 300 s catch all rule, the fixed Redis plan so a command flood is a 429 and not a bill). The scores below turn on where they differ.

## Proposal 1, Google faithful

What it gets right. It is the most complete mimicry: chat, the Activity panel with the comment trend, every Google label in Google's position, Follow with Google's refusals, the four comment display modes, the three notification levels, the Share dialog's two halves with expiry, transfer and the Review banner. Its security section is the easiest to audit as a table: the WAF rules R1 to R21 in one place with the application quotas beside them (5.3), the retention per class (5.9), the rollout that connects the private store (R2) before enforcement (R4), the key rotation on revoke (5.8), `TURBOSLIDE_MAIL=capture` so a preview never sends mail, and the collaborator announcements with the Shift+Tab roster for screen readers (2.5), which the other two omit. Section 13's ten decisions for Kevin and the cut line in risk 3 are the right shape for a founder's document.

Where it is weaker on security. Four findings of the reports are not closed. First, F6's thumbnail route: `?w=` bypasses the bearer today, and report 04's fix names a signed, short lived URL from the page; proposal 1 puts WAF rule R2 (240 per IP per minute) and a per identity render quota on the route and never the signature, so the cost attack of F6 keeps its shape across many addresses. Second, F9's export cancel token is absent. Third, F47: the re-authentication within 10 minutes for share changes is there (4.2), but the per page nonce for the window share, comment, invite and access handlers is not, so until the frame and the CSP land an `html` block on a shared deck can loosen the deck's access as the owner who opened it. Fourth, F11's forwarded host trust is unaddressed (Low). Beyond the findings, two defaults are wrong for a public editor used by a sales team: grant holders' names are shown to link visitors by default (section 13 item 6), so a forwarded link discloses the team list; and chat (2.1) is a new anonymous write channel that is never stored, has no report path and no moderation, which in a public editor is a harassment vector with no audit trail. Proposal 1 puts chat first on its own cut line, which is where it belongs.

Where the architecture is wrong. Section 1.4 changes `text.replace` ranges from markup offsets to plain text offsets and states that "records already written keep their meaning because a record's mutations are replayed against the document as it was". The sentence is copied from report 02 3.3 and it is false. `reduce.ts` lines 316 to 323 slice the string at the pointer by the range as stored; `blob-store.ts` lines 443 to 462 replay every record above a base through that reducer; `deck follow` checks the result against the record's snapshot md5. A record written with a markup range and replayed by a plain offset reducer produces a different string, so every existing deck's history would replay wrong after the change. Proposal 3 saw this and added two ops instead. Two smaller gaps: the anonymous principal's typed name has no named server home (4.1 mirrors it in `localStorage`; the roster reads identity "from the session", but the sealed cookie carries only the UUID), and the checkpoint lock in 1.1 has a 2 s TTL with a waiter that breaks it after 3 s, which cannot both hold.

Buildability and honesty. At 438 builder hours plus 72 for the integrator and verifier it is the largest plan, and it carries chat, the activity trend, `version.delete`, GitHub and four avatar tabs in the default line. It does not mention VERIFICATION-2 finding 16: the MCP stdio `tools/list` is already 27.2 MB for 81 tools against a 10 MB client buffer, and 46 more actions make the local transport unusable unless that is fixed first. The unverified Google facts are marked at the point of use and listed for Kevin, which is the standard; the one dishonesty is the replay sentence, stated as fact.

## Proposal 2, sales first and safe by default

What it gets right. It starts from four situations a sales team will actually be in (a manager reviewing by link, two reps on one deck, a deck shared wrong, nobody wanting an account) and derives five rules from them, and every default follows: Share opens on Restricted with the people field first, Anyone with the link defaults to Viewer, the footer says "Speaker notes and skipped slides never travel with a view or comment link", viewers do not see comments unless the owner turns on "Viewers can see comments" (3.6), names are hidden from link visitors unless the owner turns on "Show names to link visitors" (2.6), anonymous principals get Initials, Glyph and Dither and never a picture upload (4.7), and the refusals are written out in words (9.6). Its security section is the most auditable of the three because each subsection names the findings it closes, and the coverage is the fullest of the first two proposals: F6's signed thumbnail URLs (5.10), F9's cancel token (B6), F47's per page nonce and re-authentication (3.8), F14's registry bound to identities with caps (5.1), the `turboslide fix` rule that sanitizes legacy `html` blocks by hand and never inside `migrate` (8.1), the WAF rules as a committed `firewall/rules.json`, hour estimates per builder, eighteen risks and a thorough unverified list. Its privacy defaults are the best of the three, and it is the only proposal that cites the GDPR articles it designs against.

Where it is weaker on security. The rollout order (8.4) enforces roles and ships the Share dialog at step 5 and completes "the two stores" at step 10. Between those steps the dialog says Restricted while `deck.json` and every slide still sit on the public store under predictable paths, which is exactly report 04's F8 ("High after P1"). The proposal follows report 04 section 9's step order literally (P9 after P1 to P8) and does not list the window as a risk; proposals 1 and 3 move the private store before enforcement, and proposal 3 names the exposure (its risk 6). Second, 5.4 reads "SVG is refused on hosted transports or rasterized through sharp"; the second clause hands attacker bytes to librsvg, and the word must be refused only. Third, 5.9 and B6 promise P8, Chromium in a function or worker without the Blob write token and the bearer, inside this round; that is a second Vercel project or a worker deployment reached by a signed internal request, proposals 1 and 3 place it in round four, and in a round this size it will slip, so promising it is the one scope claim in the document I do not believe. Fourth, 1.7 keeps pending operations "in memory only", so a tab that closes while offline loses them, against the first decision row's promise that no accepted keystroke is dropped. Two details: F11 is unaddressed (Low), and the CSP text in 5.7 omits `worker-src`, which the dither worker needs.

Architecture. It shares proposal 1's `text.replace` change with the same copied sentence (1.3), and the anonymous principal's typed name has the same unspecified home. The `RealtimeChannel` interface is clean and the tier table is clear.

Buildability and honesty. About 430 builder hours, three merges, about seventy actions and P8 in scope; B1 owns identity, access, the agent surface, the two stores and uploads at once, which is wide. The MCP buffer is not mentioned. The unverified list in section 13 is the best of the three; the rollout hole and the P8 promise are the two places the document is less honest than it reads.

## Proposal 3, architecture first

What it gets right. It organizes the round as four truths with one owner each (the document and its log, the deck's side records, the room, identity) and every later decision falls out of that. It makes the one correctness catch of the round: instead of changing `text.replace`, it adds `text.splice` and `text.mark` in plain offsets (2.1), leaves `text.replace` in the union as a whole value write, and so keeps the meaning of every record already written; I verified the reason against `reduce.ts` and `blob-store.ts` and it is right. It gives the anonymous principal a server home, `principal:<id>` in Redis with a 90 day sliding TTL (5.1), which is both the missing piece of proposals 1 and 2 and a data minimization rule. It puts the realtime layer in a framework free `packages/realtime` with one key builder from the slug validated deck id (F34), keeps kill switches in Redis so no third storage product is added (0.26), and specifies the storage migration properly: `meta.json` with a cursor, a copy with `overwrite: false` and an etag check per document, a dual read window, batch deletes off the request path, a rollback flag before the cutover (9.5), with the interim exposure named as risk 6 and the cutover ordered before enforcement is announced.

On the security findings it is the most complete: F6 (the signed thumbnail URL), F9 (the cancel token), F11 (`TURBOSLIDE_TRUST_PROXY`), F14 (the registry bound to identities), F34 (the key builder), F47 (the nonce and the re-authentication) all appear, plus items the others lack: the download token's spent set moved to Redis so a token minted on one instance cannot be replayed on another, different secrets per environment, `access.json` dropped from bundle unpack so an imported bundle cannot smuggle a record (6.6), the room going read only when a document passes 25 MB (6.4), the You need access page rate limited as its own public write surface (6.3), `linkId` never the token in a log line (6.11), and a per change test table mapped to `pnpm check` step numbers (6.13). Its honesty is the best of the three: "Nothing was run" up front, P8 and WebSockets named as round four, the MCP 10 MB buffer named as a precondition to any MCP work (0.24), the presigned upload's dependency on `handleUploadPresigned` named as risk 9, the migration's irreversibility after cutover named as risk 5, and the Photograph preset's values marked as this proposal's own choice (0.19). That preset (black 120, white 230, gamma 0.9, the middle of the GT deck's recorded ranges) is also the real answer to Kevin's "huge feature": proposals 1 and 2 default the dither to the pipeline's identity and call it the one click brand look, but the deck's sixteen pictures were not made with the identity LUT. The IndexedDB mirror of pending operations with "3 unsaved changes from this browser; Apply or Discard" (2.6) is the right offline story, and the degrade to anonymous only when `DATABASE_URL` is absent (5.3) means a pilot can run every collaboration feature with no account store at all.

Where it is weaker. It has no hour estimates, which the other two have and MILESTONES-3 needs. B3 owns identity, better-auth, `authorize()`, the rate limit middleware, headers and CSP, `safeFetch`, uploads, the sanitizer and the frame, and two Playwright specs; that is two builders' work and it is the round's critical path. The per checkout token for the local agent routes (04 F13, 10 F48) is not restated in 2.10 or 6.8. Viewers see comments read only on the view route by default (4.4, and section 12 records the choice), which is Google's behaviour and the leak proposal 2 closes for the sales case. The default role for Anyone with the link is not stated in 4.10. The `html` block's editing surface (a source panel, since the frame is opaque) is implied and not spelled out. `renderThumbs` is missing from the flag list in 0.26. Ease for sales scores 7 rather than 9 because the document is written for the builders and the sales voice of proposal 2 has to be grafted in.

## The winner

Proposal 3 wins with 40 against 36 and 32. It is the only proposal whose realtime design does not corrupt the replay of existing history, the only one that names where an anonymous person's name lives and for how long, the only one that specifies the storage migration a public multi user editor cannot ship without, and the only one that names the MCP buffer defect that would otherwise make the local agent transport unusable the day the actions land. Its security coverage is the fullest and its honesty about round four is the plainest. Proposal 2 contributes the defaults and the sales voice the synthesis must keep; proposal 1 contributes the accessibility row, the decisions list and the auditable tables.

## What must not ship

1. Changing `text.replace` ranges to plain text offsets (proposal 1 section 1.4, proposal 2 section 1.3, inherited from report 02 3.3). `reduce.ts` lines 316 to 323 slice the stored string by the range and `blob-store.ts` lines 443 to 462 replay every record through that reducer, so every existing deck's history would replay wrong. The synthesis uses proposal 3's two new ops and leaves `text.replace` as it is.
2. Chat inside the file (proposal 1 section 2.1). An unstored anonymous message channel in a public editor has no audit trail, no report path and no moderation; it is round four, designed with the same logging and abuse limits as comments, as proposals 2 and 3 have it.
3. Enforcing roles and showing Restricted in the Share dialog while documents still sit on the public store (proposal 2 section 8.4, steps 5 to 10). The private store cutover of proposal 3 section 9.5 precedes the enforcement step and the announcement, and the interim is written into the risks.
4. Rasterizing SVG uploads through sharp on hosted transports (proposal 2 section 5.4, the second clause). SVG is refused hosted; a checkout keeps it for the GT import.
5. Promising P8 (Chromium in a function or worker without the Blob token and the bearer) inside round three (proposal 2 sections 5.9, 5.10 and B6). Egress denial, the pinned lookup in capture contexts and the dropped web security flags ship now; the separate project is round four, as proposals 1 and 3 have it.
6. Showing grant holders' names to link visitors by default (proposal 1 section 13 item 6, following Google). Off by default with the owner's switch, as proposals 2 and 3 have it.
7. Comments visible to viewers on the view route by default (proposal 1 section 3.2, proposal 3 sections 4.4 and 12). Graft 1 replaces it.
8. Shipping the thumbnail route with the `?w=` bearer bypass behind a WAF rule alone (proposal 1 section 5.3, R2). Report 04 F6's fix is a signed, short lived URL from the page carrying the deck id, width, revision and role, as proposals 2 and 3 have it.
9. Adding the round's actions to the MCP tool list before `tools/list` fits the SDK's 10 MB stdio buffer (proposals 1 and 2 are silent; VERIFICATION-2 finding 16 measured 27.2 MB for 81 tools). Proposal 3's B1 fixes it first.
10. Pending operations kept in memory only (proposal 2 section 1.7). The IndexedDB mirror of proposal 3 section 2.6, capped at 1 MB and 24 hours, cleared by "Forget this browser".
11. The Activity panel's comment trend bar and `version.delete` in the default line (proposal 1 sections 3.9 and 3.10). Neither is in the sales trace; `version.delete` is a destructive owner action that needs the re-authentication rule and a confirm and can wait for round four with the activity dashboard.
12. A checkpoint lock whose TTL is shorter than its waiter's break (proposal 1 section 1.1, 2 s and 3 s). Proposal 3's `SET NX PX 5000` with a 1 s heartbeat and a 3 s break.

## Corrections proposal 3 needs before it becomes SPEC-3

1. Split B3 into an identity and accounts builder (the cookie, the principal record, better-auth, the alias table, the avatar route, `accounts.spec.ts`) and a security platform builder (`authorize()` call sites, the rate limit middleware and quotas, headers and CSP, `safeFetch`, the upload pipeline, the sanitizer and the frame, `security.spec.ts`), with hours per builder as proposal 2 gives them.
2. Add the per checkout token for the local agent routes (report 10 F48; graft 12).
3. Add `renderThumbs` to the flag list and state that `uploads` off also closes the presigned route.
4. State the default link role (Viewer) and the "Viewers can see comments" and "Show names to people with the link" switches, both off, in 4.7's `settings` and 4.10's gear.
5. Say how an `html` block is edited once it renders inside an opaque frame: an "Edit HTML" source panel, as proposals 1 and 2 have it (proposal 1 section 5.4, proposal 2 section 5.3).
6. The `/s/<token>` exchange is a GET that writes a grant on the session, so the widened CSRF middleware must guard unsafe methods only on that path and the route must accept a top level navigation (`Sec-Fetch-Mode: navigate`, `Sec-Fetch-Dest: document`) and refuse fetch and subresource modes; the verifier's e2e opens the link from a page on another origin and from a bare address bar and asserts both land.
7. "Forget this browser" clears the IndexedDB pending mirror and the `localStorage` name mirror together, so a shared machine hands over clean (section 5.4 names only the cookie and the mirror).
8. Keep `worker-src 'self' blob:` in the CSP (6.9 has it) and add it to the hosted smoke row, because the dither worker is the first worker the studio ships and a CSP that blocks it fails silently in the sandbox.

## Decisions this judgment takes for Kevin

The proposals leave several product choices to Kevin. Judging as Kevin, and open to being overturned by him:

- Viewers do not see comments by default; the owner's switch turns it on per deck (graft 1). The sales case decides it.
- Link visitors do not see grant holders' names by default; the owner's switch turns it on (rejection 6).
- Anonymous link visitors may comment when the link's role allows, under their label, with the comment quotas (all three proposals agree; Google allows it).
- `html` blocks in a deck shared beyond its owner render only under the owner's switch, off by default (all three agree).
- Chat is round four (rejection 2).
- The Background dialog's Dither toggle applies the Photograph preset, because the dialog is the sales path and the preset is the GT deck's recorded look; the Format options section keeps Neutral as the identity and shows which preset is active (proposal 3 sections 8.6 and 8.9). This is the one place this judgment goes past proposal 3's text, which puts the preset beside the toggle as a chip.
- The "guest" word beside a typed name stays; "visitor" is the alternative if Kevin prefers it.
- Two Marketplace products beside Blob (Upstash Redis on the fixed plan, Neon Postgres on the free tier) are acceptable; proposal 3's degrade to anonymous only without `DATABASE_URL` means the second can wait until the first week of sign in is needed.

# Google Slides parity specification, round three

The binding specification for the third Google Slides parity round of Turboslide, written 2026-09-13 against `main` at `61b16e4` (round two shipped: `docs/gslides-parity/SPEC-2.md`, `MILESTONES-2.md`, `BUILD-STATUS-2.md`, `VERIFICATION-2.md`; `28cb63b` on top changes the README only). This document is an addendum in the shape of SPEC.md and SPEC-2.md: where it is silent, those two stand; where they disagree, this document wins for round three. It is the synthesis of the three design proposals in `docs/gslides-parity/design-3/` (`proposal-1-google.md`, `proposal-2-sales.md`, `proposal-3-architecture.md`) under the three judgments (`judge-1.md`, `judge-2.md`, `judge-3.md`), which chose proposal 3 (119 points against 108 and 103) as the skeleton and named the grafts from proposals 1 and 2 that this document carries. The eleven research reports of `docs/gslides-parity/research-3/` are the facts; section 19 lists them and every URL they read, with the date.

Kevin's directives, verbatim: (1) "...it has all the features and exact behaviors of google slides...literally search up and research everything google slides offers...mimic it perfectly. then clean up our interface and make it so much easier to use, remember this is actually going to be used by majority sales in our org"; (2) "keep going on all of these and dont stop until literally all google slides features are supported with full agent queryability and editability esp on locals"; (3) "we must, must must be able to drag and move around ANYTHING, including backgrounds and shaders but all text in the same exact way the google slides is like a canvas. bring in ALL the canvas features" (done in round two); (4) this round: "make sure its 'multiplayer' too with all collab features with optional logins and avatar creation. address all layout shifts and prevent cyberattacks and introduce rate limiting. also add more features like being able to add images in background and apply dithers over those, those are some huge features we like".

## How to read this specification

- "Report 02 7.5" cites `docs/gslides-parity/research-3/02-realtime-architecture.md` section 7.5; "research/10 C2" cites round one's `docs/gslides-parity/research/10-identity-sharing-and-presence.md`. "P1", "P2" and "P3" are the three proposals; "J1", "J2" and "J3" the three judges. SPEC, SPEC-2, VERIFICATION-2 and AGENTS.md are the repository documents by those names.
- "Principal" is an identity: an anonymous browser (a sealed cookie) or a signed in account. "Grant" is a role given to a principal or an email. "Room" is the set of connections open on one deck and the state they share. "Checkpoint" is a committed revision on the store. "Label" is the generated name an anonymous principal carries (`Titanium 471`). "Checkout" is a clone with `decks/` on disk; "hosted" is `turboslide.vercel.app`.
- Facts about Turboslide are lines of source at `61b16e4`, named by path. `packages/schema/src/actions.ts` holds 105 action ids in `ACTION_IDS` (counted once, from that array; the table grows to 169, section 12). `MUTATION_OPS` holds 15 ops and grows to 17. `packages/chrome/src/menus/model.ts` holds the round two rows; section 13 names the rows that change status. `SCHEMA_VERSION` is 1 and stays 1. `scripts/check.mjs` lists 25 steps and grows to 28.
- Google Slides is the behaviour reference for collaboration (section 0 says where a row departs and why); for accounts, sharing infrastructure, storage and the dither, Google's own choices are out of reach and the design substitutes standalone equivalents. Where a Google fact rests on a single source or on a judge's product knowledge, the row says so and the verifier lists it for Kevin (section 18).
- Every capability a person gets in the UI is also an action in `packages/schema/src/actions.ts` with CLI, MCP and window handlers, working on a local checkout's `decks/` folder with no server as well as against the hosted studio (section 12). Comments, presence reads, share settings, account state and dither settings included.
- Rules of the text: plain technical English, sentence case, no em dashes, no metaphors, no trailing periods on headings, full sentences in body text. Labels are Google's words; agent vocabulary never appears in the default view or its tooltips; no Google icon or artwork is copied.

### 2.1 Truth one: the document and the version log

Nothing changes in what the document is or how it moves: a `Write` of mutations against a `baseRevision`, applied atomically by `applyWrite` (`packages/schema/src/reduce.ts`), validated, logged as `versions/<n>.json` with its inverse, mirrored by the `BlobStore` with the snapshot protocol of SPEC-2 8.2. Every exporter, the renderer, the linter, `deck.pull` and `deck.push` keep reading this and nothing else. What changes is the path into it: hosted writes from the editor go through the room (section 3) and reach the log through the checkpointer; agent writes go through the room and force a checkpoint so the answer carries a revision; CLI writes on a checkout go through `FileStore.write` as today and the room learns of them through `fs.watch`. `VersionRecord` gains one optional field, `ops?: { fromSeq: number; toSeq: number }`, so a record names the stream range it coalesced (02 7.8).

### 2.2 Truth two: the deck's records

Two records live beside the document under the deck's prefix and share its lifecycle (`deck.remove` deletes the prefix; a bundle carries neither by default):

- `access.json`: the owner, the general access mode, the links, the publish token, the grants, the pending requests, the settings, the deck's `assetKey`, and its own `revision` (6.1). Written with `ifMatch` by owners and sharing editors; cached in Redis for 60 s with a `PUBLISH` on every write so other instances drop their copy. On a checkout it lives at `decks/<id>/.turboslide/access.json`, gitignored, and a missing file means the folder's holder is the owner (09 1.6).
- `comments/index.json`, `comments/<threadId>.json`, `comments/authors.json`: the threads with their own revision counter (08 2.2). On a checkout the folder is `decks/<id>/comments/`, gitignored by default in this public repository because a comment names people; a private deck repository removes the one line.

Neither enters `deck.json`, the snapshots or the mutation log. `isMirroredDocument` (`packages/store/src/blob-store.ts:148-157`) excludes `access.json` beside `leases.json`, and the mirror gains `comments/` beside `slides/` and `versions/`.

### 2.3 Truth three: the room

The room is every connection open on one deck and the state they share: the operation stream since the last checkpoint, the presence roster, the client bindings, the checkpoint lock and the per identity budgets. It lives in Redis hosted and in process memory on a checkout, behind one framework free interface in `packages/realtime` (3.2). It is never written to Blob, never enters a version record, never reaches an export and never survives 24 hours (10 6.3).

### 2.4 Truth four: identity

A principal is an anonymous id in a sealed cookie or an account. Everything else stores `principalId` and never a name: `Author.principalId`, comment authors, presence entries, grants, inbox records, log lines. Names, marks, hues and trust states are resolved at render time through one function, `resolvePrincipal` (11 3.2, 08 6.1), backed by the principal records and the alias table (03 E2), so a record written before a person signed in renders as the person afterwards and a deleted account renders as "Deleted account" without rewriting a byte of history.

## 3. The multiplayer architecture

### 3.1 The mutation language: two new ops

Today typing is one `text.replace` per 400 ms burst whose range counts characters of the markup string (`packages/schema/src/mutations.ts:42-50`; `packages/viewer/src/InlineText.tsx` `TEXT_BURST_MS`), and marks are whole string rewrites through `styleRange` and `serializeRuns` in plain text offsets (`packages/schema/src/text.ts:578`). Two offset systems and two whole value forms cannot be transformed, and the stored inverse of every `text.replace` is a whole string replace in markup offsets (`reduce.ts:326-334`) that `documentAtVersion` (`versions.ts:146-170`) and the Blob replay (`blob-store.ts:443-455`) apply through the current reducer. So the reducer, not the record, interprets the range, and a reinterpreted range would break the restore of every version written before this round wherever a marked run precedes it. This round adds two ops and leaves `text.replace` as it is:

```ts
| { op: 'text.splice'; slideId: SlideId; blockId: BlockId; path: string; at: number; remove: number; insert: string }
| { op: 'text.mark'; slideId: SlideId; blockId: BlockId; path: string; range: [number, number];
    edit: { kind: 'marks'; set?: RunMarks; clear?: (keyof RunMarks)[] } | { kind: 'case'; mode: CaseMode } }
```

- Offsets are plain text offsets with one character per paragraph break, the system `plainLength`, `placeRuns`, `styleRange` and the comment anchors already use (`text.ts:481-500`; 08 1.1). `insert` is plain text; its marks are those of the run at `at` (the rule `insertAt` applies, `text.ts:645`); a `\n` in `insert` is a paragraph break in the four multiline pointers and refused elsewhere by the validator after the write, as today.
- The reducer (`reduce.ts` `applyMutation`) implements both over `parseText`, `placeRuns` and `serializeRuns`; the inverse of a splice is a splice, the inverse of a mark is the mark that restores the previous marks of the range (from `marksOfRange`) or the previous characters for a case change. `diff.ts` describes them in the History panel's sentences.
- The editor's `textBurstMutation` emits `text.splice`; the `text.style`, `text.case` and `text.insert` actions emit `text.mark` and `text.splice`; `text.replaceAll` keeps `block.set` (a whole value). `text.replace` stays in `MUTATION_OPS` for old records, the replay paths and agents that still send it, and is a whole value write at admission (3.5).
- Migration: none. Schema version stays 1; every stored record parses as before; `migrations.test.ts` keeps the GT deck and the templates unchanged; `versions.test.ts` and `file-store.test.ts` keep passing on the records they hold.

### 3.3 The room protocol

Routes, all plain server routes on the catch all function rule (300 s, 2 GB; never the `/_serverFn/**` rule), all under the widened CSRF middleware (8.7), all requiring `Content-Type: application/json` on POST:

| Route                     | Method | Who                                                   | Purpose                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| ------------------------- | ------ | ----------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `/api/decks/:id/stream`   | GET    | the session cookie, or a bearer or API key for agents | Server-Sent Events: `hello`, `ops`, `op`, `checkpoint`, `presence`, `leave`, `reject`, `inbox`, `access`, `resync`, a comment line every 15 s; `Last-Event-ID` resumes; closed by the server at a random point between 240 and 290 s with `retry` between 1,000 and 4,000 ms so tabs never reconnect together (10 F24); `authorize(read)` at open, re-checked every 60 s and on every `access` event |
| `/api/decks/:id/ops`      | POST   | the session or the key                                | `{ clientId, base: { seq }, entries: [{ opId, kind, mutations                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        | comment }] }`, at most 64 entries and 256 kB (02 7.2) |
| `/api/decks/:id/presence` | POST   | the session or the key                                | `{ clientId, clock, slideId, selection, pointer, follow, pointerOn, presenting }`, one per 80 ms per client, coalesced server side                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |

Messages down, each with an `id` the client echoes as `Last-Event-ID`:

- `hello { seq, revision, clientId, role, clients[], editing: number }`: the server issues `clientId` (128 random bits) and binds it to the session in Redis for 320 s, refreshed by presence (10 F26); `clients[]` is the roster filtered by role (4.8); `editing` is the count of editing connections, and above 100 the tab opens in Viewing mode with the sentence of 0.9.
- `ops { entries[] }` since the client's position, at most 2,000 entries and 1 MB per open; a position older than the head minus 2,000 answers `resync { revision }` and the client reloads at that revision (10 F25).
- `op { seq, rev, kind, author, clientId, opId, mutations | comment }` for every admitted entry, the author's own included, so the author replaces its pending copy with the admitted form. A viewer receives checkpoints and presence and never raw operations; a commenter receives operations for the slides it may read with notes fields stripped.
- `checkpoint { revision, fromSeq, toSeq, snapshot, author, note, comments: { revision, threadIds } }`.
- `presence { clientId, clock, state }` and `leave { clientId }`.
- `reject { opId, reason, mutations }` with `reason` from the fixed set `stale`, `invalid`, `forbidden`, `too-large`, `locked` (10 F29); the validator's pointer only for a slide the author may read.
- `inbox { unread }` for the principal's connections and `access { revision }` when the deck's record changed, so the tab re-reads its role within a second and a removed editor loses the stream.

Identifiers: `clientId` is server issued; `opId` is `<clientId>:<counter>`; dedup is scoped to `(deckId, clientId)` for the retention window. A POST whose `clientId` is not bound to the posting session is 403 and logged.

### 3.4 Admission and transformation

The ops route (`apps/studio/src/server/room.ts`), per POST:

1. `authorize(ctx, deckId, 'write')` for `kind: 'edit'`, `'comment'` for `kind: 'comment'`; the client binding check; the budgets (60 operations per second per client; 2,400 per minute anonymous, 4,800 signed in and agent; 2 MB or 8 MB per minute per identity; 16 MB per deck per minute; 10 F27). The 65th entry and the 257th kB are refused with 400 before any transform.
2. `base.seq` more than 500 entries behind the head answers 409 `resync` (10 F28); otherwise the instance loads the document at the last checkpoint from its mirror and applies the entries since it, at most 2,000 or 1 MB (0.53), cached per instance keyed by `seq`, so a warm instance does this incrementally.
3. Each incoming mutation is transformed from `base.seq` to the head: `text.splice` and `text.mark` through `packages/schema/src/transform.ts` (the two primitive case of client server operational transformation, insert and delete against insert and delete, ranges shifted and split; 02 3.3); `after` anchors of `slide.insert`, `block.insert`, `slide.move` and `block.move` re-resolved (a removed anchor resolves to the end of the slot or section); everything else unchanged.
4. Each mutation applies through `applyMutation`; the touched slide is validated (`validateSlide`) and the deck level ops validate the manifest; the slide size cap (200 KB) and the deck cap (25 MB) are checked before the transform; a failure is a `reject` and never enters the stream.
5. The append is one Lua script: read `deck:<id>:head`; when it equals the head the instance transformed against, `XADD` the entries, increment the head, `PUBLISH`; otherwise return the new head and the count (never the entries, 10 F34) so the instance reads them with `XRANGE`, transforms once more and retries; after two misses the instance takes a 200 ms lock and retries under it.
6. Every instance subscribed to the deck's pub/sub channel writes the admitted entries into its open streams. Redis runs the script on one thread, so two instances cannot interleave appends.

The transform module has one property test: random concurrent splices, marks and case changes on the fixture deck's Texts converge on every client under every interleaving; the interleaving anomaly of concurrent inserts at one offset (02 E17) is accepted and documented.

### 3.6 The client

The controller of `apps/studio/src/routes/edit.$deckId.tsx` gains the room client (`packages/realtime/client/room-client.ts`) that replaces the write queue and the long poll while keeping `commitAs`, the history and `setDocument`:

- Three sets: pending (sent or about to be sent, not yet in the stream), retained (acked at `seq`, not yet covered by a checkpoint), and the local document. A local gesture applies through `applyMutation`, records the undo entry with its inverse, and flushes at once for structural ops, coalesced at 50 ms during a drag (the `pos` sets) and at 100 ms while typing (one `text.splice` carries the characters since the last flush); the undo grouping keeps its own 400 ms rule (02 7.4).
- An incoming `op` from another client transforms the pending text ops against it, applies it, and re-renders the touched slides; a pending `block.set` of the same pointer stays as the local prediction, as Figma keeps unacknowledged changes (02 E1). The author's own admitted `op` drops the pending copy and adjusts the document by the difference when the server transformed it. A `checkpoint` moves the covered ops out of retained, appends the version to the History panel and moves `serverRevision`.
- Undo is per author and never reverts another author's later change (3.5); redo is the forward reapplication with the same check.
- The save words (SPEC 2.0) gain their meaning from `sync.status`: "Saving…" while `pending + retained > 0`, "All changes saved" at `revAck`, "Couldn't save, retrying" during a reconnect, and "Offline. Changes will save when you reconnect" as a fifth phrase in the same fixed slot when the stream is down and the last POST failed (Turboslide's words; Google's offline sentence is on no page read).
- Offline: the stream closes, the POSTs fail, the client retries with a capped backoff of 8 s; pending and retained ops are persisted to IndexedDB (0.7); `pagehide` with `pending > 0` shows the browser's leave warning (10 F36).
- Reconnect: `EventSource` reconnects with `Last-Event-ID`; the client resends pending ops with `base.seq`; a `resync` reloads the document at the named revision through `readEditorDeck`, then rebases pending ops (text transformed, other pointers as last writer, unplaceable ops shown with their content). Retained ops are resent only when a checkpoint confirms they were lost (3.7 e).
- The conflict card of round one is retired for text and property conflicts. It stays for two cases: a `reject` (shown inline with the content) and a `version.restore` admitted while this tab holds pending ops on the restored slides (the card lists them with Reapply and Discard).
- `sync.status` (an action, 3.10) reads `{ seq, revision, pending, retained, tier, transport, connected }` and `describe().state.sync` mirrors it for the window API.

### 3.7 Sequences

(a) A keystroke from tab A reaches tab B, both hosted, same region. A types "x" at plain offset 12 of `p1`; the reducer applies `text.splice { at: 12, remove: 0, insert: 'x' }` locally (under 1 ms); the flush at 100 ms POSTs one entry with `base.seq` 4,112 (one round trip, 20 to 40 ms US East); the instance transforms against nothing (the head is 4,112), validates the slide, runs the Lua script (a few ms), publishes; B's instance writes the `op` into B's stream (a few ms) and B applies it and re-renders `p1`. About 60 to 100 ms op to screen in `iad1`, 150 ms from the US West coast, 200 to 250 ms from Europe (02 7.10; typical round trips, measured in 16.7).

(b) Two people type in one paragraph. A inserts at 12 with base 4,112; B inserts at 8 with base 4,112. The server admits A first (seq 4,113) and transforms B's splice against it (offset 8 is before 12, unchanged; seq 4,114). B receives A's `op` before its own ack and transforms its pending splice (unchanged); A receives B's `op` and transforms its already admitted copy (offset 12 becomes 13). Both documents read the same string; no dialog, no lock, no lost character.

(c) An agent writes over `POST /api/actions/block.set` while two tabs edit. The action route resolves the API key to a principal and scopes, calls `authorize(write)`, dispatches; the store action's write is admitted through the room as author `{ kind: 'agent', name: <record name>, runId, principalId: 'agent:<tokenId>' }`, transformed against the head, appended, and the checkpointer is forced; the answer carries the new revision and the record as today. Both tabs receive the `op` within the stream's latency and the version appears in History at the checkpoint. The `baseRevision` conflict rule becomes "a slide you touched changed since your base" (a 409 with `since` and `current`); `strict: true` on the input keeps the exact revision check (02 7.9).

(d) The checkpoint. Two seconds after the last operation the admitting instance takes `deck:<id>:ckpt`, reads seq 4,100 to 4,140, coalesces A's 31 splices into one, B's 9 into one, applies two `Write`s through `BlobStore.write` (snapshot, `deck.json` with `ifMatch`, the changed slide, `versions/<n>.json` with `ops: { fromSeq: 4100, toSeq: 4140 }`), announces `checkpoint`, writes any comment entries to the sidecar, trims the stream to the larger of 10,000 entries or 24 hours and at most 16 MB (`XTRIM`), releases the lock. Ten advanced Blob operations for the two commits, about one every 20 s at typing speed, against 75 per second.

(e) Redis becomes unreachable. The ops POST fails with `redis.unavailable`; the room falls to the `blob` tier for the deck: writes go through `BlobStore.write` per 400 ms burst with a per deck admission queue of one write per second, the stream is replaced by a 1 s `head('deck.json')` poll, presence stops across instances and the title row says so. Clients keep every retained op until a `checkpoint` (now a revision) covers it; if the stream lost admitted ops, the next checkpoint's `toSeq` does not cover them and the client resends them against the Blob document, transformed against what landed. An accepted keystroke is never lost; only its position may move (02 7.1).

(f) A local checkout follows a hosted deck. `turboslide deck follow q4-review --from https://turboslide.vercel.app` opens the stream with the API key from `~/.config/turboslide/hosts.json` and `?since=<local revision>`, applies each `checkpoint`'s version record forward through the `FileStore` with the record's `createdAt` and the same reducer (the path `pull()` already replays, `blob-store.ts:340-360`), checks the result's `deck.json` md5 against the record's `snapshot`, and writes `decks/q4-review/`; the local editor on the dev server shows the external revision banner it shows for any CLI write. `--push` sends the local records the host lacks as writes with their `baseRevision` under the per slide conflict rule; a conflict prints both sides and offers `--force` or `--theirs`. `--comments` mirrors the sidecar too (08 3.1).

(g) An MCP client subscribes. `resources/subscribe` on `deck://q4-review` (and `deck://q4-review/comments`, `deck://inbox`, `deck://q4-review/presence`); the server declares `resources: { subscribe: true, listChanged: true }` and sends `notifications/resources/updated` per checkpoint, per comment checkpoint and per inbox event (02 F7); over stdio on a checkout the notification comes from `fs.watch`. `deck.watch { since, timeoutMs }` is the polling form for a transport without a stream.

## 5. Comments, notifications, activity and version history

### 5.1 The model

Threads are a sidecar per deck (2.2). The types are `packages/schema/src/comments.ts` as 08 1.1 writes them: `Thread { id (a 26 character ULID), deckId, anchor, comment, replies[], resolved?, assignee?, createdAt, updatedAt, revision }`, `Comment { id, author: { principalId, label, kind }, createdAt, editedAt?, body: { text, mentions[] }, reactions?, deleted? }`, six anchor kinds (`deck`, `slide`, `block`, `text` with a plain offset range and a quoted string of at most 200 code points, `cell` with row and column, `notes`), `Mention` as a principal id or an invitation id, bodies as plain text with `{@n}` tokens and never markup, rendered through React text nodes, reactions from a fixed palette of 24 keyed by principal, caps of 4,000 code points per body, 20 mentions, 100 replies, 200 threads per slide, 5,000 per deck (08 1.1, 1.3). `resolveAnchor(document, anchor)` is pure and runs at read time; a removed block orphans its threads without deleting them (the panel lists them under the slide with the quoted text) and an undo or a `version.restore` revives them; `slide.replace` keeps the threads whose ids survive. Text anchors shift through the same transform functions `text.splice` uses, emitted as `comment.shift` entries (0.52).

### 5.2 Sync and conflict rules

Comment operations travel on the stream as `kind: 'comment'` entries (`add`, `reply`, `edit`, `delete`, `resolve`, `reopen`, `assign`, `reassign`, `done`, `react`, `unreact`, `move`, `shift`) and reach every tab in the stream's latency; the checkpointer writes the sidecar (one `put` per changed thread, `authors.json` when a label is new, `index.json` with `ifMatch` last) and never a version record; `index.revision` is the comments' own counter (08 2.3, 2.4). Every op commutes or has one winner: add and reply append; edit is author only with the author's later arrival winning; delete is a tombstone that keeps the replies; resolve, reopen, assign and done are last writer wins; reactions are a set per emoji keyed by principal. No comment op is refused for a stale base; `edit` and `move` take `expectedUpdatedAt` and answer 409 when the thread moved (08 3.2). On the `blob` tier and on a checkout the sidecar is written directly with `ifMatch` or under the lock.

### 7.4 Sessions, linking, sign out, Forget this browser, profile

### 7.6 The avatar builder

One dialog, 560 by 640, four tabs in the order a person wants them, each showing the result at 24, 32, 64 and 256 px in light and dark chrome in a fixed 2 by 4 preview strip from first paint, the panel body 320 px for every tab (11 8 P8):

- Initials: one or two letters of the display name in Inter 500 on a square plate with a 1 px `--pt-edge` border; a 2 bit hash picks the plate's Bayer density among 1/8, 2/8, 3/8 and 4/8 so two people with one initial sit on two greys; the letters stay ink so they read at 24 px (11 6.1). The default for everyone.
- Glyph: a 5 by 5 grid of cells inside a 22 px plate, each empty or one of five glyphs the theme owns (a dot, a dash, a slash, a square, the GT mark's stroke), mirrored left to right, from the hash; "Another" rerolls a salt kept on the principal record.
- Dither: a Bayer dithered ramp whose centre, angle and curve come from the hash, at 2 px cells; "Another" as above.
- Picture (signed in principals only; the tab reads "Sign in to upload a picture" otherwise): JPEG, PNG, WebP or GIF (first frame), refused above 5 MB, a square crop dragged in the browser in a 256 by 256 box with a plate until the image decodes, a client side resize to at most 1024 px, then `account.setAvatar` sniffs the bytes, decodes with sharp under `limitInputPixels` and `failOn: 'error'`, rotates by the orientation tag, crops, resizes to 32, 64, 128 and 256 px WebP at quality 80 plus one 256 px PNG, strips metadata, and stores under `u/<avatarKey>/<sha256>-<size>.webp` on the public store where `avatarKey` is a random 128 bit value per person rotated on every change with the old prefix deleted; the original is never kept; SVG is refused (03 I4; 10 F43).

The choice is a small JSON document on the principal record, so `account.setAvatar` sets it from the CLI too, and `turboslide account me --avatar-png me.png` writes the 256 px one bit PNG.

### 7.7 Agent tokens and the CLI login

### 8.4 The html escape block and the standalone export (closes F4)

The block's five regular expressions (`packages/render/src/blocks/html-escape.ts:15-22`) miss attribute separators other than whitespace, entity encoded schemes, `formaction`, `@import` and `position: fixed`. Three layers, all three:

1. Sanitize with a parser at write time and at render time: DOMPurify 3.4.15 through jsdom on the server (`packages/render/src/sanitize/html.ts`), the browser's own window in the editor, `USE_PROFILES: { html: true, svg: true }`, `FORBID_TAGS` `script iframe object embed base meta link style form input button textarea select math`, `FORBID_ATTR: ['style']` unless a CSS value allowlist passes it, `ALLOWED_URI_REGEXP` limited to `https:`, `data:image/`, `#` and `assets/`; run in `applyWrite` for `block.insert`, `block.set`, `slide.replace` and `slide.update` on `html` blocks, in the bundle importer and in `renderHtmlEscape`; the block records `htmlSanitized: true`. A CSS tokenizer (`sanitize/css.ts`) drops `@import`, `@font-face`, `@namespace`, any `url()` outside `assets/`, `expression(`, `behavior:`, `-moz-binding`, `position: fixed` and `position: sticky`, and `!important` on `z-index`, then `scopeCss` runs as today. DOMPurify and jsdom are imported lazily and only when a deck holds an `html` block; `scripts/check-client-bundle.mjs` and the integrator's size record measure the studio function bundle before and after. Legacy blocks are sanitized by the rule `html/sanitize` of `turboslide fix`, run by hand on a deck, never inside `migrate`; until then the linter flags them.
2. Isolate at render time: the block's markup goes into `<iframe sandbox="" srcdoc="...">` (no `allow-scripts`, no `allow-same-origin`, an opaque origin) sized to the block's box, with the theme CSS the block needs and `<meta http-equiv="Content-Security-Policy" content="default-src 'none'; img-src 'self' data: https://<public store>; style-src 'unsafe-inline'; font-src 'self'">`, in the studio, the viewer, the live clone, print, present and the standalone HTML file (a file opened from disk has a `file://` origin where a same origin script is worse). The editor shows the frame and an "Edit HTML" source panel (the source drawer at the block's `html` and `css` fields), because editing inside a sandboxed frame is impossible. The PPTX and PDF exporters rasterize the block in Chromium as today; the canvas fidelity gate proves the GT deck's four imported escape blocks render the same inside the frame, and a mismatch is fixed by extending the frame's theme CSS, never by weakening the sandbox.
3. Scheme check on every link: `run.link` and `BlockLink` accept `https:`, `http:`, `mailto:`, `tel:` and the slide link forms, in the schema and in `renderRuns`, rendered with `rel="noopener noreferrer"`; comment bodies never emit an anchor.

Policy: in a deck shared beyond its owner, `html` blocks render only under the owner's `allowHtmlBlocks` switch; otherwise the block renders as its `note` in a plate. Tests: a fixture deck with the eight bypass payloads of 04 F4 renders in the editor, the viewer and the standalone file opened from disk with no dialog, no request to the beacon host and an opaque frame origin (`packages/render/src/__tests__/html-escape.test.ts`, `security.spec.ts`); a `check.mjs` step greps `dangerouslySetInnerHTML` and `innerHTML` and fails on a new call site outside the allowlisted four.

### 8.15 Risks

1. Instance spread and the checkpointer's locality. Vercel decides which warm instance takes a POST; the design pays one Redis round trip per operation plus an incremental mirror apply per instance; the preview run measures admission under 10 ms on a warm instance and the cold case of 0.53 (02 7.11 risks 1 and 4).
2. Stream loss at Redis. Eviction stays off, the stream is trimmed after every checkpoint and bounded at 16 MB, the two level acknowledgement keeps every accepted keystroke, the `blob` tier is the automatic fallback; the exposure is the checkpoint cadence, at most 10 s of admitted operations (02 7.11 risk 2; 10 F35).
3. Transform correctness. The two primitive case is small and property tested; the interleaving anomaly is accepted; everything outside text is last writer wins or re-anchoring; a hidden non convergence shows as two tabs disagreeing, which `realtime.spec.ts` asserts against.
4. Agents typing over people. An agent's whole Text rewrite wins over a person's in flight typing; the person's characters re-anchor by diff and, when the diff cannot place them, return in the recovery notice; the wording reads as "here is your text" and never as an error.

### 10.2 The pipeline and the three hosts

`ditherPicture(source, box, view, dither, theme, adjust?)` in `packages/effects/src/dither.ts` (browser safe, no encoder import): cover fit of the trimmed region to the screen size (box over cell) through the pinned Lanczos3 after gray or one channel, invert, minFilter and blur (the cached `toneBase`); autocontrast 0.5 then the tone LUT (black, white, gamma) with `block.adjust`'s brightness and contrast folded in; the threshold against `bayer8` (the deck's table), `bayer4` (`(m + 0.5) / 16`), `blue64` (a CC0 64 by 64 texture pinned by sha256) or `random` (a 32 bit hash of x, y and seed); polarity by the lit fraction; paint in the theme's ink and paper, three tones with titanium in the middle, or the posterised colours; nearest upscale by `cell`; `strength` under 1 composites the plane over the continuous picture (06 4.2). With `bayer8`, tone two, cell 2 and strength 1 the stages are the deck's pipeline unchanged. The same TypeScript stages run in `apps/studio/src/workers/dither.worker.ts` for the live preview, in the sheet runtime (`packages/render/src/dither-runtime.ts`) and in Node for materialization and the Editable text raster; the Rust crate gains the stages behind `EffectsBackend` as an optional accelerator; a test asserts equal bits across the worker harness and Node. The preview budget is under 100 ms at the 95th percentile from a slider event to `drawImage`, because the fit is cached and a slider re-runs only the LUT, the threshold and the paint over 360,000 cells; requests carry an id and the worker drops stale ones; the store sees one write per drag (`block.set /dither` on pointer up) (06 4.3).

### 10.3 The renderer path

`renderPicture` (`packages/render/src/blocks/picture.ts`) writes `data-dither`, `data-dither-key` and `data-dither-state` on the picture root; when `deck.assets[asset].variants[key]` lists files for the theme, `src` and the twin attributes point at the variant and the state is `variant` (no script needed: the standalone build, the CLI render, the PDF page and the Perfect raster read it); when no variant exists the image shows the continuous twin, the state is `live`, and the runtime computes the effect in the worker and draws it on `<canvas class="picture-dither">` at `position: absolute; inset: 0; image-rendering: pixelated`, sized to the screen so a 2x device shows exact cells (06 4.4). The overlay is absolutely positioned inside the picture's box, so toggling the dither or swapping a variant for a live frame moves nothing (06 6.5). Every emitted `<img>` carries `width` and `height` (9.2 E12).

### 10.4 Variants and the export paths

Variants are `assets/<asset>.dither-<key12>-light.png`, `-dark.png` (or one neutral file for `same`), 1 bit or palette PNGs, about 4 KB at cell 2 and 16 KB at scale 2, written by `picture.materialize` in one `asset.set` through `putAsset` (8.5) and pruned when no picture references the key (06 4.5, 6.1). Perfect PPTX: the exporter materializes every referenced variant before the shoot, every page is shot in state `variant`, a two tone background page encodes as a 1 bit PNG at both scales with crisp `cell * 2` device pixel cells at 2x, the residual lists `dither: <slideId>#<blockId> <key12> <state>` and `perfect` stays at or under 0.001. Editable text PPTX: a covering picture at the bottom of the stack travels as `slide.background = { data }` with the variant's bytes, a picture at its box as a picture; `strength` under 1 composites in Node with sharp. PDF prints the page reading the variant; the standalone build inlines variants under the `two-color` rule and refuses a referenced picture without a variant with "materialize first"; JPEG is the page raster; TXT is unchanged (06 4.6). Materialization is CPU (under a second per picture in Node) inside the export budgets, rate limited as a capture class action (8.3), and routed through the render worker's queue for a deck with more than 64 dithered pictures.

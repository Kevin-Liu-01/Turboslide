### The show's contract (what B1's present layer, the standalone motion script and `motionCss` agree on)

1. `renderSlide` writes `data-block` on every top level block root under `blockAttrs` or `motion` and `data-motion="1"` on the slide root when `slideHasMotion(slide)` (request 1 to B4). The slide root already carries `data-slide`.
2. When the show mounts a slide it stamps `data-para="<index>"` on the nodes `paragraphNodes(root)` lists for every block a By paragraph effect targets (the `.para` spans of a multiline Text, else the item nodes of `.plain`, `.rows` and `.refs`, else the root), adds `is-hidden` to every block of `hiddenAtStart`, and sets `data-step="0"` on the slide root.
3. Playing step k: the slide root gets `data-step="k"`; every entrance target loses `is-hidden` and gains `is-entering`; every exit target gains `is-leaving`; every Spin target gains `is-emph`; every Play effect goes to the media controller with its `delayMs`. The keyframes begin at the hidden state and `animation-fill-mode: both` holds it through the delay, so the layer needs no per effect timers before the step ends. After `step.durationMs` the layer drops `is-entering` and `is-emph`, and swaps `is-leaving` for `is-hidden`.
4. Going back one step restores the state before the step: entrance targets back to `is-hidden`, exit targets shown, without animation.
5. A transition: the ancestor of both mounted slides carries `data-transition="<incoming slide id>"`; the incoming slide root carries `is-entering`, the outgoing `is-leaving`; both are removed after `durationMs`. Going back adds `data-reverse` beside `data-transition` and the incoming and outgoing keyframes swap with `animation-direction: reverse`; the Dissolve then masks the outgoing slide on top (`z-index: 3`).
6. `MOTION_BASE_CSS` is loaded once per document (the show's present layer, the standalone `#ts-motion-css`): the hidden rule, the stacking of the two slides, `will-change`, and the reduced motion rule that zeroes every duration and delay; the layer reads `matchMedia('(prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)')` and ends each step at once while keeping the step gates.
7. The rules `motionCss` writes are keyed `[data-slide="<id>"][data-step="<k>"] [data-block="<b>"].<class>` (or `[data-block="<b>"] [data-para="<p>"].<class>`), the transition's four rules by `[data-transition="<id>"]`, and the keyframes are named per effect kind (`ts-fade-in`, `ts-appear`, `ts-spin`, `ts-fly-in-left-<px>`, `ts-t-fade-in`, `ts-t-cube-in-<halfWidth>`), so several slides' sheets concatenate without collision.

### Decisions and deviations, recorded for the integrator and the verifier

1. `packages/schema/src/motion.ts` is a leaf module. Day 0's version imported `slideBlocks` from `deck.ts` at runtime while `deck.ts` evaluates `animationsSchema` from `motion.ts` at its top level; a module that imports `motion.ts` before `deck.ts` (a test, a handler module) would have met the temporal dead zone. `motionTargets(slide)` spells the same walk inside the module; `normalizeMotion` behaves as on day 0.
2. The schedule gained three fields: `MotionSchedule.slideId` (the stylesheet scopes its rules by it and a concatenated standalone sheet needs it), `MotionEffect.durationMs` (the length the effect plays, which differs from the row's `durationMs` for Appear, Disappear and Play) and `MotionEffect.box` (the positioned block's box, the fly offset's input, so `motionCss(schedule, page)` keeps its two argument signature). `motion.compile`'s output schema grew accordingly; the generated contracts (`openapi.json`, `mcp-tools.json`, the skills) are regenerated by the integrator at merge 1 (request 3). `emptySchedule` takes the slide id first; its one caller was `compileMotion`.
3. The compile semantics against 1.3's sentence "withPrevious joins the current step at delay 0": a With previous row joins at the start of the row it follows, which is 0 behind a click row or first in a step (the sentence's cases) and the After previous row's own delay when it follows one (Google's definition, R01 3: "starts at the same time the previous animation starts"). An After previous row starts at the end of the group it follows, the row before it and the With previous rows that started with it (R01 6.3 level 4: "a delay equal to the end time of the previous group"), so `effects` step 2 places b6 at 1000 (after b4's fade out and b5's fly in), not at b5's 800. This is PowerPoint's timing group and what `ooxml/timing.ts` writes on day 5; the verifier may read it against Google on the preview (16.9).
4. Appear and Disappear play as a 1 ms switch (`SWITCH_MS`), the `dur="1"` PowerPoint writes (R01 6.4); the row keeps its `durationMs` for the slider and the compiler ignores it for the two effects. By paragraph on a block with fewer than two paragraphs plays the block as one object (no `paragraph` field, no `bldP build="p"`). A Play row's length is the asset's remaining length after `startMs` and `endMs` through `mediaLength` (the extractor and the show supply `deckMediaLength(deck, slide)`; a YouTube source or an unknown length keeps the row's `durationMs`; a looping medium takes the step's length, 0 when alone).
5. The Dissolve is 64 discrete `mask-image` frames, not a position stepped sprite (R05 9.4's wording): a tiled `mask-image` cannot show one frame of a sprite over the whole slide, because `mask-repeat` tiles the sprite itself. Each frame is an 8 by 8 one bit palette PNG (about 120 bytes, transparent index 0 through `tRNS`) built by `dissolveFramePng` with a dependency free encoder (stored zlib, CRC32, Adler32, base64), frame k opaque in the cells whose Bayer rank is at most k; the 64 stops at k/63 switch discretely at their midpoints under `linear`; the whole keyframe block is about 12.5 KB, emitted once per sheet. The Bayer permutation is `BAYER8_RANKS`, the deck's own table copied from `packages/effects/src/bayer.ts` because the render package does not depend on the effects package (request 4 offers the dependency; the test pins the 64 values so the copy cannot drift).
6. The validator's severities: a repeated id, an unknown block, Play on a non media block and a second YouTube video on Play (automatically) refuse the deck (3); a direction on a non fly effect, a fly without a direction and By paragraph on a block without paragraphs are kept and reported (2), because the compiler has a defined reading for each (ignored, from or to the left, one object). The YouTube rule lives here rather than in B2's `media` family because 1.2 lists it under the `motion` code.
7. `paragraphCountOf` in `scene/extract.ts` counts a list block's items one each: the scene holds one `SceneText` per item (`<block>/items/<i>/...`), so the day 0 count (distinct `line.paragraph` values over the block's texts) read 1 for a four item list while the show reads 4 and the schema 4. A ruled row's key and value are one paragraph. The PPTX writer's By paragraph target for a list is the item shape, settled on day 5.
8. The block effects write the individual transform properties (`translate`, `scale`, `rotate`) so a block's own `transform` (a flip, a rotation) stays; the three 3D transitions write `transform` with their own `perspective(2400px)` because a perspective must be the outermost operation and `perspective` on an ancestor reaches its children alone. Easing: `ease-in-out` transitions, `ease-out` entrances, `ease-in` exits, `linear` Spin and the switches; the reversal plays the same keyframes with `animation-direction: reverse` (unverified against Google, 16.9).
9. The fly offset: from the block's box to the page edge (`-(x + w)` from the left, `page.width - x` from the right, `-(y + h)` from the top, `page.height - y` from the bottom), rounded to whole pixels; a flow layout block (no `pos`) flies by the page dimension, which always clears the edge. So the 4:3 stylesheet differs from the 16:9 one in the right and bottom offsets, the flow fallbacks and Cube's half width (`ts-t-cube-in-600` against `-800`).
10. The fixture's media records were written by hand from ffprobe (`durationMs` 1000, `codecs` `V_VP9` and `pcm_s16le`); B2's parsers may rewrite `codecs` through `media.info --refresh` without moving the schedule. The fixture's headings are sentence case with no Google label capitalised mid sentence so `copy/sentence-case` stays silent.
11. The editor walk probe was not run: no editor surface changed today (the panel, the show and the filmstrip glyph are days 3 to 7), and no dev server was started; recorded as not driven.

### The show's contract additions (beside day 1's seven points)

8. `ViewerSlide.motion?: MotionSchedule` is the payload the show reads (`motionOf(slide)`); the producers are `PresenterPage.tsx` (landed) and, by request 6, `buildViewerDeck` in `apps/studio/src/server/decks.ts` and `toViewerDeck` in `apps/studio/src/editor/controller.tsx`. Without it a slide has zero steps and the show behaves as round four's.
9. The two slides during a transition are React's incoming `.pt-slide` wrapper and a clone of the leaving one appended to `.ts-stage` with an inline `z-index` and `animation: none` on the wrapper (the keyframes run on the slide roots inside); the clone is removed after `durationMs`.
10. The document level click listener runs in the capture phase and only while `.ts-stagewrap.is-present` exists; it advances one step and stops propagation when a step remains, otherwise it lets the host's slide advance through.
11. The presenter channel is protocol 2: `state.step` and `state.steps`, `goto.step`, `media`, `mediaControl`, `stroke` (partial strokes stream with `done: false` and the same `id`), `strokesClear`.
12. The standalone file's motion payload is `#ts-motion` (`application/json`): `{ page, order: SlideId[], schedules: Record<SlideId, MotionSchedule>, autoplay?: { intervalMs, loop } }`; the runtime calls `window.__tsMotion.go(from, to)` before its own `show(n)` and yields when it answers true.
13. ### Decisions and deviations, recorded for the integrator and the verifier

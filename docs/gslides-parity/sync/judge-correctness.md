# Judge correctness: the sync model of docs/SYNC.md

The correctness judgment of the sync round's specification, written 2026-09-20 against `docs/SYNC.md` (97,941 bytes, 13:03 PDT), `docs/HOSTING-MOVE.md`, the three audits under `docs/gslides-parity/sync/` (`audit-ordering.md`, `audit-costs.md`, `audit-hosting.md`) and the sources on the shared checkout as they stood at the time of reading (`packages/store/src/blob-store.ts`, `versions.ts`, `pulse.ts`, `packages/realtime/src/blob.ts`, `admission.ts`, `protocol.ts`, `packages/realtime/client/room-client.ts`, `apps/studio/src/server/room.ts`, `apps/studio/src/routes/api/decks.$deckId.stream.ts`, `packages/schema/src/transform.ts`, `mutations.ts`). The lens is the correctness of the sync model: whether the ordering guarantees are invariants two browser drivers can falsify, whether a lost write is impossible or detected under two instances writing one deck, whether idempotency covers a retried post, whether the focus round's stream cap fix and the BLOB_BUDGET rule stay, whether undo after a remote op is defined, whether the lanes are disjoint, and whether the size is honest. No source file, Vercel setting or GitHub object was touched; no git write command ran.

Line numbers below are the tree's at the time of reading. The product round's uncommitted work moves lines under the document: `docs/SYNC.md` cites `room.ts` 1560 to 1746 for `admitOnBlob` and the tree holds it at 1705 to 1900. Where the two differ, the function name is the reference.

## 1. Verdict

Revise. The model's centre is right and the code confirms it: the record claim with `overwrite: false` plus the manifest put with `ifMatch` is the one commit point, two instances writing one deck end in one commit and one conflict, and the four anomalies the ordering audit measured are missing transforms or a field that skips them, exactly as the document says. Sections 3.3 (the server transforms before it places), 3.4 (the fields as text runs) and 3.7 (the viewer receives ops) are the right fixes and each has a two browser row. What fails the lens is around that centre: one proposed change (3.8) would introduce a class of lost write the code does not have today; the idempotency of 3.2 is stated as "with no store call" on a path where the instance cannot know the first attempt committed without one; the record's new field lands on a strict schema in a twelve hour skew window; the budget rule and the kill switch read contradict each other; three invariants are stated stronger than the mechanisms behind them; the lanes have three file overlaps the shared table does not name; and the size is a day and a half only for the P0 set.

## 2. Rejections

Each names the section and the problem, with the file or audit item that proves it.

1. Section 3.8 (the lost claim) treats a taken record as a commit: it fetches the taken record and the ones above it by number and applies them on the mirror, while today's `pull` drops every record whose revision is above the proven manifest (`blob-store.ts` `dropRecordsAbove`, 1084 to 1098: "the claim of a write in flight on another instance, stored before its commit, or the leftover of a writer that stopped before its commit") and `claimRecord` lets a later writer overwrite a claim older than `CLAIM_GRACE_MS` 60 s (1319 to 1336). A mirror that applied a claim whose writer never committed, or whose number another writer later took over, then places the next write on a document the log does not describe and commits it under `ifMatch` of the etag it synced; that is a lost write the current code cannot make. The section must apply a fetched record only after a `head deck.json` shows the manifest at or above the record's revision, or be cut (it is P1 and its value is 2 in the audit, item 13).

2. Section 3.2 (idempotency) says a resend is answered "on any instance and with no store call" from the mirror's records, but `admitOnBlob` syncs the mirror only when it is below the POST's base (`room.ts` `liveAtLeast`, 1612: the loop runs while `revision > live.document.deck.revision`), and a resend goes at the base of the lost first attempt (`room-client.ts` `flush`, `base: { seq }`; the lost answer path at 1189 to 1198 leaves `seq` where it was). The instance whose mirror sits exactly at that base makes no sync, holds no record of the first attempt, finds no origin, places the entry, and the store write's own head and pull (`blob-store.ts` 1410 to 1416) bring the mirror up and commit the entry a second time. The origin check has to run after the write path's head and pull (inside the channel's `append` after `store.revision()`, or inside `write()` before `file.write`), or a resend must carry a mark that forces one head before the check; the row `sync.resend.idempotent-across-instances` as written does not reach this case (rejection 8).

3. Section 3.2 and lane B3's "VersionRecord.origin written on day 0": `versionSchema` is `z.strictObject` (`packages/schema/src/mutations.ts` 261) and `versionRecordSchema` extends it ("extend on a strict object stays strict, a test pins it", `packages/store/src/versions.ts` 50 to 53); `readVersions` throws a TypeError on a record with an unknown field (83 to 90). Skew protection pins a tab to its deployment's functions for 43,200 s (`audit-hosting/vercel-facts.md` section 2), so for up to twelve hours after the ship an old instance reads new records from the shared store, `records()` throws for that deck, and with it `announce`, `since`, `recordsAfter` and the pull's replay stop. The document uses skew protection to argue the stream direction is safe (an old tab never receives `covers`) and is right about that; the store direction is the hole. The landing must be tolerate first (a release whose parser accepts `origin`), write second, or the origin must be written where the strict parser does not look.

4. Section 3.11 invariant 12 and section 4.5 state "one timed store call per tick per open deck per instance" while lane B1's P1 reads `flags.json` "inside the existing tick" and the row `sync.flags.read-only-on-every-instance` demands both instances read the flip within 5 s. A flags head inside a per deck tick is a second timed call per tick; the every fifteenth tick safety head (`pulse.ts` `PULSE_SAFETY_TICKS` 15) cannot carry it within 5 s, and the 10 s quiet tick of 3.10 cannot either. Either the budget gains a stated instance wide line (one `flags.json` head every 5 s per instance while any stream is open there, counted apart from the per deck tick) or the row's bound follows the tick.

5. Section 3.11 invariants 3 and 10 ("an op id is admitted at most once on any instance"; "a replay whose first attempt committed is acknowledged, never committed again") are absolute while two paths escape them: the client's `resync` reloads the document and re-folds every pending op on it with no origin check (`room-client.ts` 943 to 966; audit-ordering 3.1), and a POST whose base is more than `BASE_SEQ_WINDOW` behind the head is answered `resync` before any check (`room.ts` 1386 to 1395). A pending op whose first attempt committed and whose tab then resynced is posted again. The invariant must name the resync path's check (the client drops a pending op whose id the records above its old position name, which `readEditorDeck`'s newest 50 records can carry once they hold `origin`) or state the bound past which it stops holding.

6. Section 3.9 (what stays from the focus round's stream fix) omits the stream slot counted under the tab token and released on reconnect or lost presence (`decks.$deckId.stream.ts` 37 to 56 and 103 to 140; `room.ts` `streamCounters` 2332 to 2410, `tabTokenOf` 2325), which the brief names as what stays; the ordering audit names it in its section 1 and the document drops it. Section 3.10's idle close after five minutes then leaves two things undefined: that the slot is released at the client's close and taken again under the same tab token at the reopen (VERIFICATION C3-F1 found a closed stream holding its slot 73 s, which is why the token release exists), and how a tab with no stream learns of "a presence change" that reopens it.

7. Section 5 (the lanes) has three overlaps the shared file table does not name. 3.8 is B3's P1 and its second half ("`liveAtLeast`'s 500 ms pause runs only when the named revision is still ahead") is `room.ts`, B1's file. B5's P1 `/decks` index record "written on create, copy, rename, trash, restore and remove" needs the store's index writer in `blob-store.ts` (B3; the `index/fresh-decks.json` pattern at 628 to 740, audit-costs item 9) and the agent path's `deck.trash` and `deck.remove` dispatchers in `apps/studio/src/server/actions.ts` (776 to 786), which no lane owns; an index written from `decks.ts` alone goes stale on every CLI and MCP trash. B2's heartbeat cadence changes `PRESENCE_HEARTBEAT_MS` in `protocol.ts`, which the table gives B1 with B2 named as a reader of `Entry.covers` only.

8. Section 6.1 rows `sync.resend.idempotent-across-instances` and `sync.pull.no-listing` cannot falsify their cross instance claim. The first aborts one answer with `page.route` on production, where the resend lands on whichever instance is warm; today's per instance memory (`room.ts` `blobAdmittedBefore`, 1712) already passes the same instance case, so a green row proves nothing about another instance. The second reads `sync.status.storeCalls` from whichever instance answers `/api/actions/sync.status` (section 6.3 says the counters are per instance and the preview routes to whichever is warm), so a `list` on the instance serving A's stream is invisible to a probe that samples another. Both rows must name their cross instance half as the two instance fake test (6.4 names neither) and the production row as a smoke.

9. The header and section 1 size the round at "about a day and a half of the pipeline". The document carries about fifteen P0 and fifteen P1 items over five lanes; four items are rated effort 3 of 5 by the audits themselves (ordering items 1, 4, 10; costs item 10); B4 writes a new two browser spec of eight rows each repeated three times with 20 s offline waits and a cost probe of seven rows at three minutes each sampled on two origins (about two hours of gate wall time per FOCUS 6.2's two preview runs and one production run); and the product round's own cut was two pipeline days for a comparable count (`docs/PRODUCT.md` line 20, after judge-design rejection 1 sent a four day draft back). A day and a half holds the P0 set. The P1 list must be named as the round after, cutting first 3.8 (rejection 1), `versions/head.json` (the log is 4.4 MiB over 3,907 records with a maximum of 376 on one deck, `blob-sizing.json`; a cold pull of 376 records is 752 simple reads), the published show snapshot ($1.00 a month at 50 hours, effort 3), the `/decks` index record ($0.31 a month, effort 3, rejection 7) and the presence copy rename (the sweep covers it).

## 3. Additions

What the document must add.

1. Undo after a remote op has a definition (invariant 11) and no falsifier: no row of 6.1 and no test of 6.4 drives it, and section 7 defers round five's undo miss to "once the server transform is on production". Add the row `sync.undo.after-remote` (A types a word into a body block, B inserts a word before it in the same block, A presses Undo; A's word alone leaves both browsers within 5 s and B's stays; three of three) and, in `room-client.test.ts`, an inverse recorded before a remote insert is applied at the shifted offset, and the store echo of the author's own commit stays skipped by `transformSince` once `entryOfRecord` emits the record's `clientId` instead of `store` (today the skip is `!myClientIds.has(entry.clientId)`, `room-client.ts` in `transformSince`; with 3.2 the own echo carries the tab's id and the skip holds; with a second tab of the same person it does not, and the document must say which).

2. Invariant 2 covers every mutation, but `transformMutation` transforms the two text ops alone and passes everything else through (`transform.ts` 345 to 349), where the reducer re-anchors or refuses (`room.ts` `reanchor`, section 2.4 of the document says so); no row falsifies the non text half. Add one structural row: A and B each `pos.set` one block within 200 ms and both browsers show one position within 3 s; A moves a block while B deletes its slide and the loser reads the reducer's sentence. Kevin's directive that anything drags is what sellers do with two people on a deck.

3. The hole in the log. A manifest put that meets `BLOB_DOCUMENT_WRITE_TIMEOUT_MS` and lands after the head check (`blob-store.ts` 1492 to 1494 reads the head; a put aborted by the deadline may still land) and after `releaseClaim` deleted the record (1440 to 1444, 1531) leaves a manifest naming revision n with no `versions/n.json`. The pull's walk stops at the first missing number (1037 to 1044) and `recordsAfter` reads the mirror's records, so every other instance announces nothing above n until a reload proves the document from the snapshot. `announce` already publishes an external checkpoint when the store's revision is above the last record (`blob.ts` 274 to 286); state whether that path covers the stopped walk (the tabs reload at the head) and add the rule: the walk continues to the manifest's revision past a missing number, and the missing number is written into the sweep's report.

4. Section 3.3: state that the retry after a moved head transforms from the POST's original mutations past every record between `post.base.seq` and the new head (not the already transformed candidates past the delta alone), and that the origin check of 3.2 runs again after every sync inside the retry loop (`place()` is re-run at `room.ts` 1842 on `fresh`, which was filtered once at 1711 before any sync).

5. Section 3.2: the bound of the origin check is the records above `post.base.seq`, the set `channel.since(base)` already reads for the transform, not "the newest 50"; a first admission always sits above the base, and a client fifty commits behind is not served by the newest fifty. State that the answer to a resend synthesizes one entry per op id at the record's seq (as `result.entries` carry the client's ids today, `blob.ts` 479), so `settleBehind`'s match by `entry.opId` (`room-client.ts` 736) keeps working; "settleBehind unchanged" is true only under that rule.

6. Section 3.4: define the precedence between `deck.set /title` from `deck.rename` (`apps/studio/src/server/decks.ts` 371 to 400, the editor's rename and the CLI's) and the reducer deriving the title from the heading. Today the client couples them per burst; if the reducer derives the title, a rename that differs from the heading is undone by the next heading splice unless the rule says a set title wins until the heading changes, or the title is never derived once set.

7. Sections 3.7 and 3.10, the paused tab: the heartbeat continues at 10 s and its answer's roster is what reopens the stream on a colleague's arrival; the slot is released at the close and retaken under the tab token; the row of a paused tab reads its state to colleagues (or expires), so a chip never shows a present seller who receives nothing.

8. Section 6.1: `sync.title.concurrent-both-kept` and `sync.title.offline-both-kept` need B1's schema half and B2's client half both landed; name the dependency in the row or in the gate rule, so a partial land reads as a known mechanism and not a flake.

9. Section 6.4: the cross instance resend test, two fake instances over one fake store: instance 1 commits record n from the POST; instance 2, its mirror at n minus 1 and inside its 750 ms sync window, receives the resend at base n minus 1; the assertion is one record, one answer at seq n, and the fold applied once on a third fake's mirror.

10. `docs/HOSTING-MOVE.md` 2.2 says two deployments may serve one store for the minutes between the transfer and the redirect, "which the store's conditional commits already allow between instances (docs/SYNC.md 2.1)". That holds while both run one commit; with rejection 3 a mixed commit window breaks the older deployment's reads for every deck that receives a new record. The cutover order must either run before the sync round's origin field ships or pin both deployments to one commit for the window, and `docs/SYNC.md` must cross reference it.

11. Line citations: cite functions with the commit they were read at, or re-read at the merge; the document's `room.ts` lines are off by about 145 against the tree today.

## 4. Notes

What the lens confirmed, in the code.

- The linearization point is as described. `claimRecord` puts `versions/<n>.json` with `overwrite: false` (`blob-store.ts` 1314) and takes over a stale claim past `CLAIM_GRACE_MS` or the write's own earlier claim (1319 to 1336); the commit is `putDocument('deck.json', synced)` with `ifMatch` (1483); a precondition failure is `conflictFromStore` (1532); a deadline on the commit put is checked against the head and finishes as the commit when the bytes match (1491 to 1496). `hosted.test.ts` 369, 405 and 609 hold the fake to one commit and one conflict. The store side of a lost write under two instances is impossible by this pair; the model says so and the runs measured the loser's path (RecordTakenError, 3.1 to 4.0 s).
- `admitOps` on the memory tier has the exact shape 3.3 asks of `admitOnBlob`: `landed = channel.since(base)`, `transformEntry(entry.mutations, landedMutations)` before `landCandidate`, the retry through `appendWithRetry` transforming once more against what landed (`room.ts` 1401 to 1507). `admitOnBlob` places verbatim and transforms past `refusedUndo` alone (1768 to 1771). The fix is a port, and the document is right that no engine is missing.
- `filterEventForReader` strips every `op` for a viewer (`room.ts` 2036 to 2043); `canSeeOps` gaining `viewer` with `stripNotes` is the change and the row is a direct falsifier.
- The BLOB_BUDGET restatement of 4.5 is faithful to `pulse.ts` (`HOSTED_POLL_MS` 2000, `POLL_BACKOFF_MAX_MS` 60,000, `PULSE_SAFETY_TICKS` 15, `POLL_CALLS_PER_MINUTE_MAX` 30, `pollBackoffMs` never under the store's `retry-after`), except for the flags contradiction of rejection 4. The 10 s quiet tick and the idle close only lower the count.
- The client posting while its stream is down and owning the reopen is in the code (`flush` 1133 to 1140 posts without the stream when bound; `caughtUp` holds the first flush until the replay reached the hello's head, 855 to 859, 992). The document keeps it.
- The blob channel's `append` reports a moved head as `{ ok: false, head, count }` (`blob.ts` 422, 459 to 470), which is what the retry loop of `admitOnBlob` reads; so a stale placement is re-placed once. Addition 4 asks the document to say what the second placement transforms from.

Which invariants the two browser drivers falsify and which they do not. Invariants 1, 2 (the text half), 5, 6 and 9 have rows that fail when the invariant fails. Invariants 3 and 4 (the cross instance half), 7 (per instance counters), 8 (proven reads), 10 (the resync half), 11 (undo) and 13 (retention) rest on unit tests, of which 6.4 names the proven reads, the prune and the same instance resend and not the two instance resend, undo or the log hole.

What is right about the costs side, for the record: the ranked savings rest on measured rates and code counts, the model states its per instance and one tab per deck limits, and the correction to the ordering audit's item 6 arithmetic (a presence push moves the pulse, not the manifest; `syncNow` 1214 to 1218 pulls nothing) is right in the code.

What to leave as the document has it: the snapshot per commit (question 1's default), the record claim as early visibility, the `between` on the ops answer, the export job record joining the proof rule, the one write per second spacing, and the decision that a CRDT and round five's engine stay out.

# Proposal 3, architecture: one truth for the studio, the CLI, MCP and the exports

Designer 3 of 3 for the Google Slides parity round three, written 2026-09-13 against Turboslide `main` at `61b16e4` (the round two production table; `28cb63b` on top of it changes the README only). The angle is architecture first: the design starts from the revision store, the action table and the Blob backend, and places the change feed, the presence channel, the conflict rules, identity, permission enforcement, rate limiting, quotas and the picture effect pipeline so that the studio, the CLI, the MCP server and the exporters keep reading one truth. Every schema, store and route change is named with its migration, and every limit is a number.

Inputs read in full: the eleven reports of `docs/gslides-parity/research-3/` (cited as 01 to 11 with their sections), `docs/gslides-parity/SPEC.md` sections 1, 2.0, 6.6 and 15, `docs/gslides-parity/SPEC-2.md` sections 0, 1, 2.5, 2.6, 8 and 12, `docs/gslides-parity/VERIFICATION-2.md` sections 10 and 11, `docs/gslides-parity/research/10-identity-sharing-and-presence.md` Part C, `AGENTS.md`, `docs/hosting.md` sections 4 and 8, and the code the decisions depend on: `packages/schema/src/{mutations,reduce,actions,assets,deck,canvas,text}.ts`, `packages/store/src/{store,hosted,select,lease,watch,file-store,blob-store,blob-vercel,snapshots,versions}.ts`, `apps/studio/src/server/{write,actions,agent-actions,auth,sessions,root}.ts`, `apps/studio/src/routes/{edit.$deckId,__root,api/actions.$action,decks.$deckId.assets.$}.tsx|ts`, `apps/studio/src/start.ts`, `apps/studio/vite.deploy.config.ts`, `packages/agent/src/{dispatch,http/auth,http/dispatch,http/sessions}.ts`, `packages/mcp/src/{server,http}.ts`, `packages/render/src/blocks/{html-escape,picture}.ts`, `packages/effects/src/two-tone.ts`, `packages/materials/src/actions.ts`, `packages/chrome/src/{TitleRow,dialogs/Share,dialogs/Background}.tsx`, `scripts/check.mjs`. No web page was read for this proposal; every external fact is cited through the report that read it, with that report's source key, and the reports list their URLs and read dates. Nothing was run.

Rules followed: plain technical English, sentence case, no em dashes, no metaphors, no trailing periods on headings, full sentences in body text. Google Slides is the behaviour reference for collaboration; no Google icon, artwork or asset is proposed.

## 1. The four truths and where each lives

```
                      browser tab / CLI / MCP client
                                  |
              actions (packages/schema/src/actions.ts), one dispatcher
                                  |
        +-------------------------+--------------------------+-------------------+
        |                         |                          |                   |
   the document              the deck's records          the room            identity
   deck.json, slides/,       access.json                 ops stream          principals (cookie)
   versions/, snapshots/     comments/{index,threads}    presence roster     accounts, sessions,
   (applyWrite, checkpoint)  (ifMatch per file)          locks, budgets      passkeys, api keys
        |                         |                          |                   |
   private Blob store        private Blob store          Redis                Postgres (Neon)
   or decks/ on a checkout   or decks/<id>/comments      or process memory    or node:sqlite
                             and .turboslide/access.json                      or nothing (anonymous only)
```

### 1.1 Truth one: the document and the version log

Nothing changes in what the document is or how it moves: a `Write` of mutations against a `baseRevision`, applied atomically by `applyWrite` (`packages/schema/src/reduce.ts`), validated, logged as `versions/<n>.json` with its inverse, mirrored by the `BlobStore` with the snapshot protocol of SPEC-2 8.2. Every exporter, the renderer, the linter, `deck.pull` and `deck.push` keep reading this and nothing else. What changes is the path into it: hosted writes from the editor go through the room (section 2) and reach the log through the checkpointer; agent writes go through the room and force a checkpoint so the answer carries a revision; CLI writes on a checkout go through `FileStore.write` as today and the room learns of them through `fs.watch`. `VersionRecord` gains one optional field, `ops: { fromSeq: number; toSeq: number }`, so a record names the stream range it coalesced (02 7.8).

### 1.2 Truth two: the deck's records

Two records live beside the document under the deck's prefix and share its lifecycle (`deck.remove` deletes the prefix; a bundle carries neither by default):

- `access.json`: the owner, the general access mode, the links, the publish token, the grants, the pending requests, the three settings, the deck's `assetKey`, and its own `revision` (09 1.1, 10 5.2). Written with `ifMatch` by owners and sharing editors; cached in Redis for 60 s with a `PUBLISH` on every write so other instances drop their copy. On a checkout it lives at `decks/<id>/.turboslide/access.json`, gitignored, and a missing file means the folder's holder is the owner (09 1.6).
- `comments/index.json`, `comments/<threadId>.json`, `comments/authors.json`: the threads with their own revision counter (08 2.2). On a checkout the folder is `decks/<id>/comments/`, gitignored by default in this public repository (08 2.2 records the choice and the one line a private deck repository removes).

Neither enters `deck.json`, the snapshots or the mutation log. `isMirroredDocument` (`packages/store/src/blob-store.ts:148-157`) excludes `access.json` beside `leases.json`, and the mirror gains `comments/` beside `slides/` and `versions/` (`localDocuments` at `:199-215`).

### 1.3 Truth three: the room

The room is every connection open on one deck and the state they share: the operation stream since the last checkpoint, the presence roster, the client bindings, the checkpoint lock and the per identity budgets. It lives in Redis hosted and in process memory on a checkout, behind one framework free interface in a new package `packages/realtime` (2.2). It is never written to Blob, never enters a version record, never reaches an export and never survives 24 hours (10 6.3). Report 02 2.5 and 2.6 compared the options; Redis through the Marketplace is the one that costs nothing new to operate, keeps every function in one deployment and has the atomic primitives the ordering needs.

### 1.4 Truth four: identity

A principal is an anonymous id in a sealed cookie or an account. Everything else stores `principalId` and never a name: `Author.principalId`, comment authors, presence entries, grants, inbox records, log lines. Names, marks, hues and trust states are resolved at render time through one function, `resolvePrincipal` (11 3.2, 08 6.1), backed by the principal records and the alias table (03 E2), so a record written before a person signed in renders as the person afterwards and a deleted account renders as "Deleted account" without rewriting a byte of history.

### 1.6 The one rule that spans it

Every capability a person can use is one action of `packages/schema/src/actions.ts` dispatched through `@turboslide/agent/dispatch`, with CLI, MCP and window handlers generated from the table (SPEC 7.1; AGENTS.md "The agent surface"). This round adds six action groups (`presence`, `sync`, `comment`, `share`, `account`, `admin`) and about 64 actions (section 9.1 lists them). A window handler whose implementation needs the store, Node or a secret (comments, share, presence reads, account, materialize) joins `SERVER_SIDE_WINDOW_ACTIONS` and runs through `runDeckAction` (`apps/studio/src/server/agent-actions.ts`), so a click and an agent call take one path. The author of every action comes from the session or the token record on the server (04 F2); the body's `author` is ignored on the browser transports and the CLI keeps `--author` for the file store.

## 2. The multiplayer architecture

### 2.1 The mutation language: two new ops

Today typing is one `text.replace` per 400 ms burst whose range counts characters of the markup string (`packages/schema/src/mutations.ts:42-50`; `packages/viewer/src/InlineText.tsx` `TEXT_BURST_MS`), and marks are whole string rewrites through `styleRange` and `serializeRuns` in plain text offsets (`packages/schema/src/text.ts:578`). Two offset systems and two whole value forms cannot be transformed. Report 02 3.3 proposed changing `text.replace` to plain offsets; that would change the meaning of every record already written, so this proposal adds two ops instead and leaves `text.replace` as it is:

```ts
| { op: 'text.splice'; slideId; blockId; path: string; at: number; remove: number; insert: string }
| { op: 'text.mark'; slideId; blockId; path: string; range: [number, number];
    edit: { kind: 'marks'; set?: RunMarks; clear?: (keyof RunMarks)[] } | { kind: 'case'; mode: CaseMode } }
```

- Offsets are plain text offsets with one character per paragraph break, the system `plainLength`, `placeRuns`, `styleRange` and the comment anchors already use (`text.ts:481-500`; 08 1.1). `insert` is plain text; its marks are those of the run at `at` (the rule `insertAt` applies, `text.ts:645`), and a `\n` in `insert` is a paragraph break in the four multiline pointers and refused elsewhere by the validator after the write, as today.
- The reducer (`reduce.ts` `applyMutation`) implements both over `parseText`, `placeRuns` and `serializeRuns`; the inverse of a splice is a splice, the inverse of a mark is the mark that restores the previous marks of the range (computed from `marksOfRange`) or the previous characters for a case change.
- The editor's `textBurstMutation` emits `text.splice`; the `text.style`, `text.case` and `text.insert` actions emit `text.mark` and `text.splice`; `text.replaceAll` keeps `block.set` (a whole value). `text.replace` stays in `MUTATION_OPS` for old records, the replay path and agents that still send it, and is a whole value write at admission (2.5).
- Migration: none. Schema version stays 1; every stored record parses as before; `migrations.test.ts` keeps the GT deck and the templates unchanged.

### 2.2 The operation stream and the checkpointer

`packages/realtime` (new, framework free, no React, no `node:` import outside the Redis adapter) exports:

```ts
type Entry = {
  seq: number;
  rev: number;
  kind: 'edit' | 'comment';
  author: Author;
  clientId: string;
  opId: string;

  comment?: CommentOp;

};
type RealtimeChannel = {
  append(
    deckId: string,
    base: number,
    entries: Omit<Entry, 'seq'>[],

  since(deckId: string, seq: number, limit: number): Promise<Entry[]>;

  subscribe(deckId: string, onEvent: (event: RoomEvent) => void): () => void;

```

Implementations: `memory` (one process; `packages/realtime/src/memory.ts`), `redis` (`packages/realtime/src/redis.ts`, the Redis protocol over TCP, not the REST API, which cannot block on `XREAD`, 02 T8), `blob` (`packages/realtime/src/blob.ts`, the reduced tier over `BlobStore`). The Redis keys are built by one function from the slug validated deck id and a fixed suffix list (10 F34): `deck:<id>:ops` (a stream), `deck:<id>:head`, `deck:<id>:ckpt` (the lock), `deck:<id>:presence` (a hash), `deck:<id>:roster` (a sorted set by last heartbeat), `deck:<id>:client:<clientId>` (the session binding), `deck:<id>:streams:<identity>` (the stream count), `q:<identity>:<name>:<window>` (budgets), `access:<deckId>`, `head:<deckId>`, `principal:<id>`, `inbox:<principalId>`, `flag:<name>`.

The checkpointer (`apps/studio/src/server/checkpoint.ts`, reached from the ops route and from a scheduled tick) runs on the instance that admitted the latest operation when 2 s pass without an operation, every 10 s under continuous activity, or when the retained entries pass 1 MB, under the lock `deck:<id>:ckpt` (`SET NX PX 5000`, a random token, a heartbeat written into the value every second; a waiter breaks a lock whose heartbeat is older than 3 s, 10 F36). It reads the entries since the last checkpoint with `XRANGE`, coalesces them into one `Write` per author run (consecutive `text.splice` on one run into one splice, consecutive `block.set` of one pointer into the last value, a drag's `pos` sets into the final box), applies them through `BlobStore.write` with the record's `ops` range, announces `checkpoint { revision, fromSeq, toSeq, snapshot, author, note }` on the stream, writes the comment entries to the sidecar (08 2.4), and trims the stream to the larger of 10,000 entries or 24 hours and at most 16 MB (`XTRIM`). An agent write forces a checkpoint before it answers. Three active decks at 0.1 commits per second cost 1.5 advanced Blob operations per second, against the 75 per second cap (02 7.1).

### 2.3 The room protocol

Routes, all plain server routes (a WAF visible path each, the catch all function rule at 300 s and 2 GB, not the `/_serverFn/**` rule at 3,009 MB), all under the CSRF middleware whose filter widens from `serverFn` to these paths (10 F30), all requiring `Content-Type: application/json` on POST:

| Route                     | Method | Who                                                   | Purpose                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| ------------------------- | ------ | ----------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `/api/decks/:id/stream`   | GET    | the session cookie, or a bearer or API key for agents | Server-Sent Events: `hello`, `ops`, `op`, `checkpoint`, `presence`, `leave`, `reject`, `inbox`, `access`, a comment line every 15 s; `Last-Event-ID` resumes; closed by the server at a random point between 240 and 290 s with `retry` between 1,000 and 4,000 ms so tabs never reconnect together (10 F24) |
| `/api/decks/:id/ops`      | POST   | the session or the key                                | `{ clientId, base: { seq }, entries: [{ opId, kind, mutations                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                | comment }] }`, at most 64 entries and 256 kB (02 7.2) |
| `/api/decks/:id/presence` | POST   | the session or the key                                | `{ clientId, clock, slideId, selection, pointer, follow }`, one per 80 ms per client, coalesced server side                                                                                                                                                                                                  |

Messages down, each with an `id` the client echoes as `Last-Event-ID`:

- `hello { seq, revision, clientId, role, clients[] }`: the server issues `clientId` (128 random bits) and binds it to the session in Redis for 320 s, refreshed by presence (10 F26); `clients[]` is the roster filtered by role (2.8).
- `ops { entries[] }` since the client's position, at most 2,000 entries and 1 MB per open; a position older than the head minus 2,000 answers `resync { revision }` and the client reloads at that revision (10 F25).
- `op { seq, rev, kind, author, clientId, opId, mutations | comment }` for every admitted entry, the author's own included, so the author replaces its pending copy with the admitted form.
- `checkpoint { revision, fromSeq, toSeq, snapshot, author, note, comments: { revision, threadIds } }`.
- `presence { clientId, clock, state }` and `leave { clientId }`.
- `reject { opId, reason, mutations }` with `reason` from the fixed set `stale`, `invalid`, `forbidden`, `too-large`, `locked` (10 F29); the validator's pointer only for a slide the author may read.
- `inbox { unread }` for the principal's connections and `access { revision }` when the deck's record changed, so the tab re-reads its role.

Identifiers: `clientId` is server issued; `opId` is `<clientId>:<counter>`; dedup is scoped to `(deckId, clientId)` for the retention window. A POST whose `clientId` is not bound to the posting session is 403 and logged.

### 2.4 Admission and transformation

The ops route (`apps/studio/src/server/room.ts`), per POST:

1. `authorize(ctx, deckId, 'write')` for `kind: 'edit'`, `'comment'` for `kind: 'comment'` (4.8); the client binding check; the budgets (operations per second per client 60, per minute per identity 2,400 anonymous or 4,800 signed in, bytes per minute 2 MB or 8 MB, 16 MB per deck per minute; 10 F27).
2. `base.seq` more than 500 entries behind the head answers 409 `resync` (10 F28); otherwise the instance loads the document at the last checkpoint from its mirror and applies the entries since it (cached per instance keyed by `seq`, so a warm instance does this incrementally).
3. Each incoming mutation is transformed from `base.seq` to the head: `text.splice` and `text.mark` through `packages/schema/src/transform.ts` (the two primitive case of client server OT, insert and delete against insert and delete, ranges shifted and split; 02 3.3, E11), `after` anchors of `slide.insert`, `block.insert`, `slide.move` and `block.move` re-resolved (a removed anchor resolves to the end of the slot or section), everything else unchanged.
4. Each mutation applies through `applyMutation`; the touched slide is validated (`validateSlide`) and the deck level ops validate the manifest; the slide size cap (200 KB) and the deck cap (25 MB) are checked before the transform; a failure is a `reject` and never enters the stream.
5. The append is one Lua script: read `deck:<id>:head`; when it equals the head the instance transformed against, `XADD` the entries, increment the head, `PUBLISH`; otherwise return the new head and the count (never the entries, 10 F34) so the instance reads them with `XRANGE`, transforms once more and retries; after two misses the instance takes a 200 ms lock and retries under it.
6. Every instance subscribed to the deck's pub/sub channel writes the admitted entries into its open streams. Redis runs the script on one thread, so two instances cannot interleave appends.

The transform module has one property test: random concurrent splices, marks and case changes on the fixture deck's Texts converge on every client under every interleaving; the interleaving anomaly of concurrent inserts at one offset (02 E17) is accepted and documented.

### 2.5 Conflict rules per operation

| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |

The rule that spans the table: an operation the server admitted is never dropped afterwards, and an operation that cannot be placed is returned to its author with its content and a fixed reason, in the editor as an inline notice with a "Copy text" action and on the agent surface as a 409 body.

### 2.6 The client

The controller of `apps/studio/src/routes/edit.$deckId.tsx` gains a sync client (`apps/studio/src/sync/room-client.ts`, framework free, unit tested with a fake transport) that replaces the write queue and the long poll while keeping `commitAs`, the history and `setDocument`:

- Three sets: pending (sent or about to be sent, not yet in the stream), retained (acked at `seq`, not yet covered by a checkpoint), and the local document. A local gesture applies through `applyMutation`, records the undo entry with its inverse, and flushes at once for structural ops, coalesced at 50 ms during a drag (the `pos` sets) and at 100 ms while typing (one `text.splice` carries the characters since the last flush); the undo grouping keeps its own 400 ms rule (02 7.4).
- An incoming `op` from another client transforms the pending text ops against it, applies it, and re-renders the touched slides; a pending `block.set` of the same pointer stays as the local prediction, as Figma keeps unacknowledged changes (02 E1). The author's own admitted `op` drops the pending copy and adjusts the document by the difference when the server transformed it. A `checkpoint` moves the covered ops out of retained, appends the version to the History panel and moves `serverRevision`.
- Undo is per author and never reverts another author's later change (2.5, the conditional inverse); redo is the forward reapplication with the same check.
- Offline: the stream closes, the POSTs fail, the title row reads "Saving…" then "Couldn't save, retrying" with the pending count; the client retries with a capped backoff of 8 s; pending and retained ops are persisted to IndexedDB under `turboslide:pending:<deckId>` (at most 1 MB, 24 hours) so a closed tab's unsent edits are offered on the next open as "3 unsaved changes from this browser; Apply or Discard". `pagehide` with `pending > 0` shows the browser's leave warning (10 F36).
- Reconnect: `EventSource` reconnects with `Last-Event-ID`; the client resends pending ops with `base.seq`; a `resync` reloads the document at the named revision through `readEditorDeck`, then rebases pending ops (text transformed, other pointers as last writer, unplaceable ops shown with their content). Retained ops are resent only when a checkpoint confirms they were lost (2.7 sequence e).
- The conflict card of round one is retired for text and property conflicts. It stays for two cases: a `reject` (shown inline with the content) and a `version.restore` admitted while this tab holds pending ops on the restored slides (the card lists them with Reapply and Discard).
- `sync.status` (an action, 2.9) reads `{ seq, revision, pending, retained, tier, connected }` and `describe().state.sync` mirrors it for the window API.

### 2.7 Sequences

(a) A keystroke from tab A reaches tab B, both hosted, same region. A types "x" at plain offset 12 of `p1`; the reducer applies `text.splice { at: 12, remove: 0, insert: 'x' }` locally (under 1 ms); the flush at 100 ms POSTs one entry with `base.seq` 4,112 (one round trip, 20 to 40 ms US East); the instance transforms against nothing (the head is 4,112), validates the slide, runs the Lua script (a few ms), publishes; B's instance writes the `op` into B's stream (a few ms) and B applies it and re-renders `p1`. About 60 to 100 ms op to screen in `iad1`, 150 ms from the US West coast, 200 to 250 ms from Europe (02 7.10; typical round trips, not measured, to be measured in 11.1's preview run).

(b) Two people type in one paragraph. A inserts at 12 with base 4,112; B inserts at 8 with base 4,112. The server admits A first (seq 4,113) and transforms B's splice against it (offset 8 is before 12, unchanged; seq 4,114). B receives A's `op` before its own ack and transforms its pending splice (unchanged); A receives B's `op` and transforms its already admitted copy (offset 12 becomes 13). Both documents read the same string; no dialog, no lock, no lost character.

(c) An agent writes over `POST /api/actions/block.set` while two tabs edit. The action route resolves the API key to a principal and scopes, calls `authorize(write)`, dispatches; the store action's write is admitted through the room as author `{ kind: 'agent', name: <record name>, runId, principalId: 'agent:<tokenId>' }`, transformed against the head, appended, and the checkpointer is forced; the answer carries the new revision and the record as today. Both tabs receive the `op` within the stream's latency and the version appears in History at the checkpoint. The `baseRevision` conflict rule becomes "a slide you touched changed since your base" (a 409 with `since` and `current`); `strict: true` on the input keeps the exact revision check (02 7.9).

(d) The checkpoint. Two seconds after the last operation the admitting instance takes `deck:<id>:ckpt`, reads seq 4,100 to 4,140, coalesces A's 31 splices into one, B's 9 into one, applies two `Write`s through `BlobStore.write` (snapshot, `deck.json` with `ifMatch`, the changed slide, `versions/<n>.json` with `ops: { fromSeq: 4100, toSeq: 4140 }`), announces `checkpoint`, writes any comment entries to the sidecar, trims the stream, releases the lock. Ten advanced Blob operations for the two commits, about one every 20 s at typing speed, against 75 per second.

(e) Redis becomes unreachable. The ops POST fails with `redis.unavailable`; the room falls to the `blob` tier for the deck: writes go through `BlobStore.write` per 400 ms burst with a per deck admission queue of one write per second, the stream is replaced by a 1 s `head('deck.json')` poll, presence stops across instances and the title row says so. Clients keep every retained op until a `checkpoint` (now a revision) covers it; if the stream lost admitted ops, the next checkpoint's `toSeq` does not cover them and the client resends them against the Blob document, transformed against what landed. An accepted keystroke is never lost; only its position may move (02 7.1, 7.11 risk 2).

(f) A local checkout follows a hosted deck. `turboslide deck follow q4-review --from https://turboslide.vercel.app` opens the stream with the API key from `~/.config/turboslide/hosts.json` and `?since=<local revision>`, applies each `checkpoint`'s version record forward through the `FileStore` with the record's `createdAt` and the same reducer (the path `pull()` already replays, `blob-store.ts:340-360`), checks the result's `deck.json` md5 against the record's `snapshot`, and writes `decks/q4-review/`; the local editor on the dev server shows the same external revision banner it shows for any CLI write. `--push` sends the local records the host lacks as writes with their `baseRevision` under the per slide conflict rule; a conflict prints both sides and offers `--force` or `--theirs`. `--comments` mirrors the sidecar too (08 3.1).

(g) An MCP client subscribes. `resources/subscribe` on `deck://q4-review` (and `deck://q4-review/comments`, `deck://inbox`, `deck://q4-review/presence`); the server declares `resources: { subscribe: true, listChanged: true }` and sends `notifications/resources/updated` per checkpoint, per comment checkpoint and per inbox event (02 F7); over stdio on a checkout the notification comes from `fs.watch`. `deck.watch { since, timeoutMs }` is the polling form for a transport without a stream.

### 2.8 The presence channel

State per client: `clientId`, `clock` (increasing), `slideId`, `selection { blockIds (at most 64), caret?: { blockId, path, offset | range } }`, `pointer?: { x, y }` in sheet units inside the sheet, `follow?: clientId`, `pointerOn: boolean`, `presenting: boolean`. ## 4. Comments and share roles

### 4.1 Comments: the sidecar, the types, the anchors

Report 08 section 2 compared three homes and chose the sidecar; this proposal adopts it for the architectural reason 08 names first: a per slide `ext.comments` list would be one JSON pointer, and 2.5 makes concurrent writes to one pointer last writer wins, so two people commenting on one slide inside one checkpoint window would lose a comment. The types are `packages/schema/src/comments.ts` as 08 1.1 writes them: `Thread { id (a 26 character ULID), deckId, anchor, comment, replies[], resolved?, assignee?, createdAt, updatedAt, revision }`, `Comment { id, author: { principalId, label, kind }, createdAt, editedAt?, body: { text, mentions[] }, reactions?, deleted? }`, six anchor kinds (`deck`, `slide`, `block`, `text` with a plain offset range and a quoted string, `cell`, `notes`), `Mention` as a principal or an invitation id, bodies as plain text with `{@n}` tokens and never markup, reactions from a fixed palette of 24, caps of 4,000 code points per body, 20 mentions, 100 replies, 200 threads per slide, 5,000 per deck (08 1.1, 1.3). `resolveAnchor(document, anchor)` is pure and runs at read time; a removed block orphans its threads without deleting them and an undo revives them; text anchors shift through the same transform functions `text.splice` uses (2.4), emitted by the server as `comment.shift` entries on the stream after the text op that caused them, and by `FileStore.write` under its lock on a checkout (08 1.2).

### 4.2 Sync and conflict rules for comments

Comment operations travel on the stream as `kind: 'comment'` entries (`add`, `reply`, `edit`, `delete`, `resolve`, `reopen`, `assign`, `reassign`, `done`, `react`, `unreact`, `move`, `shift`) and reach every tab in the stream's latency; the checkpointer writes the sidecar (one `put` per changed thread, `authors.json` when a label is new, `index.json` with `ifMatch` last) and never a version record; `index.revision` is the comments' own counter (08 2.3, 2.4). Every op commutes or has one winner: add and reply append; edit is author only with the author's later arrival winning; delete is a tombstone that keeps the replies; resolve, reopen, assign and done are last writer wins; reactions are a set per emoji keyed by principal. No comment op is refused for a stale base; `edit` and `move` take `expectedUpdatedAt` and answer 409 when the thread moved (08 3.2). On the `blob` tier and on a checkout the sidecar is written directly with `ifMatch` or under the lock.

### 4.3 Mentions, notifications, the inbox, email

Mentions store principal ids or invitation ids; the autocomplete lists present people and past commenters to anyone who may comment, and grantees and raw email addresses to editors only, because naming a grantee reveals the share list and a raw email creates an invitation (08 4.1). The inbox is a per principal record shape `{ id, principalId, kind, deckId, threadId?, slideId?, actors[], count, createdAt, updatedAt, readAt? }` behind one `Inbox` interface in `packages/store`: Redis sorted set with a 30 day TTL for anonymous principals, the `inbox_notification` table for accounts, at most 500 records per principal (08 5.2; 10 F39). The stream carries `inbox { unread }` to the principal's connections. The title row's inbox plate opens the Notifications right panel; Tools > Notification settings and the panel's foot open the per deck dialog with Google's three levels (08 5.3, 5.4). Email goes only to signed in principals who hold a grant on the deck, through Resend, coalesced into 15 minute digests (2 minutes for a direct mention), capped at 10 mails per person per day, 20 mention mails per identity per day and 0 for anonymous identities, with `List-Unsubscribe` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click`; a mention of an address without a grant sends nothing and shows "Invite them from Share" (08 5.5; 10 F38).

### 4.5 Version history by author, Show changes, activity

The Versions panel groups records by day and inside a day by a 15 minute window, each window row carrying up to four author marks and a "+N", with named records standing alone (08 6.1). Show changes runs `version.diff { from, to }` (a data form of `diff.run` grouped by touched block and attributed per author) and hatches each changed block in the overlay with the author's 16 px chip; the stub retires (08 6.2; 11 6.9). ### 4.8 authorize() and the capability matrix by route

```ts
type Principal = { id: string; kind: 'anonymous' | 'account'; email?: string; admin: boolean };
type AuthContext = {
  principal: Principal | null;
  agent?: { tokenId; ownerId; scopes: Scope[]; name; runId? };
  linkGrants: { linkId; deckId; role }[];
  publishToken?: string;
};
type Capability =
  | 'read'
  | 'readSkipped'
  | 'readNotes'
  | 'comment'
  | 'write'
  | 'history'
  | 'export'
  | 'exportNotes'
  | 'share'
  | 'settings'
  | 'rename'
  | 'copy'
  | 'trash'
  | 'restore'
  | 'remove'
  | 'publish'
  | 'transfer'
  | 'presence'
  | 'follow';
type Decision =
  | { ok: true; role; via: 'owner' | 'grant' | 'link' | 'open' | 'publish' | 'admin' | 'agent' }
  | { ok: false; status: 401 | 403 | 404; code };
declare function authorize(
  ctx: AuthContext,
  deckId: string,
  capability: Capability,
): Promise<Decision>;
```

The matrix is 09 2.1 (owner everything; editor writes, comments, shares when allowed, history, exports with notes; commenter reads, comments, sees skipped slides, exports without notes when download is on; viewer reads and exports without notes when download is on; a link visitor its link's role; an agent its owner's role intersected with its scopes). Notes are read by editors and owners only, skipped slides by commenters and above (09 decision 6; a narrowing of 04 F21 for the sales workflow). The call sits first in every server function of `write.ts`, `decks.ts`, `download.ts`, `bundle.ts`, `render.ts`, `sessions.ts`, `agent-actions.ts`, in `handleActionRequest`, in the MCP tool wrapper, at the stream open (re-checked every 60 s and on every `access` event so a removed editor loses the stream within a second, 09 9), on the ops and presence routes, and on the assets route. The rules: deny by default; a lookup id that is guessed gives nothing (one 404 page for a missing and a restricted deck, 09 8.2); one refusal path; every denial logged. Status bodies: 401 `unauthorized`, 403 `{ error: 'forbidden', capability }`, 404 `not_found`, 410 for a revoked publish token, 409 with the current record for a stale `baseRevision`, 429 with `Retry-After`.

Per route (09 2.2): `/edit/:id` opens the editor in Viewing mode for a viewer (no Mode menu, a View only button at the toolbar's right end whose click runs `share.requestAccess` with role editor) and in Commenting mode for a commenter; the loader shapes the payload by role (no notes below editor, no skipped slides below commenter, no versions below editor); `/deck/:id` and `/embed/:id` answer the You need access page (HTTP 404, the request form) without a right; `/present/:id` strips notes below editor; `/print/:id` hides the notes layout and the download buttons by role; `/decks` reads the caller's index (4.11); the agent routes resolve the key record first; the thumbnail route takes a signed URL from the page (deck id, width, revision, role; 04 F6); the export and bundle routes check `export`, `exportNotes` and `write`, and the bundle tickets carry the minting identity so a ticket can no longer replace any deck (09 6.3). Menus hide rows a role cannot use (09 2.3).

### 4.9 Link sharing

Report 09 carries the token in `?k=`; report 10 F40 shows the query string leaks through Referer, history and third party scripts on an embedding page and proposes a one time exchange. This proposal takes the exchange for share links and the query for the published player:

- A share link is `https://<host>/s/<token>` (22 base64url characters, 132 bits, hashed at rest). `GET /s/:token` validates the hash against the deck's links, refuses a revoked or expired one with the 404 page, writes `{ linkId, deckId, role }` into the session's `linkGrants` (the principal record in Redis, or the identity session for an account, and for an account also `users/<id>/decks.json` `shared` with `via: 'link'` so Shared with me lists it and a revocation removes it), sets `Referrer-Policy: no-referrer` on its own response, and redirects to `/deck/<id>` for a viewer or commenter link and `/edit/<id>` for an editor link, with no token in the address. Copy link copies the `/s/` form. Rotation kills every copy in circulation (a new record); revocation is per link; expiry is optional (none, 7, 30, 90 days, a date).
- The published player is `/deck/<id>?p=<token>&present=1` and `/embed/<id>?p=<token>` (09 3.5): a cross site iframe carries no `SameSite=Lax` cookie, so the token must ride in the URL; it opens only present mode or the embed protocol, never the editor; every response carries `X-Robots-Tag: noindex`; `deck.unpublish` answers 410 afterwards. The gt-brand deck stays `open: viewer` for the Prototemplate embed until Kevin changes the iframe.
- `open` is the legacy mode shown as "Anyone with the address can view (legacy)" with a Switch to a link button (09 3.1).

## 5. Optional accounts

### 5.1 The anonymous principal

The first request without `__Host-ts_id` mints a v4 UUID (`crypto.randomUUID()`), seals it with h3's `seal` under `TURBOSLIDE_SESSION_SECRET` (`@tanstack/react-start/server` exports `sealSession`, `setCookie`; 03 A6), and sets it `Secure`, `HttpOnly`, `SameSite=Lax`, `Path=/`, `Max-Age` 400 days (03 B2). The principal id is `anon_<uuid>`. A principal record `principal:<id>` in Redis (a JSON file under `.turboslide/principals/` on a checkout) holds `{ label, name?, avatar, linkGrants[], notificationSettings, createdAt, lastSeenAt }` with a 90 day sliding TTL; the browser mirrors name and avatar in `localStorage` for instant paint and never as the truth. `Author` gains `principalId?: string`; the server derives every `Author` from the cookie or the token record and refuses a body `author` on the browser transports; `?author=` leaves `validateEditSearch`. Clearing cookies makes a new anonymous visitor, as Google's signed out person is a new animal (03 B2).

### 5.2 The display name prompt and the rules

The first edit, comment or presence join from a principal without a chosen name opens the one field dialog "How should others see you?" with the generated label prefilled, Continue and a Sign in link; declining keeps the label. `account.setName` runs the server rules of 10 F44 and 11 5.3: NFC, Cf and default ignorable code points stripped, an unterminated bidi isolate or override refused, whitespace collapsed, 1 to 40 code points with at least one letter or digit, UTS #39 Moderately Restrictive with the mixed number check, the reserved list by skeleton (`turboslide`, `admin`, `owner`, `agent`, `system`, `presenter`, `studio`, `anonymous`, `you`, `me`, `everyone`, any `agent:` prefix, every label word alone, the label grammar), per deck uniqueness by skeleton against the owner and every named collaborator in the roster and the version log (so a Cyrillic "Kevin Liu" is refused with "That name is already in use in this presentation"), and the LDNOOBW word list for the request's languages. Names are text everywhere: React text nodes, `textContent`, `title` attributes, JSON strings.

### 5.3 Sign in

better-auth 1.7.4 with the official TanStack Start integration (`routes/api/auth/$.ts` mounting `auth.handler`, `tanstackStartCookies` last; 03 C4), the Kysely adapter over Neon Postgres hosted and `node:sqlite` on a checkout (`TURBOSLIDE_AUTH_DB`), Upstash Redis as `secondaryStorage` for sessions, verification rows, challenges and the limiter, `advanced.ipAddress.ipAddressHeaders: ['x-forwarded-for']`, `secret` from `BETTER_AUTH_SECRET`. Methods, in the order the dialog offers them:

1. Email: one mail through Resend carrying a magic link (`storeToken: 'hashed'`, 300 s) and a six digit code (`allowedAttempts: 3`, 300 s, hashed), so the tab that asked can finish with the code when the mail opens on a phone (03 C2). The sending domain and address are Kevin's decision (03 J1 item 2).
2. Passkeys through the passkey plugin over SimpleWebAuthn, enabled only after the production domain is fixed, because the rpID binds every passkey to the host and `vercel.app` is on the Public Suffix List (03 C1). The dialog shows the row greyed with "Passkeys arrive once the address is final" until then.
3. GitHub through the GitHub provider when `GITHUB_CLIENT_ID` and `GITHUB_CLIENT_SECRET` are set, for engineers and agent operators (03 C3).

No passwords. The sign in dialog is one 400 by 320 box for every state (05 rule 4; 11 P7). Without `DATABASE_URL` the Sign in row is absent and the studio runs anonymous only; every collaboration feature of sections 2 to 4 still works with labels and link roles.

### 5.4 Sessions, linking, sign out, profile

Sign in sessions are better-auth's (7 days idle, refreshed daily, `ipAddress` and `userAgent` for the device list), in a cookie prefixed `__Host-` where the library permits it and `ts_` with the same attributes otherwise. Signing in links the anonymous principal to the account through the alias table (`alias { anonymousId, userId, linkedAt }`) in `onLinkAccount`; history is never rewritten and the anonymous id resolves to the account's profile from then on (03 E2); the anonymous index and inbox merge into the account's. Sign out ends the account session and keeps the anonymous cookie; "Forget this browser" replaces the anonymous cookie and the mirror. The profile dialog shows the name, the avatar builder, the email and trust state, the sessions list (browser, platform, coarse location, times, Sign out per row, Sign out everywhere else), the agent tokens list and "Delete account" with `beforeDelete` refusing while the person owns decks other people hold grants on (03 F, G). Adding a passkey, linking GitHub or changing the email needs a fresh session.

### 5.5 Agent tokens and the CLI login

Tokens are records of better-auth's API key plugin: `ts_` prefix, 32 random bytes, SHA-256 at rest, `permissions` as the scopes `read`, `comment`, `write`, `export`, `share`, `admin`, a per key rate limit, `lastUsed`; `Authorization: Bearer ts_...` resolves the record and its owner; `TURBOSLIDE_TOKEN` stays valid for `admin.bootstrap { email }` (which sets the admin role and mints the first key) and for nothing else once a key exists (09 6.1; 04 F12). `turboslide login --to <url>` runs the device authorization flow (an 8 character code at `/device`, five attempts, 30 minutes) and stores an API key in `hosts.json` with `kind: 'api-key'`; `turboslide logout` revokes it (09 6.2). The deployment admin is `TURBOSLIDE_ADMIN_EMAILS` or a bootstrapped account.

### 5.6 The avatar builder

One dialog, 560 by 640, four tabs in the order a person wants them: Initials (the letters retyped, one of four Bayer densities from the id), Glyph (a mirrored 5 by 5 field of five theme glyphs with "Another" rerolling a stored salt), Dither (a radial or angled ramp from the hash), Picture (a square crop dragged in the browser, resized to at most 1024 px client side, sent to `account.setAvatar`, sniffed and re-encoded by sharp at 32, 64, 128 and 256 px WebP plus one 256 px PNG, `animated: false`, metadata stripped, stored under `u/<avatarKey>/<sha256>-<size>.webp` on the public store with the key rotated and the old prefix deleted on every change; SVG refused; the original never kept) (03 I2 to I4; 10 F43). Every tab previews at 24, 32, 64 and 256 px in both themes in fixed boxes. The choice is a small JSON document on the principal record, so `account.setAvatar` sets it from the CLI too.

## 8. Background pictures with dithers and shader backgrounds

### 8.1 The field

`PictureDither` joins `pictureToolFields` in `packages/schema/src/blocks.ts`, so `picture` and `shot` take it (06 4.1):

```ts
export type PictureDither = {
  pattern: 'bayer8' | 'bayer4' | 'blue64' | 'random';
  tone?: 'two' | 'three' | 'original';
  steps?: number;
  cell?: 1 | 2 | 3 | 4;
  strength?: number;
  black?: number;
  white?: number;
  gamma?: number;
  invert?: boolean;
  polarity?: 'auto' | 'dark-ground' | 'light-ground' | 'same';
  blur?: number;
  minFilter?: number;
  channel?: 'gray' | 'r' | 'g' | 'b';
  seed?: number;
  preset?: 'neutral' | 'photograph';
};
```

The toggle is the field's presence: on writes `{ pattern: 'bayer8' }`, off removes it; `block.resetImage` removes it with `trim`, `mask` and `adjust`. The validator caps `blur` at 8 and `minFilter` at 9 (both cost time in the radius), `steps` 2 to 7, `seed` a 32 bit integer; a dither over an asset that carries a two tone `treatment` and no `sourceFile` is refused with "the asset has no continuous source (sourceFile); the committed twins are already dithered". The asset level `treatment` and `asset.dither` stay for the GT deck's 16 committed twins and the CLI's batch regeneration; a block level dither never rewrites an asset. `preset` is a convenience the schema records so the panel can show which row is active; it writes the numeric fields (8.9).

`Asset.variants?: Record<string, AssetVariant>` records materialized files: `{ key: sha256 of (source digest, resolved dither, cell, screen size), twins, size, scale: 1 | 2, metrics?, producedAt }` (06 4.5).

### 8.2 The pipeline and the three hosts

`ditherPicture(source, box, view, dither, theme, adjust?)` in `packages/effects/src/dither.ts` (browser safe, no encoder import): cover fit of the trimmed region to the screen size (box over cell) through the pinned Lanczos3 after gray or one channel, invert, minFilter and blur (the cached `toneBase`); autocontrast 0.5 then the tone LUT (black, white, gamma) with `block.adjust`'s brightness and contrast folded in; the threshold against `bayer8` (the deck's table), `bayer4`, `blue64` (a CC0 64 by 64 texture pinned by sha256) or `random` (a hash of x, y and seed); polarity by the lit fraction; paint in the theme's ink and paper, three tones with titanium in the middle, or the posterised colours; nearest upscale by `cell`; `strength` under 1 composites the plane over the continuous picture (06 4.2). The same TypeScript stages run in `apps/studio/src/workers/dither.worker.ts` for the live preview, in the sheet runtime (`packages/render/src/dither-runtime.ts`) and in Node for materialization and the Editable text raster; the Rust crate gains the stages behind `EffectsBackend` as an optional accelerator; a test asserts equal bits across the worker harness and Node. The preview budget is under 100 ms at the 95th percentile from a slider event to `drawImage` because the fit is cached and a slider re-runs only the LUT, the threshold and the paint over 360,000 cells; requests carry an id and the worker drops stale ones; the store sees one write per drag (`block.set /dither` on pointer up) (06 4.3).

### 8.3 The renderer path

`renderPicture` (`packages/render/src/blocks/picture.ts`) writes `data-dither`, `data-dither-key` and `data-dither-state` on the picture root; when `deck.assets[asset].variants[key]` lists files for the theme, `src` and the twin attributes point at the variant and the state is `variant` (no script needed: the standalone build, the CLI render, the PDF page and the Perfect raster read it); when no variant exists the image shows the continuous twin, the state is `live`, and the runtime computes the effect in the worker and draws it on `<canvas class="picture-dither">` at `position: absolute; inset: 0; image-rendering: pixelated`, sized to the screen so a 2x device shows exact cells (06 4.4). The overlay is absolutely positioned inside the picture's box, so toggling the dither or swapping a variant for a live frame moves nothing (06 6.5).

### 8.4 Variants and the export paths

Variants are `assets/<asset>.dither-<key12>-light.png`, `-dark.png` (or one neutral file for `same`), 1 bit or palette PNGs, about 4 KB at cell 2 and 16 KB at scale 2, written by `picture.materialize` in one `asset.set` through `putAsset` (8.10) and pruned when no picture references the key (06 4.5, 6.1). Perfect PPTX: the exporter materializes every referenced variant before the shoot, every page is shot in state `variant`, a two tone background page encodes as a 1 bit PNG at both scales with crisp 4 by 4 cells at 2x, the residual lists `dither: <slideId>#<blockId> <key12> <state>` and `perfect` stays at or under 0.001. Editable text PPTX: a covering picture at the bottom of the stack travels as `slide.background = { data }` with the variant's bytes, a picture at its box as a picture; `strength` under 1 composites in Node with sharp. ### 8.10 Hosted asset writes and large uploads

Two defects make the feature impossible hosted today and are closed first (10 F49, F51). One: `asset.add`, `asset.dither` and `material.capture` write through the plain `FileStore` of the instance's mirror (`apps/studio/src/server/actions.ts:567`), so the twin bytes and the `asset.set` record never reach the Blob store, the picture shows on that instance only and the version chain is missing a record; the fix is `DeckStore.putAsset(relative, bytes, contentType)` and `removeAsset(relative)` on the contract (a local write on `file` and `tmp`, a `put` under the deck's public prefix on `blob`), `registerAssetActionsLazily` receiving the hosted store so `commitAssets` commits through the Blob protocol, `writeUnder` in the intake and the capture replaced by `putAsset` before the record commits, and digest named twins, sources and frames with `allowOverwrite: false` on the public store (the CDN caches a public blob for a month, so a regenerated twin under the same name is stale for a month, 10 F50). The settling test stages two fake instances in `hosted.test.ts` and asserts the twin is on the store and served on the second. Two: a data URL over 3.3 MB exceeds the 4.5 MB function body; above 3 MB the editor calls `uploadPresigned` to `uploads/<principalId>/<uuid>` on the private store with a 10 minute token that carries the identity's size cap and the four content types, then `asset.add { upload }` reads the object by pathname, sniffs, decodes, re-encodes and puts the twins; the object is deleted after processing and swept after 24 hours (10 5.3). ## 13. Risks

1. Instance spread and the checkpointer's locality. Vercel decides which warm instance takes a POST; the design assumes nothing about locality and pays one Redis round trip per operation plus an incremental mirror apply per instance; 11.1's preview run measures admission under 10 ms and the descriptor count per stream (02 7.11 risks 1 and 4).
2. Stream loss at Redis. Eviction stays off, the stream is trimmed after every checkpoint and bounded at 16 MB, the two level acknowledgement keeps every accepted keystroke, and the `blob` tier is the automatic fallback; the exposure is the checkpoint cadence, at most 10 s of admitted operations (02 7.11 risk 2; 10 F35).
3. Transform correctness. The two primitive OT case is small and property tested; the interleaving anomaly is accepted; everything outside text is last writer wins or re-anchoring. A hidden non convergence would show as two tabs disagreeing, which the realtime spec asserts against.
4. Redis cost as an attack. The fixed plan has unlimited commands; the per identity budgets, the rate limits and the alert on `redisCmds` are the defence; Upstash's bill is outside Vercel's spend cap, which is why the fixed plan is the default (10 F33).
5. The storage migration. Copying every document to a private store while the deployment serves traffic is the round's one irreversible step after cutover; the dual read window, the etag verification per document and the batch deletes off the request path bound it, and `deck pull` and `deck push` remain the fallback (9.5).
6. Roles without private documents. Until the cutover, a restricted deck's document is readable from the public store by anyone who knows the store hostname (04 F8); R5 orders the cutover before enforcement is announced to the team, and the interim exposure is today's.
7. The passkey domain. Passkeys made on `turboslide.vercel.app` die with a move to a custom domain (03 C1); the design gates them on Kevin's domain decision and keeps email as the recovery path.
8. Email deliverability and abuse. Mentions and invitations send from General Translation's domain; the caps, the grant requirement, the digests and `List-Unsubscribe` are the defence; a sending domain and Resend account are Kevin's to provide (03 J1; 10 F38).
9. The 3 MB data URL ceiling and the presigned path. The presigned route depends on Vercel Blob's `handleUploadPresigned` behaving as documented and on the private store; the e2e on a preview with a 6 MB picture proves it, and the 3 MB path stays as the fallback (10 F51).
10. Layout shift zero. A zero tolerance gate on a matrix of about 300 cells is brittle under a loaded machine; the gate runs against the production preview, not the dev server, and any cell that a fix cannot remove needs a declared exception with its reason in the script (05 5.3).
11. Scope. Six builders, about 64 actions, four new packages or modules, two Marketplace installs and a storage migration in nine days is the largest round yet; the day one seams and the rollout order keep the pieces landing independently, and R7 (dithers) and R6 (sign in) can ship after R5 without blocking each other.
12. Google facts built on a reading. The filmstrip marker's drawing, the pointer's colour rule, the anonymous animal's stability, the comment placeholders, the requester's page, the owner's default notification level and the version grouping interval are single source or unverified (01 Unverified; 08 12.1); the design follows the reports' reading and the verifier lists each for Kevin.

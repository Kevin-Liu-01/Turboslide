Written 2026-09-13 against `main` at 28cb63b (the working tree of that day; the brief names 61b16e4 and none of the files this verdict reads differ between the two). The winner is proposal 1. Its mark is the product's own composition (the opener with its plate cut from the picture, the composition `plateClear` measures), it is one construction at every size from one generator, every raster it ships holds exactly two colours, it changes no `--pt-` token and adds no colour, and its home page is the most complete. It wins by one point over proposal 3, whose tile is the most legible 16 px icon of the three and whose identity is the most disciplined, and by three over proposal 2, whose build pipeline is the best engineered but whose mark fails at 16 px and whose accent and live shader break the round's monochrome and speed directives.

Two of proposal 1's decisions do not survive this review and are replaced by grafts: the hero that cuts between three frames every four seconds becomes proposal 3's still hero, and the ICO's 32 and 48 px entries lose their 2 px cells and stay solid, as proposal 3 draws them. The engineering pattern of proposal 2 (one geometry module in the theme package feeding the React mark, the build script and the CLI banner, with a manifest of hashes for `--check`) is grafted whole.

The performance plan's line citations hold (every one I checked names the code it says it names), its ranking follows the baseline, and its check script exists and ran. It has one wrong assumption about the code (the CSRF middleware covers server functions only, not a new server route), one correctness gap (slide deletions cannot move ahead of the commit), one undeclared dependency (`@vercel/functions`), one indexing regression (`/edit/$deckId` becomes server rendered with the whole deck and speaker notes in the HTML while `NOINDEX_ROUTES` does not list it), and several missing budgets (page weight, the icon set's CDN hits, cold start, the per deck social image, the fifth function directory). ## 2 Proposal 1, the dither is the identity

### 2.1 What holds up under the engineer's lens

- One construction. `dither/mark.mjs` imports `bayer8` from `packages/effects/src/bayer.ts` through Node's type stripping, computes `markBits(N)` for any multiple of 8, and every SVG, raster, the ICO, the manifest icons and the CLI banner come from that one function. At N = 8 the field collapses to one even odd path (`M0 0h16v16H0z M2 8h8v6H2z`), so the favicon, the title row mark and the terminal glyph are the same 16 unit drawing. `scripts/build-icons.ts` can keep this import unchanged, because the CLI already runs `.ts` source through type stripping (AGENTS.md).
- Every raster is two colours. Measured: `mark-16-paper.png` 208 ink and 48 paper pixels; the 32, 64, 180 and 512 px previews two colours each; `favicon-16.png`, `-32`, `-48` titanium on transparent; `icon-192.png`, `icon-512.png`, `apple-touch-icon.png`, `icon-mask-512.png` two colours each; the ICO decodes to three entries at 16, 32 and 48 with two colours each and 15,086 bytes, as the proposal states.
- The hero twins are encoded with the theme's exact colours through the pipeline's encoder: `hero-1-dark.png` is `#070707` and `#f2f2f0`, `hero-1-light.png` is `#ffffff` and `#070707`, and the pixel counts are the exact inverse (1,007,172 and 432,828 both ways), which is the check the proposal's section 7.3 asks for. The lit fractions 0.3006, 0.3075 and 0.2917 sit inside the deck's range.
- The pixel grid arithmetic is right at the two sizes the chrome uses. The title row mark at 24 px is the 16 unit path at 1.5 units per pixel: 2 becomes 3, 8 becomes 12, 10 becomes 15, 14 becomes 21, all integers, so the window's edges land on device pixels without hinting. The app bar's 16 px mark is 1:1. Proposal 1 is the only one of the three whose chrome sizes need no hand hint.
- Tokens: ten `--ts-` tokens in `apps/studio/src/styles.css`, where research-4/01 item 24 puts them; no `--pt-` change; no colour; `turboslide lint --chrome` passes by construction because every computed colour resolves inside the `--pt-` set. The blast radius over the chrome is `TitleRow.tsx` line 273, `decks.index.tsx` lines 530 to 537, `print.$deckId.tsx` line 175, `__root.tsx` head and `NotFound`, `PORTED_FROM.json`, `copy.ts` `PROPER_NOUNS`, which is the minimum any proposal needs.
- The home mock is complete: 81,627 bytes, the real `tokens.css` by relative path, all fifteen README screenshots (sixteen references, one shot used twice), 10,423 px tall at 1440, light and 390 twins present, no horizontal overflow at 390. The hero cut script (lines 443 to 448) checks `prefers-reduced-motion` and `document.hidden` as the proposal says.
- The Open Graph card is the mark's composition at card scale (the plate cut lower left from a 10 px cell screen, 22.2 percent lit), rendered by Chromium from `og.html`; the 552 px feed card keeps 4.6 px cells, above report 02's 2 px floor. It is the strongest of the three cards because the card and the mark are one drawing.
- The stacked README lockup (`lockup-stacked-ink.png`) and the horizontal wordmark (the 24 cell mark at the cap height of Inter 500 at 66 px, measured 287.06 px in Chromium) are correct lockups; the word stays live text in the product.

### 2.2 Defects and risks

1. The ICO's 32 and 48 px entries carry 2 px cells. Chrome takes `icon.svg` when the ICO link carries `sizes="32x32"` (report 02 T1), Firefox honours the SVG's media block and Safari 26 renders SVG favicons, so the ICO serves Windows surfaces (the taskbar, pinned sites, older Edge) where a 32 px entry is drawn at 16 CSS px on a 200 percent display and its 2 px cells become 1 px noise around the window. Report 02 section 5.4 measured that case at "about two thirds of the structure" and "looks like noise". The 32 and 48 px entries should be the solid form; cells belong at 48 px in the lockups and from 64 px in the icons, which is proposal 3's rule. One line in `build-assets.mjs`.
2. The hero cuts between three frames every four seconds. It respects reduced motion and pauses hidden, but a full bleed picture that changes behind the hero copy every four seconds is the one moving thing on a page whose proposal says "nothing on /home animates on load", and the numbers strip and the buttons sit at its edge. Kevin's avoid list rules out smooth scroll and eyebrows because they draw the eye away from the sentence; a hard cut does the same. The still first frame (proposal 3's decision 7) is the safer default, with the cut as an option Kevin can switch on. With the cut gone the two new duration tokens (`--ts-hero-hold`, `--ts-dur-cut`) go too, and the six `--pt-dur-*` values stay the whole motion vocabulary, as the tokens file says they are.
3. Two factual errors in the proposal's text. Section 4.3 says report 05's icons `i-bars-3-bottom-left`, `i-table-cells` and `i-check-badge` "are not in `sprite-ids.json`" and swaps them for `i-list-bullet`, `i-squares-2x2` and `i-check-circle`; all six ids are in `packages/theme/assets/sprite-ids.json`, so the swap rests on a wrong premise and report 05's choices (a table cells glyph for Tables, a check badge for Quality gates) fit better. Section 7.1 names `apps/cli/src/commands/version.ts` as the banner's home; that file is the `version save`, `version list` and `version restore` command over named versions, and `grep` finds no `--version` flag in `apps/cli/src/cli.ts` or `main.ts`, so the banner is a new flag in `cli.ts`, as proposal 3 says.
4. Section 5 item 1 cites `menus/model.ts` line 471 for the tooltip; the `title.appIcon` item with "Turboslide home" and "Every presentation on this Turboslide" is at lines 663 to 666. The content is right, the line is not.
5. `mark.svg` at 64 cells is 38,590 bytes of 4,000 `<rect>` elements. It is a source file, not a served one, and `TurboslideMark.tsx` draws from `markBits(N)` at runtime (576 rects at N = 24), so nothing ships it; the build script should still emit the large marks as one path (a run length join of adjacent cells) so the README and the 512 icon source stay small.
6. The 16 px form is the least distinctive of the three at a glance: a filled square with a rectangular window reads as a window, a checkbox or a chat glyph before it reads as a slide with a plate. The tab strip mock shows it identifiable beside grey squares, and the titanium base reads on both strips (3.25:1 on white, 3.71:1 and 4.95:1 on Chrome's dark strips, 2.48:1 on the light inactive strip, all as the proposal computes). The form earns its place at 48 px and above, where the dither makes it the product; at 16 px it is legible and plain.
7. The progress bar's ramp edge and the anonymous avatar squares put dither on chrome surfaces. Research-4/01 section 6.7 allows both; proposal 3 declines both on the ground that a progress bar is chrome. As the builder I would ship the progress ramp behind a token so the perf builder's curtain of plan section 3.5 can use it, and leave the avatar squares until the round three presence chips need a fallback glyph.

### 2.3 Scores

Distinctiveness 6 (the mark is the product at 48 px and above; plain at 16). Legibility at 16 px 7 (two colours, 2 px features, one path, reads on both strips; the ICO cells at 32 and 48 are a defect to fix). Fit with Prototemplate 8 (monochrome, square, the notch as construction, ten typographic and layout tokens in the right file; two new durations against the six). Technical and agent signal 8 (the mark explains `plateClear`, the recipe sidecar, the banner from the bitmap, the honest speed rows). Home page 8 (the most complete mock; the cut is the one wrong decision). Buildability 8 (one generator, integer grids at 16 and 24, no hand hinting, no new dependency, two lines to fix). Total 45.

## 3 Proposal 2, the shader is the identity

### 3.1 What holds up

- The build pipeline is the best engineered of the three. `tools/make-mark.mjs` `geometry(S)` computes stroke, bar, cell, pad, frame width and height per raster size with the cell grid snapped to the frame, and `mark-geometry.json` records the result and the colour count at every size (all two). `brand-manifest.json` records bytes and sha256 per icon, the shape a `build-brand --check` needs. The wordmark's outlines come from `InterVariable.woff2` through fontTools with Inter's own pair kerning applied (T to u at minus 197 units), so the README and npm lockups are the word the browser sets. `dither-record.json` names the backend that cut the hero (`native`, 94 ms), which is the only proposal that ran the napi addon.
- The home page's composition is the strongest: the hero is a deck opener (the twin under an opaque plate lower left at 740 px, the lockup as the `h1`), every number carries a source line in monospace, and the pictures band shows the pipeline as three frames (the captured frame, the screen, the live state) followed by three interface specimens (the empty state, the loading curtain with the ramp as progress texture, the anonymous authors). No other mock shows the identity in the interface this concretely.
- The favicon set is complete with two additions (dark 192 and 512 icons for the README's `<picture>`), the ICO decodes to three two colour entries, and `icon.svg` swaps plate and ink by scheme on an opaque plate, which is report 02's decision.
- The chrome integration is specified with the right lines (`TitleRow.tsx` 273, `decks.index.tsx` app bar, `print.$deckId.tsx` 175, `server.ts` 35 and 108) and the `markGeometry(size)` module in `packages/theme/src/brand.ts` with a parity test against `brand.css`, the pattern `tokens.ts` and `tokens.test.ts` already follow.

### 3.2 Defects and risks

1. The mark does not read at 16 px. The drawing is a 14 by 8 frame at 1 px with a 2 px bar and stem inside a 16 px plate: 8 of 16 rows are empty, and what remains is two small boxes side by side. On the proposal's own confusion sheet at 16 px it is the smallest and least identifiable form, and the proposal names the risk itself (risk 2: "may read as a two pane window or a media player"). The crossbar of the T is the frame's top edge thickened from 1 to 2 px, which the eye reads as a heavier frame side, not as a letter. Research-4/01 candidate A's risk ("leaves 7 of 16 rows empty") was known and the stacked variant was tried and rejected; the wide frame on the plate was kept without solving it.
2. The accent. `--ts-accent: #2f5ce0` inside material frames requires `turboslide lint --chrome` to gain an exclusion for `.ts-material` roots, puts a hue on `/home` that Prototemplate's directive keeps out of the shell, and reuses the colour the customer's `brand-blue` preset owns on the sheet (proposal 3's argument). The proposal makes the fallback one line (`ink-paper` in place of `brand-blue`), which is the right engineering, and the decision is Kevin's; as the builder I would ship the fallback and keep the accent behind the decision.
3. The hero twins are pure black and white, not the theme's colours. `hero-dark.png`, `hero-light.png`, `hero-screen-dark.png`, `figure-dark.png` and `og-band-dark.png` are 1-bit PNGs of `#000000` and `#ffffff` (Pillow mode `1`), while the page ground is `#070707` and the page ink `#f2f2f0`. Sampling the top 700 px of `previews/home.png` finds `(0, 0, 0)` on 1,470 sample points against `(7, 7, 7)` on 652 and `(255, 255, 255)` on 315: the hero band is a different black from the page, and the white cells are a different white from the plate's text. The seam is at the band's bottom edge and around the plate. Proposal 1 and proposal 3 encode with `encodePng1`'s palette and the theme's exact values. The fix is one option on the encoder call; the twins must be re-cut before they ship.
4. The mock uses eleven of the fifteen README screenshots (`01`, `02`, `04`, `06`, `08`, `09`, `10`, `11`, `12`, `13`, `14`); `03`, `05`, `07` and `15` are listed as "alternates for the builder". The round's brief asks for all fifteen on the page; the other two mocks place them.
5. The live `ShaderMount` on `/home` conflicts with the performance plan's `/home` budget. Plan section 8.1 caps `/home` at 600 KB of decoded JavaScript and section 7 sizes the Paper Shaders mount chunk at 250 to 300 KB decoded; the check reads every same origin script from Resource Timing, so a mount fetched on an idle callback after LCP is counted when it lands inside the measurement window, and `/home` fails its own ceiling. The proposal's risk 4 says `/home` "will not be light until the perf builder's split lands"; the live mount should wait for that split and for a second measurement, and the still twin is the hero this round.
6. The hero anchor puts 9,846 lit cells under the plate box (`dither-record.json` `litUnder`). The plate is opaque, so nothing shows, but the deck's own rule is zero and the proposal defers the anchor scan to the builder (acceptance 7.6.14). The scan should run before the mock's frame is called final.
7. The ICO's 32 and 48 px entries add 2 px cells to the right panel, the same HiDPI noise risk as proposal 1's item 1.
8. `geometry(S)` at the print bar's 25 px: stroke 1, pad `round(25 / 16)` = 2, interior `floor((25 - 4 - 2 - 2) / 2)` = 8, snapped to the 2 px cell 8, frame 20 wide, height `round(11.25) - 2 - 1` = 8 snapped to 8, so the drawing is 20 by 11 at (2, 7): valid, but the proposal's table stops at 16, 32, 48, 64, 180, 192 and 512, and the chrome sizes it names (32 in the title row, 32 in the app bar, 25 in the print bar) are not all in the table. The function handles them; the table should list them so the verifier has the expected pixels.

### 3.3 Scores

Distinctiveness 7 (the ramp panel is a true product motif and the frame is a slide; the whole reads as a split pane). Legibility at 16 px 5 (8 of 16 rows empty, two boxes). Fit with Prototemplate 6 (an accent and a lint exclusion, twelve tokens in a new chrome sheet, twins in the wrong black). Technical and agent signal 9 (the geometry module, the hash manifest, the recorded backend, the pipeline band, the kerned outlines). Home page 8 (the best composition, eleven of fifteen shots, the seam). Buildability 7 (the pipeline is ready; the accent, the live mount, the twin encoding and the anchor scan are open before it ships). Total 42.

## 4 Proposal 3, type first

### 4.1 What holds up

- The tile is the most legible 16 px icon of the three: a 10 by 12 unit T at 2 px strokes on an opaque plate with a 1 px frame in the edge composite (`#656565` on paper, `#888887` on ink). Measured: 16, 32 and 48 px rasters and the ICO's three entries hold exactly three colours (plate, frame, ink), the frame rectangle sits at 0.5 offsets with `crispEdges`, and `tab-strip.png` and `tab-strip-1x.png` show it reading on the light strip, the dark strip through the media block, and the dark strip with Safari's base colours. The 32 px ICO entry is solid, so a 200 percent display gets no cell noise.
- The identity changes nothing it does not have to: no accent, no `--pt-` change, the tile's frame is the line law's frame role, Inter is the mark because the product is Inter, the progress bar and the avatars stay chrome, and the hero is still by decision. The `--ts-` tokens live in `apps/studio/src/styles.css` with a data twin in `packages/theme/brand/tokens.ts` for the icon build and the OG template.
- The hero is the lightest of the three: 3,715 bytes dark and 4,315 bytes light for a 1600 by 900 twin in the theme's exact colours (16.5 percent lit), reused as the empty state figure by `background-position`, no script beyond the theme boot and the appearance control, `scroll-behavior: auto` set explicitly, `hero-report.json` recording the recipe, the renderer, the backend (`typescript`, 179.6 ms for the whole stage set) and the plate metric.
- The wordmarks are measured, not drawn: Inter Display Medium instantiated with fontTools at `opsz` 32 and `wght` 500, the T's stem and arm scanned (0.150 and 0.131 of the cap), the kerned glyph positions read from Chromium, so the horizontal, stacked and display SVGs are the word the browser sets. The display wordmark's own T carrying the cut is the one image the identity needs when Inter cannot be assumed, and it is the best of the three Open Graph cards for a reader who has never seen the product.
- The rejected variants (the 16:9 counter in the o, the doubled line T) are rendered and the reasons are measured (the doubled lines close into one grey stroke at 1x), which is the evidence standard the round asks for.
- The mock uses all fifteen screenshots (the shell card pairs `02` with `05`, the viewer card pairs `14` with `15`), the appearance control is a `role="group"` with `aria-pressed`, and the 390 px shot has no overflow.

### 4.2 Defects and risks

1. A letter on a card is the most common shape of software mark, and the T is the letter of Telegram, Trello, Typeform and Tumblr among others. The dithered foot is the distinctive element and it appears from 64 px; at 16, 20, 24, 32 and 48 px, which are every size the chrome uses, the mark is a T in a box. It also puts a second letter mark beside the GT letter mark on every sheet.
2. The 16 unit hint does not land on the chrome's two sizes. At 20 px a unit is 1.25 px and at 24 px 1.5 px; the proposal knows this, asks the brand builder to hint 20 and 24 by hand (crossbar 12 by 2 at y 3 and stem 2 by 12 at 20; 14 by 3 and 3 by 15 at 24) and shows the unhinted worst case in `chrome-mocks.png`. That is two more drawings to maintain, at 1x and 2x each, where proposal 1's 24 px mark is the 16 unit path at exactly 1.5 units per pixel with integer edges and needs none.
3. The outline rasters are anti aliased: `mark-180-paper.png` holds 12 colours, `icon-192.png` and `icon-512.png` 13, `icon-mask-512.png` 9. That is correct for a glyph outline at those sizes, but research-4/01 acceptance item 5 and report 02's hosted smoke row ("for the PNGs, that they contain two colours") must be amended for every icon, not only for the three colour tile at 16.
4. Three colours at 16 px deviate from research-4/01 item 5 by design; the proposal records the deviation and the reason (the frame is what reads on a same coloured strip). The verifier's count changes from two to three for the tile. Acceptable, recorded.
5. The hero's twin is calm to the point of empty: 16.5 percent lit, most of the band is ink with a gradient in the lower right corner, and at 1440 the picture reads as a corner texture behind a paper plate. It is the least "awesome bayer dither" of the three heroes against Kevin's directive (b), and the page's dithers band is the calmest. At 390 px the 36 px `h1` wraps to seven lines and pushes the buttons below the fold.
6. The numbers strip carries no source lines (proposal 2's does); the speed rows do, so acceptance item 10 of research-4/01 holds for the rows and not for the strip.
7. Seventeen tokens is the widest ladder of the three (sizes with line heights, two trackings, the twin, the cell, the page, the measure, the gutter). All typographic and none a colour, so the parity audit is untouched; the count is a maintenance cost, not a risk.

### 4.3 Scores

Distinctiveness 5 (a T in a box; the cut appears only from 64 px). Legibility at 16 px 9 (the best of the three, measured on both strips at 1x and 2x, solid ICO entries). Fit with Prototemplate 9 (nothing added that the shell does not already do; the tile is the frame role). Technical and agent signal 6 (the cut and the banner say dither; the mark says letter; the calmest page). Home page 7 (complete, honest, readable; the least striking hero; the 390 hero too tall). Buildability 8 (the lightest runtime footprint, the simplest set; two hand hints and an amended colour count). Total 44.

## 5 Cross cutting findings

### 5.1 The favicon pipeline

All three ICO files decode in Pillow to three BMP entries at 16, 32 and 48 with the expected colours, and all three `icon.svg` files carry the `prefers-color-scheme` block inside the SVG with no `media` attribute on the link, which is report 02's decision and Firefox's constraint. The differences that matter to the engineer:

- Proposal 1's base is titanium on transparent (reads on both strips where the block is ignored, 2.48:1 on Chrome's light inactive strip); proposals 2 and 3 use an opaque paper plate (reads everywhere, a light tile on a dark strip in Safari's base rendering). Either is defensible; the six tab screenshots of acceptance item 6 decide.
- Proposals 1 and 2 put 2 px cells in the 32 and 48 px ICO entries; proposal 3 keeps them solid. The solid entries are right for the reason in section 2.2 item 1.
- Proposal 3 omits the `monochrome` manifest icon; proposals 1 and 2 include it. Report 02 leaves it to Kevin; it costs nothing to keep.

### 5.2 The Open Graph pipeline

All three render the 1200 by 630 card from an HTML template in Chrome for Testing, as report 02 section 6.4 asks, with the dither band at 10, 8 and 12 px cells respectively, all above the 2 px floor at a 360 px unfurl. Proposal 2's band shares the twins' wrong black. The per deck card of report 02 section 7 is adopted by all three and built by none; it is a Chromium render per request and needs a cache header and a budget (section 7.4 item 5).

### 5.3 Token blast radius over the chrome and the parity audit

- Proposal 1: ten `--ts-` tokens in `apps/studio/src/styles.css`; two are durations. `turboslide lint --chrome` and `PORTED_FROM.json` unchanged apart from the new mark file. Zero risk to the parity audit.
- Proposal 2: a new `packages/chrome/src/brand.css` imported from `__root.tsx`, `packages/theme/src/brand.ts` with a parity test, twelve tokens of which two are colours, and a lint rule change (the `.ts-material` exclusion). The parity audit gains a new exclusion class, which is a permanent hole the next round can widen.
- Proposal 3: about seventeen tokens in `styles.css` with a data twin under `packages/theme/brand/`; no colour. Zero risk to the parity audit.

All three replace `GtMark` in the same four places, add `TurboslideMark.tsx` beside `GtMark.tsx` with a `PORTED_FROM.json` entry, add "Turboslide" to `PROPER_NOUNS`, and keep the GT mark on the sheet, the `mark` block, the closing plate, the icon picker and the GT template card. The parity audit's title row row changes its tooltip name in all three the same way.

### 5.4 The home route's cost against the performance budgets

Every mock is a static page over the real token file, so the route's JavaScript is the shell's and the plan's section 7 diet decides whether it meets the 600 KB ceiling; none of the three adds route code except proposal 1's twelve line hero cut and proposal 2's deferred mount (section 3.2 item 5). Image weight is where the three differ and where the plan has no budget: the fifteen README screenshots total 3,275,706 bytes, so proposals 1 and 3 put about 3.3 MB of JPEG on the page and proposal 2 about 2.4 MB, before the twins (proposal 1 six twins at about 14.5 KB, proposal 2 one at 11.9 KB plus the 800 by 450 screen and the figure, proposal 3 one at 3.7 KB). The build step that copies the shots to `apps/studio/public/home/` should also emit a 720 px wide variant and `srcset`, and `loading="lazy"` below the fold is a must; without it the page weight is the largest single cost of the route. The LCP element on all three is the hero plate's text or the twin, both in the first flush of a prerendered document, so the 800 ms cold LCP ceiling is reachable.

### 7.2 Wrong assumptions about the code

1. Section 3.8 says the event stream "authenticates the page as the server functions do today, by same origin under the CSRF middleware's rules for a GET". `apps/studio/src/start.ts` filters the CSRF middleware to `ctx.handlerType === 'serverFn'`, so a server route at `/api/events/$deckId` is not under it, and the bearer rule of `@turboslide/agent/http/auth` covers `/api/actions`, `/api/agent` and `/mcp` only. The route needs its own `Sec-Fetch-Site` or `Origin` check (an `EventSource` cannot send a bearer), or a short lived ticket in the query minted by a server function, the way the bundle routes take tickets.
2. Section 3.4 round two "puts the snapshot and the changed slide bodies together" ahead of the commit but says nothing about the removed slides, which today are deleted in the same loop as the puts (`client.del` at 725 to 727). A deletion that runs before a commit that then fails on `ifMatch` removes a body the live manifest still names, and a reader's next `pull()` of that slide gets null. Puts may move ahead of the commit for the reason the plan gives; deletions must stay after it.
3. Section 3.5 sets `pendingMinMs: 0` and leaves `pendingMs` at the router's default of 1,000 ms. The server renders the skeleton as the SSR fallback regardless, but on a same document `Link` from `/decks` to `/edit` the skeleton appears only after a second of pending, so the click to editor transition shows the old page for up to a second and then the skeleton for a frame. Set `pendingMs: 0` on the three routes too, or rely on the view transition and say so.
4. Section 3.9 builds the Linux addon on `ubuntu-latest` and commits it. The Vercel pages read for this verdict name no operating system image or glibc version for the Node 24 runtime, and a Rust cdylib built on Ubuntu 24.04 links against glibc 2.39 symbols. If the function's glibc is older the `require` fails and `select.ts` falls back to TypeScript silently, which makes the section 4 row "The image pipeline runs on the Rust addon in the function" untrue with no error. Build in a container with a glibc floor (a `manylinux`-style image or `cargo zigbuild` against 2.28), make `hosted-smoke.mjs`'s `describeBackends` assertion a hard failure on the preview (the plan adds the row; it must fail the check), and record the runtime's `ldd --version` from a preview function once.
5. Section 3.2 item 3 answers a 302 to the Blob URL for a cached thumbnail; `docs/hosting.md` names `TURBOSLIDE_BLOB_ACCESS=private` as a supported mode in which the URL is not public. The route must stream the body when the store is private.

### 7.3 Gaps

1. `ssr: 'data-only'` on `/edit/$deckId` puts the whole `EditorDeck`, speaker notes included, into the document HTML of a route that `NOINDEX_ROUTES` in `__root.tsx` does not list (`/new`, `/decks/trash`, `/print/$deckId` only). Today `ssr: false` kept the editor's HTML empty for a crawler, and `docs/hosting.md` section 7 promises that `/deck` carries no `notes` key. Add `/edit/$deckId` to `NOINDEX_ROUTES` and either strip `notes` from the dehydrated payload or accept that the editor's document carries them behind `noindex`.
2. `waitUntil` from `@vercel/functions` (sections 3.2 and 4) is a new dependency: no `package.json` under `apps` or `packages` and no catalog entry names it (`@vercel/blob` in `packages/store` is the only Vercel package). AGENTS.md requires a catalog entry and the integrator's install; the plan should declare it, or name Nitro's `event.waitUntil` if the installed `nitro` 3.0 beta exposes one (unverified).
3. The Blob thumbnail cache under `decks/<id>/.thumbs/<stamp>/...` has no retention rule. Snapshots keep the newest 50 and prune after five minutes; thumbnails are put immutable for a year and every edit of a slide creates a new stamp at three widths and two themes. `pull()` lists the deck prefix (`client.list(prefix)` at line 374) and removes "the local documents the store no longer has", so the listing grows by every thumbnail ever rendered, the listing is paged at 1,000 and billed as an advanced operation, and the mirror logic must learn to skip `.thumbs/`. Add a per slide retention (keep the newest K stamps) run in the same `waitUntil` as the render, and exclude the prefix from the mirror's listing.
4. Section 3.6 removes `assets/**` from the `decks` server asset group and names `ensureDeckAssets` for renders and exports only. `deck.create` from the GT template copies the seed's 202 twins into the new deck and, on Blob, uploads them (`docs/hosting.md` sections 2 and 4, about 290 puts); the "Makes a copy of the GT brand template" button at `decks.index.tsx` 590 to 598 therefore also needs the twins from the static URL or from Blob, or the round's most visible action breaks on a fresh instance.
5. Section 3.8's Blob mailbox is underspecified on the one step that matters: how the page's stream learns a new command id. It says the tick heads `.sessions.json` and "lists nothing", so `.sessions.json` must carry the pending command ids, which means the MCP instance writes it with `ifMatch` in a race with every attached tab's heartbeat. Either the tick lists `.commands/` (one advanced operation per 3 s per tab) or the design names the conflict rule.
6. Section 3.8 adds a function rule for `/api/events/**`; Nitro writes each rule as its own function directory (four today at about 148 MB each, `docs/hosting.md` section 6), so the plan adds a fifth copy of the server to every deployment while section 3.6 is trying to shrink them. Count it, and state the assumption that one streaming request per open tab for 13 minutes fits fluid compute's in function concurrency.
7. Section 3.1's parallel `get` of every manifest moves up to 96 KB per deck (the GT manifest's size in `docs/hosting.md`) through the function per home page load, about 1.6 MB for 17 decks; and `folders('decks/')` is a listing, which `docs/hosting.md` "Blob listing consistency" measured lagging `head` and `get`, so a deck created seconds earlier on another instance can be missing from the first list. The Recent row covers the creating browser only. State both.
8. Section 9.2's step runs `vite preview` for a TanStack Start application and hedges that it may not serve the SSR routes. The node-server build (`NITRO_PRESET=node-server` and `node .output/server/index.mjs`) is the path `docs/hosting.md` documents and should be the default, not the fallback, so `pnpm check` has one deterministic command.
9. Section 5's Speculation Rules `prerender` for `/new` from `/home` makes `readDraftDeck` run on the server per prerender (moderate eagerness fires on hover), a function invocation per candidate visitor. Cheap, but the plan should say so and cap eagerness at hover, not viewport.

### 7.4 Missing budgets

1. Page weight: no ceiling on the bytes of images per route. Section 5.4 above: the fifteen README screenshots total 3.3 MB and two of the three home mocks put them all on the page.
2. The icon set's CDN hits (`x-vercel-cache: HIT` on the second request of `/favicon.ico`, `/icon.svg`, `/apple-touch-icon.png`, `/manifest.webmanifest`, the manifest icons and `/og/turboslide.png`) are in every proposal's acceptance list and in no row of section 8; the check should assert them since it already reads response headers.
3. Function cold start: section 3.6 says the honest number "comes from the check's cold sample on a preview" and section 8 has no ceiling for it; add a row for the cold first byte of `/new` and `/decks` on a fresh instance and a report line for the function directory size.
4. The LCP element of `/home` is reported, not asserted; assert that it is the hero plate's text or the twin, so a screenshot below the fold can never become the LCP.
5. The per deck social image (`/og/deck/:deckId.png`, report 02 section 7, adopted by all three proposals) is a Chromium render per request with a 4 s budget, requested by every crawler that unfurls a link; it needs `s-maxage` per revision and a ceiling row.
6. The fifth function directory (7.3 item 6) needs a size ceiling per directory against the 250 MB cap, not only a report line for `__server.func`.
7. Memory during the batched export's merge (`peakMb`) and during a thumbnail render behind a stream have no row; the 3009 MB function can hold several concurrent streams and one Chromium.
8. Field INP is acknowledged as absent; the plan should at least reserve the row and the endpoint name so the next round does not redesign the table.

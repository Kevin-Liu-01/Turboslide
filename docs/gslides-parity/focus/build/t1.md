## Cycle 3 stream fix round two

### 1. The diagnosis per finding

**C3S-F7, the export job across instances.** The editor's queued export (`startExport`, then
`pollExport` every second; `controller.tsx` takes it when the capabilities read `sync: false`)
ran in the function's in process queue (`download.ts` `worker()`), and `pollExportFn` read that
instance's job table through `worker().job(id)`: a poll the platform routed to another instance
found no job and the handler threw `RangeError("No export job <id>")`, which the page's loop
took as the end of the export with no download and no sentence (run 2 of the preview gate, the
trace in C3S-F7). Two mechanisms sat under it. The job's record and its result lived in one
process's memory, and a `done` answer's downloads were signed one time URLs the download route
serves from that instance's job folder, so even the download of a job found in process could
land on an instance without its files. And a Vercel function is frozen once it has answered
unless the work runs under `waitUntil` (`routes/api/export.$deckId.ts`'s docblock, the 235 s
running job), so the job the start queued could stop getting CPU while the polls landed elsewhere;
the trace's job instance logged the worker's volume lines and nothing after. The other lane of
this round (T2) made the editor's choice of path wait for the capabilities (`editor/export-mode.ts`),
so a hosted studio takes the synchronous path; this lane fixes the polled path itself, which a
checkout takes and a hosted studio takes when the capabilities call failed.

**C3S-F8, the acknowledgement above a gap.** On the blob tier an ops answer's entries carry the
committed revision as their seq. The tab's stream position was one behind (the first picture's
`asset.add` had committed a revision the tab's stream instance had not announced), so the second
insert's answered entry sat above a gap in the room client's `incoming` buffer until the gap
watch reopened the stream at `GAP_REOPEN_MS` (8 s) and the replay filled it: 8.4 s against the
row's 5 s. The stream fix round's `announce(deckId, below)` in `blob.ts` `append` announces the
records between the instance's position and its own write to the streams on that instance, but the
tab's stream may sit on another instance, whose poll fills the gap only at its next tick. The
answer itself is the one channel that reaches the tab that posted, so it carries what sits
between.

**C3S-F9, the refused stream row.** The row held four raw streams from a second page of A's
context. On the HTTP/1.1 dev server the browser's six connections per host held the four streams
and B's, and the editor page's own long lived open queued in the browser behind them, so the route
never judged it while the cap was full; the page's first open reached the route 76 s later, after
the reader liveness rule had closed the raw streams, and was admitted. Run by line, with A's own
stream still open, the fourth raw open was the one refused. The premise needs the four slots
taken outside the browser's pool and A's own stream released first. A first form of the rewrite
held the streams from the test process with the cookie alone and was answered 403 for its 30 s
(three of three): the studio's cross site filter (`start.ts` `csrfFilter`, `headers.ts`) refuses a
cookie carrying request of the stream route without a browser's `origin` or `sec-fetch-site`, so
the fetches carry both.

**C3-F11.** The `afterAll`'s `deck.remove` left out `confirm: true`, which `schema/actions.ts`
requires (`confirm: z.literal(true)`), so the call was refused "invalid input at /confirm" inside
the try and every failed run left its deck under `decks/`.

**C3-F13.** `blob.ts` `startPulsePoll`'s tick reads the moved pulse through `store.sync(true)` and
`announce`, whose `store.records()` throws the store's `RangeError("No deck <id> in the Blob
store")` once Delete forever on another instance removed the manifest; the catch logged "polling
<id> failed" and scheduled the next tick, once per tick until the last stream of the deck closed.

### 6. Deviations and open items

1. Two files new beside the owned ones, for the owned mechanism: `apps/studio/src/server/export-jobs.ts`
   (the record's pure half, so the runtime never enters a unit test) and its test. Every other
   edit is in the files this lane owns.
2. The record's folder `exports/.jobs/` sits beside the deck prefixes, not under one, because the
   poll's input names the job alone (`pollExport({ jobId })`, the controller's call, another

   the deck prefix and with it the stored files; a record outlives it as a small JSON until the
   next export start prunes the records older than a day. A record holds no deck content.

   and the poll on the process that holds the job signs the one time URLs as before; a queued or
   running record found without its job there is a lost job (the process restarted) and is
   answered as a refusal rather than polled for good.

   file reads them.

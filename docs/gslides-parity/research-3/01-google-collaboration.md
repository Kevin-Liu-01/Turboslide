# Google Slides collaboration features

Report 01 of the Turboslide round three research set, written 2026-09-13. It inventories the collaboration surface of Google Slides: real time co-editing, presence, comments, the substitutes Slides offers where Docs has suggestions, the Share dialog, version history, the Activity dashboard, audience Q&A, chat and Meet. Round one's report 10 (L1) covered identity, sharing and presence from Google's pages and left a list of behaviours it could not verify. This report re-reads the primary pages, adds Google's developer documentation and a wider set of third party pages, closes several of those items, and states which remain open.

Rules followed: plain technical English, sentence case, no em dashes, no metaphors, no trailing periods on headings. No Google icon, logo or artwork is reproduced or proposed; Google Slides is named as a reference. No account was signed in; every fact comes from a public page. Every source was read on 2026-09-13. Source keys (G for a Google help page, W for a Google blog post, D for Google developer documentation, T for a third party page, S for a page seen only through a search listing, L for a local file) resolve in the Sources section.

## How to read this report

- A label in quotation marks is the exact text of the source. Behaviour is paraphrased; long passages are not copied.
- "Verified" means a Google owned page states it. "Corroborated" means two or more independent third party pages state it. "Single source" means one third party page. "Search listing" means the text was seen in a search result and the page itself could not be fetched. "Unverified" means expected from product knowledge and found on no page read; every unverified claim is repeated in the Unverified section.
- Shortcuts are given as Windows or ChromeOS first, then macOS. Google writes letters in lower case on its shortcut page; they are reproduced as printed there.
- "Title row" is the first row of the editor chrome (file name, Share, avatars). "Filmstrip" is Google's own name for the slide thumbnail list on the left (G18).
- Where round one already verified a menu placement, the row cites the round one report (L2, L3, L4) rather than repeating the search.

## 1 Real time co-editing

Google Slides keeps one copy of the presentation. Every editor's change is applied to that copy and pushed to every open tab; there is no check out, merge step or save button. The 100 tab ceiling, the pointer rules and the offline rules are the only limits Google documents. Google's own explanation of its merge algorithm is a pair of 2010 engineering posts whose bodies render only through JavaScript; their titles and dates were confirmed (W12) and the algorithm is taken from Wikipedia's summary of the 2025 Weidner, Gentle and Kleppmann paper (T22).

## 3 Comments

Comments are the only feedback channel in Slides that leaves a record. A comment is anchored to a slide, to an object, to text inside an object, to a table cell or to the speaker notes; the anchor names the object by id, so it survives text edits and dies with the object. Threads carry replies, @mentions, one assignee, emoji reactions and a resolved state. The comments panel opens from the title row and from View > Comments.

## 4 Suggestions

Google Slides has no Suggesting mode and no tracked changes. The View > Mode menu offers Editing, Commenting and Viewing (G6), and the 2015 access indication post shows Google deliberately kept Suggesting in Docs while giving Sheets, Slides and Drawings a comment only button (W5). The substitutes Google offers in Slides are: comments anchored to the object under review (section 3); Commenting mode, which lets an editor review without the risk of moving anything (G6); Approvals, which lock the file and record a decision for Enterprise and Education Plus accounts (G19); and version history, which shows each editor's changes in colour (section 5). A round three design that wants "suggest a change to this object" has no Google reference to copy and must be its own.

## 5 Share dialog and roles

The Share dialog is Drive's dialog, shared by Docs, Sheets, Slides and Vids. It has two halves: named people with per person roles, and a general access setting for the link. Ownership, expiry and download control are per person; there is no per link expiry for files.

## 11 Reading for the round three design

These are the facts a designer should carry into SPEC-3, stated without the reasoning above.

- Google merges concurrent edits without locks, dialogs or leases; the server orders operations and every tab converges. A design with per object soft locks departs from the reference and must justify it.
- Presence has one avatar surface (title row), one filmstrip marker, one text caret and one optional pointer. Follow is the only click behaviour of an avatar and is refused for anonymous people.
- Names are shown only for people the owner invited; everyone else is an animal, signed in or not. Anonymous people can edit and comment when the link role allows, cannot be followed, and are attributed collectively in history.
- Comments anchor to the slide, an object, text in an object, a table cell or the notes, by object id. A thread has replies, @mentions, one assignee with Done, emoji reactions, resolve and reopen, a link, and a per file notification setting with three levels. The panel has For you, search and a type filter; no sort is documented.
- Slides has no suggestions. Approvals is Google's locked review flow and is limited to Enterprise tiers.
- Roles are Viewer, Commenter, Editor, Owner; expiry, download control and ownership transfer are per person; general access is Restricted or Anyone with the link with its own role.
- Version history needs edit access, colours each editor, names up to 40 versions, restores without loss, and copies a version to a new file.
- The Activity dashboard exists only for Workspace domains; the rest of the world gets a privacy switch.
- Q&A is a presenter tool with a public link, anonymous questions and votes, separate from comments.
- Capacity numbers Google publishes: 100 simultaneous editing tabs, 600 direct recipients, 20 collaborators for pointers, 40 named versions.

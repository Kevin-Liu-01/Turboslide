## 1. What the three designs share, and what this lens can separate

All three designs put the channel on one SQLite backed Durable Object per deck over hibernating WebSockets, with the admission of `room.ts` 1693 to 1927 moved into the object, a ticket minted by the Vercel loader carried on the `Sec-WebSocket-Protocol` header, the heartbeat as a `setWebSocketAutoResponse` pair, no standing timer, presence out of SQLite, and the same per frame estimate: 10 to 30 ms a frame in region, 120 to 170 ms keystroke to screen (`research-options.md` 65; `research-durable-objects.md` section 5 says no first party millisecond figure exists). On the steady state keystroke path the three are the same design and the preview row is the first number anyone will have. What the lens can separate:

1. The first keystroke after a pause. The object hibernates after 10 s without an event (`research-durable-objects.md` section 4) and the heartbeat does not wake it, so a seller who reads for 15 s and then types pays the constructor. A rebuilds the live document from a Vercel route (`design-a.md` 2.3 item 7, risk 2: a function invocation, the store's mirror sync, the document as JSON, "an estimated 150 to 400 ms on that keystroke"; risk 18: the 4.5 MB response cap against a 25 MB deck). B rebuilds from document chunks it wrote to SQLite after each commit (`design-b.md` 3.2, "microseconds"). C rebuilds from `doc` rows per slide plus one R2 `head` to compare the etag (`design-c.md` 2.5), a network call on that path, unmeasured.
2. The two cross instance rows. `realtime.share-link.every-instance` (1 s) and `realtime.departed-guest.name-stable` (3 s) are not the channel's: `audit-sync.md` 97 names the per instance grant cache (`access.ts` 168, `indexFactsFor` 212 to 234) and 98 names the file store per instance. A keeps both at the blob tier's standing and says so (`design-a.md` section 8). B meets both: `ts_principal` on D1 behind the Worker (`design-b.md` 4.7) and an uncached grant miss over R2's strongly consistent read (4.4). C meets both: the principal store on D1 (`design-c.md` 4.6) and the loader's fresh index read over R2 (section 8).
3. The two deployment window and the rollback. A detects a foreign record by the revision jump on the checkpoint answer, reloads the document and reloads the tabs (`design-a.md` 3, 5.2), which leaves the object's acknowledged entries applied without a transform past the foreign write, the class `docs/SYNC.md` 3.2 records today. B and C re-admit the uncommitted entries transformed past the foreign records before the tabs reload (`design-b.md` 3.3 item 3; `design-c.md` 3.3), so an acknowledged word survives the window. B drains open decks before a rollback through an `rt_open` registry and has a runtime flag (`design-b.md` 5.5); C drains by a flag and waits 30 s (`design-c.md` 5.2); A has no kill switch without a deploy and bounds the window by the 2 s alarm (`design-a.md` 5.3, risk 14).
4. Placement. B pins the object to `enam` with a `locationHint` (`design-b.md` 4.2); A and C take no hint (`design-a.md` 4.3; `design-c.md` risk 9). A deck first opened in Europe keeps its object there in A and C; in B a European pair pays the ocean on every frame. Neither is right for every seller; the cross region hand row decides.

## 3. The free tier math, checked

The arithmetic of the three cost sections reproduces from the stated counts: A 230 object requests and 432 to 576 rows written per editor hour (`design-a.md` section 6), B 156 and 456 (`design-b.md` 6.1), C 155 and 216 to 360 (`design-c.md` 6.1). At 50 editor hours a day every design holds the Free plan: requests 11.5, 8 and 8 percent; rows written 22 to 29, 23 and 11 to 18 percent of 100,000 (`research-durable-objects.md` 1.1 for the caps). At 500 none holds: rows written pass the cap in every design (A 216 to 288 percent, B 228, C 108 to 180), and A's requests pass it too (115 percent) because A keeps the 2 s idle alarm. The forcing facts are the same three in all three notes with their sources: the Free cutoff for the rest of the UTC day (pricing line 30), the Worker's cap spendable by a stranger on `workers.dev` (websockets page line 471), and the rows written line at ten times today's load. C's rows written margin is the best because it does not rewrite document chunks and drops a partition instead of deleting rows (`design-c.md` 3.2, open on `DROP TABLE`'s accounting, `research-durable-objects.md` open 1). What stays on Vercel differs: A about $7 a month at 50 editor hours and $70 at 500 (the checkpoint route and the Blob records, `design-a.md` section 6); B the Pro seat plus about $0.14 (`design-b.md` 6.4); C $2 to $10 for the render service (`design-c.md` 6.4). B is the only one that reaches a $0 Cloudflare line with the Vercel usage line gone, and its R2 checkout is a payment stop that could put the store back on Blob (`design-b.md` risk 5).

## 5. The winner under this lens, and the grafts

The winner is A, at 38, because the channel that meets the rows is the same object in all three notes and A is the one the pipeline can land in two to four days without moving the store or the accounts; its two latency gaps are closed by grafts from B that cost a lane day each, and its two unmet rows are the account database's and not the channel's, which Kevin can decide separately. If the lens were latency alone with no weight on size, B would edge A by the wake path and the two met rows.

Grafts, one line each:

1. B 3.2: write the live document to the object's SQLite as chunks after each commit, so a wake rebuilds locally and A's seed route leaves the first keystroke after a pause (closes A risk 2 and 18; costs B's three rows written per commit).
2. B 3.3 item 3: on a refused or jumped commit, reload from the store, re-admit the uncommitted entries transformed past the foreign records, commit, then publish the external checkpoint; replaces A 5.2's reload that drops the transform.
3. B 5.5: an open deck registry and a drain before a rollback, plus a runtime flag the object reads on wake (A 10.14 names a KV namespace or a flags object as the carrier without D1).
4. B 4.4, stated as a code change: an index read that finds no grant for the deck is not cached, with the index read strongly consistent; on Blob the row still needs the bus (A section 8).
5. B 2.3 item 3: hello, replay and the first live frame from one event handler with no await between the SQLite cursor and the frames, so no entry falls between the replay and the live stream.
6. C 3.2: partition the entries by month and drop a table instead of deleting rows, with the day 0 probe of `DROP TABLE`'s accounting; lowers A's rows written line from 432 to 576 toward 216 to 360 per editor hour.
7. C 8: a `cost.do.rows-written` row with a production threshold (a day over 50,000 holds the next ship), so the Free cutoff is seen before 00:00 UTC reddens the matrix.
8. C 1.5: the loader reads the object's document at the head over the bearer route instead of the store at `covered` plus a replay, so a reload reads nothing behind by the checkpoint interval.
9. C 2.2: a `reauth` frame with a 10 s grace on an access change instead of closing every socket, so an editor whose role did not drop keeps the socket.
10. B 4.2 against A 4.3 and C risk 9: decide placement before the ship (an `enam` hint or none) and read the cross region hand row on the chosen one; neither note has a number.
11. C 5.1: the reverse delta pass as the store's rollback, for whichever design ever moves the store.

## 1. Kevin's directive and the two goals

Kevin's directive of 2026-09-20, verbatim: "make syncing and order of stuff much better and cleaner and more optimized for costs. also host this on gt cloud vercel, to save on costs, though keep the turboslide.vercel.app on my personal vercel, just make the one on my personal vercel not cost anything yk".

The two goals of this document, in order:

1. A write path whose order is defined and proven. Two people on one deck today lose words on a title slide, see concurrent inserts in opposite orders on a body block, and a colleague on the viewer floor never receives an edit while their revision counter climbs (audit-ordering section 1, runs 1 to 4). Each of those is a missing transform or a field that skips the transform, and each becomes a matrix row driven with two browsers on every origin (section 6).
2. A bill that scales with use. Production spent about $8.2 in the seven days to 2026-09-20 ($4.95 of provisioned memory for 466.6 GB h, $1.20 of active CPU for 9.36 h, $2.03 for 3.38 M invocations, at iad1 prices read 2026-09-20; `audit-costs` section 3 as corrected 2026-09-20 from `metrics/gbhr-7d-by-path.json`, `cpu-7d-by-path.json` and `invocations-7d-by-path.json` summed), three quarters of it one poll storm, and since the fix $0.6 to $1.1 a day, of which 88 percent is the provisioned memory of the session long poll held 25 s per call on editors, viewers and shows alike. The model gives $102.58 a month on Pro at 50 editor hours a day with no included Pro amounts, $82.58 after the team's $20 credit applied once (audit-costs section 7 and `costs-model.md` as recomputed 2026-09-20, prices read 2026-09-20), and the ranked savings of section 4 take most of it out without touching what the focus round's storage tier design got right.

## 2. The write path as built, and what the ordering audit measured

### 2.1 Who assigns the position

On production the realtime tier is `blob` (`TURBOSLIDE_REALTIME=blob`, `packages/realtime/src/select.ts` 74 to 85). There is no operation stream in front of the log: every ops POST is one commit and the seq of an entry is the revision its record made (`packages/realtime/src/blob.ts` header and `append` 416 to 489, `admitted = entries.map((entry) => ({ ...entry, seq: revision }))` at 479). The position is assigned by the instance and made true by the store in three steps (audit-ordering 3.1):

1. The instance's reducer bumps the revision on its mirror (`packages/schema/src/reduce.ts` 557; `packages/store/src/blob-store.ts` `file.write` at 1429 inside `runWrite`).
2. The instance claims the record number `versions/<n>.json` with `overwrite: false` (`blob-store.ts` `claimRecord`, 1296 to 1339; the put at 1314). A number another writer holds is `RecordTakenError` (435); a claim older than `CLAIM_GRACE_MS` 60 s (451) or the same write's own earlier claim is taken over (1319 to 1338). The write discards the mirror, waits `CLAIM_RETRY_MS` 200 ms (556) and runs once more from the store's document (`runWrite(1)`, 1533 to 1543).
3. The instance commits by putting `deck.json` with `ifMatch` set to the etag it synced (`blob-store.ts` `putDocument('deck.json', synced)` at 1483; `packages/store/src/blob-vercel.ts` passes `ifMatch` to `@vercel/blob` 2.8.0). A precondition failure is the conflict outcome (`conflictFromStore` 1377, called at 1532). A commit put that meets `BLOB_DOCUMENT_WRITE_TIMEOUT_MS` (731, 20 s) is settled by a head: bytes equal to ours is our commit (1491 to 1496).

The manifest put with `ifMatch` is the linearization point, and it is correct and measured: two POSTs from one base 165 ms apart were answered revisions 15 and 16, the second with `between` naming the first (audit-ordering 4.3, `run3-ordering-run.json` phases.closed.rounds[0].wire); `hosted.test.ts` 369, 405 and 609 hold the fake to one committed manifest and one conflict under two writers. 173 ops POSTs over the four runs were all answered 200; no run met a 409 or a 503.

The instance's admission on this tier is `admitOnBlob` (`room.ts` 1705 to 1900): a base more than `BASE_SEQ_WINDOW` (500) behind the head is answered 409 `resync` before anything else (`checkBaseWindow`, `packages/realtime/src/admission.ts` 73 to 76, called at `room.ts` 1386); the entries this instance admitted before are filtered out once, before any sync (1711 to 1716, `blobAdmittedBefore` 1684); the mirror is synced to at least the client's base (`liveAtLeast` 1586 to 1626: up to `LIVE_AT_LEAST_ATTEMPTS` 6 forced syncs `LIVE_AT_LEAST_PAUSE_MS` 500 ms apart, and only while `revision > live.document.deck.revision`); `place()` (1733) runs each entry through the reducer against the live document as it stands, transforming only past the undo of entries refused earlier in the same POST (`refusedUndo` 1738, 1769 to 1771, 1787); the channel appends, and on a head that moved under the append the mirror is synced to that head and `place()` runs once more (the loop at 1831 to 1848, `BLOB_APPEND_RETRIES` 1 at 1698). A second miss answers 409 `resync`; a miss at the same revision answers 503 `store_busy` (1849 to 1866). The memory tier's `admitOps` (1315 to 1507) transforms every entry past `channel.since(base)` before it places (`transformEntry` 1107, called at 1440 and again at 1488 inside `appendWithRetry`); `admitOnBlob` does not.

### 2.3 How another instance learns of a commit

An instance with a client stream open on the deck heads `.turboslide/pulse.json` every 2 s (`pulse.ts` 23, `HOSTED_POLL_MS`; `blob.ts` `startPulsePoll` 327) and every fifteenth tick heads the manifest instead (`PULSE_SAFETY_TICKS` 15, `pulse.ts` 27). When the etag moved it reads three things together (`blob.ts` `readMoved` 306): the manifest through `store.sync(true)`, which heads `deck.json` and pulls only when that etag moved (`blob-store.ts` `syncNow`, 1208 to 1226), the presence record (`presence-store.ts` `readRemote`: a head, then a proven get when the head moved), and the comments index (`comments-store.ts` `watchSidecarIndex`). Then `announce` (`blob.ts` 258 to 290) reads the mirror's records and publishes an `op` and a `checkpoint` per record above the position it announced last, to the streams on this instance; a revision the store reached without a record the mirror holds is published as an external checkpoint (275 to 286), which every tab answers with a reload at the head.

So per remote commit, per reading instance: one `list` of the deck prefix (one advanced operation per 1,000 files under the prefix; one live deck holds about 2,900 files, three pages, `store-counts-2026-09-20.txt`), two simple reads per new record, one simple read of the snapshot, one head of the presence record, one head of the comments index, on top of the tick's head. The listing is the one advanced operation on this path and it is redundant for the records, which the same function then walks by number (audit-ordering item 3).

A correction to the ordering audit's arithmetic, read in the code: a presence push moves the pulse but not the manifest, so the other instance's `sync(true)` heads `deck.json`, finds the etag unchanged and pulls nothing (`syncNow`, 1214 to 1218). A presence push costs a reading instance three heads and one proven get, all simple, and no listing. Two idle tabs on two instances therefore cost about 24 advanced operations a minute (a push every 15 s per tab, three puts each: the copy, the record, the pulse; `presence-store.ts` `PRESENCE_PUSH_SPACING_MS` 68, `PRESENCE_SHARED_TTL_MS` 70, `pushNow`), not the 32 the ordering audit's item 6 counted; the eight listings it added are not made. The consequence for the plan is in section 7: the source aware pulse read of item 6 saves simple operations alone and is left out.

The measured propagation from A's keystroke to B's screen: 1,948 ms on average, 662 ms at best and 3,000 ms at worst over 30 edits (audit-ordering 4.2), which is the POST plus up to one tick plus the pull.

### 2.4 How the client orders remote ops against its own

`packages/realtime/client/room-client.ts` holds `pending` (applied locally, not in the stream), `retained` (acknowledged at a seq, not yet covered) and `server` (the document at `seq`); `local` is `server` with `pending` folded on top (`fold`). A flush posts the unsent pending ops as one POST with `base: { seq }`, at once for a structural op, 50 ms into a drag, 100 ms while typing (`FLUSH_MS`), one POST in flight at a time (1127 to 1145); the POST needs the client id and its binding, not the stream (1137), and the first flush waits until the replay caught the stream up (`caughtUp` 1140). The blob channel folds a POST's entries into one mutation list and commits them as one revision (`blob.ts` `append` 434 to 436, `foldMutation`; `coalesce.ts` 93 to 109), so every entry of a batch shares one seq and the client buffers siblings per seq (`take`, `drain`).

A remote entry moves the pending text ops past it with the schema's transform (`transformPast`; `packages/schema/src/transform.ts` 347 to 356 `transformMutation`), and a pending op that no longer applies is returned to its author. Non text mutations are not transformed: `transformMutation` passes them through (348) and they are refolded on the new server document where the last writer wins per pointer (`coalesce.ts` 102 to 105, `samePointer`) or the reducer re-anchors or refuses them (`room.ts` `reanchor` 1134); a `slide.set` of a slide counts as a rewrite of every text on it (`transform.ts` 332, `rewritesText`). This is operational transformation for the two text ops and a rebase for everything else.

The client's own acknowledgement on the blob tier has three paths, because a record carries `clientId: 'store'` and `opId: 'store:<n>'` and never the client's op id (`blob.ts` `entryOfRecord` 115 to 126): the POST's answer names the entries with their seq; a store echo that arrives first is matched to a posted batch by its base and the byte equality of its folded mutations (`settleOwnEcho` 691, read at 773); an entry at or behind the position from a resent POST settles by op id (`settleBehind` 734 to 736, which skips entries whose `clientId` is not the tab's). When the answer is lost the ops stay pending with their record kept for the echo, the client marks itself offline and backs off (1189 to 1197). A `resync` answer reloads the document through `onResync` and re-folds every pending op on it with no check (943 to 967; the `resync` code at 1237). The server side is a per instance memory of the last 512 admitted op ids (`room.ts` 1666 to 1697, `rememberBlobAdmitted`, `blobAdmittedBefore`), whose own comment says it does not catch a resend that lands on another instance.

### 2.5 Snapshots, the reload and the viewer

Every commit stores the whole document as canonical JSON under `snapshots/<md5 of the deck.json bytes>.json` before the manifest flips (`blob-store.ts` round two; `packages/store/src/snapshots.ts` 1 to 9), because the public store's CDN serves an overwritten `deck.json` body stale and the etag names the snapshot that proves the current document (`pull` step 1, 1100 to 1108). Retention keeps the snapshots of the newest 50 records and every named version (`snapshots.ts` `SNAPSHOT_KEEP_RECORDS` 22), with a five minute grace (`SNAPSHOT_GRACE_MS` 30), and the prune runs after every commit. The version records are never compacted: a cold mirror pulls every record by number (`pull`, up to 10,000), `store.records()` parses the whole folder on every call (`versions.ts` 75 to 95) and `announce` calls it on every tick that moved (audit-ordering 3.6). The pull's walk stops at the first number it cannot fetch (1035 to 1043, `break`), and `dropRecordsAbove` removes from the mirror every record whose revision is above the proven manifest (1084 to 1098): a claim in flight or a leftover is never applied. The checkpointer of `apps/studio/src/server/checkpoint.ts` runs on the memory and redis tiers only.

A reload reads `readEditorDeck` (`apps/studio/src/server/write.ts` 258 to 347; its input is a hand parser of `deckId` and `atLeast`, 259 to 267, not a strict schema): the room's mirror synced by force, the document at the head, the newest `EDITOR_VERSIONS_KEPT` 50 records without their mutations (165), `room.seq` equal to the revision; the stream then opens at `since=seq` and replays the records above it (`room.ts` `replayFor` 2230), and `hello.covered` is `live.seq`. Three reloads in parallel after 61 revisions took 1,778, 2,065 and 2,032 ms and read byte equal documents at revision 61 (audit-ordering 3.7 and 4.2 phase 4). Unacknowledged ops survive a reload in the pending store (`pending-store.ts` 1 to 12: IndexedDB under `turboslide:pending:<deckId>:<clientId>`, at most 1 MB and 24 hours, per origin; `room-client.ts` `offerPersisted` 1423 re-applies the queue on the current document and drops an op the document no longer takes).

A reader with the `viewer` role receives checkpoints, presence and the deck level notices and never an `op` frame (`room.ts` `filterEventForReader` 2035 to 2046, `canSeeOps` at 2036); the controller moves the revision on a checkpoint and nothing else (`apps/studio/src/editor/controller.tsx` 1659 to 1674, "the revision moved and nothing else"). On production a second anonymous principal on a deck made from `/new` lands on the viewer floor (`apps/studio/src/server/authorize.ts` `shadowStanding`, 188 to 194); the product round changes the default general access of a new deck to Anyone with the link, Editor (`docs/PRODUCT.md` section 1, the decisions), which is on the tree and not on production.

The heading and the lead of a title slide and the big text of a statement slide are slide fields, not blocks (`packages/viewer/src/InlineText.tsx` 278 to 283 `isSlideField`), and every burst on them is `slide.set` of the whole value plus `deck.set /title` (`textBurstMutation` 380 to 382 routes a slide field to `textCommitMutation`). A `slide.set` is never transformed (`transform.ts` 348) and the coalesce keeps the last value per pointer (`coalesce.ts` 46 to 47); the open session re-sends its own text after every change it did not make (`text-fit.ts` `sessionReconcile` 'resend'; `Editor.tsx` 1681 to 1745). The deck's title is also written by `deck.rename` from the home page's card menu, the editor and the CLI as one `deck.set /title` (`apps/studio/src/server/decks.ts` 374 to 401).

## 3. The cleaner model

The log, the position and the commit stay what they are: one record per commit under `versions/<n>.json`, the number assigned by the instance's reducer and made true by the claim and the conditional manifest put. What changes is around it. Items marked P1 in this section are specified here so the round after builds against one model, and section 5.2 names them; they do not land in this round's build.

### 3.1 Where the sequence is assigned

In one place, as today: the revision is the sequence, the manifest put with `ifMatch` is the commit point, and the claim stays as the early visibility of the record and the second guard on the same race (`blob-store.ts` `claimRecord` and round three). Nothing measured contradicts this; the item that changes is what the instance does before it places (3.3) and how a resend is recognised (3.2). A claim is not a commit: a record whose revision is above the proven manifest is never applied to a mirror or a tab (`dropRecordsAbove` 1084 to 1098 keeps this today, and 3.8 keeps it for the round after).

### 3.2 Idempotency: the record names its origin

The version record gains `origin: { clientId, opIds }` (`packages/store/src/store.ts` `VersionRecord` 37; the parse in `packages/store/src/versions.ts` 53 to 62; `blob-store.ts` `write` carries it from the `Write`, which gains the same optional field in `packages/schema/src/mutations.ts`; the blob channel's `append` fills it from the folded batch's entries, `blob.ts` 434 to 436).

The landing is tolerate first, write second, because the parsers are strict: `versionSchema` is `z.strictObject` (`mutations.ts` 261 to 268), `versionRecordSchema` extends it and stays strict (`versions.ts` 51 to 53, "a test pins it"), and `readVersions` throws a `TypeError` on a record with an unknown field (84 to 89), which stops `records()`, `announce`, `since` and the pull's replay for that deck on every instance that reads the record with the old parser. So the round ships two deployments in order: deployment N carries the parser that accepts `origin` and writes nothing new; deployment N plus 1 writes it. N plus 1 goes out only once N is the production deployment that an Instant Rollback would land on, and the ship note records that a rollback of N plus 1 lands on N and never earlier. The windows in which an older deployment reads records a newer one wrote are the seconds of a deploy's alias switch, the rollback week of `docs/HOSTING-MOVE.md` section 6, and the hosting cutover's window in which two deployments serve one store (`docs/HOSTING-MOVE.md` 2.2 and 2.6); the rule there is that the move runs before this round ships, or both deployments pin to one commit for the window. Skew protection does not shorten or lengthen these windows: the project setting reads 43,200 s (`audit-hosting/vercel-facts.md` section 2), but `apps/studio/src` sends no deployment id (no `skew`, `__vdpl`, `x-deployment-id` or `dpl=`; grep 2026-09-20), so the setting pins no tab to an old deployment (judge-operations addition 8). The earlier draft's sentence that skew protection pins a tab for 43,200 s is withdrawn.

Three readers change:

- The origin check runs where the mirror is proven, not where it is guessed. `admitOnBlob` syncs the mirror only while it is below the POST's base (`liveAtLeast` 1586 to 1626), and a resend goes at the base of the lost first attempt (`room-client.ts` `flush`; the lost answer path 1189 to 1197 leaves `seq` where it was), so an instance whose mirror sits at that base holds no record of the first attempt and would place the entry again; the write path's own head and pull (`blob-store.ts` 1410 to 1414) then bring the mirror up and commit it a second time. The check therefore runs in two places: once in `admitOnBlob` before placement against the mirror as it stands (cheap; it catches the same instance and the already synced cases and retires the per instance memory of 1666 to 1697), and once in `blob-store.ts` `write()` between the pull at 1414 and `file.write` at 1429, against the records the pull just proved. A `Write` whose `origin.opIds` a record above its `baseRevision` names is answered `{ ok: true, replayed: <that record> }` with no claim and no commit; the channel's `append` turns that into `{ ok: true, entries }` with one synthesized entry per op id at the record's seq (as `result.entries` carry the client's ids today, `blob.ts` 479), so `settleBehind`'s match by `entry.opId` (`room-client.ts` 734 to 736) keeps working. The bound of both checks is the records above the POST's base, the set `channel.since(deckId, base)` already reads for the transform of 3.3 (`blob.ts` 491 to 493, `recordsAfter` 228); a first admission always sits above the base, and a client fifty commits behind is not served by the newest fifty. The check runs again after every sync inside `admitOnBlob`'s retry loop (3.3).
- `entryOfRecord` (`blob.ts` 115 to 126) emits the record's `clientId` and, as a new optional `covers: string[]` on `Entry` (`packages/realtime/src/channel.ts`, `protocol.ts` `entrySchema` 139 to 152, a strict object that the same deployment N tolerates first), every op id the record folded; the seq stays the revision. A record written before this round carries no origin and travels as today.
- The client settles by id: an entry whose `covers` names a pending op acknowledges it at the entry's seq and applies the folded mutations once (`room-client.ts` `settleOwnEcho` becomes the id match, with the byte equality kept as the fallback for records without origin; `settleBehind` matches by `entry.opId` as today under the synthesized entry rule above). The resync path gains its check: `resync` (943 to 967) re-folds every pending op on the fresh document, so the resync read carries `since: <the tab's position before the resync>` (an addition to `readEditorDeck`'s hand parsed input, 259 to 267, safe across deployments) and answers the `origin.opIds` of the records above it, mutations stripped, up to `REPLAY_MAX_ENTRIES` 2,000 (`protocol.ts` 30); the client drops every pending op named there as acknowledged at that record's seq. A POST answered `resync` by the base window (`BASE_SEQ_WINDOW` 500) reaches the same path. A pending op whose first attempt is more than 2,000 commits behind the head is dropped with the queue and returned to its author with the reject card's sentence, as an op the document no longer takes is today (`offerPersisted`). That is the bound of invariants 3 and 10.

### 3.4 Every text a person can type into is a text run

`text.splice` and `text.replace` accept a slide field target: `blockId` `heading`, `lead` or `big` on a title or statement slide (`packages/schema/src/mutations.ts` schema; `reduce.ts` at the `text.splice` case). `textBurstMutation` stops routing slide fields to `textCommitMutation` (`InlineText.tsx` 380 to 382); `rewritesText` (`transform.ts` 317 to 340, the `slide.set` case at 332) treats `slide.set /heading` as a rewrite of that field alone, not of the whole slide; `sessionReconcile`'s 'resend' path (`text-fit.ts` 54 to 58, `Editor.tsx` 1681 to 1745) is no longer reached for a field, since the field now receives splices like a block (audit-ordering item 1).

The deck title follows the heading in the reducer instead of riding every burst, under one precedence rule, because `deck.set /title` is also what `deck.rename` writes from the home page's card menu, the editor and the CLI (`apps/studio/src/server/decks.ts` 374 to 401): the reducer derives the title from the first slide's heading only while the title equals the heading's value before the splice (the title was following the heading); once a `deck.set /title` made them differ, the title is never derived again until a later `deck.set /title` makes them equal. ### 3.11 The invariants

1. One ordered log per deck. The revision is the sequence; the manifest put with `ifMatch` is the one commit point; a record is claimed with `overwrite: false` and never overwritten; a record above the proven manifest is a claim and is never applied. A missing number in the log is recorded, never a stop: the reader's walk continues to the manifest's revision and the tabs above the hole reload once (3.6).
2. Every admitted entry is placed against the live document after a transform past every record between its base and the head, on every tier; a retry after a moved head transforms the original mutations past the whole set; the answer carries the placed mutations. Text ops are transformed; every other mutation is re-anchored or refused by the reducer, whose sentence the loser reads (`room.ts` `reanchor` 1134; row `sync.structural.concurrent`).
3. Every record names its origin. An op id is admitted at most once on any instance while its first attempt's record is within `REPLAY_MAX_ENTRIES` (2,000) of the head; a resend is answered with the seq its first admission made, from the records above the POST's base, after the write path's head and pull and again after every sync inside the retry loop; a pending op older than the bound is dropped with the queue and returned to its author.
4. The client acknowledges by op id: from the answer, from the echo's `covers`, from the replay, or from the resync read's `origin.opIds` above its old position; the byte match is the fallback for a record without origin.
5. Every text a person can type into is a text run, transformed on both sides; a whole value set of a field rewrites that field alone; a set title wins over the derived one until a set makes them equal again.
6. A reader receives every op frame its role may see; a viewer and a commenter receive ops with the notes stripped; in a connected tab the revision never runs ahead of the document.
7. The head is read, never listed: the pulse etag is the one timed read per tick; the manifest etag names the snapshot; records are read by number; `list` runs in the prune, the removal, the sweep and the listing page only.
8. Every read of an overwritten path is proven: `head` first, the body accepted only when its md5 equals the version, else the copy under the version.
9. The stream opens at the client's position and the replay covers exactly the gap; `hello.covered` is the live seq; the ops answer's `between` fills what sits under an admitted entry.
10. Unacknowledged ops survive a reload in the pending store and are replayed against the current document; a replay whose first attempt committed is acknowledged, never committed again, under the bound of invariant 3; the resync path drops what the records above the old position name.
11. Undo is per author and moves its inverse past every remote entry since it was recorded (`room-client.ts` `transformSince` 1533 to 1541, over the last `RECENT_ENTRIES` 2,000): an inverse recorded before a remote insert applies at the shifted offset; the author's own echo is skipped by `clientId` (today `!myClientIds.has(entry.clientId)` skips nothing on the blob tier because every record reads `store`; with 3.2 the own echo carries this tab's id and the skip holds); a second tab of the same person carries its own client id, which `myClientIds` does not hold, so its entries are remote to this tab and the inverse is transformed past them, which is the right reading. A structural inverse the reducer refuses shows the reducer's sentence.
12. The budget: at most one timed store call per tick per open deck per instance while a client stream is open there and none otherwise; once `flags.json` lands (the round after), one instance wide `flags.json` head every 5 s while any stream is open on the instance, counted apart from the per deck tick; every call under a deadline; a 429 or a 5xx backs the poll off to 60 s and tells the streams.
13. Retention runs on a schedule: the prune every 20th commit and at the last stream's close; the sweep once a day (the round after); a request makes no garbage it does not name for deletion.

### 4.5 The BLOB_BUDGET rule, restated with this round's numbers

Per open deck per instance, while a client stream of the deck is open there:

- One timed store call per tick, and none without a stream. The tick is 2 s while an op landed on this instance in the last 30 s or the deck has company (another instance's roster row, a second own row, or a second stream here; 3.10) (30 calls a minute, `POLL_CALLS_PER_MINUTE_MAX`), 10 s when the one tab is alone and quiet (6 a minute). The round after adds one instance wide line beside it: one `flags.json` head every 5 s per instance while any stream is open on the instance, counted apart from the per deck tick (12 a minute per instance, about $0.0003 an hour), so the kill switch row keeps its 5 s bound without a second call in the deck's tick. When the round after closes the idle stream after 5 minutes, a forgotten tab costs no timed call from its sixth minute.
- A moved pulse costs the reads it names and no listing: one `deck.json` head and, when that etag moved, the records by number and the snapshot the etag names; one presence record head and, when moved, one proven get; one comments index head and, when moved, one get.
- An edit costs the writer five advanced operations (the snapshot, the record, one slide body per changed slide, the manifest, the pulse) and two or three simple ones; one `list` every 20th commit for the prune; no listing when another instance wrote in between.
- A presence push is three puts at most every 5 s per deck per instance, on a change that travels or a row whose remaining life is under 15 s of 30; the client heartbeat is 5 s while active and 10 s when quiet, so an idle tab pushes every 20 s.
- The card thumbnail renders once at rest, on hide and on stream close, with the 8 s floor.
- A 429 or a 5xx backs the poll off, doubling to 60 s and never under the store's `retry-after`, and tells the streams; every call runs under its deadline (`BLOB_READ_TIMEOUT_MS` 10 s at 593, `BLOB_DOCUMENT_WRITE_TIMEOUT_MS` 20 s at 731, `BLOB_WRITE_TIMEOUT_MS` 90 s at 728); the SDK's retries stay off.

### 6.2 The runs and the verdict

- Every ordering row is drivable with two browsers on every origin: the node-server build locally (the memory tier), the enforce preview (the blob tier, `TURBOSLIDE_AUTHORIZE=enforce`, `TURBOSLIDE_ASSIST=fixture`) and production after the ship, from the repository root with `PLAYWRIGHT_BASE_URL=<origin>`, one worker, retries 0, viewport 1440 by 900 (FOCUS.md 6.1). Each two person row repeats its exchange three times inside the test and passes only when every repeat passes. The spec creates its decks from `/new` and tears them down in `afterEach` and in a `finally` (File > Move to trash, Delete forever on `/decks/trash`, a 404 on `/edit/<id>`).
- The gate runs once on the enforce preview built from the ship's commit and once on production after the alias moves. A row passes only when it passes in the last run of each origin; `retries` stays 0; the run ledger lists every run (FOCUS.md 6.2). A rerun happens only after a code change named by its commit.
- The known mechanism class: a row whose table entry names two lanes' halves, red on a tree where one half is not merged, is written into the ship note by id with the missing half named; it is never a flake and never chased by a rerun of the same tree.
- The recorded and not chased class: a row red on one origin and green on the other whose mechanism, read from the run's JSON, is a second browser bound (a word past 5 s, a chip past 35 s, a reload past 5 s) is a blob tier timing flake; it is written into the ship note by id with its measured seconds and is never chased in this round. A row red on both origins, or red once with any other mechanism, fails the ship until a rerun after a named code change reads it green (docs/PRODUCT.md 8.2).
- The cost rows: each records its counts (function requests a minute from the page's request log; `storeCalls` from `sync.status`) beside its ceiling in the run's JSON. A cost row over its ceiling on the preview holds the ship. Every ceiling sits at about 1.5 times the model's expected count after the fix and always under today's measured count, so a miss reads as the fix's absence and never as a timing (question 4).
- The hosted smoke keeps running as it did (`node scripts/hosted-smoke.mjs <origin> --token-env TURBOSLIDE_TOKEN`), and the new write probe with it (FOCUS.md 6.2).

### 6.3 The call counting harness

- In process: `boundedBlobClient` (`blob-store.ts` 781) counts every call by deck prefix and operation (`head`, `get`, `put`, `list`, `del`) over a sliding 60 s window, in memory per instance; `sync.status` gains `storeCalls: { head, get, put, list, del, windowMs, instance, lists }` for the deck named (`lists` names the folder of every `list`: `versions`, `snapshots`, `assets`, `thumbs`, `presence`, `deck`, `other`; the ship's addition, so the pull row reads its own listing), read through `/api/actions/sync.status` with the bearer (`apps/studio/src/server/agent-actions.ts`), where `instance` is a random id the process mints at start so a probe can tell two answering instances apart. The unit tests count with `blob-fake.ts`'s `calls` (the fake records every operation): `hosted.test.ts` asserts one edit is five puts and no list on the writer, one commit costs a reading instance no list, and the twentieth commit lists once.
- On a deployment: `scripts/probes/sync-cost-probe.mjs` drives one state per process for 3 minutes at human speed (the shape of `audit-costs/measure.mjs`), records every request the page made, samples `sync.status.storeCalls` five times over the window, keeps the maximum per operation per `instance` and records how many instances answered (the counters are per instance and the preview routes to whichever instance is warm; C3S.3a saw two), writes the counts beside the ceiling to the run's JSON, and exits 1 on a row over its ceiling. On the preview the probe sends `VERCEL_OIDC_TOKEN` as `x-vercel-trusted-oidc-idp-token`; on production nothing but the bearer for `sync.status`. It never prints the bearer.
- Locally the same probe runs against the node-server build; the store there is the file store, so `storeCalls` reads zero and the row asserts the function request counts alone.

# Judge sales: the focus round document read as a seller

Judgment of `docs/FOCUS.md` (the focus round, 2026-09-15) from the sales user's side, written 2026-09-15. The lens is the seller of `docs/gslides-parity/research/07-sales-users.md`: a person who opens a deck that exists, tailors a few slides per prospect, presents over a call and sends a link or a PDF. Three questions were asked of the document: does the core set of section 2 cover every weekly task of the research and nothing a seller never touches; would any parked feature of section 3 send a seller back to Google Slides; is the wording plain. Every claim below names the audit row, the matrix id or the file at `ec61c6b` that proves it. Nothing was run against production for this judgment; the evidence is the eight audits, `core-matrix.json`, the research and the production sources read with `git show ec61c6b:<path>`.

## Verdict

Revise. The core set is the right set for a seller and the parked set holds nothing a seller needs weekly, but the matrix that is supposed to prove the core set misses rows for six interactions the research names as the seller's own, section 1 misreads the shapes audit, the matrix assigns severities to rows nobody drove, and the sharing question is left open in section 9 while the rules of section 1 already answer it. The wording is mostly plain; five terms break the copy rules.

## 1. Does the core set cover the seller's week

The paragraph after the population table in research 07 lists the seller's week: open a deck that exists, duplicate or delete slides, replace a logo or screenshot, retype a name and a few numbers, reorder, hide the slides that do not apply, present over a call, send a PDF or a link. Section 2 of the document maps every item of that list to menu rows, toolbar controls, right click rows and matrix ids, and the matrix has rows for each: `decks.list.open-recent`, `slides.duplicate.*`, `slides.delete.*`, `images.replace.upload`, `text.title.*`, `text.find-replace.replace-all`, `slides.reorder.*`, `slides.skip.*`, `present.*`, `export.pdf.file`, `share.copy-view-link`. On coverage of that paragraph the document is complete.

The research's wider "Top tasks" table adds tasks the paragraph does not name, and here the document has gaps. Each gap below is an interaction that exists on production, that the research names, and that has no matrix row, so under rule 1 of section 1 the feature it belongs to is not in the default view until the row is added and driven.

1. Start from beginning. `title.slideshow.startFromBeginning` is a Now row on production with the chord Cmd+Shift+Enter (`packages/chrome/src/menus/model.ts` at `ec61c6b`, lines 718 to 722). Research 07's shortcut table lists "Cmd+Shift+Enter from the beginning on Mac" and design rule 8 names "present from beginning" as part of the Slideshow button. Section 2.7 lists `title.slideshow` and `title.slideshow.presenterView` only, section 3.2 does not park the row, and the matrix has no `present.*` row for it. The row is in neither set. The Later stub `title.slideshow.presentOnAnotherScreen` (line 723) is in neither set either.
2. The blank screen keys. Production binds B or . to a black slide and W or , to a white one (`packages/viewer/src/present/presentKeys.ts` at `ec61c6b`, lines 8 to 9, with the test at `__tests__/present-keys.test.ts` lines 36 to 39). Research 07's shortcut row "While presenting" lists "B black, W white". Section 2.7's list "The show's keys" and the fifteen `present.*` rows omit them. A seller who blanks the screen to talk over a call has no row.
3. Skipped slides and speaker notes stay out of the links. The Share dialog on production says "Speaker notes and skipped slides never travel with a view or comment link" (audit-present row 17). Research 07 design rule 24 makes this the seller's pricing slide protection and the frustration table's row "Skipped slides are still visible in the shared file" names the leak. No audit drove a skipped slide through the View link, the Present link or the card's Present (audit-present's decks had no skipped slide; audit-decks row 40 presented three unskipped slides). `apps/studio/src/routes/deck.$deckId.tsx` at `ec61c6b` carries no skip handling and neither do `BookView.tsx`, `SlideView.tsx` or `Stage.tsx` in `packages/viewer/src`; only the renderer's standalone build and the print document leave skipped slides out (`packages/render/src/__tests__/gslides.test.ts` lines 309 to 347). The matrix's only skipped rows are the four export rows. The claim the dialog makes to the seller is not in the matrix.
4. Drop a file on a picture to replace it. Section 2.4 cites design rule 13 ("dropping a file on an image replaces it and keeps the frame"). The code path exists on production (`packages/viewer/src/Editor.tsx` at `ec61c6b`, the docblock at lines 2580 to 2584 and `onDrop` at 3985 to 4004, which passes `blockId` and `replace`). audit-images row 7 dropped on the empty slide only. The matrix's one Replace image row is `images.replace.upload`, broken on all six tries (audit-images row 57), so the seller's fastest logo swap has no row at all.
5. File > Make a copy. Section 2.1 lists `file.makeCopy.entire` as a core row. The matrix's only copy row is `decks.card.make-a-copy`, the card menu on `/decks` (audit-decks row 37, flaky). The editor's dialog carries a Remove speaker notes checkbox (`packages/chrome/src/dialogs/MakeCopy.tsx` at `ec61c6b`, lines 10 to 11 and 106 to 109), which research 07 rule 19 and the task "Make a customer-facing copy" name; nobody drove the File route or the checkbox.
6. Slides through the clipboard. Section 2.2 puts `edit.cut`, `edit.copy` and `edit.paste` on the `filmstripCard` target in the core set; research 07's task "Paste slides from another deck" is the seller's way to reuse an approved slide. No `slides.clipboard.*` row exists and no audit drove a card copy and paste, within a deck or between two decks in two tabs.

Smaller rows of the same kind: `format.bulletsNumbering.numbered` and the chords Cmd+Shift+7 and Cmd+Shift+8 (section 2.3) have only the broken toolbar rows `text.list.bulleted-toolbar` and `text.list.numbered-toolbar` (audit-text rows 38 and 39) and the one menu row `text.list.bulleted-menu-preset`; `toolbar.insertLink` has no row beside `text.link.cmd-k-enter`; `format.formatOptions` on a text box has no row; `slide.moveSlide.toBeginning` and `.toEnd` by their chords have no row (`slides.reorder.cmd-up-down` covers one step, `slides.reorder.menu-to-end-undo` the menu); `view.showSpeakerNotes` has no toggle row (the four `slides.notes.*` rows type, switch, resize and reload).

One more coverage point about numbers. The seller "retypes a name and a few numbers", and the research names the pricing slide and "any slide with numbers" as what changes per prospect. Section 2.2 keeps all 21 layouts in the core set, and the audit shows what they place: `Title and table` puts a table with runs `table/rows/0/cells/0` and so on, `Ruled rows` and `Ruled statement list` put key and value runs, `Status board` puts `board/rows/0/name` and `board/rows/0/note`, `Big number` puts `big/text` (audit-slides rows 44 to 55). Those runs are where a seller's numbers live. No `text.*` row types into any of them; audit-text drove the title, the subtitle and a text box only. Section 3.2 parks `insert.table` and every `format.table.*` row and section 3.3 parks the table tail, which is right for a seller who never inserts a table, but the document does not say what a seller can do with a table a core layout has placed: type in a cell, or not, and add a row, or not. That sentence is missing, and the rows that would prove it are missing.

## 2. Does the parked set send a seller back to Google Slides

Nothing in section 3 is in the seller's weekly paragraph. The parked rows that appear in the research's wider task table are these, with the judgment on each:

- Tables and charts (`insert.table`, `insert.chart.*`, `format.table.*`, `format.chartType.*`, `format.editData`; open question 2). A seller who tailors an existing deck rarely inserts a table or a chart; the research's complaint is about editing the numbers of a chart that is already there. Parking Insert > Table and Insert > Chart does not send a seller back to Google Slides. A chart that a deck already carries does: with `format.editData` parked, the only way to change its numbers is the switch. The answer to open question 2 from this side is: park Insert > Table and Insert > Chart, treat the table a core layout places as text (the rows of section 1 above), and say in section 3 that a chart inside a deck is edited under Advanced tools until a charts audit runs.
- `file.makeCopy.selected` (research rule 19, "Selected slides"). The seller's way to send a subset is Skip slide plus the PDF, and both are core. Parking is acceptable; the document should name rule 19 as the departure.
- `arrange.group`, `arrange.ungroup` (the research's "Align and arrange objects" row and the shortcut table). audit-arrange rows 9 to 13 passed on the first try. A seller who moves a logo and its caption together will miss them, but finishes with a marquee. Section 8 already names them the first rows to return.
- `toolbar.paintFormat` (the research's "Copy formatting between objects" row and design rule 3). Not weekly; the research's own frustration table says Paint format "copies too little". Parking is fine.
- `insert.slideNumbers` (the research's "Number slides" row, "a small task but a common one before export"). Not weekly. Parking is fine; name the departure.
- `view.gridView` (the research's "Reorder slides" row). The tile does not drag (audit-slides row 78), so hiding it is the rule applied. Fine.
- `format.alignIndent.justified` (the shortcut table's Cmd+Shift+J). Not weekly. Fine.

What would send a seller back to Google Slides today is not a parked feature. It is the core set's broken rows: every link is an edit link (audit-present rows 26 and 29), Cmd+K puts the URL into the caption (audit-text row 43), a logo cannot be uploaded while a write is pending and cannot replace an existing one by upload (audit-images rows 6, 7, 8 and 57), and an undo of a saved delete can be refused with a JSON dialog (audit-slides rows 93, 95 and 96). Section 5 ranks all four in its first six rows, which is the right order for a seller.

## 3. Is the wording plain

Mostly. The sections read as full sentences in sentence case, every row is named by its id, and the "Today" sentences report what happened. Five terms break the copy rules the round set:

- "green" for a row that passed, eleven times (`FOCUS.md` lines 19, 22, 166, 226, 230, 618, 625). The introduction promises to keep the audits' words, which are works, broken, flaky and not driven. "Passed" or "works" is the word.
- "safety net" (line 89) is a metaphor. "Version history restores an earlier version" says it.
- "Lanes" and "integrator seams" (line 170 and the Lane column of section 5) are the round's internal names. A reader outside the round cannot resolve "B2" or "seams"; name the area of the code or the team.
- "the cheapest rows to bring back" (lines 137 and 616). "The least work to bring back" is plain.
- Section 7's heading "What works today" over sentences that describe production after section 5's fixes (see rejection 12 below).

The rest of the document's vocabulary matches research 07's naming table: Slideshow, Presenter view, Share, Copy link, Download, Version history, Replace image, Format options, Change background, Search the menus. Section 7's README draft uses the same words. No engineering noun of the research's retire list appears in section 7.

## 6. Notes

- Section 5's ranking is right for a seller. Ranks 1 to 6 are the four things that stop a seller in the first week: the link that edits, the link that garbles the caption, the picture that will not land or replace, and the undo that the server refuses. Section 2's "Today" sentences and section 5's mechanisms match the audits row for row on every entry checked (audit-decks summary line 11 and rows 34 to 37 and 51; audit-slides rows 59 to 64, 88 to 96, 104 to 106; audit-text rows 21, 28, 34, 38, 39, 43, 54; audit-images rows 1, 6 to 8, 34, 57; audit-arrange rows 23, 25, 41, 42, 47, 48, 56 to 59, 63; audit-present rows 26, 29, 31, 33, 35, 44; audit-export rows 15, 31, 32, 35, 39).
- The counts of section 6.3 hold against `core-matrix.json`: 353 rows, 274 works, 40 broken, 21 flaky, 18 not driven; the severity split 12, 46, 17 and 4 matches the file's `severity` field, which is present on the 79 rows that are not works. Rejection 9 is about where those severities came from, not their arithmetic.
- Open question 3 (the Later stubs hidden or disabled): hidden. A disabled row costs a seller a scan and gives nothing, and Search the menus stays clean.
- Open question 4 (a rectangle and an ellipse without the rounded rectangle): worth keeping. The seller's weekly shape is a highlight box on a screenshot and a line or an arrow between two boxes (audit-shapes rows 9 and 25 pass, row 45 not driven); a rounded corner is not what a seller stops for.
- Open question 5 (the Font control): keep it visible and disabled with its sentence "The GT theme sets Inter" as the one exception to hidden, not disabled. A seller who pastes from an email and sees the font change will look at that box, and the sentence answers the question; a hidden box does not.
- The shapes decision of section 4 reads right from this side: a seller who picks a callout and gets a box is worse off than one who finds no callout (audit-shapes summary line 15).
- The GT brand deck's contents were not checked here; whether the company template carries tables or charts decides how much addition 2 matters and is one question for Kevin.
- Sales conventions the document keeps and should not lose in revision: no confirmation on delete with Undo in the snackbar (audit-slides row 17), notes and skipped slides off by default in every export dialog (audit-export rows 9 and 10), Slideshow and Share at the top right (audit-decks row 15), Presenter view as a second window (audit-present row 14).

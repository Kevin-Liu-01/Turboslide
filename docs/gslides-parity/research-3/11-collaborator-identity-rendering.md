## Decisions in one page

1. One identity mark per principal, monochrome, on every surface. The mark is the avatar of report 03 Part I2 (initials on a Bayer dithered plate, or the glyph field), drawn by one function at 24, 16 and 14 px. It appears in the avatar row, on the filmstrip card, in the caret's and pointer's name flag, at the corner of the "being edited by" outline, on the comment card, in version history and on the Show changes overlay. It is the primary channel; a person tells collaborators apart by reading it.
2. Hue is a second channel for live surfaces only, and it is the one sanctioned colour exception of this round. A fixed palette of six hues in the luminance band 0.1064 to 0.2622 (section 2) colours the remote caret, the remote pointer, the remote selection outline, the Following ring and a 2 px live stripe on a present collaborator's chip. Hue never appears on a stored surface (comments, the version list, Show changes), because a hue belongs to a session in a room and those surfaces attribute work to a principal across sessions.
3. A mark is never colour alone. Every hued element carries the initials chip and the name flag; every hue passes 3:1 against `#070707`, `#ffffff` and `#f2f2f0`, so it holds on both papers and both inks; over a dithered picture the mark carries a two ring halo (1 px `#070707` inside 1 px `#ffffff`) because no colour passes 3:1 against a 50 percent Bayer field's mean in both themes (brand blue reaches 2.96 and 2.65).
4. Assignment is hash preferred, room resolved. The preferred slot is `sha256(principalId) mod 6`; on joining a room the roster grants the preferred slot if free, else the least used slot with the lowest index; the grant lives as long as the roster entry (120 s past the last heartbeat, report 02 Part 4), so a reload keeps the hue. Above six live people hues repeat and the chip disambiguates; live pointers stop at 20 (report 02 Part 4).
5. Agents are monochrome and dashed. An agent's chip is a 50 percent Bayer field with a centred solid square and a dashed border; its outline and flag are dashed ink; it takes no hue and draws no pointer. A person can never mistake an agent for a colleague.
6. The anonymous label is `<Material> <NNN>`: a word from a closed list of 64 material and element names owned by the theme (no animals, no given names, cross lingual cognates) and a number 100 to 999 with a deny list, both derived from the principal id. The space is 57,280 labels (64 words by 895 numbers); twenty people in one room collide with probability 0.33 percent; a collision inside one deck's history renders a "(2)" suffix. Version history attributes per label, never collectively. After Forget this browser the person is a new label; the old edits keep the old one.
7. Every name says what it is. A generated label reads as a label; a self declared name carries the word "guest" in `--pt-ink-2` after it; a verified account carries the check badge glyph and the email in the tooltip. Reserved names, UTS #39 confusable and mixed script checks, bidi control stripping and the 40 code point cap are server rules handed to report 10 and stated in section 5.
8. The filmstrip marker is a chip inside the card frame, not a highlight around it: the frame's rings already mean selected and current, a highlight is colour alone, and a dithered thumbnail swallows a ring.
9. Every presence surface has a reserved slot: `--pt-presence-w: 184px` in the title row, a fixed 120 px name flag, a 20 px minimum comment count chip in tabular figures, an absolute Following plate over the stage. Section 8 lists the rows for report 05's verifier.
10. Acceptance is arithmetic: a unit test recomputes the WCAG ratios of the six hues against the three solids and the pairwise Lab distances under Machado's protan and deutan matrices, and fails below 3:1 and 20 ΔE; a Playwright walk with twenty simulated collaborators over `/edit/gt-brand` on the `mood-compass` slide in both themes screenshots every surface and asserts zero layout shift entries; the label generator is tested for determinism, collisions and word lists.

## 2 The constraint and the options

Turboslide's chrome is monochrome: `--pt-ink #070707`, `--pt-paper #ffffff`, `--pt-ink-2 #3a3d44`, `--pt-titanium #8a8f98`, hairlines at 18 and 9 percent of ink, radius 0 everywhere but the search pill and segmented controls, Inter as the one face (`packages/chrome/src/tokens.css:18-59`); dark is a remap to paper `#070707`, ink `#f2f2f0`, ink-2 `#b9bcc3`, titanium unchanged, hair at 22 and 10 percent (`tokens.css:92-103`). The sheet carries the same nine tokens per theme (`packages/theme/src/tokens.ts:26-51`) and the four semantic hues `ok #12a37a`, `warn #f0a020`, `no #e5484d`, `info #2f5ce0`, the same in both themes, allowed on icons only ("Semantic color lives only here", `packages/theme/src/gt-ink-paper/sheet.css:382-401`); brand blue `#2f5ce0` is `GT_PALETTE.blue` (`packages/materials/src/presets.ts:21`). The overlay draws only chrome tokens: hover in `--pt-hair`, the selection ring in `--pt-ink` "as a state", handles hair at rest and ink while dragged (`packages/chrome/src/Overlay.css:1-25, 98-161`). A two tone picture is a field of `#070707` and `#ffffff` cells in light and `#070707` and `#f2f2f0` in dark, each cell 2 sheet pixels (`packages/effects/src/ramp.ts:11-14`, report 06 §4.2 stage 6), so a mark over a mood or opener slide sits on a checker of the two extremes.

### 2.1 The arithmetic

WCAG 2.2 defines relative luminance as `L = 0.2126 R + 0.7152 G + 0.0722 B` over linearised sRGB channels, `R = RsRGB / 12.92` when `RsRGB <= 0.04045` and `((RsRGB + 0.055) / 1.055) ^ 2.4` otherwise, and contrast as `(L1 + 0.05) / (L2 + 0.05)`, "where L1 is the relative luminance of the lighter of the colors", ranging "from 1 to 21" (W2). 1.4.3 asks 4.5:1 for text and 3:1 for large text (W1); 1.4.11 asks 3:1 for "User Interface Components" and "Graphical Objects" "against adjacent color(s)", where for a component "'adjacent colors' means the colors adjacent to the component", with the example that a white input with a dark border on a white page is measured against the external white (W3); 1.4.1 forbids colour as "the only visual means of conveying information" (W1). The numbers below were computed with the script `contrast.mjs` kept in the scratchpad (the same formulas; the acceptance test of section 9 recomputes them in the repository).

Two consequences before any hue is chosen. A hairline is 1.51:1 on paper and 1.80:1 on dark paper, so a chip's `--pt-hair` border is decoration and never the mark's boundary; the boundary must be the plate itself or a `--pt-edge` frame (62 percent ink, the thumbnail frame token, `tokens.css:27`). Titanium text is 3.25:1 on paper, below 1.4.3's 4.5:1, so any word this design adds beside a name (the trust word of section 5) is set in `--pt-ink-2` (10.88:1), not titanium; the save words already use titanium at 12.5 px (`packages/chrome/src/TitleRow.css:114-115`) and that is an existing exception this report does not extend.

The band that holds in both themes against both solids is L in [0.1064, 0.2622]. The band that also holds against both fields' means is [0.1064, 0.1148], eight thousandths wide: no palette fits it, and a 1 px stroke over a 2 px checker is not perceived against the mean anyway. So a hue is legible over a dithered picture only with its own boundary, and the two ring rule follows from the last two rows: a `#ffffff` ring passes against any field with mean at or below 0.30 and a `#070707` ring against any field with mean at or above 0.1064, and the two conditions together cover every density from 0 to 8 of 8. The mark on a picture therefore carries 1 px `#070707` inside 1 px `#ffffff` (absolute values, not theme tokens), and one of the two rings is a 3:1 edge whatever the field. This satisfies 1.4.11's intent, "distinguishable by people with moderately low vision" (W3), where a single ring cannot.

Brand blue passes both themes with 3.58:1 against `#070707` as its tightest pair, so it is one of the six. Of the GT semantic hues only `no` red joins it; `ok` green misses the dark ink by 0.14 and amber is a light theme colour only. No published palette was built for both a white and a near black ground, which is why this report computes its own.

How many hues a person can tell apart. Miller's 1956 review puts absolute judgement of one dimension at "2.6 bits" mean, "the magical number seven, plus or minus two"; Eriksen's figures are "3.1 bits for hue" (about 8 to 9 categories) and "2.3 bits for brightness" (about 5) (M1). Wilke's data visualisation text recommends "three to five different categories" for a qualitative scale and says that at "eight to ten different categories or more, the task of matching colors to categories becomes too burdensome", recommending direct labels beyond "about eight" (K1); Okabe and Ito's palette holds eight colours including black and was built to be "unambiguous both to colorblinds and non-colorblinds" (K2). Red green colour vision deficiency affects "One in twelve Caucasian (8%), one in 20 Asian (5%), and one in 25 African (4%) males" (K2). Under the Machado, Oliveira and Fernandes simulation at severity 1.0 (K3, matrices reproduced in the script), the pool of hues inside the band collapses to a blue to yellow axis plus lightness for protan and deutan viewers, so the count of hues a sales team can rely on is not eight; it is what the pairwise distances below say.

A designer's set of blue, red, violet, teal, olive and magenta (`#2f5ce0 #d7263d #7a3ae6 #007d7d #8e6a00 #c2007a`) scores 31.1 normal but 2.3 deutan (blue against violet) and 13.5 for red against olive, which is why the computed set carries no red and no violet: on the deutan axis they are the same colour as blue and olive. Four hues (slots 1 to 4) keep 35 ΔE under every simulation; eight drops to 18. The floors the acceptance test pins are 3:1 against the three solids and 20 ΔE pairwise under normal, protan and deutan; design-3 may swap a member for taste as long as the floors hold. In both themes the same six hex values are used; no dark remap exists because every member passes both papers and both inks.

### 2.3 Option (b): monochrome only

Channels available without hue: Bayer density of a plate through `bayer8` thresholds (`packages/effects/src/bayer.ts:16-43`; 64 levels, 8 practical steps), the glyph field of report 03 I2, a numeral, and stroke style. What each yields when a mark is judged alone, which is how a caret across the canvas is judged:

- Density. A 24 px chip at 2 px cells holds 12 by 12 cells, 18 cells per eighth. Brightness judged alone carries 2.3 bits, about five categories (M1); the plate must also keep its initials legible, so the usable range is 1/8 to 4/8 with ink initials (the field mean at 4/8 is 0.501, ink on it 10.6:1); at 5/8 and above ink letters fall to 8.2, 5.8 and 3.4:1 and paper letters do not yet pass 4.5:1, so four densities is the honest count. At cursor size a 12 by 16 px arrowhead covers about 24 cells; one eighth is three cells and no density is readable.
- The glyph field. Recognisable as a figure (GitHub's identicon argument, report 03 I1), but a figure is matched, not named; a person cannot say "the person with the third glyph" while a caret moves. It distinguishes many identities in the avatar row and none at cursor size.
- Numerals. A number on the chip (the roster position) is readable at 24 px and at 14 px in a flag and disambiguates any count, but it has to be read; it is the fallback channel above six live people.
- Stroke style. Solid, dashed and dotted are three; dashed is taken by the agent (decision 5), which leaves two for people. A 1 px outline's style is legible at 24 px and at cursor size.

Count: at 24 px, judged alone, four densities times two stroke styles is eight identities before reading; at cursor size two. Hue adds a channel with six members for most viewers and about four under red green deficiency (the 22 ΔE floor). Monochrome only is enough for the avatar row and the panels, where a person reads the chip; it is not enough for a moving caret on a 1600 by 900 sheet at stage scale, where the person needs a glance channel that the flag then confirms.

### 2.4 Option (c): the hybrid, recommended

Monochrome marks everywhere (the chip of 03 I2 with the density and glyph channels), the six hue palette on live canvas and filmstrip surfaces only, the two ring halo over pictures, and the rule that a hued element is always accompanied by the initials chip and, on the canvas, the name flag. This gives the glance channel where it is needed, keeps the chrome's stored surfaces in ink and paper, confines the colour exception to sessions (a hue is granted by a room and expires with it), and satisfies 1.4.1 by construction because the chip and the flag carry the identity when the hue does not. The cost is a palette that is not the brand's: five of the six are new values that live in one file (`packages/identity/src/hues.ts`, section 7) and appear nowhere else in the system.

## 4 The anonymous label

Report 03 B3 asks for two closed sets the theme owns, a material and a number, and gives no lists. The lists and their rules:

### 4.1 The word list

Sixty four words, all materials or chemical elements, chosen so that each is a cross lingual cognate or a plain material noun, none is an animal, none needs translation to read as neutral, and none must appear as a given name in the Social Security Administration's national baby names data (any year; the files carry name, sex and count, K5) or in the LDNOOBW English list (K4); the tests of section 9 enforce the last two conditions, and the list is provisional until they run. The list is data in `packages/identity/src/labels.ts`, tested as section 9 states:

Ink, Paper, Titanium, Graphite, Carbon, Basalt, Granite, Quartz, Cobalt, Zinc, Nickel, Copper, Steel, Iron, Tin, Pewter, Marble, Linen, Cotton, Felt, Vellum, Foil, Glass, Mica, Shale, Clay, Brass, Bronze, Chrome, Nylon, Cork, Wax, Resin, Enamel, Lacquer, Gesso, Plaster, Cement, Gravel, Pumice, Tungsten, Vanadium, Chromium, Manganese, Platinum, Palladium, Iridium, Osmium, Rhodium, Bismuth, Gallium, Indium, Tantalum, Niobium, Lithium, Magnesium, Silicon, Boron, Argon, Neon, Krypton, Xenon, Helium, Silica.

Words rejected by the rules, so the reasoning is visible: Amber, Ruby, Opal, Pearl, Jade, Jasper, Ivory, Ash, Flint, Slate, Coal (Cole), Silver and Steel as surnames are borderline; Silver is dropped and Steel is kept because Steel does not appear as a given name in the SSA data at the count the test uses (section 9 states the threshold and the test fails if the data says otherwise); Aluminium is dropped for its two spellings; Lead is dropped because it reads as the verb; Sand and Card are dropped as common nouns that read as mistakes.

### 4.2 The number and the deny list

A number from 100 to 999 (900 values) minus a deny list: 187, 311, 420, 666, 911, and the two digit range is not used, which removes 14, 18, 69 and 88 without a list (the ADL records 88 as "a white supremacist numerical code for 'Heil Hitler'", often combined with 14 as 1488, K6). The label is `<Word> <NNN>`, for example "Titanium 471".

### 4.3 Derivation and the collision space

`sha256(principalId)`: bytes 0 to 3 as a 32 bit integer mod 64 select the word; bytes 4 to 7 mod 895 select the number from the allowed set (900 minus 5). The space is 64 × 895 = 57,280 labels. With n people in one room the collision probability is about `1 - exp(-n(n-1) / 2N)`:

| Space                                     | Two people in twenty collide | Two people in one hundred collide |
| ----------------------------------------- | ---------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| 48 words × 90 two digit numbers = 4,320   | 4.30 percent                 | 68.2 percent                      |
| 64 × 90 = 5,760                           | 3.24 percent                 | 57.7 percent                      |
| 64 × 900 = 57,600                         | 0.33 percent                 | 8.2 percent                       |
| 64 words × 24 second words × 90 = 138,240 | 0.14 percent                 | 3.5 percent                       |

Three digits are what make the two token label viable; a third token would read as a sentence and overflow the 120 px flag. Two people with one label in one room or one deck history are told apart by their marks (the density and glyph come from other hash bytes) and by a "(2)" suffix that the renderer adds to the second principal's label within that deck, ordered by first appearance in the deck's version log; the suffix is display only and never stored.

### 4.4 Stability, version history and Forget this browser

The label is a pure function of `principalId`, and `principalId` is the server sealed `__Host-` cookie of report 03 B2 and J item 1. Version records carry the principal (03 J item 1 adds `principalId` to `Author`) and the renderer computes the label and the mark at render time (03 E2's alias resolution), so a record written by an anonymous person reads "Titanium 471" in version history with the same chip as the avatar row showed while the person was present. Google files anonymous edits under "All anonymous users" (01 §6, single source); research/10 C3 item 2 asks that two people be two labels; this design follows research/10 because the principal is stable, and it says so in the panel's tooltip: "Titanium 471 · not signed in". When the person later signs in, the alias table maps the principal to the account and old records render the account's name with the check badge (03 E2). After Forget this browser (03 Part F) the cookie is replaced: edits made afterwards carry a new principal and a new label, the old edits keep the old label forever, and nothing links the two, which is the privacy meaning of the button. Comments follow the same rule; a comment left as "Titanium 471" stays "Titanium 471".

### 4.5 A typed name never looks like a label

The server refuses a self declared name that matches the label grammar (a list word followed by a space and three digits) or that is a list word alone, so nobody can pass as generated (section 5.3). The generated label is offered prefilled in the prompt of 03 B3 ("How should others see you?") and a person who keeps it keeps a label, not a name.

### 5.3 Reserved names and the server rules, handed to report 10

The server validates a display name on every write that sets it (`account.setName`, the prompt of 03 B3) and on every `Author` it derives; `authorSchema` today requires only a non empty string (`mutations.ts:159-163`), so all of this is new. Report 10 owns the implementation and the tests; the rules are:

1. Normalise to NFC; strip every character of General_Category Cf and every Default_Ignorable code point, which removes the bidi controls LRE U+202A, RLE U+202B, PDF U+202C, LRO U+202D, RLO U+202E, LRI U+2066, RLI U+2067, FSI U+2068, PDI U+2069 and the marks LRM U+200E, RLM U+200F, ALM U+061C (UAX #9 revision 51, U1); refuse rather than repair a string that still contains an unpaired isolate or override after stripping (the Trojan Source defence: "throw errors or warnings for unterminated bidirectional control characters", U3). Collapse runs of whitespace to one space; trim; refuse leading or trailing punctuation.
2. Length at most 40 code points after normalisation (03 B3), at least 1 letter or digit.
3. UTS #39 restriction level Moderately Restrictive (section 5.2 of the report: "Latin and any one other Recommended script, except Cyrillic, Greek"), with Identifier_Status Allowed characters only (U2); apply the mixed number check (UTS #39 5.3) so "Maya 1٢3" is refused.
4. Reserved names, compared by the UTS #39 skeleton (confusables.txt, U2) after case folding: `turboslide`, `admin`, `owner`, `agent`, `system`, `presenter`, `studio`, `anonymous`, `you`, `me`, `everyone`, any string beginning `agent:`, every word of the label list alone, and any string matching the label grammar of 4.5.
5. Per deck uniqueness by skeleton: a typed name whose skeleton equals the skeleton of the deck owner's display name, or of any name present in the deck's roster or version log under a different principal, is refused with "That name is already in use in this presentation"; the person may add a distinguishing word. This is the impersonation rule: "Kevin Liu" spelled with a Cyrillic а is the same skeleton as "Kevin Liu" and is refused.
6. Refuse a name that appears in the LDNOOBW list for the request's `Accept-Language` languages plus English (K4); the check is a word boundary match on the skeleton.
7. Names are text everywhere: React text nodes in the chrome, `textContent` in the overlay, `title` attributes for tooltips, JSON strings on the agent surface; no name enters `innerHTML`, a URL, a CSS value or an SVG attribute unescaped (report 04 8.1 and 8.4).

For the chrome these rules mean that a name is at most 40 code points, contains no control characters, and is drawn at a fixed width with an ellipsis, so no surface needs to size itself to a name.

- Idle: the flag fades to opacity 0 over `--pt-dur-toast` after 3 s without a caret change and returns on the next change; the caret dims to opacity 0.5 after 30 s without any presence update (report 02's stale window) and leaves with the roster entry at 120 s. Under reduced motion opacities switch without transition.
- The own caret is the native caret and has no flag.

### 6.6 The pointer

- Shape: an SVG polygon in a 12 by 16 box, tip at the pointer position, points (0,0) (12,10) (7,11) (10,16) (8,16) (5,12) (0,16), filled in the hue with the two ring halo as strokes (1 px `#070707` inside, 1 px `#ffffff` outside, `stroke-alignment` emulated by two polygons). It is Turboslide's own polygon, not Google's arrow.
- Flag: the flag of 6.5 with its top left at (14, 16) from the tip.
- Motion: `transform: translate3d` from the last received position to the new one over 80 ms linear (report 02's batch interval), so a 12 Hz stream reads as continuous; under reduced motion the pointer snaps. Pointers hide in present mode and above 20 live participants (01 §1, 02 Part 4).
- The own pointer: never drawn. Its state is the toolbar toggle at the right end, the `cursor-arrow-rays` glyph (`icons.tsx:562`) as a `.pt-ib.pt-icon` with `aria-pressed`: at rest (off) the glyph in `--pt-ink-2`; on, the `is-on` treatment (ink text and ink border, `tokens.css:237-240`) and the tooltip "Showing my pointer"; off, "Show my pointer". View > Live pointers carries the two menu items of 01 §1 with the same state; the setting is per deck in the presence record, off by default, and requires an editor role.

### 6.7 The "being edited by" selection outline

- A 1 px outline in the hue at the block's box edge, drawn from the remote selection's block ids through the same box measurement the own ring uses; for a positioned block it turns with `pos` as the own ring does (`Overlay.css:258-267`). Over a picture (a block on an `is-picture` slide, `packages/theme/src/gt-ink-paper/stage.css:8-35`, or a picture block with a dither under it) the outline gains the 1 px `#070707` companion inside it.
- The flag of 6.5 at the box's top right, outside the box, right aligned, bottom aligned to the box's top; the own chip sits above the box's top left, or below it when there is no room above (`chipStyle`, `Overlay.tsx:61-67`), so the two never overlap. When two or more people select one block, one outline is drawn in the first holder's hue and the flags stack leftwards, at most two, then a 120 px "+N" flag.
- When the own selection and a remote selection share a block, the own ink ring wins at the box edge and the remote outline is not drawn; the remote flag stays, so the reader sees "Maya is here" without a second rule within 4 px.
- Agents: section 3.3, dashed ink, flag "Agent · <runId>".
- Ownership is presence, not a lock (report 02 7.5); the outline says who is there and forbids nothing.

### 6.8 The comment marker and the count chip

Placement, threads and the panel belong to report 08; the identity parts are fixed here. The marker is a 20 by 20 ink plate with the `chat` glyph (`icons.tsx:437`) at 12 px in paper, at the anchored object's top right outside the box or at the slide's top right for a slide comment, with the two ring halo over pictures; a count chip at its right, 16 px high, minimum width 20 px, "3" in 11 px tabular figures, ink on a paper plate with a `--pt-edge` border, drawn even when the count is 1 so opening a second thread moves nothing. The comment card in the panel: a 24 px chip, the name and trust word, the time in `--pt-ink-2`; mentions render as text with the mentioned principal's 14 px chip inline. No hue anywhere in comments.

### 6.9 Version history's per author mark and Show changes

- The version list gains a 16 px chip before the author in `.ts-version-meta` (`VersionsPanel.tsx:249-262` and `:385-388`) and in the history panel's meta line (`HistoryPanel.tsx:56-60`); the suppression of the default `studio` author (`VersionsPanel.tsx:255`) goes once every author carries a principal. The author reads with its trust word.
- Show changes (Later in SPEC 2.1's File table; the checkbox exists at `VersionsPanel.tsx:102`): a canvas overlay from `diff.run` between the selected version and its predecessor. Each changed block gets a hatched plate: 1 px `--pt-hair` lines at 45 degrees on a 6 px pitch, a 1 px `--pt-ink` outline, and the author's 16 px chip at the block's top right outside the box. A second author in the same version hatches at 135 degrees, a third at 0, a fourth at 90, a fifth with a 25 percent Bayer field; beyond five the chip alone distinguishes. A removed block is a hatched ghost at its last box with a diagonal strike; an inserted block has the outline at 2 px; a text edit underlines the changed runs 2 px in ink for an insert and strikes them for a delete, with the chip at the run's end. No hue, for the reason in decision 2: a version's authors have no session in the room.

### 6.10 Reduced motion and both themes in one line each

Reduced motion: no interpolation on the pointer, no fades on flags, the Following plate and joining chips; every state switch is immediate. Themes: every token swaps by the existing remap; the six hues and the two halo values do not swap; the halo makes the white ring visible on dark paper and the dark ring visible on light paper, which is intended.

## 7 Rendering

### 7.1 One function

A new package `packages/identity` (or `packages/effects/src/identity.ts` if a package is judged too much), browser safe, with no dependency beyond `@turboslide/effects` for `bayer8`, `ditherGray` and `encodePng1`:

```
markSpec(identity): MarkSpec
  { variant: 'initials' | 'glyph' | 'picture' | 'agent', initials, density: 1..4, glyphSeed, pictureUrl?,
    presenter: boolean, self: boolean, hue: { slot: 1..6, hex } | null, trust: 'label' | 'guest' | 'verified' | 'agent' }
renderMarkSvg(spec, size: 24 | 16 | 14, theme, { halo, live }): string      // inline SVG for the chrome and the overlay
renderMarkBits(spec, size, theme): BitImage                                 // one bit raster, cells at 2 px
renderMarkPng1(spec, size, theme): Uint8Array                               // encodePng1(bits, { palette: [ink, paper] })
labelFor(principalId): string                                               // section 4
hueFor(slot): string                                                        // the six values, section 2.2
```

`encodePng1(bits, options)` exists with a two entry palette option (`packages/effects/src/png1.ts:10-15, 47`), so the PNG path is the SVG path's cell grid packed to one bit; the same `BitImage` drives both, which is what 03 I2 asked for ("the same function renders SVG for the UI and a 1 bit PNG"). The SVG draws one `rect` per lit cell with `shape-rendering: crispEdges`, at most 121 rects at 24 px, so a row of five chips is under a kilobyte of markup. The chrome's `<IdentityChip>` component, the overlay's flags, the filmstrip's markers and the CLI's `--avatar-png` output all call these.

### 7.2 Nothing reaches an export

Presence is not document data and never enters the revision log or the Blob store (report 02 Part 4). The exporters render the document through the render worker with no session, so no caret, pointer, outline, avatar or Following plate can appear in PPTX, PDF, HTML, TXT or JPEG. Comment markers are document data and their export treatment is report 08's; this report only fixes that a marker's chip, if exported, is the 16 px one bit PNG of 7.1 so the export shows the same mark a person saw. The Playwright walk of section 9 is the one place marks are captured, and it captures the editor, not an export.

### 7.3 What `presence.list` and `account.me` return

Report 02 7.9 adds `presence.list`, `presence.follow`, `presence.unfollow` and `sync.status` to the action table and `describe().state.presence` to the window handler; report 03 J adds `account.*`. So that an agent reads the same mark a person sees, the roster entry carries the computed mark, not the inputs to it:

```
presence.list -> {
  deckId, cap: 20, pointersVisible: boolean,
  self: Participant, others: Participant[]
}
Participant {
  clientId, principalId, kind: 'human' | 'agent',

}
account.me -> {
  principalId, kind, label, trust, email?, mark: MarkSpec,

```

The CLI prints `turboslide presence list` as a table (chip variant, label, trust, slot, slide) and `turboslide account me --avatar-png me.png` writes the 256 px one bit PNG; the MCP server exposes the same two reads; `window.turboslide.studio.describe().state.presence` is the same array.

## 9 Acceptance

1. `packages/identity/src/hues.test.ts`: recompute the WCAG 2.2 luminance and contrast of the six hues (2.2) against `#070707`, `#ffffff` and `#f2f2f0` with the formulas of W2 and fail below 3.0; recompute the flag text pair (paper on ink) and fail below 4.5; recompute the pairwise CIE76 distances of the six under identity, protan and deutan (the Machado matrices pinned as data with their source, K3) and fail below 20; assert the two halo values are exactly `#070707` and `#ffffff`; assert the trust word's colour token composes to at least 4.5:1 on both papers (`--pt-ink-2`, 10.88 and 10.59). The test also asserts that `--pt-hair` composed on paper is below 3:1, so nobody later makes it a chip's boundary.
2. `packages/identity/src/mark.test.ts`: for 1,000 random principal ids, `renderMarkBits` at 24, 16 and 14 px lights the same cells in SVG and PNG (decode the PNG with the package's own reader used by `png1.test.ts`); densities fall in 1/8 to 4/8; the agent spec lights exactly 50 percent; the glyph field is mirror symmetric.
3. `packages/identity/src/labels.test.ts`: `labelFor` is deterministic and matches the grammar; the 64 words are each absent from the LDNOOBW `en` list (K4, vendored with its licence) and absent from the SSA national names files at 5 or more occurrences in any year (K5, downloaded by the test's fixture script and pinned by hash; the test states the threshold it used); no number in the deny list is produced over 100,000 ids; the word and number distributions over 100,000 ids pass a chi square test at the 1 percent level; two principals with one label in one deck render "Titanium 471" and "Titanium 471 (2)" in first appearance order; a typed name matching the grammar or a list word is refused by the validator of 5.3.
4. `packages/identity/src/names.test.ts` (shared with report 10): the reserved list, a Cyrillic а in "Kevin Liu" against the owner's name, each bidi control of 5.3 item 1, a 41 code point name, mixed digits, and a name with an unterminated RLO are refused; 40 code points of Latin plus Han are accepted.
5. `apps/studio/e2e/presence.spec.ts` (Playwright): open `/edit/gt-brand` on the `mood-compass` slide (`decks/gt-brand/slides/mood-compass.json`, kind `mood` with a cover picture) in light and dark; drive twenty simulated participants through the presence channel (a test hook that posts presence batches for twenty client ids with names of 1 to 40 code points, one agent and one presenter among them, selections across the plate's blocks and carets inside the paragraph); screenshot the avatar row (expect four chips and "+16"), the roster menu, the filmstrip cards for the slides they hold, the canvas with twenty flags, the outline on the plate's paragraph with two flags and "+1", the Following plate, the pointer toggle in both states, the version list with three trust states, and Show changes on a two author version; assert hue slots cycle so that slots 1 to 6 hold four, four, three, three, three and three participants; assert every flag measures 120 by 18; run the layout shift observer of report 05 5.2 for the whole walk and assert zero entries; compare the screenshots against golden files in both themes at 1x and 2x.
6. `pnpm check` gains the four unit files and the spec as steps; the chrome lint (`turboslide lint --chrome`, `SPEC.md:100`) is taught that `.ts-flag`, `.ts-remote-outline`, `.ts-remote-pointer` and `.ts-following` may carry the six hues and the two halo values and nothing else may.

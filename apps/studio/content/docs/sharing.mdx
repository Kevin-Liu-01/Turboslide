## Share by link

<Steps>
  <Step title="Choose who can open it">
    Under General access, choose Restricted or Anyone with the link. Restricted gives access only to
    you and the people that you add.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose what they can do">Beside it, choose Viewer, Commenter or Editor.</Step>
  <Step title="Copy the link">
    Click Copy link and send the address. The first Copy link makes the address. A second Copy link
    copies the same address.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Figure
  name="share-dialog"

  caption="The sharing dialog with Anyone with the link and Viewer chosen."

| Role      | Can do                                                   | Version history |
| --------- | -------------------------------------------------------- | --------------- |
| Viewer    | Open and present the presentation.                       | No              |
| Commenter | Everything a viewer can do, and add and answer comments. | No              |
| Editor    | Everything a commenter can do, and change the slides.    | Yes             |

Only editors and the owner see the speaker notes. Commenters, editors and the owner see skipped slides. A viewer's copy of the presentation leaves out the skipped slides.

Under More, the dialog also makes three fixed links: a view link, a present link that opens the slideshow and an edit link. You can rotate or revoke each link, so that an old address stops working. A link never lets its holder share the presentation, change the sharing settings, or trash or remove the presentation.

## Share with people by name

When you are signed in, click More in the sharing dialog. Add people by email and pick a role for each person. The people list shows the owner first, then each person with their role. Use the menu on a person's row to change the role, set an expiration date or remove access.

A person without access who opens a restricted presentation sees a page where they can request access. The owner sees the request in the sharing dialog and can approve it there. A request that nobody answers expires after 30 days.

The owner can always share. An editor can share while the setting **Editors can change permissions and share** is on. This setting is one of the five settings under the gear in the sharing dialog.

## What other people see

A person who may not share sees only their own access and their own request in the people list. They see no links, no pending owner and no address of another person. This applies to every viewer and commenter, to everyone who opened the presentation by a link, and to editors while editors cannot share.

When a response names another person, such a reader gets a stand-in in place of that person's id. A stand-in is `ref_` followed by 22 characters. The stand-in for one person stays the same on one presentation and is different on every other presentation. Version authors, comment authors, the people who have the presentation open and the activity rows use the same stand-ins.

## Version history and copies

Viewers and commenters cannot open version history. The same rule applies to an API key with their access. Such a key cannot list versions, watch the changes of the presentation or compare two revisions. A viewer's live connection starts from the presentation as it was handed over, with no earlier changes. Editors can open version history. An editor who may not share sees the other authors as stand-ins.

Make a copy with Copy comments needs the right to share. Without that right, Make a copy shows "Only people who may share this presentation can copy its comments. Make the copy without Copy comments."

## Pictures in a restricted presentation

The studio serves the thumbnails of a restricted presentation only to people who can open it. Only their own browser keeps a copy. The studio serves its pictures under the presentation's own key or to its readers. A request for the same picture by its file name returns not found.

## Work together

Several people can edit one presentation at the same time. Changes from other people appear as they make them. The title row shows a chip for each person who has the presentation open. View > Live pointers shows where their pointers are on the slide.

Click a person's chip to go to the slide that they have open. To keep following them, click the people button after the chips and choose Follow on their row. The people button reads Collaborators, or +N when more than four people are present. While you follow, the stage shows Following, their name and Stop. Every editor and owner can follow, including people who opened an edit link without an account. An agent cannot follow.

## Comments

To comment on an object, some text or a table cell, select it and choose Insert > Comment (Cmd+Option+M). You can also comment on the whole slide. View > Comments shows, expands, minimizes or hides the comments. The Comments panel lists every comment with its replies.

## Signing in

You do not need an account to make or present a presentation. On turboslide.com, you sign in with Continue with Google. Signing in adds three things:

- You can open your presentations on another computer.
- You can share a presentation with people by name.
- Your name and picture show to the people you work with.

If this browser made presentations before you signed in, the sign in page asks what to do with them. For example, it asks "This browser has 3 decks made before you signed in. Add them to name@example.com?" Add Them moves the presentations to your account. Keep Them Separate keeps them only in this browser, and the page does not ask again. When you sign out, this browser keeps the presentations that it made on its own.

A presentation made without an account belongs to this browser. If you clear the site data of the browser, you lose access to it. To keep a copy, download a Turboslide file under File > Download.

Your profile lists your sessions and your API keys. Sign out everywhere else signs out your other devices within a minute. This browser stays signed in.

Delete account removes the account after you confirm. Every presentation that the account owns moves to the trash. Turboslide does not remove it from the trash on a schedule. The admin of the studio removes it permanently. The account's access to other people's presentations and its profile picture are removed.

<Callout title="On a studio that signs in by email">
  The link and the six digit code in a sign in email sign in only the browser that asked for them. A
  link opened in another browser asks for the eight character code that the first browser shows
  under Check your email. Both expire in 5 minutes.
</Callout>

## Share from an agent

The actions of the sharing dialog also run on the command line, over MCP and over HTTP. An agent acts as the person who owns its API key. Each write is recorded under that person as `agent:<tokenId>`. The scopes of the key limit what it can do. The scopes are read, comment, write, export, share and admin. A key without the share scope gets the ids of other people as stand-ins.

```sh
turboslide share access <id> --mode link --role viewer
turboslide share invite <id> --email name@example.com --role commenter
turboslide share get <id>
```

The `x-turboslide-author` header and `--author` only label a run. They do not change the person under whom the write is recorded.

# The sync architecture as built, and its defects

The research pass of the realtime round, area "the sync architecture as built and its defects". Written 2026-10-01 against the worktree `Turboslide-realtime` at `f50b209d` (`origin/main`). Every line number below is that tree's. Production is `https://www.turboslide.com`, the team project `turboslide-gt`, serving `241c1df1` since 05:30Z on 2026-10-01 (`docs/gslides-parity/focus/VERIFICATION.md` PT.2), with `TURBOSLIDE_REALTIME=blob`, `TURBOSLIDE_AUTHORIZE=shadow`, no `REDIS_URL` and no `DATABASE_URL`. The measurements are the ledgers' own, each named with its date: the ordering and costs audits of 2026-09-20 (`docs/gslides-parity/sync/audit-ordering.md`, `audit-costs.md`), the sync round's production run of 2026-09-22 (`docs/gslides-parity/sync/build/ship.md` section 9), the collab audit of 2026-09-28 (`docs/gslides-parity/polish/audit-collab.md`), the people round's production table of 2026-09-30 and the polish round's of 2026-10-01 (VERIFICATION.md PP and PT). Prices were read on 2026-10-01 from the pages named in section 4. Nothing on the store, on Vercel or on GitHub was changed by this pass; the one command run was `vitest run src/redis.test.ts` in `packages/realtime` (17 of 17 passed).

The short version. The log, the commit point and the idempotency are sound. The lag is the transport around them: on the blob tier there is no channel between instances, so every remote change travels as a store write, a poll of a pulse object once every 2 s (10 s for a tab alone), a manifest head and a pull; every cross instance fact (the roster, the link index, the identity, the access record) is a record on the Blob store with a per instance cache of 5 s in front of it. A word from A reaches B in about 2 s on average and up to 5 s, a joiner's chip in 2.6 to 9.9 s, and a save takes 1.1 to 3.7 s. The redis tier in the code replaces the poll with pub/sub and the per instance presence with one roster, has a contention test that holds 1,000 appends from two instances under 50 ms each, and has never run outside the in process fake.

## 1. The model in one page

### 1.1 The parts

- One ordered log per deck: `versions/<n>.json` on the Blob store, one record per commit, the record's `revision` is the sequence (`packages/realtime/src/blob.ts` 1 to 5 and 187 to 200, `entryOfRecord`: `seq: record.revision`).
- The manifest `deck.json`, overwritten in place; its etag is the md5 of its bytes and names the snapshot `snapshots/<md5>.json` that proves the current document (`packages/store/src/blob-store.ts` 1327 to 1333).
- One mirror of the deck per function instance, a folder under the instance's temp volume, synced by a head of `deck.json` and a pull when the etag moved (`blob-store.ts` `syncNow` 1555 to 1571, the 750 ms window at 1183, `pull` 1260 to 1550).
- The pulse `decks/<id>/.turboslide/pulse.json`, overwritten after every commit, presence push and comments push, so one head says whether anything moved (`packages/store/src/pulse.ts` 1 to 19, `putPulse` 151 to 167, `headPulse` 170 to 173).
- The room per deck per instance, holding the live document and the channel (`apps/studio/src/server/room.ts` `createRoom` 773 to 1037); on the blob tier the room's `live()` is the mirror's document and the room's `follow` and `watch` are off (794 to 800, 890 to 891, 1009 to 1016).
- The blob channel, which turns the store into a `RealtimeChannel`: `append` is a store write, `since` reads the mirror's records, `subscribe` starts the pulse poll while a client stream is open on the instance (`blob.ts` `blobChannel` 295 to 922).
- The stream, Server-Sent Events per tab (`apps/studio/src/routes/api/decks.$deckId.stream.ts`), 240 to 290 s long with a `retry` of 1 to 4 s (`packages/realtime/src/protocol.ts` 49 to 51), a comment line every 15 s (45), one slot per tab under the tab token (`room.ts` `createStreamCounters` 2872 onward, the route at 113 to 118).
- The room client in the tab, with three sets: `pending` (applied locally, not yet in the stream), `retained` (acknowledged at a seq) and `server` (the document at `seq`), and `local` as `server` with `pending` folded on top (`packages/realtime/client/room-client.ts` 1 to 12, 560 to 561).

### 1.2 Who assigns order

The instance that admits a POST assigns the position, and the store makes it true. The reducer bumps the revision on the mirror (`blob-store.ts` `file.write` 1827), the instance claims `versions/<n>.json` with `overwrite: false` (`claimRecord` 1670 to 1702; a number another writer holds is `RecordTakenError` 1701, a claim older than `CLAIM_GRACE_MS` 60 s or the same write's own earlier claim is taken over, 1680 to 1697), and the instance commits by putting `deck.json` with `ifMatch` set to the etag it synced (`putDocument('deck.json', synced)` 1880; `putDocument` 1636 to 1641). The manifest put with `ifMatch` is the one linearization point; a precondition failure is the conflict outcome (`isBlobPreconditionError` at 1948). Two writers from one base end with one commit and one conflict; the two instance tests of `packages/store/src/hosted.test.ts` (1771, 2051, 2532) pin it, and the ordering audit measured it on production: two POSTs 165 ms apart were answered revisions 15 and 16, the second with `between` naming the first (`audit-ordering.md` 3.1).

A claim is not a commit. A record whose revision is above the proven manifest is dropped from the mirror until the manifest reaches it (`dropRecordsAbove` 1432 to 1447, the newest `CLAIMS_CHECKED` 4 by number), so no tab ever applies a write that did not commit.

### 1.3 A keystroke's path to the other tab, hop by hop

Every hop is named with its code and the latency the ledgers measured on production. A and B are two tabs of two people on two instances, the common case on fluid compute (the platform routes every request on its own; `blob.ts` 507 to 510).

1. A types. The viewer records a `text.splice` and the room client applies it to `local` at once and schedules a flush: structural ops at once, drag `pos` sets 50 ms, typing 100 ms (`room-client.ts` `FLUSH_MS` 189, `scheduleFlush` 1427 to 1441). One POST is in flight at a time (1443 to 1448); the first flush after a stream open waits for the replay to catch up (`caughtUp`, 1455); a flush needs the client id and its binding, not the stream (1452).
2. `POST /api/decks/<id>/ops` with `base: { seq }` and up to 64 entries (`protocol.ts` 26; the route `apps/studio/src/routes/api/decks.$deckId.ops.ts` 40 to 154: same origin and JSON, the caps at 65, `decideFor(write)` at 82, `admitOps` at 91). The tab keeps a record of the batch (its base, op ids and folded bytes) so a store echo can settle it before the answer (`room-client.ts` 1490 to 1498).
3. `admitOnBlob` on A's instance (`room.ts` 2094 to 2349). It brings the mirror up to at least the POST's base (`liveForBase` 1938, `liveAtLeast` 1965 to 2016: up to 6 forced syncs 500 ms apart), refuses a base more than `BASE_SEQ_WINDOW` 500 behind with 409 `resync` (2107 to 2109), reads the records between the base and the mirror's revision from the mirror with no store call (`readLanded` 2111 to 2114, `channel.since` → `recordsAfter`, `blob.ts` 355 to 372), answers any op id those records already cover without placing it (`splitReplayed` 2038 to 2060, the origin check's cheap pass), transforms each fresh entry's mutations past the landed records' mutations with the memory tier's `transformEntry` (2203 to 2205; `transformEntry` 1485), then past the undo of entries refused earlier in the POST (2211), and runs the reducer and the validator (`landCandidate` 2218).
4. `channel.append` (`blob.ts` 680 to 785), on the deck's serial queue per instance (`queued` 340 to 345). It reads the head (685; a head that moved is a miss the admission retries once, `BLOB_APPEND_RETRIES` 1, `room.ts` 2071 and 2287 to 2308), folds the batch's mutations into one list (698 to 700, `coalesce.ts` `foldMutation` 98 to 114), names the origin `{ clientId, opIds }` (707 to 710), waits out the spacing of one write per second per deck per instance (712 to 713, `minWriteSpacingMs` 297), and calls `store.write` (718 to 724).
5. `BlobStore.write` (`blob-store.ts` 1766 to 1976), the four rounds: a head of `deck.json` and a get of `leases.json` together (1771 to 1774), a pull when the mirror is behind (1781), the origin check against the records the pull proved (1807 to 1826: a write whose op ids a record above its base names is answered `replayed` with no claim and no commit), the reducer (1827), then round two, the snapshot, the claim and the changed slide bodies together (1862 to 1868), round three, the manifest put with `ifMatch` (1880), round four, the removed bodies and the shifted comment sidecars (1920 to 1935), the pulse put (1940) and the prune every twentieth record (1944). Measured as the POST's latency: 673 ms mean, 435 to 1,378 ms over 30 POSTs on 2026-09-20 (`audit-ordering.md` 3.2); 655 ms per one word session on 2026-09-28 (`audit-collab.md`, the readings table); the ordering probe on the blob tier read the acknowledgement at 656 ms mean on 2026-09-21 (VERIFICATION.md "Sync and costs round, pass 2" S.7).
6. The answer to A. `admitOnBlob` returns the admitted entries at their seq, the rejected ones with a sentence, and `between`, the records other instances committed between the tab's position and this write (`room.ts` 2335 to 2347, `BETWEEN_MAX_ENTRIES` 256 at 1341). The tab takes `between` first and the entries after (`room-client.ts` 1527 to 1528), settles its pending ops by op id and moves `seq`; the record leaves the echo list (1529). A's own instance's streams receive the op and a checkpoint at once (`blob.ts` 779 to 782), so a second tab of A's on the same instance reads the word with the POST.
7. B's instance learns of the commit from its pulse poll. While a client stream of the deck is open on B's instance, the channel heads the pulse once per tick (`blob.ts` `startPulsePoll` 501 to 675, the head at 624) and every fifteenth tick heads the manifest instead (612 to 622, `PULSE_SAFETY_TICKS` 15). The tick is 2 s while an op landed on this instance in the last 30 s, the roster holds another row, or a second stream is open here, and 10 s otherwise (`pulse.ts` `pollTickMs` 77 to 86, `pollCompany` 108 to 117; `blob.ts` `nextTickMs` 523 to 544). A moved etag reads three things together, the manifest through `store.sync(true)` with its pull, the presence record and the comments index (`readMoved` 474 to 487). The pull is one head of `deck.json`, one get of the snapshot the etag names, and one head plus one get per new record by number, with no listing (`blob-store.ts` 1286 to 1302, 1308 to 1313, 1340 to 1380); a warm mirror learning of one commit pays one hit and one miss.
8. `announce` (`blob.ts` 388 to 422) publishes an `op` and a `checkpoint` per record above the position it announced last to the streams on B's instance (405 to 406); a restore or the first record above a hole in the log travels as an external checkpoint, which every tab answers with a reload at the head (395 to 401); a revision the store reached with no record the mirror holds is announced the same way (408 to 421).
9. The stream writes the frame to B's tab (`decks.$deckId.stream.ts`, the subscription taken before hello at 211 and `filterEventForReader` on every event, `room.ts` 2566 to 2600: owners and editors receive every op, commenters and viewers receive ops with the notes stripped).
10. B's room client takes the entry (`room-client.ts` `take` 1172 to 1185): below its position it settles what it still names, at the position it applies, above it buffers by seq and drains contiguously (`drain` 1125 to 1138); a gap that nothing fills for `GAP_REOPEN_MS` 8 s reopens the stream so the replay fills it (`watchGap` 1148 to 1161). A remote op moves every pending text op past it with the schema's transform and re-derives `local` (`applyEntry` 959 to 1070, the remote path at 1028 to 1045); an entry this copy cannot apply schedules a resync at its revision (1046 to 1056).

Measured end to end: 1,948 ms mean, 662 to 3,000 ms over 30 words on 2026-09-20 (`audit-ordering.md` 3.3); 1,960 ms mean and 4,121 ms max on production on 2026-09-22, with one word of thirty at 6,376 ms in the run of record (`sync/build/ship.md` 9.3); a filmstrip reorder on B in 1.2 s and a slide added by A on B in 2.8 s on 2026-09-28 (`audit-collab.md`, the readings table). The sum is the POST (about 0.7 s), up to one tick (2 s, or 10 s for a tab the instance counts alone), and the pull (a head and one to three gets).

### 1.4 What the server transforms, and what the client does with echoes

The server transforms text ops only: `transformEntry` moves `text.splice` and `text.mark` past the landed mutations and passes every other mutation through (`room.ts` 1485 to 1510; `packages/schema/src/transform.ts`), and the reducer re-anchors or refuses the structural ones (`reanchor` 1512). The client does the same on its side (`room-client.ts` `transformPast` 516 to 533, `defaultTransform` 431). This is operational transformation for the two text ops and a rebase for everything else; a `slide.set` of a slide counts as a rewrite of every text on it (`rewritesText` 448).

The tab acknowledges by op id. A record carries `origin: { clientId, opIds }` (`blob.ts` 707 to 710; `packages/store/src/versions.ts` `RECORD_ORIGIN_WRITES` 30 is `true`, the field written since the sync round's deployment N plus 1), so its stream entry carries `clientId` and `covers` (`entryOfRecord` 187 to 200), and the tab settles every pending op the `covers` name at the entry's seq and applies the fold once (`room-client.ts` `settleByCovers` 870 to 897, called at 983); the byte match of `settleOwnEcho` (899) stays as the fallback for a record written before the round. A resent POST whose first attempt committed is answered with one synthesized entry per op id at the record's seq, on the admission's pass (`splitReplayed`) or the store's (`blob-store.ts` 1807 to 1826; `blob.ts` 750 to 765), and nothing is claimed or committed twice (`hosted.test.ts` 2532: the resend lands on an instance whose mirror never saw the first attempt and is answered with record 1).

Undo is per author: `transformSince` moves an inverse past every remote entry that landed after the op was recorded, skipping the tab's own entries by client id or by `covers` (`room-client.ts` 1970 to 1983, the markers at 2013 to 2027), over the last `RECENT_ENTRIES` 2,000 (232).

### 1.5 How a reload and a reconnect recover

A reload reads `readEditorDeck` (`apps/studio/src/server/write.ts`), which syncs the mirror by force to at least the revision the tab named (`liveAtLeast`), answers the document at the head and, when the read carries `since`, the origins of the records above it (`write.ts` 465 to 486, `originsSince` 283). The stream then opens at `since=seq` and the replay is the records above it (`room.ts` `replayFor` 2764 to 2790; a position more than `REPLAY_MAX_ENTRIES` 2,000 behind is a `resync`, `admission.ts` 85 to 89); `hello.covered` is the live seq (the stream route 225 to 236). Three reloads in parallel after 61 revisions took 1.8 to 2.1 s and read byte equal documents (`audit-ordering.md` 3.7); 1.4 to 2.8 s on production on 2026-09-22 (`ship.md` 9.3, `sync.reload.same-document`).

A reconnect after the browser's `online` event reopens the stream at once and holds the resend for the hello, so the replay transforms the pending ops before they go (`room-client.ts` `onBrowserOnline` 1861 to 1880, `holdForHello` 1083 to 1099, released by the hello, a failed reopen or `RECONNECT_HOLD_MAX_MS` 8 s at 206). A lost POST answer leaves the ops pending with their record kept for the echo and backs off from 500 ms to 8 s (1503 to 1517); a 5xx or a 429 does the same with the answer's `retry-after` (1569 to 1584); a 404 is retried `NOT_FOUND_RETRY_MAX` 5 times, since the store's head of a deck made seconds ago answers null on another instance for a while (1603 to 1614). A resync re-reads the document with `since` and drops the pending ops the answered origins name (`settleFromResync` 1215 to 1250, `resync` 1252 onward). Unacknowledged ops survive a reload in IndexedDB (`pending-store.ts`; `offerPersisted` 1797). Measured: the offline replay converged 2.8 to 6.8 s after the reconnect on production on 2026-09-22, every word kept, the resend's offset shifted past the other writer's words (`ship.md` 9.2).

## 2. The redis tier as built

### 2.1 What it changes

`selectRealtime` picks `redis` when `REDIS_URL` is set or `TURBOSLIDE_REALTIME=redis` names it (`packages/realtime/src/select.ts` 58 to 89); the studio builds the channel over an `ioredis` client with `lazyConnect: false` and `maxRetriesPerRequest: 3` (`room.ts` 150 to 166, `ioredisCommands` in `redis.ts` 58 to 95: one connection for commands and one duplicate for pub/sub).

- The sequence. One Redis stream per deck with ids `<seq>-0` and one head counter, appended by one Lua compare and append: the script reads the head, refuses when it is not the writer's base (answering the head and how many entries to read), else appends every body, sets the head and publishes one message carrying the first seq and the bodies (`packages/realtime/src/lua.ts` `APPEND_SCRIPT` 18 to 41; `redis.ts` `append` 279 to 299). The writer's loop transforms past what landed and retries, and after two misses takes the deck's append lock for 200 ms so a busy room cannot starve one writer (`packages/realtime/src/admission.ts` `appendWithRetry` 167 to 233). The memory tier's `admitOps` (`room.ts` 1693 onward) is the admission that runs on this tier; `admitOnBlob` is the blob tier's alone.
- Fan out. Every instance with an open stream subscribes once per deck to `deck:<id>:events` (`redis.ts` `subscribe` 309 to 349); a message's entries are delivered in order and a gap after a dropped message is filled from the stream with `XRANGE` (`handle` 215 to 249; the test at `redis.test.ts` 162). No poll of the store on the read path.
- Presence. A hash and a sorted set per deck with a 120 s expiry, each `set` published at once (`redis.ts` 363 to 428; `PRESENCE_EXPIRY_MS` `protocol.ts` 68), so a joiner's row reaches every instance within the message's delivery.
- The version log. The checkpointer coalesces the stream into version records: one record per author per contiguous run, after 2 s idle, 10 s at most under continuous typing, 2,000 entries or 1 MB, at once for an agent write and a named version, under `deck:<id>:ckpt` (`apps/studio/src/server/checkpoint.ts` 11 to 37; `packages/realtime/src/coalesce.ts` `coalesceEntries` 122 to 150). The stream keeps 10,000 entries behind the last checkpoint (`STREAM_RETAIN_ENTRIES` 37). Store writes therefore drop from one per POST to one per run.
- The locks, the budgets and the flags as Lua scripts (`lua.ts` 48 to 108; `redis.ts` 430 to 467); an unreachable Redis reads the `realtime` flag as off and the tabs fall to the blob tier (`redis.ts` 461 to 464; `docs/hosting.md` 726 to 729).
- The studio's other per instance stores take the same connection when it exists: the rate limiter (`apps/studio/src/server/ratelimit.ts` 14 to 21 and 510), the studio session directory (`sessions.server.ts` 22), better-auth's secondary storage (`auth/secondary-storage.ts` 3), the inbox and the notify route (`actions.ts` 787, `routes/api/notify.$.ts` 91), the principal records (`docs/hosting.md` 692 to 700). The command frame that would replace the agent session poll needs this channel and no other (`docs/sessions-polling.md` 3.4, 392 to 416, and section 5 at 644 to 660).

### 2.2 Its tests

`packages/realtime/src/redis.test.ts`, 17 tests, all against the in process fake `redis-fake.ts` (which runs the same Lua steps one command per asynchronous turn): 1,000 appends from two contending instances over 8 workers with no lost entry, every op id once, no writer over 16 attempts, the 95th percentile wait under 50 ms and the maximum under 50 ms on an unloaded machine, every listener seeing every op once in order (46 to 160); the gap fill when pub/sub drops every third message (162); the `NOSCRIPT` reload and one subscription per deck per instance (188); the flags' 5 s cache and the unreachable rule (213); the lock token and a malformed message (241). `apps/studio/src/server/checkpoint.test.ts` drives the checkpointer over the memory channel with fake timers (the idle and maximum triggers, the lock break, the follower's entries, the two author split). The run on this pass: 17 passed in 491 ms.

### 2.3 What it lacks to run on production

1. A Redis. `REDIS_URL` is set by an Upstash install from the project's Storage tab (`docs/hosting.md` 806 to 809); the account boundary says no agent installs a Marketplace product, so this is Kevin's step. The REST API cannot subscribe or block, so the channel needs the Redis protocol (`redis.ts` 1 to 4; Upstash's REST page lists `SUBSCRIBE` over Server-Sent Events and no blocking commands, https://upstash.com/docs/redis/features/restapi, read 2026-10-01).
2. A run against a real Redis. Every test meets the fake. No end to end spec names the tier (`grep` over `apps/studio/e2e` finds none; `scripts/probes/new-write-probe.mjs` and `core-walk/toolkit.mjs` only mention it), and no deployment has run with `REDIS_URL`: the production environment table of `audit-costs.md` section 2 lists no `REDIS_URL` and no Upstash pair, and no production table in VERIFICATION.md names the tier.
3. The connection shape under fluid compute. One `ioredis` client plus one subscriber connection per instance; the client is built with `lazyConnect: false` at module load (`room.ts` 156), so a cold instance connects on its first room request; nothing closes the connections when an instance is paused, which Upstash's connection count will show once it runs.
4. The blob tier's shared half is off on redis. The pulse, the presence record and the per instance link and access caches are not what the redis channel reads, but `access.ts` keeps its 5 s caches with a process local drop bus on every tier (`access.ts` 208 to 211, 212 to 234); a cross instance drop over `PUBLISH` exists for the access record on redis per `docs/hosting.md` 696 and needs reading in `access.ts` before the tier ships.
5. The checkpointer's holes. On redis the record is written after the run, so a reload between the append and the checkpoint reads a document behind the stream and the replay fills it (`createRoom` `syncLive` 801 to 827 on this tier); the memory tier's rows pass this path on every gate (VERIFICATION.md, the memory tier runs), the blob store under it with two instances does not have a test.
6. The rows. `sync.*` rows run on the memory tier and the blob tier only (`docs/SYNC.md` 6.2); a redis preview needs the same gate once, and the cost probe's `sync.status.storeCalls` reads Blob calls alone, so the Redis command count needs its own row.

### 2.4 The latency it would give

A keystroke's path becomes: the flush (0 to 100 ms), the POST, the EVALSHA append (one round trip to Redis, single digit ms from iad1 to a store in the same region), the `PUBLISH` inside the script, the subscriber's delivery on every instance (one round trip), the SSE write. The server side is one Redis round trip plus the transform where the blob tier is a four round Blob write plus up to one tick plus a pull: the POST's own time drops from about 650 ms to the function's work plus two Redis round trips, and the cross instance hop from 0 to 2 s (10 s alone) to the message's delivery. The contention test's bound is under 50 ms per append at the 95th percentile with two instances racing (`redis.test.ts` 146 to 152). A joiner's presence reaches every instance on its first `set`. A reload between the append and the checkpoint reads the store's document plus the stream's tail (`syncLive`), so it never waits on a record put. The numbers above are the test's and the design's; the first measured number comes from the gate on a redis preview (2.3 item 6).

## 3. The defects, numbered

Each with its mechanism in the code and the measurement that found it. Severity is the collab audit's scale (3 a seller calls it broken, 2 a seller notices, 1 a close look notices).

1. Two people typing in one title keep both words, but the merge lands in 2.6 to 4.6 s and the first word after a join can pass 5 s (severity 2). Mechanism: hop 7 of 1.3. The second typist's instance learns of the first's commit on its next pulse tick (2 s), and a tab the instance counts alone ticks at 10 s until a read of the presence record shows a second row (`pulse.ts` 77 to 86; `blob.ts` 523 to 544, the `wake` at 568 to 576 and `nudge` at 586 to 592 shorten it when the joiner's stream or presence POST lands on this instance, and not when both land elsewhere). Measured: 2.6, 4.1 and 4.6 s on 2026-09-28 (`audit-collab.md` item 24 and the readings table); the sync round's production run read round 1 of `sync.title.concurrent-both-kept` past 5 s twice, the first exchange after B's join each time, rounds 2 and 3 under 4.6 s (`ship.md` 9.3); the verifier's pass 2 read C's first two words at 5,072 to 5,085 ms with the quiet tick named as the cause (VERIFICATION.md S.6 F1 of "Sync and costs round, pass 2"). The row stays red on production as Kevin's standing row (PP.3, PT.4).
2. Neither typist sees the other's caret (severity 2). Mechanism: the shared presence record strips the live fields; the caret, the pointer and the selection stay per instance (`packages/store/src/presence-store.ts` `PRESENCE_OMITTED_FIELDS`, the collab audit's reading at item 14; `select.ts` 23 `BLOB_TIER_NOTICE` "Live cursors need a Redis store on this deployment"). Measured: no caret or flag for B on A's stage while both typed (`audit-collab.md` item 14, `04-r1-a-both-editing.png`); `view.live-pointers.second-browser` parked on every production table since the return round (VERIFICATION.md S.3, PP.3).
3. A joiner's chip takes 2.6 to 9.9 s, a viewer's more than 12 s (severity 2). Mechanism: the joiner's presence POST sets the row on its instance's copy and the push to the record waits for the spacing of 5 s (`presence-store.ts` `PRESENCE_PUSH_SPACING_MS` 76, `pushNow` 622 onward); the other instance reads the record on its pulse tick (2 s, or 10 s alone) or on the presence store's own 5 s poll (`PRESENCE_POLL_MS` 74, `roster` 834 to 838, `watch` 860 to 868). Three waits in series: up to 5 s, up to 2 or 10 s, and the stream write. Measured: 9.9, 2.6 and 4.8 s on 2026-09-28 (`audit-collab.md` item 13); `aSeesBMs` 10,377 against `bSeesAMs` 2 in the polish verifier's pass (VERIFICATION.md 8607: "A's instance polls the pulse every 10 s while its one tab is alone"); `collab.presence.join-within-2s` red twice on the polish preview (8305).
4. A save takes 1.1 to 3.7 s (severity 1). Mechanism: the four round write of hop 5 (a head and a get, a pull when behind, three puts in parallel, the conditional put, the pulse put), the spacing of one write per second per deck per instance (`blob.ts` 712 to 713) when two POSTs of one deck land on one instance within a second, and the 100 ms flush. Measured: "Saving…" stood 2.4, 3.6 and 2.7 s for one word, 1.05 s on the perf deck, 655 ms per one word session over thirty (`audit-collab.md` item 24 and the readings table); 673 ms mean per POST on 2026-09-20 (`audit-ordering.md` 3.2).
5. The loser of two writers waits 2 to 4 s (severity 2). Mechanism: `RecordTakenError` discards the mirror, sleeps `CLAIM_RETRY_MS` 200 ms and runs the whole write again from a fresh head and pull (`blob-store.ts` 1949 to 1957, `CLAIM_RETRY_MS` 604, `discard` 1732); the admission's retry after a moved head syncs the mirror with `liveAtLeast`'s 500 ms pauses (`room.ts` 2287 to 2308, 1962 to 1963). ## 4. The costs

## 5. What is sound and must stay, and what causes the lag

### 5.1 Sound, keep it

- The version log and the commit point: one record per commit, the claim with `overwrite: false`, the manifest put with `ifMatch`, a claim never applied until the manifest reaches it (`blob-store.ts` 1670 to 1702, 1880, 1432 to 1447). Measured correct under two writers on production and pinned by `hosted.test.ts`.
- The authority of the server: the admission transforms before it places on every tier (`room.ts` 2203 to 2205; `admitOps` on the memory and redis tiers), the reducer and the validator judge every entry, and the answer carries the placed mutations, so two browsers derive one document (`sync.block.concurrent-same-offset-order` green on production on its rerun, 1,875 to 3,886 ms; `ship.md` 9.3).
- Idempotency by origin: the record names its client and op ids, the resend is answered from the record on any instance, the tab acknowledges by id, the resync read answers the origins above the old position (`blob.ts` 707 to 710, 750 to 765; `room.ts` 2038 to 2060; `blob-store.ts` 1807 to 1826; `write.ts` 465 to 486; `room-client.ts` 1215 to 1250).
- The snapshot proof against the public store's stale CDN (`blob-store.ts` 1327 to 1333), the records read by number with no listing (1308 to 1380), the hole rule (`HOLE_WALK_LOOKAHEAD` 617; `blob.ts` 395 to 401).
- The client's sets and the reconnect order: `pending`, `retained` and `server`; the hold for the hello; `between` on the answer; the gap watch; the pending store (`room-client.ts` 560 to 561, 1083 to 1099, 1527, 1148 to 1161).
- The stream slot under the tab token and the pulse on a budget: one timed store call per tick per open deck per instance, none without a stream, the backoff on a refusal (`room.ts` 2872 onward; `blob.ts` 795 to 863, 651 to 660).

### 5.2 The causes of the lag

1. The poll. Nothing pushes between instances; a commit on A's instance reaches B's instance on B's next head of the pulse, 2 s apart while the deck has company and 10 s apart for a tab the instance counts alone (`pulse.ts` 30 to 32, 77 to 86; `blob.ts` 608 to 665). This is the 0 to 2 s (0 to 10 s after a join) in every propagation number of sections 1.3 and 3, and the 2,000 simple operations an hour per open deck per instance that pay for it.
2. The per instance presence. A roster row goes through the presence record with a 5 s push spacing and a 5 s read poll on top of the pulse tick, and the live fields do not travel at all (`presence-store.ts` 74 to 78, 622 onward; `select.ts` 23). Defects 2, 3, 10 and the join half of 1 and 13 are this.
3. The write's rounds and the spacing. A commit is a head and a get, a pull when behind, three puts, a conditional put and a pulse put in series (about 650 ms), one write per second per deck per instance, and a lost claim is a discard, a 200 ms sleep and the whole write again (`blob-store.ts` 1766 to 1976; `blob.ts` 712 to 713). Defects 4, 5 and 13 are this.
4. The per instance caches with no bus between instances: the identity (5 s), the link index (5 s), the access record (5 s), the principal store (a file per instance), the room's resolved identity (5 s) (`access.ts` 70 and 168; `room.ts` 2354; `room.ts` 563 to 611). Defects 7 and 8 are this; the redis tier's `PUBLISH` drop is what the code names for it.

The design choice behind all four is the same one: the Blob store is the only thing every instance shares. The redis tier (section 2) is the code's own answer, built and tested against a fake and never run. The build that follows this pass should measure it once on a preview before anything else is redesigned.

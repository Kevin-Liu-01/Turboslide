The binding specification of the people round (2026-09-29), sized for one build of about a day of the pipeline: five lanes with disjoint files (section 5), twenty one matrix rows (6.1), one gate run on the enforce preview, one accounts run on a local node server and one gate run on production after the ship (6.2). `docs/POLISH.md`, `docs/OBJECTS.md`, `docs/VECTOR.md`, `docs/FEATURES.md`, `docs/PRODUCT.md`, `docs/SYNC.md`, `docs/RETURN.md`, `docs/FOCUS.md` and `docs/gslides-parity/focus/AMENDMENTS.md` A1 stand under it; the parked list of record is `docs/gslides-parity/focus/ship-c1a7ff3.json` on origin/main. Google Slides is the parity reference as `docs/gslides-parity/people/audit-parity.md` states the target: one monochrome mark per person on every surface, a hue on live surfaces only, and four kinds of person a reader meets (an account with a picture, an account without one, a guest with a typed name, a label).

Everything below was measured first. The audit is `docs/gslides-parity/people/AUDIT.md` (36 deduplicated defects, three options, eleven questions) with its seven notes beside it (`audit-identity.md`, `audit-presence.md`, `audit-authorship.md`, `audit-avatars.md`, `audit-accounts.md`, `audit-production.md`, `audit-parity.md`); the production readings are `docs/gslides-parity/people/drive/facts.json` and the pictures beside it, driven against https://www.turboslide.com on 2026-09-29 with two anonymous browsers, the scratch deck removed by its id. A citation such as "AUDIT.md defect 6" names that entry of AUDIT.md section 2; "avatars 7" names defect 7 of `audit-avatars.md`; "production gap 1" names gap 1 of `audit-production.md`. ## 1. Kevin's ask and what the round does

Kevin, verbatim, 2026-09-29: "deploy everything on main and fix cache errors. then update the /home. also figure out a way to fix how people appear in turboslide with account distinguishing and avatars (limited by file size)". Mid turn, with a screenshot of the Version history panel in dark chrome: "fix the avatar touching border". The panel's window row was fixed on main as `cdbd0dd5` before this round started (AUDIT.md defect 1: the row's first 16 px chip sat at x 1 on the panel's `--pt-hair` border; it now sits at x 17 with the standalone rows). This round is the rest of the sentence.

What the round does, in three sentences. An account is told from a guest on every surface: a 14 px check badge and the word "signed in" after an account's name, "guest" after a typed name, a label as a label, and the sentence each state carries in its tooltip (AUDIT.md defect 7). One person is one id across the room, the comments and the versions: the room's identity path reads the sign in session, old records written before a sign in render as the account through the alias table, the own chip and the roster's own row read one source, and two people with one label are told apart (AUDIT.md defects 6, 8, 9, 10, 11, 18). An account carries a picture uploaded under a file size cap: the browser crops a square and resizes it to 256 px WebP, the server refuses anything over 512 KB, sniffs, decodes and re-encodes the ladder, the public store serves it from a digest named path, and every chip draws it with the initials plate as the fallback (AUDIT.md defect 12).

Two facts frame every row. Production (https://www.turboslide.com, the project `turboslide-gt`, and https://turboslide.vercel.app from the same main) runs `TURBOSLIDE_REALTIME=blob`, `TURBOSLIDE_AUTHORIZE=shadow`, `TURBOSLIDE_MAIL=off`, no `DATABASE_URL` and no `REDIS_URL` (`docs/HOSTING-MOVE.md` 20; AUDIT.md section 1 "Production"), so every person on production is an anonymous principal with a label or a typed name, `GET /api/auth/get-session` answers 404, and no surface there can show an account, a session or a picture (accounts, "What a seller sees on production"). Every account row of this round is therefore verified on a local node server with `TURBOSLIDE_AUTH_DB` (sqlite) and `TURBOSLIDE_MAIL=capture`, the way `scripts/check.mjs` `SERVER_ENV` (164 to 173) and `apps/studio/e2e/accounts.spec.ts` (17 to 30) run today; the production gate reads the anonymous rows (6.2). Second, two `.ts-chip` rules collide on production and every mark there is drawn at 12 px inside its 24 px box (AUDIT.md defect 3; production 3), so every picture in the audit folder shows a shrunken mark; the geometry of section 3 is judged only after that rename lands.

The pipeline in one sentence: the browser crops and resizes, the request is capped at 512 KB, the server sniffs, decodes and re-encodes the ladder under a rotated key with the digest in the name, the public store serves it for a year, and every chip draws it with the initials plate under it.

### 4.1 The client crop and resize (B3)

`packages/chrome/src/dialogs/AvatarBuilder.tsx` 147 to 162 (`pick`) and 258 to 295 (the Picture panel):

- `pick(file)` keeps the MIME check (`image/(png|jpeg|webp|gif)`) and a guard on the original file at 25 MB (the anonymous picture tier of `docs/security.md` section 10), the guard against decoding a phone's 50 MB file; its refusal reads "Pictures up to 25 MB". It then decodes with `createImageBitmap(file, { imageOrientation: 'from-image' })`, so the orientation tag is applied before anything is drawn and a GIF's first frame is what decodes.
- The 256 by 256 box (`dialog.avatarBuilder.crop`, `accounts.css` 269 to 273) draws the bitmap scaled so its shorter side is 256 px and takes a pointer drag that moves the picture under a fixed square (the box is the crop); the crop is the square of the bitmap under the box in source pixels, clamped inside the picture. There is no handle, no zoom and no rotation this round. The sentence under the box reads "Drag to crop" while a picture is loaded (`ACCOUNT.avatar.crop`, `strings.ts` 259), which is now true.
- Apply draws the cropped square into a 256 by 256 canvas and calls `canvas.toBlob(cb, 'image/webp', 0.8)`. If the blob is over 512 KB the dialog refuses before encoding a data URL, with the sentence "The resized picture is over 512 KB. Choose another picture" in its error row (`dialog.avatarBuilder.error`), and no request leaves. A photograph at 256 px and quality 0.8 is 10 to 40 KB, so the refusal is for a hostile or broken encoder result, not a person's picture.
- Otherwise the blob is read into a `data:image/webp;base64,` URL and `account.setAvatar({ variant: 'picture', picture })` is called with the string; the shell type (`editor-shell.ts` 534 to 539) takes `picture?: string` in place of `File` and `salt?: number` in place of the string (avatars 5). `EditorRoot.tsx` 823 to 828 forwards `picture` and, on the answer, writes `avatar` and `pictureUrl` back and refreshes presence (3.11).
- The sentence under the control at all times (in place of "Pictures up to 5 MB", `strings.ts` 261): "Cropped to a square and sent at 256 px, under 512 KB. JPEG, PNG, WebP or GIF". The privacy sentence and the cache sentence of 4.5 sit under it.
- The dead `<span hidden>{scale}</span>` (101) and `.ts-avatar-scaled` (`accounts.css` 247 to 250) go (avatars 16).

The builder never sends a crop to the server: the browser crops, so the server's optional `crop` argument stays for the CLI's uncropped file (4.7) and the attention strategy stays its fallback (`avatar.ts` 135 to 141).

### 4.2 The request cap

512 KB on every transport, before any decode:

- `AVATAR_MAX_BYTES` (`avatar.ts` 34) becomes 512 KB and `AVATAR_MAX_INPUT_PIXELS` (36) 1024 by 1024, since the browser resized; `processAvatar` (102 to 172) refuses over the cap before the sniff as today (107).
- The `account.setAvatar` handler (`auth/actions.ts` 341 to 377) checks the data URL string's length before `Buffer.from` (`dataUrlBytes`, 262 to 270): a string over 512 KB times 4/3 plus the header is refused with the cap sentence before decoding.
- The schema bounds the string: `picture: z.string().max(700_000)` (`packages/schema/src/actions.ts` 5425 to 5428), so the JSON is refused at validation on every transport; the description keeps "a data URL" and drops "a file path", which never worked (avatars 8).
- `/api/actions` keeps its 1 MB `WRITE_BODY_LIMIT` (`packages/agent/src/http/dispatch.ts` 21, 96): a 512 KB picture is about 700 KB of JSON and fits; the server function's 4.5 MB platform limit is far above. The two transports and the client agree on one number, and the dialog says it.
- The cap sentence on the server is `AVATAR_TOO_LARGE` (`avatar.ts` 42), reworded to "Pictures up to 512 KB after resizing"; the client's sentence of 4.1 names the same number.

### 4.5 The link visitor, the privacy sentence and the cache sentence

- A link visitor on a deck open to anyone with the link sees the role initial and never the picture (default 6; 3.27): `rosterEntryForReader` swaps the whole mark for the role's, and the payload's `identities` for such a reader carry the same role view for verified people, so the comment cards and the version rows a link visitor reads never show the picture either. Grant holders and the owner see it.
- The privacy sentence in the Picture panel, under the control: "Your picture shows on every presentation you open while signed in, to people invited by email. People who arrive by a link see a role initial instead". One sentence per thought, as Kevin's rule reads.
- The cache sentence beside it: "A replaced or removed picture can stay readable at its old address for up to a year in caches". The reason: the edge keeps the old file until its `max-age` expires and the store's purge is not exposed per path (AUDIT.md section 4 "Option B, Risks, The CDN cache"); the URL is unguessable (a 22 character key and a 64 hex digest) and unlisted, and it stops being drawn anywhere at once.

### 4.6 The janitor for orphan files under u/

A failed `put` between the files and the record write (`avatar.ts` 297 to 310) leaves files under a key nothing references; deletion and rotation leave none by design. `sweepOrphanAvatars(store, profiles, { dryRun, olderThanHours: 24 })` in `avatar.ts` lists `u/`, groups by key, keeps every key a `ts_profile.avatarKey` names and every key younger than 24 hours (an upload in flight), and removes the rest; it refuses with one sentence when the deployment has no identity database (`runtime.auth === null`), because with no profiles every file would read as an orphan. It is exposed as the admin action `admin.avatar.sweep { dryRun }` (HTTP and CLI, the admin group, a request to the integrator for the schema header) and unit tested with a stub store and stub profiles. It is never a sweep by date or name of anything outside `u/`; every other round's cleanup keeps skipping `u/`.

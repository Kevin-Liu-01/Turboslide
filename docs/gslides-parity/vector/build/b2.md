### 2. The readings that differ from docs/VECTOR.md, and why

1. The arc's end point (2.1). The document says the end point is the centre plus the point at
   t₀ plus the sweep, with t₀ the parametric angle of the start. That is right on a circle and at
   multiples of 90 degrees (the pinned three and the pie of the defaults are unchanged), and wrong
   on a wide ellipse elsewhere: the pie's second adjust handle sits at `(x2, y2)`, the point its
   own guides compute at the geometric end angle `enAng` (`cat2 wd2 ht2 wt2`, `sat2 hd2 ht2 wt2`),
   and an arc that sweeps the parametric angle lands somewhere else. The interpreter converts the
   geometric end angle `stAng + swAng` on its own (t₁ = atan2(wR sin θ₁, hR cos θ₁)) and lands the
   end point there; `geometry.test.ts` pins a pie from 45 to 200 degrees on 240 by 160 ending at
   `(x2, y2)` within half a pixel. With the sweep added in parametric space the cloud at 400 by 400
   ended a vertex at y −2.5 and Curved down arrow at 200 by 100 one at y 103; both are inside
   their boxes now. The `large` flag reads the same in both spaces (the conversion keeps a half
   turn a half turn).
2. The pie's text rectangle. The file's `pie` entry names its rectangle's guides in the wrong
   order (`l="il" t="ir" r="it" b="ib"`, presetShapeDefinitions.xml), which inverts it; an inverted
   or empty rectangle answers the whole box, so the label stays visible. Every other preset's
   rectangle at the defaults has a positive size and lies inside the box at the three sizes.
3. The four callouts' pointer at the defaults sits below the box: `adj2` 62500 puts the tip at
   1.125 of the height (`yPos = vc + h × 62500 / 100000`), as PowerPoint draws it. So "every
   vertex inside the box within 1 px at the defaults" (6.3) holds for 131 presets, and for the
   callouts with the tip excepted; the test says so. Row `shapes.geometry.callouts.wedge-rect`
   reads the tip "left of the box, below its middle": the numbers are `(hc − 0.20833 w, vc + 0.625 h)`
   = (70, 180) at 240 by 160, inside the box horizontally and below it.
4. Axis aligned lines write `H` and `V` (compared on the half pixel grid), so `rect`'s path is the
   box path byte for byte and `roundRect`'s equals ship one's string. A vertex the file repeats
   (Rectangular callout's `xt,yt` equals `x1,t` at the defaults) writes as a repeated `H40`; the
   walk's vertex counts read the repeats as the file has them.
5. An `arcTo` whose two radii are both 0 writes nothing and moves nowhere (a rounded rectangle
   at `adj` 0 is the box, as shapes.test.ts asserted before); one collapsed radius still lands its
   end point and writes a flat arc, which SVG draws as a line. A division by zero in a guide
   answers 0, so a zero box evaluates every preset to finite numbers (tested).
6. The shade of the curved arrows. Row `shapes.geometry.arrows.curved-right` and the 6.3 bullet
   name a `darken` path; the file's `curvedRightArrow` (and the other three curved arrows, the
   ribbons, the scrolls, `cube`, `foldedCorner`, `smileyFace`) shade with `darkenLess`; `bevel`
   alone carries `darken`. The sheet draws `darkenLess` as the ink overlay at 0.15. B5's driver
   accepts either reading and records the value.
7. The star's inner radius. Rows `shapes.geometry.shapes.star5-adjust` and
   `shapes.geometry.resize-keeps-adjust` say Adjust 30 makes "the inner radius 30 percent of the
   outer". The file's guide is `iwd2 = swd2 × a / 50000`, so `adj` 30000 puts the inner radius at
   60 percent of the outer and the default 19098 at the pentagram's 38.2 percent; 50000 is a flat
   decagon. The product does what the file says; request 3 asks B5 to read 60 percent (and 38.2
   after Cmd+Z). The invariant the rows measure, the fractions holding through a resize, holds.
8. The multi path count. 2.3 draws a shade mode as the base path under an overlay path, so the can
   (norm, lighten, none) renders four `<path>` elements, the overlay marked `data-shade="lighten"`;
   the row text says three. B5's driver reads at least three and the joined `d` of every path
   with a `d` equal to `shapePath`; the overlay repeats its base's `d`, so a driver that joins
   every `<path>` reads the base twice and should skip `[data-shade]`. Request 3.

# Proposal 2: sales first, the rest behind Google's labels

Design proposal for round five of the Turboslide Google Slides parity work (motion, media, templates, PPTX import, page setup and print, ODP and SVG, preferences and text tools, the equation editor, Edit theme and a second theme, chat, the deferred engineering), written 2026-09-14 against `main` at `d5d7f07`. This is the second of three proposals. Its angle is the sales organisation that will use the product: the rep who brings a PowerPoint deck from last quarter, the demo video that must play on a call, the pricing rows that reveal one click at a time, the branded template with the company's mark in the corner, the three per page leave behind with note lines, and the manager who tunes the deck with the rep on a call. Every design choice below is the shortest path that is also the honest one, with the rest of Google's surface behind Google's own labels in Google's own positions.

## 1. Motion

### 1.1 The document shape

Two fields on `SlideBase` (`packages/schema/src/deck.ts:115-158`), additive at version 1, the shape R05 3 fixed so the exporters and the editor agree:

```ts
transition?: {
  kind: 'none' | 'dissolve' | 'fade' | 'slideRight' | 'slideLeft' | 'flip' | 'cube' | 'gallery';
  durationMs: number;              // 100 to 5000; the speed slider stores a duration
};
animations?: Animation[];          // the Motion panel's list in play order

type Animation = {
  id: string;                      // a slug unique in the slide
  blockId: BlockId;                // the object; a media block for playMedia
  effect: 'appear' | 'disappear' | 'fadeIn' | 'fadeOut' | 'flyIn' | 'flyOut'
        | 'zoomIn' | 'zoomOut' | 'spin' | 'playMedia';
  direction?: 'left' | 'right' | 'top' | 'bottom';   // flyIn and flyOut
  trigger: 'click' | 'afterPrevious' | 'withPrevious';
  durationMs: number;              // 100 to 5000
  byParagraph?: true;              // text blocks: one step per paragraph
};
```

A transition belongs to the slide it leads to, which is where `p:transition` lives (R01 6.1). There is no deck level transition: "Apply to all slides" is an action that writes the same `transition` on every slide in one write, the way PowerPoint and Google both store it (R05 3). The validator (`packages/schema/src/validate.ts`) gains the code `motion`: an animation whose `blockId` names no block on the slide, a `direction` on a non fly effect, a `byParagraph` on a block without a Text, a `playMedia` on a block that is not `media`, and a duration outside the range are severity 2 findings with a fix that removes the row. `slide.set` and `slide.update` write both fields through the existing mutation ops; the operation stream carries them as any slide field.

Durations. Google prints no numbers on its slider (R01 8 item 3), so the numbers are Turboslide's: the slider runs from 100 to 5000 ms with three labelled stops, Slow 2000, Medium 1000 and Fast 500, for transitions and animations alike (R05 4). A new transition takes Medium (1000 ms); a new animation takes Fast (500 ms), the value PowerPoint writes in its own files for Fade, Fly and Spin (R01 6.5), so a deck exported without touching a slider looks native in PowerPoint. Both defaults are marked unverified against Google in the fixture.

### 1.3 The schedule

`compileMotion(slide, blocks): MotionSchedule` in `packages/render/src/motion.ts`, pure, tested in Node, the one function the show, the standalone motion script, the PPTX writer and the `motion.compile` action read (R05 4). The semantics are PowerPoint's and, by every description R01 collected, Google's: a `click` animation opens a step; `withPrevious` joins the current step at delay 0; `afterPrevious` joins it at the previous effect's end; a first `withPrevious` or `afterPrevious` joins step 0, which plays on entry; `byParagraph` expands to one effect per paragraph, each its own step under `click`, chained under `afterPrevious`, together under `withPrevious`; entrance effects hide their block before their step, exit effects hide it after, `spin` never changes visibility; `playMedia` lasts the media's trimmed length or the step's length when looping. `motionCss(schedule)` writes the keyframes both runtimes load, so the curves are equal by construction (R05 9.4): the Bayer sprite dissolve in the house pattern, the crossfade, the pushes, the flip, the cube and the gallery, with `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` setting every duration to 0 and keeping the step gates.

### 1.4 Present mode and Presenter view

The Slideshow (`apps/studio/src/components/Slideshow.tsx`) and the present model gain a step: `advance()` consumes a step of the current slide before the play index moves, and `back()` steps to the previous step's end state before moving to the previous slide (Google's Left arrow behaviour is unverified, R01 8 item 5; stepping back through animations is PowerPoint's and is chosen because a presenter who overshoots a reveal wants the reveal back, not the slide). The transition of the incoming slide plays first, then step 0. The key resolver (`presentKeys.ts:77-116`) is unchanged: `next` and `previous` are the same rows, and the Slideshow decides whether they mean a step or a slide. The `state` message of the channel (`presentSync.ts:17-35`) gains `step` and `steps`, so the presenter console shows "Step 2 of 4" beside the counter and its next preview shows the state after the next click (a live clone with `data-step` one ahead) while steps remain, and the next slide at rest otherwise. That preview is a Turboslide addition: Google's console is not documented to preview the next step (R01 8 item 7), and a seller who is about to click wants to know what appears.

Auto-play. The Options menu's Auto-play submenu (today a stub, R07 3.4) becomes Google's: every 1, 2, 3, 5, 10, 15, 30 seconds and every minute, Play, Loop, a session setting that resets when the show ends (R01 4). Each tick is one `advance()`, so a step and a slide change each take a tick (the reading R05 13 item 15 marks unverified; it is the one reading under which a reveal deck plays as authored). A click or a key pauses; Play resumes.

Present on another screen and Presentation display options (the two Slideshow rows) flip to Now on browsers with the Window Management API and show disabled with the drag clause elsewhere (R11 7): the show goes fullscreen on the chosen screen and the presenter window opens on the other; the Presentation API is not used because its receivers are Cast devices.

### 1.5 The exports

Editable text PPTX. Two post process modules (R05 5, 6, 11): `ooxml/transition.ts` writes the `mc:AlternateContent` wrapper after `</p:clrMapOvr>` with `p14:dur` in the Choice and `spd` in both branches (fast under 750 ms, med under 1500, slow above), the ECMA element for Dissolve, Fade and the two pushes (`p:push dir="l"` for Slide from right, the push reading R05 2 chose), and `p14:flip`, `p14:prism` and `p14:gallery` with a `p:fade` fallback; `ooxml/timing.ts` writes the five level tree with the presets of R01 6.4 (Appear 1, Fly 2 with the direction bits 1, 2, 4, 8, Fade 10, Zoom 23 with 16 and 32, Spin emphasis 8), the click groups, the `onBegin` condition on an entry step, `p:bldP build="p"` with `p:pRg` per paragraph, and the media nodes of section 2.6. The report gains `motion: { transitions, animations, media, equations }` and a residual line per animation whose block exported as no shape.

Perfect PPTX, PDF, JPEG, PNG, SVG and the thumbnails. The still rule (1.6). Nothing is written.

Standalone HTML. `renderStandalone` writes `#ts-motion` (the compiled schedules) and `#ts-motion-css` only when a slide carries motion; a second standalone script `viewer/standalone/motion.ts`, ported like `runtime.ts`, owns the steps, the transitions and the media controller; the frozen runtime gains one hook, `window.__tsMotion?.advance(dir)` before `go(k)`, recorded in SPEC 5.3's amendment list (R05 9.2). `build.run` and `export.run` for HTML gain `autoplay: { intervalMs, loop }` with Google's intervals, and the Download dialog's Web page row offers "Auto-advance every" with the same list and "Restart after the last slide", the two checkboxes of Google's Publish dialog (R01 4).

### 1.6 The still rule

A still is the document with every object at rest: an object with an entrance animation is drawn, an object with an exit animation is drawn, a Disappear removes nothing, a Spin rotates nothing, a video shows its poster, an audio shows its speaker glyph even when Hide icon when presenting is set, and transitions are absent by definition (R05 9.1). The rule holds because the render surface never carries present mode state: `hiddenAtStart` becomes a class inside the show and the HTML runtime only, never in the sheet route or the print document, so the Perfect gate and the PDF gate are unchanged and compare-to-shoot stays at 0.5 percent. The two alternatives were rejected for the sales case: a still of the first state hides the pricing rows a procurement reader needs, and a still of the last state can empty a page.

## 2. Media

### 2.1 The blocks and the asset

`MediaBlock` is R05 3's shape unchanged: `type: 'media'`, `kind: 'audio' | 'video'`, `source: { asset } | { youtube }`, `poster?`, and `playback` with `start: 'click' | 'auto' | 'manual'`, `loop?`, `volume?` (0 to 100), `mute?`, `stopOnSlideChange?: false`, `hideIcon?`, `startMs?`, `endMs?`. The asset record becomes the union of R11 1.4: today's picture record unchanged plus `MediaAsset` with `kind`, `role: 'media'`, `file`, `mime`, `bytes`, `sha256`, `durationMs | null`, `size?`, `codecs`, `poster?`, `title?`, `source`. The block's `poster` wins over the asset's. The catalog entry (`packages/schema/src/catalog.ts`) lists `media` under Figures with `export: 'mixed'`; the validator gains the three rules of R11 1.4.

### 2.2 Intake and security

The accepted formats are the intersection of what every browser plays and what both PowerPoints play (R11 1.1): video `mp4` (H.264 with AAC) and `webm`; audio `mp3`, `m4a` and `wav`. Everything else is refused with one sentence naming the list. The sniff is by container signature in a framework free module both intakes and the bundle scan import (R11 1.2); duration, dimensions and codecs come from Turboslide's own bounded parsers (R11 1.3). Content types join the Blob table, the asset route, `INLINE_TYPES` and the upload grant (R11 1.5). The quotas are `largestAudioBytes` (25 MB anonymous, 50 MB account and agent), `largestVideoBytes` (25, 200, 200 MB), `mediaPerDay` (10, 100, 300) and `mediaBytesPerDay` (200 MB, 5 GB, 10 GB), and `DECK_CAPS.mediaBytes` is 300 MB (R11 1.7). The bundle cap rises to 500 MB with a streaming unpack (Kevin confirms, section 13).

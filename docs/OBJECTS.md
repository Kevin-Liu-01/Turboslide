# The objects round

The binding specification of the objects round (2026-09-25), sized for one build of about a day of the pipeline: five lanes with disjoint files (section 5), about forty matrix rows (6.1), one gate on the enforce preview and one on production after the ship (6.2). `docs/VECTOR.md` (every preset drawn from its geometry, the Shape grids), `docs/FEATURES.md` section 2 (ship one's editing model for tables, charts and diagrams), `docs/RETURN.md` 2.4 to 2.7 (how the objects returned and the five table fixes), `docs/PRODUCT.md`, `docs/FOCUS.md` and `docs/gslides-parity/focus/AMENDMENTS.md` A1 stand under it. Google Slides is the parity reference: a shape drawn by a drag is drawn as it grows, a resize shows the object at its new size at every frame, a table's empty cells are visible as a grid, a chart redraws as its box changes.

Everything below was measured first, on production `https://turboslide.vercel.app` (the return round plus ship one; the vector round had not landed there at 00:52 UTC on 2026-09-26: Insert > Shape still listed the Shapes submenu with its three rows, `audit/a00-insert-shape-menu-page.png`) and, for the geometry interpreter and the frame cost, on this worktree's dev server at 5bf158a. The scripts are under the scratchpad's `objects/` folder (`lib.mjs`, `gestures.mjs`, `components.mjs`, `extra.mjs`, `local.mjs`, `summarize.mjs`; playwright-core from the worktree, headless Chromium at 1440 by 900 with deviceScaleFactor 2, the mouse moved in 12 steps with the facts and a screenshot of the sheet after every step, 60 ms after each step). Every frame and every JSON they wrote is under `docs/gslides-parity/objects/audit/` (the frame tables: `gestures-frames.md`, `local-frames.md`, `extra-frames.md`; the step logs: `components-b.json` light, `components-c.json` light, `components-d.json` dark; the defect list with value and effort: `audit/audit.md`). Every scratch deck was trashed and deleted forever through the product and answered 404 (the `teardown` field of each JSON). "Frame" below names a file in that folder.

## 2. The live gestures

### 2.1 The rule

Every gesture draws the object itself at every frame through the draft document, with the real geometry: a shape re-pathed at its size with its adjust values, a text block reflowed, a picture scaled with its crop, a table re-laid with its columns, a chart redrawn, a diagram re-laid, a line's ends followed. A click places the object at once: the press draws it at the tool's default size at the press point and the release commits it. A drag from Insert > Shape draws the shape as it grows from the press point with the grammar's default fill (`toolBlock`, `packages/viewer/src/Gestures.tsx` 1487 to 1520: the plate fill and the ink stroke). The readout stays (the size beside the box, the angle beside a rotation, the column width beside a seam; `gesture-life.ts`). The ring and the handles are drawn from the object's box at every frame, and a rotated object's ring turns with it, during the drag and after.

### 2.3 The mechanism of each gap

1. The draw draws the marquee. `armDraw` (`packages/viewer/src/Editor.tsx` 5723 to 5810) is the draw tool's press: its `move` computes `drawnBox` (`Gestures.tsx` 1523 to 1560) and calls `setMarquee(drawn.dragged ? drawn.box : null)` (5779) with the size readout; nothing touches the draft document, so the sheet draws the slide as committed and the overlay draws `.ts-marquee` (`Marquee.tsx` 60) in the marquee's ink. The object is made only in `finishDraw` (5783 to 5801) through `insertDrawn` (5635 to 5684), which runs `commitCanvas` and the store's reducer, so the first frame with the shape is the one after the release.
2. The click draws nothing before the release. The same path: with no travel `drawnBox` answers the default box and `dragged` false (`Gestures.tsx` 1531 to 1534), `setMarquee(null)`, and the insert waits for `finishDraw`. The release is fast (5 to 48 ms to the element, `gestures.json` `polls`) because `commit` applies the write through the page's reducer synchronously (`Editor.tsx` 1361 to 1390); on a slide that is not a canvas yet `commitCanvas` first awaits `measuredCanvas` (1573 to 1585).
3. The rotated ring. The ring's box is the measured box of the selected block (`Editor.tsx` 6337 to 6351, `measuredSelectionBox` from `boxes.blocks`), and `measureBoxes` reads `getBoundingClientRect` of the `.free` wrapper (`Freeform.tsx` 273 to 300), which for a rotated wrapper is the axis aligned bounding box (707 by 707 for 680 by 320 at 45 degrees). The overlay then turns that box by `objectTransform(view.selectionPos)` (`packages/chrome/src/Overlay.tsx` 422 to 431, the `.ts-turn` layer at 541 to 548), so the ring is the bounding box turned again. During the drag the draft's `pos.rotate` changes and `selectionPos` follows, but the handles and the ring are laid out from the measured box each frame, so the ring reads upright and growing. The handles' own union box is already `pos` (`Gestures.tsx` 300 to 302, `placedOf`), which is why the handles sit on the ring's corners in both states.
4. The connector follows at the release. `preview` applies the gesture's mutations alone (`Editor.tsx` 2688 to 2700); `followConnectors` runs in `finishCanvasGesture` through `followAfter` (2826 to 2832; `packages/schema/src/connect.ts` 279 to 320), so an elbow attached to a moved shape stays where it was during the drag and jumps at the release (`c24-lines-b-moved.png` shows the state after; the frames of 6.1 `gestures.move.connector-follows-live` measure the drag).
5. The cadence. `move` calls `preview` on every pointermove whose mutations differ from the last (2702 to 2716), and `preview` calls `setDraft(applyMutations(docRef.current, all).document)`, which re-renders the whole slide through `renderSlide` in the `html` memo (1026 to 1042) and sets it as innerHTML (`SlideView.tsx`), then `measure` reads every box in the layout effect (1347 to 1353). No frame is skipped and no work is deferred; the numbers of 2.2 say this is affordable on the slides measured, and nothing today measures or bounds it.

### 2.4 The design

The draw and the placement (B1, `Editor.tsx` `armDraw`, `insertDrawn`; `Gestures.tsx` one helper). `armDraw` builds the same `block.insert` the release will commit, from the pointer down: a new pure helper `drawDraftMutation(canvas, tool, id, box, options)` in `Gestures.tsx` answers `toolInsertMutation` for the tool at the box (the default box at the press point while the pointer has not travelled `DRAW_MIN_PX`, the drawn box after, Shift and Option as `drawnBox` already reads them), with the connector snap of `insertDrawn` for a line kind, and the press previews it through the draft path `beginGesture` already has: `setDraft(applyMutations(docRef.current, [convert?, insert]).document)` with the provisional conversion of a slide that is not a canvas (2655 to 2661). The pointer's every move updates the box and previews again; the release commits the same mutation with the same id (the id is freed once at the press, `freeId`, so the preview and the commit name one block and the selection that follows, `pendingSelect`, finds it). The marquee stays as the hairline around the growing shape with its readout (the readout is the ring's now: the shape is drawn, the marquee is its frame). A text box previews as its frame alone (an empty text block draws nothing; the caret opens at the release as today, `pendingEdit`). A table or chart tool placed by a click previews the whole table or chart at the default size from the pointer down. Escape, a lost capture and a blur cancel the draft as they cancel a gesture (`cancelBy`). The draft's block is rendered by the renderer as it stands (`renderShape` at the box's size, the preset's geometry through `shapeGeometry`, `primitives.ts` 356 to 372), so a preset shape grows with its adjust fractions and its default fill from the first pixel.

The rotated ring (B1, `Editor.tsx` `selectionBox`). For a single selected object that carries `pos` the ring's box is its `pos` box (`anchorPos`, already computed at 6331 to 6335), during a gesture the draft's `pos` (the draft is the shown document, so `anchorBlock` reads it), and the measured box stays the ring for objects without `pos` (a grammar field, a conversion's virtual object) and for the hover ring and the multi selection's extra rings, which the overlay never turns. The overlay changes nothing: it already turns the box it is given about its centre. The handles of a rotated object are then on the same box as the ring (2.3 item 3). The frame edges (`Overlay.tsx` 433 to 436) follow the ring box as today.

The connector during a move (B1, `Editor.tsx` `preview`). The preview's mutations gain `followAfter(canvas, all, touched)` on the draft, the same call the release makes, so an attached connector re-routes at every frame (`followConnectors` is arithmetic over the slide, `connect.ts` 279 to 320); the release keeps its own call and the commit is unchanged.

The cadence and the budget (B1, `Editor.tsx` `preview`, a new `packages/viewer/src/gesture-frame.ts`). `preview` no longer calls `setDraft` on the event: it stores the latest mutations in a ref and schedules one `requestAnimationFrame` callback when none is pending; the callback applies the latest mutations once and clears the pending flag, so a burst of pointermoves inside one frame renders once and the last position wins. `gesture-frame.ts` is the pure record of a gesture's frames: `frameRecord({ start, end })` per rendered frame (the time from the callback's start to the layout effect's measure), the running `maxMs`, `meanMs`, `frames` and `skipped`; `Editor.tsx` writes it on the gesture and publishes the last gesture's record through the window API as `describe().state.gesture` (`{ frames, maxMs, meanMs, skipped, degraded }`) so a driver reads it without instrumentation. The budget: a rendered frame over 32 ms twice in a row (two 60 Hz frames) sets `degraded`, and while degraded a move gesture takes the wrapper path (the moved objects' `.free` wrappers take their new `left` and `top` inline from the mutations, the way the text session writes the wrapper's height at 1803 to 1812, and no `renderSlide` runs until the release) and a resize or rotation renders every second frame (the ring, the handles and the readout still move every frame, since they are the overlay's and cost nothing); a frame back under 16 ms twice clears it. What is skipped is only the sheet's re-render of frames the budget cannot afford, never the ring, the readout or the commit. The measurement of record is 6.1 `gestures.frame.cost-budget`: on a deck the driver grows to 24 slides through `slide.new` (the number the round measures against, so the filmstrip and the store carry a deck a seller has), the measured slide holding a 5 by 6 table, a column chart and a picture, the move of the table and the resize of the chart read `maxMs` under 32 and `skipped` 0 on the preview and on production.

The measurement of a frame's cost before the build: `local.json` `costs` (2.2). After the build the same script runs on the lane's server and the numbers go in `docs/gslides-parity/objects/build/b1.md` beside the row's reading.

## 1. The ask and the decision

### 1.1 Kevin's message, verbatim

Written in chat on 2026-10-02:

### 1.2 The decision in five sentences

1. Build Direction A, the quiet object, which all three judges chose (40, 39 and 41 of 50): the page is one eight slide deck, each band holds one of its slides as a made object the visitor works on, and Present shows that deck with the visitor's changes in it.

3. 4. Move the agents band to second place so a seller meets what Google Slides does not do within the first 1,800 px, and paint every band's end state at rest.
5. Ship it as seven pushes after Round 1's last push (21): the landing follows push 15's `/home` on production and replaces it with its own first push.

## 2. The page, section by section

### 2.0 What every section shares

**The column.** The branch's grammar (`apps/studio/src/components/home/grammar.css`): the 1,104 px column with one rail on each side drawn once by `.ts-rails`, a 40 px gutter, so the content is 1,024 px wide at 1440 (x 208 to 1232, the rails at x 168 and 1272). Under 1,136 px the column is `min(1104px, 100% - 32px)`; under 720 px the gutter is 0, so at 390 the content is 358 px wide (x 16 to 374). Each band is a `section[data-band]` labelled by its h2, with 96 px of padding above and below at 1,024 px and over (A proposed it to shorten the page) and 64 px under 720 px, closed by `.ts-seam` with its 9 px crosses. The hatch strip appears once, before "Turboslide today", where the page turns from demonstrations to evidence.

**Type.** Inter only (`'Inter', 'Inter Fallback', ...` from `packages/fonts/src/inter.css`), `font-optical-sizing: auto`, weights 400 and 500 only. The h1 is in 2.2. The h2 is `clamp(30px, 3.9vw, 54px)`, weight 500, -0.034 em, line height 1.04 (54 px at 1440, 30 px at 390). The lead is 19 px on 1.55 in `--pt-ink-2` (17 px under 720 px). Captions are 14 px on 1.45 in `--pt-titanium`. Labels are 13 px. Table figures are 32 px, weight 500, tabular (24 px under 720 px). Monospace only on the agents band's one `#101010` panel (SPEC-4 0.20): `--pt-mono` (`ui-monospace, 'SF Mono', Menlo, Consolas, monospace`, `packages/chrome/src/tokens.css` 135), 14 px on 20 px lines with 20 px of padding a side at 1,024 px and over, 12 px on 18 px lines with 12 px a side under 720 px. The widest advance in that stack is 0.602 em (Menlo, and DejaVu Sans Mono behind `monospace`), so 64 columns take 539 px of the 548 px inside the 588 px panel and 44 columns take 318 px of the 334 px inside the 358 px panel; the panel's text is formatted to those two widths.

**Colour.** The paper and ink tokens of `packages/chrome/src/tokens.css`. `#2f5ce0` (`--pt-select`) appears only while something is being worked on: the visitor's selection ring, handles and chip, the snap guide, the Tailor highlight, the lifted slide's outline and the focus ring. Nothing blue is drawn at rest. The text caret is the browser's, in the text's colour, as the editor's is (no `caret-color` rule in `packages/chrome` or `packages/viewer`). The agent's ring and flag are ink. No other hue: an error on a panel is set in the panel's own text colour.

**Shape.** Square corners everywhere, buttons and fields included (grammar.css "no radius"). No shadows, gradients or blur. Ruled rows, never boxed cards. Heroicons 20 solid only in key cells (the parts table and Version history's author cell), never before a heading, never `sparkles` or `cursor-arrow-rays`.

**Slides.** Every slide on the page is the `renderSlide` output (`@turboslide/render/slide`) of the page deck (the landing fixture after the agents run, below), rendered at build in both appearances; the renderer never ships to the browser (173 KB gzip, `research-product.md` 2.2). The build fails unless a slide's two renders differ only in the sheet's `data-theme` and its picture sources, so the document inlines one HTML per slide instance and the page sets `data-theme` from its appearance; every picture inside a slide is replaced at build by the page's own print or still (2.6, 2.8). Ten instances are in the markup: the hero (slide 1), the agents band (slide 5), the Tailor stage (slide 2) and its four thumbnails (slides 2 to 5), the canvas band (slide 6), the Present band (slide 3) and the close (slide 8). Slide 7's HTML and slide 5's three earlier states travel in the live module, which the show and the print need. Section 4.1 costs them. A slide sits in a `HomeSheet` stage whose container units make 1 unit equal 1/1,600 of the sheet. `HomeSheet` reads `slides.generated.ts` only behind `import.meta.env.SSR`, which the client build folds to false so Rollup drops the module; on the client it renders the same element with an empty `dangerouslySetInnerHTML` and `suppressHydrationWarning`, which React leaves untouched after hydration, so the route chunk holds no slide markup. No sheet wider than a thumbnail draws a frame: its edge is shown by its own rails, rules and crosses, as the deck draws them, so each side of the column shows two lines, the page rail and the sheet's rail (judge 1's "border lines"). Thumbnails (144 by 81 and 80 by 45 px) keep the renderer's 1 px `--pt-edge` frame, because at that size their rails sit 5 px inside the edge and the frame is the only edge that reads. Under 720 px slide type takes a landing only reading floor (titles at least 17 px, body at least 12 px, credits at least 10 px), a recorded deviation from `renderSlide`'s proportions (A risk 2). Slides draw in the page's appearance, as A's did; a non GT theme fixes its own colours in both appearances, as a customer theme does in the product.

**The page deck.** Eight slides in the GT theme. The fixture made for the landing (`apps/studio/home-deck/`, section 6.1) holds seven of them, every slide but `next-steps`, so every slide carries Turboslide's words and the lighthouse plate's GT sentence leaves. The build runs the agents band's three commands (2.4) on a temporary copy of the fixture; the eight slide deck that results is the page deck, and everything the page draws at rest is rendered from it. The fixture itself is the run's start state (slide 5 absent, counters "n / 7").

**Kinds.** Each section says what it is:

- _Product render_: build output of product code, checked by `scripts/build-home-assets.ts --check` (the slides from `renderSlide`, the CLI's recorded answers, the parts of the CLI's PowerPoint and PDF files, the facts).
- _Live page code_: the lazily loaded module `apps/studio/src/components/home/live/` acting in the visitor's browser on the rendered slides, drawn in the product's grammar. Where it copies editor behaviour, or shows something the product does in another form, it says "replica".
- _Staged sequence_: a recorded timeline played over rendered slides, captioned as staged on the page.
- _Film_: none. The dither is never shipped as video (`research-motion.md` 4.8: the codecs smear 1 bit cells at 3 to 6 times the bytes), and every feature here is cheaper and clearer as a page the visitor touches (`research-product.md` 6).

**At rest.** The markup of every band, the hero included, is its end state, the fields and pictures included (their stills are in the markup or requested with the band, 2.2, 2.3, 4.2), and nothing on the page shows an empty frame waiting for a press. Every band but the hero first paints that end state: its one shot motion starts from a pose the script sets only when it arms below the viewport. The hero is the one exception, and only with script: the boot script adds `ts-intro` before the first paint, which sets H1 to H5's start pose (rails at scale 0, the subtitle untyped, the field still hidden), and the sequence ends on the markup's end state by T0 + 4.5 s (3.2). Without script, under reduced motion and for a crawler the hero's first paint is its markup.

**Undo.** Each band that edits keeps its own undo stack in `state.ts`. The band's Undo button, or Cmd or Ctrl+Z while focus is inside the band and not in a text field, undoes that band's newest change: the hero, Tailor (names, kit and filmstrip order, one stack in that order of time) or the canvas. In the agents, Present and export bands, or with focus outside every band, Cmd or Ctrl+Z does nothing, so a key never changes a band the visitor cannot see. Inside a text field it is the field's own undo. The page has no redo.

### 3.3 The stagger rule

Items in one group arrive in reading order: 55 ms apart for items (names, rows, mark pieces), 60 ms for lines (rails). No stagger falls outside 40 to 90 ms. A group spans at most 330 ms: past seven items, the rest arrive with the seventh. A sweep of cells is timed by each cell's Bayer threshold times the duration and is not a stagger. A group that follows another inside one sequence starts on the 500 ms beat (A3 after A2).

### 3.4 What never moves

The h1 and every heading; every lead, caption and paragraph (no reveal on prose); the navigation and its 24 px mark; the page's rails, seams, crosses and the hatch strip; the parts table; button positions (only the 0.98 press); the page itself (native scroll, `scroll-behavior: auto` on `html`, no smooth scrolling, scroll capture, inertia or parallax, no scroll linked animation); existing Version history rows; the counters (they cut); anything in the first viewport before the LCP paints. Nothing moves on hover by more than the ground colour. No element animates `left`, `top`, `width` or `height`; positions move by `transform`.

Every change of state the table in 3.2 does not list is a cut, in every mode: among them slide 5 entering or leaving band 3's filmstrip and the counters renumbering on Run Again or a move (A8), the run's history rows leaving, the panel's screen changing, the step label, the Tailor count and status sentence, the snackbar leaving (T7), the Present list's chosen slide, the Command row's resting sentence giving way, the skip link appearing on focus and the Layout row's word. A change that is a cut is never a motion that starts on its own, so 3.7 does not count it.

### 3.5 The in view and pause rules

- One `IntersectionObserver` for every one shot motion (F1, C1, E1, K1), `threshold: 0.35`. A motion plays once and its target is unobserved. A band already above the viewport when first observed renders final at once. The hidden first pose is set by the script only when the observer arms with the band below the viewport, so a page without script, a crawler and a reduced motion visitor get the final markup.
- When the tab hides (`visibilitychange`), every running sequence finishes at its end state.
- Any new input on a band finishes that band's running sequence at its end state before it acts. Any key, press or wheel in the hero ends the hero sequence.
- A `matchMedia('(prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)')` change finishes every running animation and applies the reduced rules from then on.
- `requestAnimationFrame` runs only while a field prints or a sequence runs. At rest: 0 calls and `document.getAnimations()` empty.
- `?slow=10` multiplies the page's timers by ten for frame strips (A's capture method); it does nothing else.

### 3.6 Reduced motion

Under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` the boot script never adds the intro class (the hero's markup, the field's still included, is the first paint), the duration tokens are 0 ms, no Web Animation is created, every field and picture is printed at its final tone at once, Undo, the ring, the kit and the show cut, the seam sits at 50 percent and the mark is still. Colour changes (the Tailor highlight, hover grounds) still happen, without transitions. Every interaction still works.

### 3.7 The 5 s rule

Every motion that starts on its own ends within 5 s of its start, inside WCAG 2.2.2 (https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pause-stop-hide.html, read 2026-10-02 by the motion researcher): the hero sequence by T0 + 4.5 s (at most T0 + 4.37 s by 3.2's arithmetic), F1 in 1.5 s, C1 in 2.4 s, E1 in 0.6 s, K1 in 0.93 s. Nothing loops, so the page has no Pause Motion control. Motions the visitor starts end within 5 s each: a Run step, from the press to the flag leaving, about 4.1, 4.1 and 3.5 s with the fixture's name and at most about 4.8 s with a 24 character name and two line answers (2.4); Tailor (at most 55 ms × 6 + 900 + 160 ms, 1.4 s); a kit (0.5 s); the show (0.5 s in, 0.4 s out).

### 4.2 The lazy loading line

Nothing below the first screen is requested before the LCP. After `load` and one idle callback (capped at 1.5 s), the page imports the live module. ## 5. Accessibility

- **Structure.** `lang="en"`; one `h1`; `header`, `main#top.ts-product[data-page="home"]` and `footer`; each band a `section` labelled by its h2; the skip link first in the Tab order, moving focus to the hero section.
- **Keyboard.** Every object on the hero and canvas slides, every filmstrip slide, every row of the Present list, field, tab, button and link is reachable with Tab in reading order. Objects take Google's keys as the editor binds them: the arrows nudge 1 unit and Shift with an arrow 10, Option or Alt with Left or Right turns 15 degrees and with Shift 1 degree, Enter edits, Escape returns focus to the object. Cmd or Ctrl+Z undoes the newest change of the band that holds focus and does nothing elsewhere (2.0's Undo rule). The transport tabs use a roving tabindex with tab panels. Run keeps focus while a step plays (`aria-disabled`). Cmd+Enter or Ctrl+F5 opens the show (2.7). The show moves focus to its stage, traps focus, gives Space and Enter to a focused button and returns focus to Present. The seam is a slider. The filmstrip moves with Cmd or Ctrl and Up or Down.
- **Character key shortcuts (SC 2.1.4).** No key without a modifier acts unless focus is on the object, field or control it changes: the arrows, Enter and Escape act on a focused object, the arrows on a focused filmstrip slide, list row, tab or the seam, Space and Enter in the show by the rule above. A's S and the `[` and `]` keys are gone, so the page needs no setting to turn a shortcut off.
- **Focus.** A 2 px `#2f5ce0` outline with a 2 px offset on every control. A focused slide object draws the editor's selection (ring, handles, chip), which is its focus indicator (judge 3 confirmed it reads).
- **Names.** The h1 and every text object keep their text as their name; an object's wrapper carries `aria-roledescription="text box"` and its role chip's word; nothing renames a heading. Canvases are `aria-hidden`; each printed picture has the slide's alt text ("The Blue Marble, NASA's photograph of the Earth"). The export picture's alt reads "Slide 7 as the one picture the Perfect file holds"; the Editable text side is its own text, and its picture carries the slide's alt text.
- **Announcements.** One polite live region per band: the Command row's line, the Tailor count and result, the kit sentence, the agent step's result ("Slide 5 added"), a typed command's first answer line, the show's counter ("Slide 3 of 8"), "Slide 2 moved to place 4".
- **Contrast.** Ink on paper 20.14:1 and 17.97:1, ink 2 10.88:1 and 10.59:1, titanium 4.70:1 and 6.20:1, `#2f5ce0` on white 5.63:1 and on ink 3.58:1, the chip's white text 5.63:1 (`docs/brand.md` 12). Type never sits on dither: plates and the hero's clear zone hold it. The kit change keeps every text box on its own ground in every frame. The agent flag is paper text on ink.
- **Targets.** Buttons at least 40 px tall (44 px under 720 px). Handles draw 11 px squares inside a 24 px hit area for a fine pointer and 32 px for a coarse one (SC 2.5.8).
- **Dragging (SC 2.5.7).** The seam has a click; the filmstrip has Move Up and Move Down. The slide objects demonstrate a drag gesture, so dragging is essential to them; their keyboard paths are the alternative.
- **Touch and zoom.** Pinch zoom and text selection allowed. A first tap selects an object without moving it. A filmstrip slide lifts after a 350 ms press. At 320 CSS px nothing scrolls sideways; panel lines and the Command row wrap at spaces, never inside a token.
- **Motion.** Section 3.6. Nothing flashes.
- **Print.** Print This Deck and Cmd or Ctrl+P print the visitor's deck; without the live module, the hero slide.

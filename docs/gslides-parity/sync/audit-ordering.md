# Syncing and the order of stuff: the write path and its guarantees

The audit of the round after the product round, area "Syncing and the order of stuff" (Kevin's directive of 2026-09-20: "make syncing and order of stuff much better and cleaner and more optimized for costs"). Read on `main` at cbd4495 with the product round's uncommitted tree, measured on production (https://turboslide.vercel.app, the blob tier, anonymous principals, shadow authorization) on 2026-09-20 with four probe runs of two and three headless Chromium browsers at human speed on four scratch decks, each trashed and deleted forever afterwards (every run's teardown reads `{"trashed":true,"deletedForever":true,"deckStatus":404}`). The probes, their logs, their JSON and their screenshots are under `docs/gslides-parity/sync/audit-ordering/`. Every claim about the code names the file and the line.

## 1. Summary

What a seller feels today. One person typing in one tab is fine: thirty edits three seconds apart each landed at exactly the next revision with an ops POST answered in 673 ms on average (435 to 1,378 ms), a second tab of the same person read every edit in order with none lost, 1.9 s after the keystroke on average and 3.0 s at worst, and a reload of either tab read the same document at the same revision. Two people on one deck is not fine. On a title slide, the words one of them types are deleted from both browsers whenever the two type into the title within a few seconds of each other or one of them was offline, and both title rows read All changes saved throughout: the heading of a title or statement slide travels as a whole value (`slide.set /heading`), so the last write wins and the open session re-sends its own text after every change it did not make (six or seven commits per exchange). On a body block the two converge and nothing is lost, but the two browsers show two concurrent inserts in opposite orders for as long as the session stays open, because the server applies a POST's splices at their original offsets against a newer document while the client shifts them. A colleague who opens the deck from its address is View only on production and their tab never receives the edits while its revision counter climbs; only a reload shows them. Every one of those is invisible to the writer.

What it costs. One edit is six advanced Blob operations (the snapshot of the whole document, the record, the slide, the manifest, the pulse and the prune's listing) and two or three simple ones; every instance with a tab on the deck then lists the whole deck prefix once per commit it learns of, one advanced operation per thousand files under the prefix. Two idle tabs on two instances make about 32 advanced operations a minute through the presence pushes and the pulls they trigger. The store holds 13,546 files under `decks/`: 70 live decks and 59 leftover folders of removed decks with 2,505 orphan records, and 4,249 presence state files of which one live deck holds 2,284. At Vercel's prices ($5.00 per million advanced operations, $0.40 per million simple, $0.023 per GB month) none of this is expensive in dollars yet (about $18 a month of edits at 30 sellers making 1,000 edits a day, and the same order again in presence); the waste is in the shape, and the shape is what makes the two person defects hard to fix.

The ten items this round should do first, in this order: 1 (the title field as text ops, the lost words), 3 (the viewer's live document), 2 (the server transforms a POST past what landed, as the memory tier does), 4 (no listing in the pull), 5 (the record carries the client op ids, resends idempotent on any instance), 6 (a presence move does not pull the manifest), 8 (the ordering rows in the matrix, driven by this audit's probe on the memory tier locally and the blob tier on the preview), 9 (four advanced operations per edit instead of six), 7 and 11 (the copies and the leftovers, one scheduled sweep), 10 (the compaction record). Items 12 and 13 can wait.

What to leave. The linearization itself: the record claim and the conditional manifest put are correct and measured (two writers, one winner, one conflict, no lost record), and the round five sync engine rework is not the answer (docs/gslides-parity/product/audit-gaps.md section 4 reads why). The pulse on a budget, the stream slots under the tab token, the client posting while its stream is down and the export job record stay as the focus round built them; nothing measured here contradicts them. A CRDT for text stays out: the two text ops with a server side transform are enough for a sales deck, and the defects found are missing transforms and a field that skips them, not a missing algebra.

## 3. The model as built

Every claim names the file and the line on `main` at cbd4495 with the product round's uncommitted tree.

### 3.1 Who assigns the position

On production the realtime tier is `blob` (`TURBOSLIDE_REALTIME=blob`; `packages/realtime/src/select.ts` 74 to 85). On this tier there is no operation stream in front of the log: every ops POST is one commit and the seq of an entry is the revision its record made (`packages/realtime/src/blob.ts` 1 to 5 and 475, `admitted = entries.map((entry) => ({ ...entry, seq: revision }))`). The position is assigned by the instance and confirmed by the store in three steps:

1. The instance's local reducer bumps the revision on its mirror (`packages/schema/src/reduce.ts` 557, `next.deck.revision = document.deck.revision + 1`; `packages/store/src/blob-store.ts` 1429 `file.write`).
2. The instance claims the record number `versions/<n>.json` with `overwrite: false` (`blob-store.ts` 1308 to 1339 `claimRecord`; a number another writer holds is `RecordTakenError`, retried once from the store's document after 200 ms, `CLAIM_RETRY_MS` 555, `runWrite(1)` 1564).
3. The instance commits by putting `deck.json` with `ifMatch` set to the etag it synced (`blob-store.ts` 1478 to 1483, `putDocument('deck.json', synced)`; `blob-vercel.ts` 219 passes `ifMatch` to `@vercel/blob` 2.8.0). A precondition failure is the conflict outcome (`blob-store.ts` 1531, `conflictFromStore`).

So the brief's premise that Blob has no conditional put does not hold: the manifest put with `ifMatch` is the linearization point, measured against the live store on 2026-09-11 (`blob-vercel.ts` 37 to 41, the strong etag rule). Two fluid compute instances that take writes for one deck in the same moment end with one committed manifest and one conflict (`hosted.test.ts` 369 and 405 hold the fake to it), and the runs measured it: in run 3 part b two POSTs from one base 165 ms apart were answered revisions 15 and 16, the second with `between` naming the first (`run3-ordering-run.json` phases.closed.rounds[0].wire). The record claim is a second guard on the same race that also makes the record visible to other instances before the commit (`blob-store.ts` 1296 to 1307).

What the instance does with a conflict: `apps/studio/src/server/room.ts` `admitOnBlob` (1560 to 1746) places the POST's entries against the mirror at or above the client's base (`liveAtLeast`, 1445 to 1483: up to six forced syncs 500 ms apart), appends, and on a head that moved re-syncs to that head and places once more (`BLOB_APPEND_RETRIES` 1, 1558 and 1683 to 1701). A second miss is a 409 `resync` to the client (1710 to 1717); a miss at the same revision (a claim in flight elsewhere, an unprovable mirror) is a 503 `store_busy` and the client resends after a second (1703 to 1709). The client's `resync` reloads the whole document through `readEditorDeck` and refolds its pending ops on it (`packages/realtime/client/room-client.ts` 908 to 932; `apps/studio/src/editor/controller.tsx` 1771 to 1779). No run met a 409 or a 503: 173 ops POSTs over the four runs, every one answered 200.

Vercel prices `put`, `list` and `copy` as advanced at $5.00 per million and `head` and origin reads as simple at $0.40 per million (vercel.com/docs/vercel-blob/usage-and-pricing, read 2026-09-20; `del` is free; the Hobby plan includes 2,000 advanced operations a month and locks the store past them). Six advanced operations per edit is $0.00003 per edit, $18 a month at 600,000 edits a month (30 sellers, 1,000 edits a day, 20 days). The measured POST latency of a single writer is the sum of these calls: 673 ms on average, 435 to 1,378 ms (run 2 phase 1, 30 POSTs).

### 3.3 How another instance learns of a commit, and what it costs

An instance with a client stream open on the deck heads `.turboslide/pulse.json` every 2 s (`pulse.ts` 23 `HOSTED_POLL_MS`; `blob.ts` 347 to 403 `startPulsePoll`) and every fifteenth tick heads the manifest instead (`PULSE_SAFETY_TICKS` 27). When the etag moved it reads three things together (`blob.ts` 306 to 319 `readMoved`): the manifest through `store.sync(true)` (one `head`, then `pull()` with its listing and its record reads), the presence record (`presence-store.ts` 526 to 552, a `head` and a proven `get`), and the comments index (`comments-store.ts` 629 to 660, a `head` and a `get` when moved). Then `announce` (`blob.ts` 258 to 287) reads the mirror's records and publishes an `op` and a `checkpoint` per record above the position it announced last, to the streams on this instance only.

So per remote commit, per reading instance: one `list` of the deck prefix (one advanced operation per 1,000 files under the prefix; one live deck on production holds about 2,900 files, three pages), two simple reads per new record, one simple read of the snapshot, two of the presence record, one of the comments index, on top of the tick's head. The listing is the one advanced operation on this path and it is redundant for the records, which the same function walks by number right after (1035 to 1043); it is what the comments sidecar and nothing else needs (item 3).

The presence record travels the same way: a push is three puts (the copy under its md5, the record with `ifMatch`, the pulse) at most every 5 s per deck per instance, and a heartbeat pushes once a row's remaining life is under half of 30 s (`presence-store.ts` 22 to 30, 66 to 70, 654 to 670). Two idle tabs on two instances therefore push about four times a minute each, and every push moves every other instance's poll into a `readMoved` with its listing: about 32 advanced operations a minute for two idle tabs, $0.01 an hour, the same order as the edits themselves (item 6).

The measured propagation: 1,948 ms on average from A's keystroke to B's screen, 662 ms at best and 3,000 ms at worst over 30 edits (run 2 phase 1; `run2-ordering-run.json` phases.serial.tokens), which is the POST (673 ms) plus up to one poll tick (2 s) plus the pull. In run 1 the same arithmetic showed on B's revision counter alone (718 to 4,400 ms behind the POST), since B was a viewer and received no ops.

### 3.4 How the client orders remote ops against its own

`packages/realtime/client/room-client.ts`. The client holds `pending` (applied locally, not in the stream), `retained` (acknowledged at a seq, not yet covered) and `server` (the document at `seq`); `local` is `server` with `pending` folded on top (1 to 12, 600 to 632 `fold`). A flush posts the unsent pending ops as one POST with `base: { seq }` (1119 to 1131), at once for a structural op, 50 ms into a drag and 100 ms while typing (176 `FLUSH_MS`), one POST in flight at a time (1093 to 1097). The blob channel folds a POST's entries into one mutation list and commits them as one revision (`blob.ts` 434 to 436, `foldMutation`; `coalesce.ts` 93 to 109), so every entry of a batch shares one seq and the client buffers siblings per seq (438, 870 to 888 `take`, 828 to 839 `drain`); run 2 phase 3 shows it: A's two offline ops were one POST answered `admitted: [61, 61]`.

A remote entry moves the pending text ops past it with the schema's transform (784 `transformPast`; `packages/schema/src/transform.ts` 347) and a pending op that no longer applies is returned to its author (785 to 790); non text mutations are not transformed, they are refolded on the new server document and the last writer wins per pointer (`coalesce.ts` 102 to 105, `samePointer`; `rewritesText` 342 to 366 for a text op under a whole text rewrite, and `transform.ts` 332 treats any `slide.set` of the slide as a rewrite). This is operational transformation for the two text ops and a rebase for everything else, not a CRDT. An entry that cannot apply on this copy schedules a resync at its revision (795 to 805).

The server side of the same transform exists on the memory tier only: `admitOps` transforms each entry past `channel.since(base)` before it places it (`room.ts` 1262 to 1301), while `admitOnBlob` places the entries verbatim on a document at or above the base (1588 to 1658; item 4). The measured effect is section 4.4.

The client's own acknowledgement on the blob tier has three paths, because a record carries `clientId: 'store'` and `opId: 'store:<n>'` and never the client's op id (`blob.ts` 115 to 126 `entryOfRecord`): the POST's answer names the entries with their seq (1161 to 1195); a store echo that arrives first is matched to a posted batch by its base and the byte equality of its folded mutations (639 to 688 `settleOwnEcho`); an entry at or behind the position from a resent POST settles by op id (699 to 716 `settleBehind`). The server side is a per instance memory of the last 512 admitted op ids (`room.ts` 1509 to 1549 `rememberBlobAdmitted`), which its own comment says does not catch a resend that lands on another instance (1516 to 1520; item 5).

### 3.5 What undo does after a remote op

Undo is per author: the controller records the inverse of each local write (`room-client.ts` 1442 to 1468 `apply` returns `inverse` and a local clock) and asks `transformSince(mutations, at)` to move that inverse past every remote entry that landed after it (1486 to 1495; the markers at 1513 to 1524), over the last 2,000 entries (`RECENT_ENTRIES` 194). Text inverses transform; structural inverses are re-applied as they are and re-anchored by the reducer (`room.ts` 1024 to 1049 `reanchor`) or refused by it, in which case the reducer's message is the seller's reject card (`room.ts` 1079 to 1125 `landCandidate`). The version log's own undo (`documentAt`, `versions.ts` 188 to 226) replays the inverses of every later record from the current document and needs the log to be contiguous (`assertContiguous` 159 to 181). Nothing measured here drove undo; the round five stress probe's finding (an undo after a resize doing nothing about once in 300 tries, SPEC-5-amendments row 78) is the open case and belongs with item 4, since a stale server placement is one way an inverse misses.

### 3.6 Snapshots and compaction

Every commit stores the whole document as canonical JSON under `snapshots/<md5 of the deck.json bytes>.json` before the manifest flips (`blob-store.ts` 1431 to 1435, 1463; `snapshots.ts` 1 to 9). Retention keeps the snapshots of the newest 50 records and of every named version (`snapshots.ts` 22 `SNAPSHOT_KEEP_RECORDS`, 90 to 101) and the prune runs after every commit with a five minute grace (`blob-store.ts` 1527; `snapshots.ts` 30). The version records themselves are never compacted: `versions/<n>.json` grows by one per commit for the life of the deck, a cold mirror pulls every record by number (`blob-store.ts` 1035 to 1043, up to 10,000), `store.records()` parses the whole folder on every call (`versions.ts` 75 to 96), and `announce` calls it once per poll tick that moved (`blob.ts` 234). The checkpointer of `apps/studio/src/server/checkpoint.ts` (a coalesced write after 2 s of quiet, 10 s under typing) runs on the memory and redis tiers only; on the blob tier the coalescing unit is one POST (`blob.ts` 434 to 436) and `admitOnBlob` never schedules it (items 9 and 10).

### 3.7 What a reload reads

`apps/studio/src/server/write.ts` 258 to 347 `readEditorDeck`: `roomFor` (the room's store, a mirror on this instance), `liveAtLeast(room, atLeast)` (one forced `sync`, a `head deck.json` and a `pull` when the etag moved, `room.ts` 1466 to 1470), then `store.read()`, `store.listVersions()` and `store.leases()` together (a `get leases.json`; the other two read the mirror). The payload carries the document at the head, the newest 50 records without their mutations (`EDITOR_VERSIONS_KEPT` 165) and `room.seq = live.seq`, which on the blob tier is the revision (`room.ts` 484 to 489). There is no tail of operations to read after the snapshot: the record is the operation. The stream then opens with `since=seq` and replays the records above it (`room.ts` 2081 to 2107 `replayFor`, `decks.$deckId.stream.ts` 222 to 236), and `hello.covered` is `live.seq` (`room.ts` 715 to 716). Measured: three reloads in parallel after 61 revisions took 1,778, 2,065 and 2,032 ms to a connected, settled editor, and the three documents were byte equal at revision 61 (`run2-reload-a.json`, `run2-reload-b.json`, `run2-reload-c.json`).

### 3.8 What a viewer's stream carries

`room.ts` 1886 to 1916 `filterEventForReader`: a reader with the `viewer` role receives checkpoints, presence and the deck level notices and never an `op` or `ops` frame. The controller's handling of a non external checkpoint moves the revision on the snapshot and nothing else (`controller.tsx` 1659 to 1674, "the revision moved and nothing else"), and its stream handler refreshes the version list and shows the external banner (1731 to 1741); nothing reloads the document. On production a second anonymous principal on a deck made from `/new` lands on the viewer floor (`apps/studio/src/server/authorize.ts` 188 to 194 `shadowStanding`; `docs/PRODUCT.md` section 2 rank 3 names the default as the product round's change, not yet on production). Item 2.

### 3.9 What a title slide's text is

The heading and the lead of a title slide and the big text of a statement slide are slide fields, not blocks (`InlineText.tsx` 278 to 283 `isSlideField`, 272 to 275 `slideTextPath`), and every burst on them is `slide.set` of the whole value plus `deck.set /title` for the heading (`textBurstMutation` 360 to 408 routes a slide field to `textCommitMutation` at 380 to 382; 302 to 313). A `slide.set` is never transformed (`transform.ts` 343 to 346, "every other mutation passes through unchanged") and the coalesce keeps the last value per pointer (`coalesce.ts` 46 to 47). The open session learns of a collaborator's change through `sessionReconcile` (`text-fit.ts` 54 to 58): another plain text is `'resend'`, and the editor re-sends the editable's text (`Editor.tsx` 1681 to 1745). Item 1; the measured effect is section 4.3.

## 5. The cleaner model

The log, the position and the commit stay what they are: one record per commit under `versions/<n>.json`, the number assigned by the instance's reducer and made true by the claim and the conditional manifest put. What changes is around it.

1. One ordered log per deck with the sequence assigned in one place. The revision is the sequence and the manifest put with `ifMatch` is the one place; the claim stays as the early visibility of the record. The record gains `origin: { clientId, opIds }` and the channel's `entryOfRecord` emits them, so every instance answers a resend from the log and the client acknowledges by id (item 5).
2. The server transforms before it places, on every tier. `admitOnBlob` transforms each entry past the records between the POST's base and the live revision, with the `transformEntry` the memory tier uses, and answers the placed mutations; the client and the server then derive one document and the open session has nothing to repair (item 4).
3. Every text a person can type into is a text run. The heading, the lead and the big text become targets of `text.splice` and `text.replace`; `slide.set` on them is a rewrite of that field alone; the deck title follows the heading in the reducer (item 1).
4. The head is read, never listed. The poll reads the pulse body (a simple `get`) and syncs only what its `source` names; the pull walks the records by number from the mirror's last and proves the document from the snapshot the manifest etag names, and reads the comments sidecar through its index head; `list` is left to the prune, the removal and the listing page (items 3 and 6). A cheaper head store than a Blob `head` per 2 s per instance is the costs audit's question; a `head` is $0.40 per million and a tick is $0.00072 an hour per open deck per instance, so the listing and the puts, not the head, are what to remove.
5. Compaction on a schedule. The prune of snapshots every 20th commit or on the idle tick; a snapshot every 20th revision and on `version.save` once the documents live on the private store; a compaction record every 200 revisions with the older records archived; a daily sweep that removes folders without a manifest, orphan snapshots and presence copies older than an hour (items 7, 9, 10, 12).
6. The reconnect covers exactly the gap. It does today: the stream opens at `since=seq`, the replay is the records above it (`replayFor`), `hello.covered` is `live.seq` and the ops answer's `between` fills what sits under an admitted entry; the reopen after 20 s offline replayed 56 to 60 and the resend went at base 60 (run 2 phase 3). The one gap is the viewer, whose stream carries nothing to fill it (item 2).
7. The invariants as matrix rows, driven from the probes on two tiers (item 8, section 6).

What this leaves alone: the stream slots under the tab token, the client posting while its stream is down and owning the reopen (measured: A posted its queue 220 ms after its stream reopened), the pulse on a budget, the export job record, the one write per second per deck per instance spacing (no run met it; the cadence of a typing seller is one POST per acknowledged POST).

# Judge 1: collaboration fidelity and the sales paths

Written 2026-09-13 against the three round three proposals in `docs/gslides-parity/design-3/` and the eleven research reports in `docs/gslides-parity/research-3/`, with the code at `main` `61b16e4`. Judge 1 of 3 scores as a Google Slides power user and a UX researcher of sales teams. The two questions this judge asks of every proposal are whether its collaboration, comments and sharing behave the way Google Slides behaves, in Google's positions and with Google's words, and whether a salesperson who opens a deck from a link, never signs in, and later does sign in, gets the shortest honest path at every step. Architecture fit, security and buildability are scored from the proposals' own text against the research and the code.

## Method

- All three proposals were read in full: `proposal-1-google.md` (747 lines), `proposal-2-sales.md` (828 lines), `proposal-3-architecture.md` (885 lines).
- Where the proposals disagree, or where one makes a claim the others do not, the research report was opened at the point of disagreement: report 01 sections 2, 3, 5, 9, 11 and Unverified; report 02 sections 3.3 to 3.5; report 03 Parts B, I3, J and J1; report 04 F6, F9 and P8; report 06 sections 1.3 and 4.10; report 08 sections 3.3, 9 and 12.1; report 09 Decisions, 2.2, 2.4 and 3.6; report 10 F32, F40 and F47; report 11 section 6.2 and the layout shift rows; round one's `research/10-identity-sharing-and-presence.md` Part C2 and C3.
- Code facts checked at `61b16e4`: `packages/schema/src/mutations.ts` lines 42 to 50 (the `text.replace` range is documented as `[start, end)` in the markup string); `packages/schema/src/reduce.ts` lines 383 to 392 (`version.restore` diffs against a resolved version); `packages/store/src/versions.ts` line 168 (an older version is resolved by applying each record's stored `inverse` through `applyMutations`); `packages/store/src/blob-store.ts` line 451 (push re-submits a record's stored `mutations`); `packages/chrome/src/TitleRow.tsx` lines 22 to 25 and 259 to 290 (the right group today is the comments stub, Slideshow and Share).
- No Google page was opened and no account was signed in. Every Google fact below comes from the reports, which read the public pages on 2026-09-13; the URLs are repeated in the Sources section with that date. Two facts come from this judge's product knowledge and are marked as such where used.
- Rules of the text: plain technical English, sentence case, no em dashes, no metaphors, no trailing periods on headings, full sentences in body text.

## Proposal 1, Google faithful

Fidelity 9. This is the proposal a Google Slides power user recognises surface by surface. Every collaboration table has a Google column and a Turboslide column, and the Turboslide column takes Google's label, position, shortcut and rule wherever a report records one: the four presence surfaces including chat, Follow with every stop Google lists and every refusal, the overflow chip opening "Join chat", the Last edit tooltip and dot, the Share request dot and Review banner, the six comment anchors, `r`, `e`, `j`, `k`, `u`, Ctrl+Enter and the two step chords in the shortcuts dialog, the four display modes with Ctrl+Alt+Shift+j, "Assign to", "Reassign", "Done", "Re-open", "Get link to this comment", the three notification levels under Tools > Notification settings and in the panel's foot, the two halves of the Share dialog with the gear's two switches, "Add expiration", "Transfer ownership" with Google's accept flow, Publish to web with Stop publishing, version history grouped with Show changes, the Collaborators list on Shift+Tab, and the 100 editing tabs ceiling with a sentence. It follows Google on the two points where the other two depart: viewers see comments read only and named people are named for everyone in the room. Where Google's page is silent it says so and names its own words. The point lost: a commenter link lands on `/deck`, Turboslide's viewer shell, where none of the comment controls the proposal designs exist, so the manager in the sales trace who holds a commenter link has one more step than in Google, and the roster menu's list beyond "Join chat" is Turboslide's own, which the proposal admits.

Ease for sales 8. The anonymous path is right: a sealed cookie, one prompt on the first write with a label prefilled, a chip that says "· guest" beside a typed name, "Go to slide 12" where Follow is refused, and a click by click trace from a first open to a revoked prospect link with no conflict card anywhere. The Following plate "Following Maya · Stop", the inline recovery with the person's own text, and "Not signed in. Sign in, or copy an edit link, to get back to this presentation from another browser" are the right sentences. Two things hold the score at 8. The proposal has the largest surface of the three (chat panel, activity panel with a trend bar, inbox, comments, versions, profile, avatar builder) and states no rule about what stays one step behind a control, so the title row and the Tools menu carry the most a rep has never seen. And the dither toggle gives a two tone screen at the pipeline's identity, so the "GT deck's photograph look" Kevin asked for needs the Ink point and Paper point sliders on every photograph; nothing offers the deck's recorded values in one click.

Architecture fit 7. The stream, the checkpointer, the sidecar, the access record, `authorize()`, the alias table, `resolvePrincipal`, `packages/identity` and the forty odd actions through the table all fit the round two foundation, and every new document field is optional at schema version 1. The point lost is the change to `text.replace`: the proposal moves its range from markup offsets to plain offsets and states that "records already written keep their meaning because a record's mutations are replayed against the document as it was". The code replays stored records through the current reducer (`versions.ts:168` resolves an older version by applying stored inverses; `blob-store.ts:451` re-submits stored mutations on push; `deck follow` in the proposal's own 1.8 replays records forward), so a reinterpreted range changes what an old record does wherever a marked run precedes it, and restoring a version older than the round would produce a different document. Proposal 3 caught this and added new ops instead. A second, smaller point: the action count of decision 13 does not match section 3.10.

Security 7. The plan of reports 04 and 10 is applied step by step with the WAF table, the per identity quota table, the deck caps, the kill switches, the sandboxed and sanitized `html` block, the re-encoded uploads, `safeFetch` with pinned DNS, the widened CSRF filter, the headers and nonce CSP, the two Blob stores under unguessable keys, the client upload path, structured logging and retention, each with a test. Three items of the reports are missing: the per page nonce on the share and comment window handlers (report 10 F47, High), the signed thumbnail URL (report 04 F6, High), and the export cancel token (F9). The first is defense in depth once the frame and the CSP land, but the proposal's own rollout lands the CSP report only for two weeks, so the window is real.

Buildability 6. About 438 builder hours plus 72 for the integrator and verifier in nine days, six builders with disjoint files, day by day deliverables and an acceptance command per builder, and an ordered cut line (chat, the trend bar, `version.delete`, two avatar tabs, GitHub sign in, the Play button) whose every cut leaves a menu row present and disabled with a clause. That is the right shape. Six is the honest score for the round that would build every collaboration surface Google has, including chat and the accessibility rows, and would also have to carry the `text.replace` change through `InlineText`, the reducer and every replay path.

## Proposal 2, sales first and safe by default

Fidelity 7. Where Google has a surface the proposal takes Google's position and label: the Share dialog's two halves, the gear, Publish to the web, the comment card and panel with For you, the display modes, the chords, Notification settings, View > Mode with Commenting, the View only button, Follow with Google's stops. It departs in four places and names each: viewers do not see comments unless the owner turns a switch on, link visitors see grant holders as "An editor" unless the owner turns a switch on, chat waits for round four, and deleting a first comment keeps the replies. The first two are defaults of switches in Google's gear rather than moved controls, so a Google user finds the switch where the gear is, but a team that expects Google's behaviour meets both on day one. The commenter landing is inconsistent between 3.8 and 9.3, and the title row puts the presence slot to the right of the comments glyph and the inbox plate where this judge's product knowledge puts Google's avatars to the left of the comment icon.

Ease for sales 9. The proposal starts from four situations a sales team lives in (a manager reviewing by link, two reps on one deck, a deck leaking through a wrong share, nobody wanting an account) and derives five rules it then applies in every section: the shortest path is the safe path, progressive disclosure, honest words, zero shift, one click brand look. The consequences are concrete and right: Share opens on Restricted with the people field first and Anyone with the link defaults to Viewer; the footer reads "Speaker notes and skipped slides never travel with a view or comment link"; accounts appear in the own chip's menu and nowhere else; section 9.6 lists every refusal in the words the rep reads; section 2.7 says in plain words what a rep sees when a second rep joins; the trace lands the manager in Commenting mode with Insert > Comment live and no View only button because the manager may comment. The default that hides comments from viewers is the one departure from Google this judge would keep, because the sales trace of round one's research/10 C2 shows Google's own answer is a copy without notes and comments, which Turboslide's users will not make; the notes and skipped slides rule of report 09 decision 6 already departs for the same reason. The point lost is offline: the pending queue lives in memory only, so a closed tab loses unsent edits where the other two recover them.

Architecture fit 7. The `RealtimeChannel` interface with three implementations and `selectRealtime(env)` beside `selectStore`, the sidecar, the access record with the matrix table tested, the per identity index, `packages/identity`, about seventy actions through the table, schema version 1 with every field optional, and a rollout order that never breaks a link. It shares proposal 1's `text.replace` change and the same replay claim, which the code does not support, and that is the point lost. The `Inbox` interface with a Redis home for anonymous principals and the identity database for accounts is the right split.

Security 8. The most complete security section of the three in coverage: every F number of reports 04 and 10 named against a change and a test, including the F47 nonce and re-authentication, the F6 signed thumbnail URL, the F9 cancel token, the registry caps of F14, the MCP sessions bound to keys, and P8 (Chromium out of the secret holding function) inside the round. The hosted asset write fix lands first because it loses work today. Eighteen risks are named with mitigations. The point lost is P8's realism inside nine days beside everything else; it is the right item and the wrong round for it, and the proposal's own order puts it tenth of eleven steps.

Buildability 6. About 430 builder hours, six builders with disjoint files, three merges with the seams landing on day one, an eleven step rollout that keeps every existing link working if the round ships in parts, and the most honest risk list. Against that: seventy actions is the largest table growth, P8 adds a second project or the worker path, B1 owns identity, access, both stores, bundles, the home page and the agent auth at once, and the 3.8 versus 9.3 inconsistency must be settled before B1 and B3 build the exchange route and the shell.

## Proposal 3, architecture

Fidelity 8. The collaboration surfaces follow report 11's drawings and Google's positions (the presence slot between the comments glyph and Slideshow as report 11 6.2 placed it, the filmstrip chip, the caret and flag, the Following plate, View > Live pointers with Google's two items, View > Mode with Commenting, Tools > Notification settings and Activity, the Share dialog's two halves, Publish to the web with Stop publishing, version history by author), and it keeps Google's rule that a viewer sees comments read only. It departs on the roster names as proposal 2 does, defers chat, and fires the name prompt on presence join, which means the first open of any editor asks a name before the person has done anything, a behaviour Google never has and report 03 B3 did not propose. The trace in 10.1 contradicts its own 2.8 on whether a link visitor sees Kevin's name. The fine points are good: Kai on a phone sees no pointer (computer only), the assignee's tick reads Done, the Share dialog's Review slot reads "No pending requests" when empty.

Ease for sales 7. Three things here are the best in the set: the Photograph preset chip (black 120, white 230, gamma 0.9, the middle of the GT deck's recorded ranges) beside the Dither toggle, so a rep reaches the deck's photograph look in one click instead of two sliders; the offline recovery sentence "3 unsaved changes from this browser; Apply or Discard"; and the reserved "Uploading 6.2 MB" line in the Background dialog. The studio also degrades cleanly: without `DATABASE_URL` the Sign in row is absent and every collaboration feature still works anonymously. Against that, the prompt on presence join is the one intrusive moment of any anonymous path in the three proposals; "Undo skipped: Maya changed this since" reads colder than proposal 1's and 2's snackbar; the proposal has no situations table and no refusals list, so a builder infers the words; and link visitors see "An editor" for the signed in owner, which in a mostly anonymous team means the one person who did sign in becomes the one person nobody can name.

Architecture fit 9. The four truths with one owner each (the document and its log; the deck's side records; the room; identity) are the clearest statement of where every byte of this round lives, and each row of the decisions table cites its source or says "this proposal". Two decisions are the reason for the score. `text.splice` and `text.mark` are new ops in plain offsets, so `text.replace` keeps its meaning for every record already written and for agents, the reducer gains two cases with exact inverses, and schema version stays 1 with no reinterpretation; this is the correct reading of the code the other two missed. The storage migration is an admin action with a cursor, etag verification per document, a dual read window, a flag rollback before the cutover and batched deletes after it, where the other two describe a background copy. Beyond those: `packages/realtime` is framework free with a fake transport for the room client's tests; the principal record lives on the server with a 90 day TTL and the browser mirror is "never the truth"; kill switches are Redis flags with a CLI and no third product; the `access` stream event makes a tab re-read its role; `SERVER_SIDE_WINDOW_ACTIONS` gives a click and an agent call one path; the MCP stdio tool list is brought under the SDK's buffer before any MCP work, which shows the proposal read VERIFICATION-2. The point lost is a fourth new package and the integrator owning `edit.$deckId.tsx` plus seven server files while also merging and deploying.

Security 8. The plan is applied change by change with a test table naming the `pnpm check` step for each, including the F47 nonce, the F6 signed thumbnail, the F9 cancel token, the two layer rate limit table with numbers, the deck caps with the room going read only over 25 MB, the three layer `html` block fix, the upload pipeline, `safeFetch`, the widened CSRF filter, the headers and CSP, secrets, the dependency gate, logging with retention per class, and the kill switches. It is the only proposal that names the interim exposure of documents on the public store until the cutover (risk 6) and orders the cutover before enforcement is announced. P8 is deferred with its egress and flag parts landed now, which this judge reads as the realistic choice.

Buildability 6. No hours are estimated; the day plan and the seams of 11.2 (types, channel, identity, chrome contract, renderer DOM fixed on day one) are the strongest device for parallel builders in the three proposals, and R6 (sign in) and R7 (dithers) can land after R5 without blocking each other. Against that: B3 owns identity, accounts, `authorize()`, rate limits, headers, `safeFetch`, uploads, the sanitizer, the frame and two specs, which is two builders' work; the storage migration is an irreversible live step inside the round; the two internal contradictions must be settled; and about sixty four actions plus four new modules is the same order of scope as the other two.

## Winner

Proposal 3 wins, 38 to 37 to 37. On this judge's own two criteria proposal 1 leads by two points on fidelity and proposal 2 by two on ease, and proposal 3 sits between them on both. The total goes to proposal 3 because of what can and cannot be grafted. Every fidelity row proposal 1 wins (chat's position, the roster names, the accessibility rows, the tabs ceiling, the title row order, viewers reading comments) is a label, a default or a menu row that the synthesis adds to proposal 3's chrome contract in an afternoon; every ease row proposal 2 wins (the situations table, the five rules, the footer sentence, the refusals list, the own chip menu, the Commenting mode landing) is a rule and a set of sentences that bind any of the three skeletons. Proposal 3's two architectural differences run the other way: the additive text ops and the specified storage migration cannot be grafted onto proposals 1 or 2 without rewriting their sections 1 and 8, and without them the synthesis ships a reducer that misreads every version written before this round. A collaboration feature that corrupts version history on restore is not faithful to Google, whose version history is the one collaboration surface reps trust most.

## Ideas that must not ship

1. Changing the meaning of `text.replace` ranges from markup offsets to plain offsets (proposals 1 and 2). `versions.ts:168` and `blob-store.ts:451` replay stored records through the current reducer, so the change corrupts the restore of any version written before this round wherever a marked run precedes the range. Proposal 3's new ops replace it.
2. The name prompt on presence join (proposal 3). The first open of an editor is not a write; the prompt fires on the first edit, comment or lease (report 03 B3), as proposals 1 and 2 have it.
3. A pending queue that lives in memory only (proposal 2). A closed tab loses the rep's last edits; the IndexedDB mirror of proposals 1 and 3 is required.
4. Landing a commenter link on `/deck`, the viewer shell (the section text of all three, and report 09 2.2). None of the comment controls exists there; Google opens the editor in Commenting mode, and proposal 2's trace does the same.

In a mostly anonymous team the rule makes the one signed in person the one nobody can name, and the prospect case it protects is already closed by presence being off on `/deck`, `/embed`, `/present` and `/print` and by view links defaulting to Viewer.

## Decisions for Kevin

1. Roster names for link visitors. Google shows named people's names to everyone in the room (this judge's product knowledge; the reports record only who gets named). Proposal 1 keeps that with an owner switch to hide; proposals 2 and 3 hide by default per report 10 F32. This judge recommends Google's default, because the team is mostly anonymous, presence is already off on the view routes, and view links default to Viewer; the switch covers the deck where a competitor might hold an editor link.
2. Whether viewers see comments. Google shows them (unverified on a public page, report 08 12.1; every comparable product does). Proposal 2 hides them from viewers by default with a gear switch; proposals 1 and 3 show them read only. This judge recommends proposal 2's default, as the one deliberate departure in the collaboration surface, for the same reason report 09 decision 6 hides notes and skipped slides from viewers: the view link is the prospect handoff and the Google workflow of making a copy without comments will not be followed here. The footer sentence names it either way.
3. Chat in this round (proposal 1) or a stub in Google's position with the panel in round four (proposals 2 and 3). This judge recommends the stub unless day 6 shows time; a sales team chats in Slack.
4. The title row order: presence slot left of the comments glyph (proposal 1 and this judge's product knowledge of Google) or between the comments glyph and Slideshow (report 11 6.2, proposals 2 and 3). This judge recommends proposal 1's order and asks the verifier to list the fact as unverified.
5. The Photograph preset values (proposal 3: black 120, white 230, gamma 0.9) beside the identity defaults, and whether the Background dialog's Dither toggle applies the preset by default for a photograph so the one click look is the tuned one. This judge recommends the toggle at identity with the preset chip one click away, as proposal 3 has it, since a uniform screen warning at identity is visible in the metrics line.
6. Whether anonymous link visitors may comment under their label when the link role allows (all three say yes; Google lets signed out people comment as "Anonymous", report 01 section 3). This judge recommends yes.
7. The word "guest" beside a typed name (all three), against "visitor". This judge recommends "guest", the word Figma and Notion use for an outsider.
8. The decisions all three proposals hand up unchanged from the reports: the production domain before passkeys ship, the sending domain and address for Resend, whether two Marketplace products beside Blob (Upstash Redis, Neon Postgres) are acceptable, and whether GitHub sign in is wanted for a sales audience or only for engineers and agent operators.

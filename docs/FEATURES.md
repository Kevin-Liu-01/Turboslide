# The features round

The binding specification of the features round, written 2026-09-20 by the round's synthesizer from the four production audits under `docs/gslides-parity/features/` (`audit-objects.md`, `audit-fonts.md`, `audit-logos.md`, `audit-shaders.md`, with their evidence folders beside them), `docs/PRODUCT.md` (sections 4, 7, 8 and 9), `docs/RETURN.md` (sections 1, 6 and 8), `docs/FOCUS.md` (section 6), the identity record (`docs/brand.md`), the grammar (`docs/grammar.md`), the menu model (`packages/chrome/src/menus/model.ts`) and the core matrix (`docs/gslides-parity/focus/core-matrix.json`, 698 rows in the row shape its `$comment` names), and revised the same day against the two judgments under the same folder (`judge-seller.md`, `judge-design.md`; section 10 records where each was answered). Production is https://turboslide.vercel.app serving `main` at `9293df4`, the return round plus the caret hotfix. The shared checkout carries the product round's uncommitted tree, which is the base this round lands on; where a file's line numbers differ between production and the checkout the audits say so (audit-objects, the paragraph before section 1). A citation of the form "audit-logos 12" names item 12 of that audit's table, and the item's own "Today" cell carries the screenshot and the source lines. The round five branch was read and never checked out, merged or cherry picked. This document touched no source file and ran no git write command.

Every audit drove production through the product's own controls with headless Chromium (playwright-core from the repository root) at 1440 by 900, and at 1280 by 800 where the audit says so, at human speed, on scratch decks created from `/new` and trashed and deleted forever afterwards, each confirmed to answer 404; each audit's method section records this. A row is "works", "broken", "flaky" or "not driven"; this document keeps those four words and reports what happened. The proposals and the "after this round" sentences are specification, not measurement, and read as such.

## 1. Kevin's directive and what this round adds

Kevin's directive of 2026-09-20, verbatim: "make the ui for editing stuff like tables and diagrams and more much more inutitive and easy. also make sure were using the riht rsms inter and add a few more fonts and integrate thesvg.org for easy logo adding and searching!!!! this is the time when we can add new features like adding a shader library with all the controls we have in glyphfield, the logo feature, and more". His earlier words that still hold: "remember this is actually going to be used by majority sales in our org"; "add more font options too"; "we must, must must be able to drag and move around ANYTHING"; the click model of `docs/gslides-parity/focus/AMENDMENTS.md` A1; multiplayer with optional logins.

What this round adds, in the order Kevin named it, each a feature a brand's sellers and marketers reach for and each intuitive on first use, which this document takes to mean four things: the first gesture a seller tries does the thing (one click on a cell of a table places the caret there, as Google, Notion and Pitch do, audit-objects section 2 "Start typing in a cell"; a double click on a chart opens its numbers, section 2.2 rank 7; typing on a grid cell edits it); every word a seller reads is the seller's (Shader, Logo, Preset, "Ink on paper", "Free to use"), never an id or a designer's noun (research 07 rule 22 as audit-shaders 13 applies it; a licence identifier is an id, 4.6); every gesture is one undo step with a snackbar carrying Undo where the result is not under the pointer (audit-objects 4.3); and the brand kit's colours lead every plate a new feature draws (`docs/PRODUCT.md` 4.1; audit-objects 19; audit-shaders 8).

1. Tables, charts, diagrams, word art and labelled shapes edit directly (section 2). The objects work today and are one gesture too far: a table needs a double click to type into, a row is four clicks away, Bold on a range opens an engineering dialog, the chart grid ignores typing, a diagram step is two objects with the same box (audit-objects section 1). Section 2 gives each object an inner layer under the A1 click model, amended for tables so that the first click places the caret while a drag from inside still moves the table.
2. The right Inter and more fonts (section 3). The upright is Rasmus Andersson's Inter 4.1 byte for byte; the italic is the 4.0 file, the code names a release tag that does not exist, the licence link a marketer would click answers 404, the metric matched fallback face is declared and never used, and the sheet has no tabular figures for a seller's numbers (audit-fonts 1.1 to 1.3). The product round ships the 26 family catalog and the dropdown (`docs/PRODUCT.md` 4.2); this round fixes the Inter files and adds Geist, Geist Mono and six more open families on top of it.
3. A logo picker over thesvg.org (section 4). A seller who wants a customer's logo leaves the product, and when they come back with the SVG every logo site offers first the intake refuses it in a developer's sentence (audit-logos section 4). Section 4 gives Insert > Logo with the seller's own brand first, a server route that stores the mark on the deck, a daily refreshed index the picker works from offline, Replace image > Logo for the deck the seller already has, and Tailor's Find the customer's logo.
4. A shader library with Glyphfield's controls (section 5). A shader exists and nobody but a GT designer can reach or finish one: Insert > Material is behind the switch, the pickers are text rows, Format options shows the raw schema, the frozen frame never reaches a viewer, and the palette is General Translation's constants (audit-shaders, the summary). Section 5 gives the gallery, the Shader section with Glyphfield's control groups as sliders, the automatic still, the kit's colours as the palette and the `shader.*` actions. This round ships the shader as an object a seller places and resizes on a slide; the kit's background shader, the form that makes it a brand asset set once in the kit, is the shader lane's first P1 item and lands only if that lane's P1 condition holds (5.1, 5.2).

## 2. The editing model for tables, charts, diagrams, word art and labelled shapes

### 2.1 The model

The model keeps the click model of AMENDMENTS.md A1 for the object as a whole and gives each structured object a second, inner layer that Google Slides, Notion, Pitch and Keynote all have (audit-objects section 4): once the object is selected, its parts are direct.

The table. A pointer down in a cell of an unselected table followed by a move past the move threshold drags the table (A1 rule 2; `objectPressPlan` in `packages/viewer/src/Selection.tsx` decides the press, `Editor.tsx` 5034 to 5041, and the gesture is decided at the threshold, before any caret is placed). A pointer up without a move selects the table (the ring, the eight handles, the chip) and places the caret in the pressed cell at the click point, so one click types where Google, Notion and Pitch type in one click and Turboslide took two today (audit-objects section 2, "Start typing in a cell"; row 2 of `run-tables-rows.json`). On the selected table a click on another cell moves the caret; a pointer down in a cell followed by a move selects a range (`table-range.ts` `armCellRangeDrag` already does this from an open cell); a Shift click extends one; the table moves by its ring band, its chip and its eight handles. Escape closes the cell and keeps the table selected; a second Escape clears the selection. With the table selected and no cell open (after Escape, or after a marquee, a Tab from another object or the filmstrip selected it), a printable key or Enter opens the remembered cell with the caret at the end and appends (2.2 rank 4). The arrows cross cells at the text edges; Bold, Italic, size, colour and Align on a range write every cell; every table command keeps the caret or the range. Seams between rows, a "+" on the right and bottom edges and the row and column heads are P1 (2.3). If Kevin keeps A1's first click for tables (question 8), the model costs one click more than Google to type in a cell, section 1 drops the caret from its list of first gestures, and the two rows of rank 2 change their interaction texts and no file list.

The chart. The grid owns its keys; a double click on the chart opens its numbers; a click on a bar, a point or a slice of the selected chart selects its cell; an Edit data button sits under the selected chart. The diagram: a step is one shape with its label, a double click opens the label, Tab moves to the next label, a drag moves the step and its connector follows, Delete removes it. Word art scales its letters with its box (P1); a shape's label is centred by default.

One undo per gesture is measured today and kept (audit-objects 4.3: a typed cell, a seam drag, an insert, a fill, a merge, a kind change and a removed category are each one Cmd+Z); every item below is one commit with one history entry through the editor's `commit(mutations, label)`, and a live preview during a drag never writes (the stepper rule of `docs/RETURN.md` section 6). Format options stays the complete second path and the right click menus keep every row they have (audit-objects 4.4); nothing in this section removes a route a seller knows.

### 4.2 The index route and its refresh

P0, B6 with B7 (audit-logos 10 and 13). ### 5.5 The resting still, its key, its storage and the exports

P0, B5 the client capture and B7 the hosted bound, the exporters and the web page (audit-shaders 2, 1, 9, 21; judge-design rejections 9 to 12 and additions 3 to 6). The resting rule for shaders: a commit of any recipe field, a resize of the box or a kit colour change schedules a capture 800 ms after the last change; a held slider or a held handle previews and never commits, so nothing captures until the release. The label never renders on a surface a viewer sees: before the first frame the block shows the live canvas in the editor and a plate with no text elsewhere; the filmstrip card shows the frame once it exists (audit-shaders 21).

The frame's shape. The still is rendered at the block's box aspect, not at 16:9, so the still equals the editor at rest for a box with `height` set (SPEC-5 0.3's resting rule; today `.material` is `aspect-ratio: 16 / 9` unless `height` sets it, the img `object-fit: cover` and the canvas fills the box, `block-css.ts` 81 to 83, and the object family fits the shape to its canvas, `catalog.ts` 41 to 47): the capture canvas has the long side 3200 and the short side from the box's aspect (3200 by 1800 for the default box, 3200 by 800 for a 4:1 band, 1800 by 3200 for a 9:16 box), and the same rule binds the hosted job.

The client's pixel path, from `capture.ts` 222 to 244 and `mount.ts` 31 to 32: `mountMaterial` with `contextAttributes: { preserveDrawingBuffer: true, antialias: false }` and `minPixelRatio: 2` on a hidden host sized to half the target in CSS px (`packages/viewer/src/shader-frame.ts`, new); wait for the first `ResizeObserver` callback (`parentWidth` set), clear `devicePixelsSupported` so a 1x display also reaches the target pixels, `setMinPixelRatio(2)`, wait until the canvas is the target size, `setFrame(anchor)`, then `canvas.toBlob('image/png')` in the same frame; the mount is disposed after the blob. The frame goes up as one write, `shader.frame { slideId, blockId, frameKey, bytes, baseRevision }` (5.8), whose hosted handler stores the asset, writes the block's `/asset` and removes the block's previous frame asset when no other block, slide background or kit names it, all in one revision; the asset's `source` is `{ kind: 'material', ..., backend: 'client', renderer: <the WebGL renderer string>, recipeKey, frameKey }`; when WebGL is unavailable the client asks the hosted `shader.capture` (the fallback and the agent's route), which records `backend: 'swiftshader'` or `'angle-metal'`.

The key. Two keys live on the asset. `recipeKey` stays what `capture.ts` line 3 defines, sha256 over materialId, uniforms, size, timeMs and backend, the capture's own identity. `frameKey` is new and is what decides staleness: sha256 over `materialId`, `preset`, the resolved uniforms, the anchor, the six palette colours resolved from the kit (5.7) and the box's aspect rounded to two decimals, without the pixel size and without the backend, so a client frame and a hosted frame of one recipe carry one `frameKey` and both are fresh (judge-design addition 5); the aspect is in the key because it decides the frame's shape, and a 1600 by 900 and a 3200 by 1800 capture share it. `frameKeyOf(block, kit)` in `packages/materials/src/recipe-key.ts` computes it from the document at read time, so the block stores nothing new; a frame is stale when `frameKeyOf(block, kit) !== asset.source.frameKey`, is still drawn, and is re captured, never regenerated silently (SPEC 5.4). `capture.test.ts` gains the case "a client frame and a hosted frame of one recipe are both fresh".

The storage rule (judge-design addition 6). The frame asset's id is `frame-<the first 16 hex of frameKey>`, so a repeated recipe reuses the asset, a slider dragged back to its value writes nothing (the id exists and the block already names it), and two clients capturing one key resolve to one asset (a `shader.frame` write naming an id whose stored digest equals the new bytes stores nothing and writes the block alone, and a `BlobExistsError` on the put is success). A superseded frame of the same block is removed by the `shader.frame` write itself when no other block, slide background or kit names it, in the same revision, so a block never keeps more than one frame. An orphan, an asset with `source.kind: 'material'` that no block, no slide background and no kit names (the frame of a deleted block, the frames a kit colour change re keyed across a deck), is removed by `pruneFrameAssets(client, deckId)` in `packages/store/src/frames.ts` (new), modelled on `pruneThumbs` (`blob-store.ts` 288) and on the snapshot prune's grace (`snapshots.ts` 28, `SNAPSHOT_GRACE_MS`): one `list` of the deck's `assets/` prefix, every unreferenced frame older than five minutes deleted from the store and from `deck.assets`, run by the `shader.frame` handler behind the response (`waitUntil`, the thumbnail route's pattern, `thumbs.ts` header); Cmd+Z past a removed frame restores the recipe, the frame is stale by key and re captured 800 ms later. The bytes per capture (the liquid metal diamond at 3200 by 1800 as PNG) are measured by `capture.test.ts` on the checkout's capture fixture and written into the ship note beside the prune's count, never typed here.

The capture in a shared editor (judge-design addition 3). The client whose own commit changed the recipe schedules the capture, from the editor shell's `commit()` path and never from the document observer, so a follower that receives the entry from the room never captures and draws the frame it receives through the `shader.frame` write that follows; the outside write shows the frame and fires no snackbar. The frame write is a system write: it goes through the controller's write queue (`controller.tsx` 2350 to 2352, `await idle()` then `baseRevision: latest().serverRevision`), so it serialises behind the seller's pending commits on this client and the seller's next commit sees its revision as the base; it pushes no history entry, so Cmd+Z skips it and restores the recipe alone; on a 409 (`ConflictError`, 2355 to 2360) the client re reads the head, drops the write when the block is gone or `frameKeyOf` no longer equals the frame's key, and retries once otherwise. The follower's own edit to the same shader is a new recipe commit, so it captures its own frame under a new key.

The hosted job (`material.capture` in the function's Chromium, `apps/studio/src/server/actions.ts` 690 to 707; the walk's Place ran 93 s and printed the Chromium command line into the dialog, audit-shaders 1) gets a 30 s bound in the render worker queue and answers `RenderError` with one sentence, "The frame could not be rendered. Try again, or place the shader without a frame"; the dialog shows that sentence and never a log, and Place reads "Placing" with the elapsed seconds.

The exporters (audit-shaders 9): the scene carries `shaders: [{ blockId, recipe, asset }]`; the PDF, the JPEG, the PNG and both PowerPoints draw the frame (`assets/<id>@2x.png`, the long side 3200) at the block's box, the Editable PowerPoint as a picture named `ts:<slide>#<block>` with the recipe in `descr`; a shader with no frame or a stale frame at export time is one row of the report ("2 shaders had no frame; the export waited 8 s for them") shown in the Download dialog before the file. The web page export (`file.download.html`, `model.ts` 989; built by `runTurboslide build` in `apps/studio/src/server/download.ts` 637 to 697, B7's file) carries the frame as the block's picture and mounts no live shader this round; round five's standalone motion script has no match on the checkout and is not named (judge-design rejection 10). The Paper credit line stays on the asset: "Shader: Liquid metal, Paper Shaders, rendered in Turboslide"; for the studio field, "Shader: Studio field, Prototemplate, rendered in Turboslide".
